## History

Cruz Azul began in 1927 as the works team of the Cooperativa La Cruz Azul cement company in Jasso, Hidalgo, and it remained a cooperative-owned club long after the rest of Mexican football was bought by television and business groups. Promoted to the top flight in 1964, the club moved to Mexico City in 1971 and became the dominant team of the decade: seven championships between 1969 and 1980 under the Hungarian coach Jorge Marik and then Raúl Cárdenas, with the Argentine goalkeeper Miguel Marín, the striker Horacio López Salgado and the midfielder Fernando Bustos. The nickname La Máquina, "the machine", came from the way that side ground opponents down.

The eighth title, in 1997 under Luis Fernando Tena, was won with Carlos Hermosillo's "golden goal" penalty in the final against León, after which he collapsed with a cut head and the trophy became one of Mexican football's iconic images. Then the title drought began. Cruz Azul reached the 2001 Copa Libertadores final against Boca Juniors as the first Mexican club to do so, and lost on penalties; it lost the Apertura 2008, Clausura 2008, Apertura 2009, Clausura 2013 (to a 93rd-minute goalkeeper's header for América) and Apertura 2018 finals. "Cruzazulear", to throw away a winning position, entered the language.

## Trajectory

The curse ended on 30 May 2021 when Juan Reynoso's team beat Santos Laguna 2-1 on aggregate in the Clausura 2021 final at the Azteca, the first title in 23 years and the ninth overall. Reynoso's side had been the best in the league for two years, including the Clausura 2020 that was abandoned by the pandemic with Cruz Azul top. The years since have been typical: a shambolic 2023 under a revolving door of coaches and a sporting director scandal, then a fine Clausura 2024 under Martín Anselmi, lost in the final to [América](/clubs/club-america).

Continentally the club's record is stronger than its domestic one. Seven CONCACAF Champions Cup titles (1969, 1970, 1971, 1996, 1997, 2014 and 2025) equal América's record, the last won 5-0 on aggregate against Vancouver Whitecaps in June 2025 under Vicente Sánchez. Since the cooperative's governance scandals of the early 2020s, the club has been run by Víctor Velázquez's board; it left its old Estadio Azul in 2018 for the Azteca, then returned to the renovated ground, now the Estadio Ciudad de los Deportes, in 2024 during the Azteca rebuild. The head coach for Apertura 2026 is not confirmed in the club's squad data as of November 2026, so none is listed here.

## Players the club produced

Cruz Azul's academy produced the goalkeeper Óscar "Conejo" Pérez, who played until 46, the midfielder Gerardo Torrado's successor generation of Jesús Corona and Roberto Alvarado, and, most recently, defenders sold to Europe such as Jorge Sánchez. The club's great imports were Marín and Bustos from Argentina, the Chilean Francisco "Chamagol" González, the Argentine-Mexican playmaker Christian "Chaco" Giménez, who became the symbol of the 2010s, and the Peruvian Yoshimar Yotún.

## Identity and playing style

La Máquina's self-image is of relentless, organised football rather than individual flair, and its best modern coaches, Reynoso and Anselmi, have both built possession-based 3-4-3 or 4-3-3 systems with a strong [high line](/tactics/high-line) and quick vertical passing through the Uruguayan Ignacio Rivero and the Argentine Lorenzo Faravelli. Ángel Sepúlveda and Carlos Rotondi have been the finishers, and the Colombian goalkeeper Kevin Mier the best signing of the 2024-25 period. The club's psychological identity, the fear of the final, has receded since 2021 but has not disappeared.

## Rivalries

The Clásico Joven with [América](/clubs/club-america) is the sharpest, a cross-city fixture that has produced the 2013 and 2024 finals and years of shared-stadium tension. Games with [Pumas UNAM](/clubs/pumas-unam), the other capital club, and with [Guadalajara](/clubs/guadalajara) are the next most heated.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Cruz Azul are third in the Apertura 2026 table on 29 points, level with América and Chivas, with Sepúlveda, Rotondi, Mier and Rivero as the core and the CONCACAF title of 2025 the most recent trophy. The manager position is not confirmed in the current squad data, a familiar sign of the club's instability. Supporters will expect the Liguilla in December to bring a tenth star.
