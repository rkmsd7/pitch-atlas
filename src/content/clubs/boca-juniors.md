## History

Boca Juniors was founded on 3 April 1905 by five Genoese immigrant teenagers in the dockside barrio of La Boca, and the Italian port origins survive in the nickname Xeneizes, from the Genoese word for Genoese. The blue and gold, legend says, were taken from the flag of the first ship to enter the harbour the day the founders could not agree on colours; the Swedish flag won. The club turned professional in 1931 and won the first professional championship with the striker Francisco Varallo, and it built La Bombonera, the "chocolate box" with its three steep stands and one flat side, in 1940 on a cramped plot in the barrio. The stadium is the club's identity: it is said to beat rather than shake.

Boca became "the club of half plus one" of Argentines in the 1940s and 1950s, the team of the working class and the interior, with Antonio Rattín as captain in the 1960s and the goalkeeper Hugo Gatti and the defender Roberto Mouzo, the all-time appearance leader, in the 1970s. Under Juan Carlos Lorenzo the club won its first Copa Libertadores in 1977, on penalties against Cruzeiro, retained it in 1978 and beat Borussia Mönchengladbach for the Intercontinental Cup. [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona) arrived from Argentinos Juniors in 1981, won the Metropolitano that year, left for Barcelona, and returned for a final, troubled spell in 1995-97 alongside [Carlos Bilardo](/managers/carlos-bilardo) and then Héctor Veira as coach.

## Trajectory

The club's greatest era began in 1998 with the appointment of Carlos Bianchi and the ownership of Mauricio Macri, who later became president of Argentina. Bianchi's Boca, with Juan Román Riquelme as the playmaker, Martín Palermo as the striker, Óscar Córdoba in goal and the brothers Guillermo and Gustavo Barros Schelotto, won the 1998 Apertura unbeaten, the 1999 Clausura, the 2000 Copa Libertadores against Palmeiras and, in November 2000, the Intercontinental Cup 2-1 against Real Madrid in Tokyo, Palermo scoring twice in the first six minutes against the galácticos. A second Libertadores followed in 2001 against Cruz Azul; Bianchi returned in 2003 to win a third, against Santos, and a second Intercontinental against Milan on penalties.

The run continued without him: the 2004 and 2005 Copa Sudamericana titles, the 2005 and 2006 league titles under Alfio Basile, the 2007 Copa Libertadores under Miguel Ángel Russo with Riquelme back from Villarreal and producing the best football of his life in the final against Grêmio. [Carlos Tevez](/players/carlos-tevez), an academy product, was the symbol of the 2003-04 team and returned in 2015 and 2018. Six Libertadores, four Recopas and three Intercontinental Cups between 1977 and 2008 make Boca the most decorated club in South American international competition after Independiente.

The 2018 Copa Libertadores final against [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate), the first Superclásico final, was the decade's defining event: the second leg was moved to Madrid after Boca's bus was attacked outside the Monumental, and River won 3-1 after extra time at the Bernabéu. Boca won the league in 2017-18, 2019-20 and 2022, the Copa de la Liga in 2020 and 2022, and reached the 2023 Libertadores final under Jorge Almirón, losing 2-1 to Fluminense at the Maracanã after beating Racing and Palmeiras on penalties.

Riquelme was elected president in December 2023 after four years as vice-president running the football department. His presidency has been turbulent: a sequence of coaches (Diego Martínez, Fernando Gago, Russo again), the first missed Libertadores qualification in years, and the June 2025 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup), where 50,000 Boca fans turned Miami into La Boca for a 2-2 draw with Benfica, a 2-1 defeat to Bayern Munich and a 1-1 draw with Auckland City that eliminated the club. Russo, suffering from cancer, died in October 2025 and Claudio Úbeda finished the year. The club's current squad data lists Rodolfo Arruabarrena, the former Boca left-back who coached the club in 2014-15, as head coach as of November 2026.

## Players the club produced

The academy at Casa Amarilla has produced Riquelme, Tevez, Fernando Gago, Éver Banega, Nicolás Gaitán, Leandro Paredes (who returned from Roma in 2025), Cristian Medina, Valentín Barco (to Brighton), Exequiel Zeballos and, among the older generation, Ángel Clemente Rojas, Alberto Tarantini, Óscar Ruggeri and Diego Latorre. The great signings were Varallo, Rattín, Gatti, [Maradona](/players/diego-maradona), Palermo (the all-time top scorer with 236 goals), the Barros Schelottos, the Colombian Córdoba, Juan Sebastián Verón's contemporaries Diego Cagna and Sebastián Battaglia, and Edinson Cavani, who joined in 2023.

## Identity and playing style

Boca's football identity is less a system than a temperament: "huevo", guts, and the Bombonera's demand for intensity, with the best teams combining a hard midfield (Battaglia, Cagna), a number ten (Riquelme) and a centre-forward who scores ugly goals (Palermo, Cavani). Bianchi's 4-3-1-2 was pragmatic, deep-defending and lethal on the counter, built on Riquelme's pauses and Palermo's finishing; it was the opposite of the River Plate school of [César Luis Menotti](/managers/cesar-luis-menotti) and closer to the [Bilardo](/managers/carlos-bilardo) tradition of results above aesthetics. Russo's and Úbeda's 2025 teams were a 4-3-3 built around Paredes' distribution, Merentiel's movement and Zeballos' dribbling; Arruabarrena has kept that core and asked for more pressing. See [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) for the family of approaches the club has favoured.

## Rivalries

The Superclásico with [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate) is the most intense derby in world football, the club of the port against the club that left the port for the rich northern suburbs, and it has been decided in Libertadores finals (2018), semi-finals (2004, 2019) and countless league matches; the 2004 semi-final, won by Boca on penalties after Tevez's chicken-dance celebration, and the Madrid final are the modern touchstones. Games with [Racing](/clubs/racing-club), [Independiente](/clubs/independiente) and [San Lorenzo](/clubs/san-lorenzo), the other members of the big five, complete the calendar, and the 2023 Libertadores defeat gave Fluminense a place in the club's demonology.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Boca are second in Group A of the Torneo Clausura with 30 points from 15 games, three behind Instituto, with the knockout phase of the tournament, the final weeks of the Argentine season, ahead. Arruabarrena is listed as head coach, Paredes captains a squad that includes Cavani, Merentiel, Palacios, Zeballos and the goalkeeper Agustín Marchesín, and the club has qualified for the 2027 Copa Libertadores through the annual table. Riquelme's presidency, the Bombonera expansion project and the eternal question of whether Boca can win a seventh Libertadores before River wins a fifth remain the stories.
