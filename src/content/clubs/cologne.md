## History

1. FC Köln were formed on 13 February 1948 by the merger of Kölner BC 01 and SpVgg Sülz 07, and within fifteen years were the best team in West Germany. They won the championship in 1962 under Zlatko Čajkovski and, as founder members, the first Bundesliga in 1963-64, with Hans Schäfer, Wolfgang Overath and Karl-Heinz Schnellinger. The goat mascot, Hennes, first given to the club in 1950 by a circus owner, has been succeeded nine times and is as recognisable as the badge.

Hennes Weisweiler, the former Gladbach coach, won the 1978 double with Dieter Müller (34 goals in 1976-77), Toni Schumacher, Heinz Flohe and the young Pierre Littbarski, Köln's second Bundesliga title and their last. Cup wins in 1977, 1978 and 1983, a UEFA Cup final in 1986 (lost to Real Madrid), and second-place finishes in 1989 and 1990 under Christoph Daum kept them among the elite into the 1990s, with Klaus Allofs and Littbarski the stars. Then the structure collapsed: Köln were relegated for the first time in 1998 and have been a yo-yo club ever since.

Six promotions and six relegations this century tell the story. [Lukas Podolski](/players/lukas-podolski) emerged in 2003-04 as a teenager scoring in a relegated side, left for Bayern and came back; Anthony Modeste's 25 goals under Peter Stöger took the club to fifth in 2017 and a Europa League trip to Arsenal that 20,000 fans attended; a year later they were bottom. Steffen Baumgart's 2021-22 side finished seventh and reached Europe again; a FIFA transfer ban over the signing of a Slovenian teenager contributed to relegation in 2024. Gerhard Struber, replaced by Friedhelm Funkel for the run-in, took them up as 2. Bundesliga champions in 2025, and Lukas Kwasniok steered the first season back to a safe mid-table finish.

## Trajectory

No club in Germany has moved between divisions more often this century: relegated in 2002, 2004, 2006, 2012, 2018 and 2024, promoted in 2003, 2005, 2008, 2014, 2019 and 2025. The highs (fifth in 2017, seventh in 2022) and the lows (bottom with 22 points in 2018) are extreme for a club with a 50,000 stadium and the fourth-largest membership in Germany. The 2025-26 season, with a ninth-place finish, was more settled than most, and René Wagner was appointed head coach for 2026-27.

## Players the club produced

Köln's academy has produced Overath, Schumacher, Littbarski, Thomas Häßler, Lukas Podolski, Jonas Hector (the long-serving captain and Germany left-back), Timo Horn, Yann Aurel Bisseck and, most famously of the recent era, [Florian Wirtz](/players/florian-wirtz), who left for Leverkusen at 16 in 2020 in a move the club still resents. Damion Downs and Jan Thielmann are recent graduates in the first team. Among signings, Dieter Müller, Klaus Allofs, Tony Woodcock, Modeste and goalkeeper Marvin Schwäbe stand out.

## Identity and playing style

Köln's identity is emotional rather than tactical: the carnival club, the goat, the "Mer stonn zo dir" anthem, and a crowd that fills the RheinEnergieStadion in the second division. The best modern Köln sides, under Stöger and Baumgart, were hard-pressing, [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) teams with a big striker and crosses from wide. Kwasniok's promotion-era side pressed high in a 3-4-3; René Wagner has continued with a [back three](/tactics/back-three), a compact block and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through fast wingers, with a focus on not conceding.

## Rivalries

The Rhine derby against [Borussia Mönchengladbach](/clubs/borussia-monchengladbach) is the one that matters, a rivalry dating to the 1960s between two clubs 30 kilometres apart. [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen), 20 kilometres up the river, are the works team Köln fans disdain and now envy. Fortuna Düsseldorf are the Rhineland neighbours, and the derbies with all three are among the loudest fixtures in German football.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Köln are 14th in the Bundesliga on nine points from eight games (two wins, three draws, three defeats), with nine goals scored and 12 conceded. They lost 1-0 at Frankfurt on 16 October, drew 0-0 at home to Schalke on 24 October and 1-1 at Union Berlin on 1 November. The next fixture is the Rhine derby at home to Leverkusen on 8 November, followed by a trip to Bayern on 21 November.

Wagner's side is hard to beat but short of goals; Timo Hübers, Eric Martel and Florian Kainz are the senior players and Downs the striker the club hopes will grow. The aim is a second consecutive season of safety, and the derby against Leverkusen is the measure the city cares about most.
