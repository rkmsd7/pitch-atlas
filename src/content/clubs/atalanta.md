## History

Atalanta were founded in 1907 by students in Bergamo who named the club after the Greek huntress and, after a 1920 merger with Bergamasca, settled on the black and blue stripes they still wear. For most of the 20th century the club was the archetypal Italian provincial side: a Coppa Italia in 1963, won 3-1 against Torino with a hat-trick from Angelo Domenghini, a Cup Winners' Cup semi-final in 1988 reached while in Serie B, and a 1990-91 UEFA Cup quarter-final under Emiliano Mondonico, and otherwise a cycle of relegations and promotions, with 13 trips to Serie B and even a 1981-82 season in Serie C1. The youth sector at Zingonia was the pride of the club, producing a stream of Italy internationals from Gaetano Scirea to Roberto Donadoni.

Everything changed with the appointment of [Gian Piero Gasperini](/managers/gian-piero-gasperini) in June 2016. Under the Percassi family, who had bought the club in 2010, and with the American billionaire Stephen Pagliuca as co-owner from 2022, Gasperini took a side that had finished 13th and turned it into the most consistently exciting team in Europe. Atalanta finished fourth, seventh, third, third, third, eighth, fifth, fourth and third in his nine seasons, reached the Champions League quarter-finals in 2020 in a tie against PSG lost in the last three minutes in an empty Lisbon stadium, and on 22 May 2024 won the Europa League, beating a Bayer Leverkusen side unbeaten in 51 matches 3-0 in Dublin with a hat-trick from Ademola Lookman. It was the club's first major trophy since 1963 and the first European trophy in its history.

## Trajectory

The 2000s were the yo-yo years: relegation in 2003, promotion in 2004, relegation in 2005, Serie B champions in 2006, relegation again in 2010 and a Serie B title in 2011 followed by a six-point deduction for the betting scandal in 2011-12. From 2011 the club has been in Serie A without interruption, and from 2017 it has finished in the European places every season but one. Gasperini left for Roma in June 2025 after nine years and 300 games. [Raffaele Palladino](/managers/raffaele-palladino), a former Atalanta striker who had impressed at Monza and Fiorentina, took over for 2025-26 and finished seventh, which was judged a failure; he was sacked on 9 June 2026 and [Maurizio Sarri](/managers/maurizio-sarri) was appointed six days later, the most famous coach the club has ever hired.

The Percassi model, young players bought from across Europe and sold two years later at a multiple, combined with the Zingonia academy's output, has made Atalanta profitable in every season since 2017 while playing in the Champions League in five of them. The club bought the Gewiss Stadium from the city in 2017 and rebuilt it stand by stand around a playing schedule, the first club in Italy to own its ground outside Juventus, Udinese and Sassuolo. Luca Percassi, the president's son, runs the club day to day; Pagliuca's Bain Capital money paid for the final phase of the stadium.

## Players the club produced

Zingonia's roll of honour is remarkable for a club of its size: Gaetano Scirea, Juventus' sweeper and the 1982 World Cup winner, Roberto Donadoni, Giampaolo Bellini, who played 403 games for the club, Riccardo Montolivo, Giacomo Bonaventura, Giorgio Scalvini, now a 22-year-old Italy international at the heart of the defence, and the Italy goalkeeper [Gianluigi Donnarumma](/players/gianluigi-donnarumma)'s predecessor Marco Sportiello. [Filippo Inzaghi](/players/filippo-inzaghi) scored 24 goals in 1996-97 on loan from Parma to win the Capocannoniere before Juventus bought him; Christian Vieri also played a season there. The Gasperini years were built on signings who became stars, Papu Gómez, Josip Iličić, Duván Zapata, Robin Gosens, Rafael Tolói, Marten de Roon, Teun Koopmeiners, sold to Juventus for €55m, Lookman and Ederson. Marco Palestra, an academy full-back, was sold to Chelsea for €57m in summer 2026, the latest in a pipeline that has funded a new stadium.

## Identity and playing style

Gasperini's Atalanta was the most imitated side in Italy: a 3-4-2-1 with man-to-man [pressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) all over the pitch, centre-backs who followed their men deep into the opposition half, wing-backs who played as wingers and two attacking midfielders who swapped positions constantly. It scored 98 league goals in 2019-20. Sarri has brought the opposite philosophy: a 4-3-3 of fixed positions, short passing and pressing triggers rather than man-marking, the "Sarrismo" that made Napoli beautiful and won a Scudetto at Juventus. The transition has been painful. Nikola Krstović, the Montenegrin striker signed from Lecce in 2025, has six league goals, Gianluca Scamacca is back from injury, Charles De Ketelaere plays off the striker, Ederson and the returning Franck Kessié, signed free from Al-Ahli, run the midfield, and Eljif Elmas and Jonathan Rowe are on loan. Giacomo Raspadori and Kamaldeen Sulemana give Sarri options wide.

## Rivalries

The oldest enmity is the Lombard derby with Brescia, a fixture now in abeyance with Brescia in the lower leagues after their 2025 bankruptcy. Atalanta's ultras have a long friendship with Eintracht Frankfurt's and historic hostility to [Roma](/clubs/roma), Napoli, Verona and Fiorentina. The games against [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) and [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) are regional derbies that Atalanta have won often enough since 2017 to make them genuine rivalries, and Gasperini's return with Roma, a 1-0 Roma win in Bergamo in September 2026, had the feel of a family quarrel.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Atalanta are 13th in Serie A with 11 points from ten matches, three wins, two draws and five defeats, and have conceded 20 goals, a figure that would have been unthinkable in the Gasperini years. The 0-3 home defeat to Parma and the 4-4 draw with Frosinone are the low points; wins over Sassuolo, Bologna and Venezia keep them clear of trouble. The club is in the Conference League for the first time, where a 3-1 win over Pafos was the best result of the opening phase, and the Coppa Italia round of 16 against Udinese is scheduled for January.

Sarri's job is not yet under public threat, Antonio Percassi having said in October that the club will give the new system time, but the squad is also in transition: de Roon and Palestra are gone, Berat Djimsiti left for Saudi Arabia, and the summer signings Thomas Kristensen, from Udinese for around €22m, and Gianluca Gaetano, from Cagliari, have not yet settled. The redeveloped Gewiss Stadium, finished in 2024 with capacity around 25,000, is the lasting monument of the Gasperini decade; the question now is whether the club can be something other than his creation.
