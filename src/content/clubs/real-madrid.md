## History

Real Madrid were founded on 6 March 1902 as Madrid Football Club, a group of students and Catalan-born enthusiasts meeting above a shop on Calle Alcalá. King Alfonso XIII granted the "Real" title in 1920. They won the first Copa del Rey editions in a cluster (1905-08) and were one of the founding clubs of [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) in 1929, but for the first half-century they were not Spain's dominant force; Athletic, Atlético and Barcelona all had stronger spells.

The club's modern identity was built by Santiago Bernabéu, a former player who became president in 1943 and spent the post-war years constructing a stadium far too big for the moment (opened 1947, later named after him) and then filling it with the finest footballers on earth. The signing of [Alfredo Di Stéfano](/players/alfredo-di-stefano) in 1953, after a tug-of-war with Barcelona, changed European football. Around him Bernabéu assembled Ferenc Puskás, Francisco Gento, Raymond Kopa and Héctor Rial, and Madrid won the first five European Cups from 1956 to 1960, the last of them the 7-3 destruction of Eintracht Frankfurt at Hampden Park that remains the benchmark final. A sixth came in 1966 with the home-grown "Yé-yé" side.

The 1980s belonged to the Quinta del Buitre, five academy graduates led by Emilio Butragueño alongside the Mexican striker Hugo Sánchez, who won five straight league titles (1986-90) and two UEFA Cups (1985, 1986) but repeatedly fell short in the European Cup. The 32-year wait for the continental title ended in 1998 under Jupp Heynckes, with Predrag Mijatović's goal against Juventus, and the Galácticos era followed: Florentino Pérez won the presidency in 2000 by promising [Luís Figo](/players/luis-figo) from Barcelona, then added [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane), [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) and David Beckham. Zidane's volley at Hampden in the 2002 final won the ninth European Cup.

The years between 2003 and 2006 were a trophyless lull, and the arrival of [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) in 2010 was a response to [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola)'s Barcelona. Mourinho's 2011-12 side set a then-record 100 points and 121 goals. The decade that followed was the most successful in the club's history: La Décima in 2014 under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti), three consecutive Champions Leagues from 2016 to 2018 under [Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager), with [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo), [Sergio Ramos](/players/sergio-ramos), [Luka Modrić](/players/luka-modric) and [Toni Kroos](/players/toni-kroos) at its core, then the fourteenth in 2022 and the fifteenth in 2024, the latter won by a new generation of [Vinícius Júnior](/players/vinicius-junior), [Jude Bellingham](/players/jude-bellingham) and [Rodrygo](/players/rodrygo).

Three other sides deserve their own line. The 1960s "Yé-yé" team of Amancio, Pirri, Velázquez and Zoco won five titles in six years and the 1966 European Cup with an all-Spanish eleven, the last for a generation. The 1980s Quinta won with Hugo Sánchez scoring 38 goals in 1989-90, every one of them with a single touch. And the 2016-18 team, which went to Cardiff and beat Juventus 4-1 and then to Kyiv to beat Liverpool 3-1 with Gareth Bale's overhead kick, won three European Cups in a row, something no club had done since Bayern in 1976 and no club is likely to repeat.

## Trajectory

Madrid have never been relegated and have finished outside the top four in La Liga only a handful of times in the last 70 years. The twenty-first century divides cleanly. From 2000-01 to 2002-03 they were champions twice and European champions twice. A four-year gap followed before Fabio Capello's 2006-07 title and Bernd Schuster's 2007-08 defence, after which Guardiola's Barcelona pushed them into second place three years running despite 96 points in 2009-10. Mourinho's 2012 title broke that spell, Ancelotti's 2014 side won La Décima but finished third, and from 2016-17 to 2023-24 the club won five league titles in eight seasons, interleaved with the three-peat and two further Champions Leagues.

In Europe the record is simply unmatched: 15 European Cups, including six in the nine seasons from 2014 to 2022 and five in the nine seasons from 2016 to 2024. The club also claimed the FIFA Intercontinental Cup in December 2024 and reached the semi-finals of the expanded Club World Cup in 2025.

The most recent two seasons were comparative disappointments by Bernabéu standards. [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) replaced Ancelotti in June 2025 but left by mutual consent on 12 January 2026 with the team second; Álvaro Arbeloa finished the season, Madrid again finished runners-up, eight points behind [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), and lost the Supercopa final 3-2 to Barcelona in Jeddah. [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe) was the league's top scorer with 25 goals. Mourinho was appointed on 13 July 2026, thirteen years after his first spell ended.

A note on the Champions League record since 2014, because it explains the club's self-image. In 2014 Madrid beat Atlético 4-1 in Lisbon after Sergio Ramos's 93rd-minute header; in 2016 they beat Atlético again on penalties in Milan; in 2017 they beat Juventus 4-1 in Cardiff with two from Cristiano Ronaldo; in 2018 they beat Liverpool 3-1 in Kyiv; in 2022 they came through PSG (Benzema hat-trick in 17 minutes), Chelsea and Manchester City (two Rodrygo goals in the 90th and 91st minutes) to beat Liverpool 1-0 in Paris; and in 2024 they beat Dortmund 2-0 at Wembley with Carvajal and Vinícius scoring. The 2025 and 2026 campaigns ended in the quarter-finals, to Arsenal and then to the eventual winners PSG, which is part of why Mourinho was recalled.

## Players the club produced

Madrid's reputation is for buying stars, but the academy at Valdebebas has produced more than it is credited for. The Quinta del Buitre (Butragueño, Míchel, Martín Vázquez, Sanchís, Pardeza) were all La Fábrica graduates. [Raúl](/players/raul) came through in 1994 and became the club's all-time top scorer until Ronaldo, [Iker Casillas](/players/iker-casillas) kept goal for the ninth and tenth European Cups, and Guti, Álvaro Arbeloa, Dani Carvajal, Nacho and Lucas Vázquez all won Champions Leagues as home-grown players. More recently Carvajal left in summer 2026 after 12 years and joined Getafe in October.

The signings are the stuff of legend: [Di Stéfano](/players/alfredo-di-stefano), Puskás, Figo, Zidane, Ronaldo, Beckham, [Roberto Carlos](/players/roberto-carlos), [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo), [Modrić](/players/luka-modric), [Kroos](/players/toni-kroos), [Benzema](/players/karim-benzema), [Vinícius](/players/vinicius-junior), [Bellingham](/players/jude-bellingham), [Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe). Spain's 2026 World Cup-winning squad contained Madrid's Dean Huijsen and Dani Carvajal, and the Brazilian core of Vinícius, Rodrygo and Éder Militão remains the spine of the Seleção.

## Identity and playing style

Madrid's house style is harder to pin down than Barcelona's because the club has always valued winning over method. Its truest identity is the counter-attack and the comeback: the Bernabéu's belief that any deficit can be overturned in a frenzied 20 minutes, from the 1980s UEFA Cup nights to the 2022 run past PSG, Chelsea and Manchester City. Mourinho's first spell was the purest expression of [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) football at the top level, Ancelotti's teams blended calm possession with [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into the wide forwards, and Zidane's three-peat side was the model of game management.

Mourinho's second spell, as of November 2026, has started with a compact mid-block, a double pivot of [Aurélien Tchouaméni](/players/aurelien-tchouameni) and [Federico Valverde](/players/federico-valverde), and rapid transitions through Mbappé, Vinícius and the €125m summer signing Yan Diomande from RB Leipzig. The coach has repeatedly asked for patience with the 19-year-old Ivorian, who scored on his first league start in the 3-0 win at Racing Santander on 1 November, Madrid's sixth consecutive victory. Bellingham remains the free-roaming number 8 and Thibaut Courtois the last line. [Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) are already more rehearsed than in the Ancelotti years.

The Bernabéu itself is part of the identity. Rebuilt between 2019 and 2024 with a retractable roof, a retractable pitch and a 360-degree screen, it now hosts concerts and NFL games as well as football, and the club's revenues, above €1bn a year, are the highest in the sport. Florentino Pérez, president since 2000 (with a gap from 2006 to 2009), has run the club on the principle that the best players should always play in white, and the stadium is the stage he built for them.

## Rivalries

The Clásico against [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) is the biggest fixture in club football, loaded with the politics of Madrid versus Catalonia, Di Stéfano's contested transfer, Figo's 2000 defection and the Mourinho-Guardiola years. The derby with [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) has sharpened since [Diego Simeone](/managers/diego-simeone) arrived: the 2014 and 2016 Champions League finals, both won by Madrid, are the defining chapters. [Athletic Bilbao](/clubs/athletic-bilbao) are the old rival of the 1930s and the club Madrid have played most often. In Europe the great modern rivalry is with [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), met in 2012, 2014, 2017, 2018 and the 2024 semi-final.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Madrid sit third in [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) after 11 games, with 27 points, behind Barcelona (30) and Atlético (28). The defining result of the autumn was the Clásico win at Camp Nou on 25 October, matchday 10, Mourinho's first competitive Clásico since 2013, after which he told his players not to go over the moon. Madrid beat Inter 2-1 at home in the opening Champions League match and Leipzig 4-1 before the Clásico. Valencia at Mestalla follow on 8 November.

The summer rebuild was substantial: Diomande from Leipzig, Bernardo Silva from Manchester City, Ibrahima Konaté from Liverpool, Marc Cucurella and Denzel Dumfries in defence, alongside the 2025 arrivals Trent Alexander-Arnold, Dean Huijsen and Álvaro Carreras. Carvajal, Lucas Vázquez and Modrić are gone; Mbappé, fresh from the 2026 World Cup Golden Boot (10 goals), finished second to Harry Kane in the 2026 Ballon d'Or. Mourinho's contract runs to 2029 and the brief is unchanged: the 37th league title and a sixteenth European Cup.
