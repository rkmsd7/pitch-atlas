## History

Internazionale was born out of an argument. On 9 March 1908 a group of members of the Milan Cricket and Football Club, unhappy that the parent club refused to field foreign players, walked out of a meeting at the Orologio restaurant and founded a club whose very name declared that it would be open to the world. Within two years Inter had their first title, in 1909-10, with a Swiss captain and a squad that mixed Italians with expatriates. The fascist regime forced a merger and a rename, Ambrosiana-Inter, in 1928, and under that name the club won the 1930 and 1938 Scudetti, the second with [Giuseppe Meazza](/players/giuseppe-meazza) at his peak. Meazza, the Milanese boy who scored 284 goals for the club and won two World Cups for [Italy](/nations/italy), remains the player the stadium is named after.

The defining era came under Helenio Herrera in the 1960s. His Grande Inter, built on the [catenaccio](/tactics/catenaccio) of a sweeper behind man-markers and the lightning counter-attacks of Jair and Sandro Mazzola, won three league titles and two European Cups in 1964 and 1965, beating [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) in Vienna and [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) at a waterlogged San Siro. Giacinto Facchetti, the attacking left-back who invented the role, captained that side and later became club president. The two Intercontinental Cups won against Independiente in those years are still on the honours list.

The decades that followed were strange for a club of Inter's size. There were titles in 1971 and 1980, and the 1988-89 "team of the record" under Giovanni Trapattoni, with [Lothar Matthäus](/players/lothar-matthaus), Andreas Brehme and Jürgen Klinsmann, which collected 58 points from 34 games. Three UEFA Cups arrived in 1991, 1994 and 1998, the last with [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) scoring in the final against Lazio. But under Massimo Moratti, who bought the club in 1995 and spent lavishly, Inter went 17 years without a Scudetto while [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) and [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) hoarded them. The 5 May 2002 collapse at Lazio, which handed the title to Juventus on the final day, is the low point most supporters still cannot discuss calmly.

Calciopoli changed everything. With Juventus relegated and Milan docked points, Inter were awarded the 2005-06 title in the courts and then won the next four on the pitch under Roberto Mancini and [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho). The 2009-10 season was the summit: Mourinho's side beat [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea), CSKA Moscow, [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in a semi-final remembered for ten men defending the Camp Nou, and [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) 2-0 in Madrid with two goals from Diego Milito, to complete the first treble by an Italian club. [Javier Zanetti](/players/javier-zanetti), the captain who played 858 games for Inter, lifted the trophy. [Samuel Eto'o](/players/samuel-etoo) and [Wesley Sneijder](/players/wesley-sneijder) were the other pillars of that side.

The Moratti era ended in 2013 and a decade of foreign ownership, first Erick Thohir, then Suning, brought instability and a Champions League drought before Antonio Conte's 2020-21 title. [Simone Inzaghi](/managers/simone-inzaghi) added a 20th Scudetto, the second star, in 2023-24 and took Inter to two Champions League finals, losing to [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) in 2023 and heavily to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) in Munich in 2025. Oaktree Capital took control from Suning in May 2024 when the Zhang family defaulted on a loan.

## Trajectory

Inter are the only club to have played every season of Serie A since the national league began in 1929, a record that is the club's quiet point of pride against both Milan neighbours and Juventus. Within that unbroken top-flight run there have been long valleys. Between the 1989 title and the 2006 court-awarded one Inter finished as low as 13th in 1993-94, the year they also won the UEFA Cup, and spent most of the Moratti years finishing between third and eighth while spending more than anyone in Italy.

The 2006-2010 run of five straight titles was followed by a slow decline: sixth in 2011-12 and ninth in 2012-13, Inter's worst finish of the century, while Juventus began their nine-year reign. The club clawed back to the Champions League places from 2018 and has been first or second in six of the last seven seasons. The 2025-26 title, won with 87 points and 89 goals under [Cristian Chivu](/managers/cristian-chivu), the club's former defender who was promoted from the youth sector in the summer of 2025 after Inzaghi left for Saudi Arabia, was the 21st in the club's history. Inter also won a tenth Coppa Italia, beating [Lazio](/clubs/lazio) 2-0 in the final on 13 May 2026.

In Europe the club has been a constant presence in the knockout rounds since 2021, and the 2026-27 league phase began with a 3-0 win over [Feyenoord](/clubs/feyenoord) on 3 November.

## Players the club produced

Inter's identity has always been about who they bought as much as who they bred. [Giuseppe Meazza](/players/giuseppe-meazza), a product of the youth ranks, is the greatest home-grown figure. Sandro Mazzola, son of the Grande Torino captain Valentino, came through to lead the attack of Herrera's side. Giacinto Facchetti was signed as a teenager from Trevigliese and became the model modern full-back. Walter Zenga, Giuseppe Bergomi, who played in the 1982 World Cup final at 18 and spent his whole career at the club, and later Marco Materazzi were the backbone of the defence across different eras.

The foreign imports are the ones who define each decade: Matthäus and Brehme in the late 1980s, the Brazilian [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) in 1997 and Christian Vieri, Zanetti and Iván Córdoba, the Argentine spine of the 2000s, then Eto'o, Sneijder, Milito and Júlio César for the treble. Mario Balotelli and Davide Santon came out of the academy in that era. More recently the club has produced or polished [Nicolò Barella](/players/nicolo-barella), signed from Cagliari in 2019 and the engine of three title-winning midfields, [Lautaro Martínez](/players/lautaro-martinez), bought from Racing in 2018 and now captain and the club's third-highest scorer, Alessandro Bastoni, Federico Dimarco, a lifelong Interista from the academy, and Pio Esposito, the 21-year-old striker who graduated from the youth system in 2025 and scored in the 2025-26 title run.

## Identity and playing style

Inter's defining tactical word is still [catenaccio](/tactics/catenaccio), a legacy of Herrera, even if the modern club plays nothing like it. Inzaghi's 3-5-2, with its wing-backs high, Çalhanoğlu dictating from deep and the strikers combining in tight spaces, turned Inter into the most fluent passing side in Serie A, and Chivu kept the [back three](/tactics/back-three) structure while demanding more aggressive [pressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) and quicker vertical passing. The 2025-26 champions scored 89 league goals, the club's highest in the three-points era, with Marcus Thuram and Lautaro sharing the load and Bastoni's long diagonals from the left of the three a trademark.

The summer of 2026 saw an unusual Premier League influx: [Manuel Akanji](/players/manuel-akanji) and John Stones from [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city), Curtis Jones from [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) for €30m and Djed Spence from [Tottenham](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur), while Denzel Dumfries left for [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) and Davide Frattesi went on loan to Lazio. Chivu's Inter remain a possession side built around Barella's energy and Çalhanoğlu's distribution, with Stones sometimes stepping into midfield from the right of the three in the [inverted](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) way Guardiola taught him.

## Rivalries

The Derby della Madonnina against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan), named for the golden Madonna on the Duomo, is the only derby in Europe where both clubs share one stadium and still argue about who owns the city. Inter won six in a row across 2023-24 and ended Milan's title defence with a derby win that sealed the second star in April 2024; Milan answered with a 3-1 win on 31 October 2026, Inter's only league defeat so far this season. The Derby d'Italia with [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), a name coined by Gianni Brera in 1967, became bitter after Calciopoli and the 1998 Iuliano-Ronaldo penalty controversy, and the two clubs' supporters have never reconciled over the 2006 title. Games against [Napoli](/clubs/napoli) and [Roma](/clubs/roma) have decided recent title races, and the 2-2 draw with Roma in September 2026 was the first time Gasperini's side had taken points at San Siro in three attempts.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Inter are second in Serie A on 25 points from ten matches, three behind the surprise leaders Lazio and one ahead of Roma, with Marcus Thuram top of the league scoring charts on five goals. The 3-1 derby defeat to Milan on 31 October was the first league loss of the title defence; the week before, Inter had beaten Monza 4-1 and the Champions League league phase began well with the 3-0 win over Feyenoord. Chivu, who won the Scudetto in his first full season, has the Supercoppa Italiana final on 22 December as the next trophy on offer, with Inter qualifying as champions.

The squad is older in places, with Henrikh Mkhitaryan at 37 and Çalhanoğlu and Piotr Zieliński both 32, which is why Marotta and Piero Ausilio pursued younger legs in Jones, Spence and the returning Aleksandar Stanković. The question for the season is whether Lautaro, Barella and Bastoni, the three players who have carried the club since 2019, can drive a second straight title under a coach still learning the job, and whether Inter can finally win a Champions League final after two defeats in three years.
