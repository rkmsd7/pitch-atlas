## History

Deportivo Alavés were founded in 1921 in Vitoria-Gasteiz, the capital of the Basque Country, and reached the top flight for the first time in 1930. They spent most of the century in the lower divisions, including a decade in Tercera in the 1980s, and did not return to [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) until 1998 under José Manuel Esnal "Mané", the coach who made the modern club.

The Mané era was extraordinary for a provincial side. Alavés finished sixth in 1999-2000, their best ever placing, and the following season reached the UEFA Cup final in Dortmund with a team of Jordi Cruyff, Javi Moreno, Cosmin Contra, Iván Alonso and Martín Astudillo. The 16 May 2001 final against Liverpool, lost 5-4 to a golden-goal own goal by Delfí Geli in the 117th minute after Alavés had come from 3-1 and 4-3 down, remains the most celebrated European final of the era. Mané left in 2002, the club was relegated in 2003, and Dmitry Piterman, the Ukrainian-American owner who insisted on picking the team from the dugout, presided over a promotion in 2005 and a chaotic relegation in 2006.

The decline went as far as Segunda B in 2009, where Alavés spent four seasons, before the Querejeta family's ownership rebuilt the club: promotion in 2013, the Segunda title in 2016, and under Mauricio Pellegrino an unforgettable 2016-17 season with a 2-1 win at Camp Nou and a Copa del Rey final against Barcelona, lost 3-1. Six seasons in La Liga ended with relegation in 2022; Luis García Plaza brought them back through the 2023 play-offs, beating Levante on a 101st-minute winner in the final.

## Trajectory

Alavés have been relegated from the top flight four times since 2000 (2003, 2006, 2022 and the 2009 drop from Segunda) and promoted four times (2005, 2013, 2016, 2023). Since the 2023 return they have finished tenth, 15th and, in 2025-26, 14th on 43 points, in a season of change: Eduardo Coudet, the Argentine who had taken over in 2024, left by mutual consent on 3 March 2026 with the team 16th, and Quique Sánchez Flores, the former Atlético, Valencia, Watford and Sevilla coach, was appointed the same day on a contract to 2028 and secured survival.

## Players the club produced

Alavés's own academy has rarely supplied the first team's stars; the local products of note are Manu García, the captain and midfielder who returned from a decade away to lead the 2016 promotion, and Víctor Laguardia, the long-serving centre-back. The club's identity comes from bargains and loans: the 2001 side was assembled cheaply, Theo Hernández's 2016-17 loan from Atlético launched a Milan and France career, Marcos Llorente and Rodrigo Battaglia passed through, and Joselu's 2019-20 loan preceded his Real Madrid Champions League heroics. Fernando Pacheco was the goalkeeper of the 2016-22 era.

## Identity and playing style

Alavés are a defensive, hard-working club by necessity. Mané's teams were compact 4-4-2 sides that counter-attacked with pace; Pellegrino's were organised and opportunistic; Coudet attempted a more possession-based game with mixed results. Quique Sánchez Flores, 61 and in his second decade as a coach, is a mid-block specialist whose Watford and Sevilla sides defended deep and played [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into a physical striker. His Alavés use a 4-4-2 or 4-2-3-1 with Toni Martínez, Lucas Boyé and Mariano Díaz rotating up front, Carles Aleñá and Denis Suárez providing technical quality in midfield and Antonio Sivera in goal, and the team's best moments come from set pieces and transitions. José Luis Oltra joined as assistant for 2026-27.

## Rivalries

The Basque hierarchy places Alavés below [Athletic](/clubs/athletic-bilbao) and [Real Sociedad](/clubs/real-sociedad), and matches against both are the biggest at Mendizorrotza. Eibar, 50 kilometres away, are the closest geographical rival, and [Osasuna](/clubs/osasuna) of Navarre complete the regional set. Liverpool, oddly, are the club's most famous opponent.

## Current situation

Alavés are ninth on 7 November 2026 with 13 points from 12 games (three wins, four draws, five defeats), a position flattered by an extra game played but comfortably above the bottom three. Sánchez Flores's second season has been steady: a draw at home to Málaga in October, a mid-table rhythm and a squad built around the experienced spine of Sivera, Jonny, Nahuel Tenaglia, Facundo Garcés, Aleñá, Suárez and the forwards Boyé and Martínez.

The target, as in every year since 2023, is survival with a margin, and the club's calm ownership under Alfonso Fernández de Trocóniz gives the coach the stability he did not always have at bigger clubs.
