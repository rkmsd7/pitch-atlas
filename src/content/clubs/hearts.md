## History

Heart of Midlothian were founded in 1874, taking their name from a dance hall which in turn had borrowed it from Walter Scott's novel and the old Edinburgh tolbooth, and they are the oldest of the capital's senior clubs. Hearts won two titles before the First World War with Bobby Walker, the first great Scottish forward, and in 1914 almost the entire first team enlisted together in McCrae's Battalion; seven of them died on the Western Front, and the memorial at Haymarket is a central part of the club's identity.

The golden age came after the Second World War under Tommy Walker, with the "Terrible Trio" of Alfie Conn, Willie Bauld and Jimmy Wardhaugh and, later, Dave Mackay in midfield before his move to Tottenham. Hearts won the League Cup in 1954, the Scottish Cup in 1956, the title in 1958 with a record 132 goals and only one defeat, and the championship again in 1960. No Scottish club outside the Old Firm has won two titles so close together since.

The decades that followed were leaner and included relegations in 1977, 1979 and 1981. Alex MacDonald's side came within seven minutes of the 1986 title, losing 2-0 at Dundee on the final day while Celtic won 5-0 at St Mirren, a day that still hurts in Gorgie. John Robertson, the club's record league scorer, Gary Mackay and Craig Levein defined that era. Jim Jefferies' 1998 Scottish Cup, won 2-1 against Rangers, ended a 36-year wait for a trophy.

The Vladimir Romanov era from 2005 brought both the best and worst of modern Hearts: a second-place finish and Scottish Cup in 2006 under George Burley and then Valdas Ivanauskas, the 5-1 cup-final demolition of Hibernian in 2012 with two goals from Rudi Skácel, and then administration, a points deduction and relegation in 2014. Ann Budge and the Foundation of Hearts, the supporter-owned body that now controls the club, rebuilt it: promotion in 2015, third place in 2016, another relegation when the 2019-20 season was curtailed, promotion again in 2021 under Robbie Neilson and third place in 2022 and 2024.

## Trajectory

Hearts have been the best non-Old Firm club in Scotland for most of the twenty-first century, with third-place finishes in 2003, 2004, 2009, 2011, 2016, 2022 and 2024 and a second in 2006. The two relegations, in 2014 and 2020, were both the product of circumstances off the pitch rather than form: administration in the first case, a curtailed season in which Hearts sat bottom in the second. Europe has brought Europa League and Conference League group stages in 2022-23 and 2023-24, and the 2025-26 season under Derek McInnes saw Hearts challenging in the upper reaches of the table before he left for Rangers.

## Players the club produced

Hearts' Riccarton academy has produced Craig Gordon, Scotland's goalkeeper for a generation, and Aaron Hickey, sold to Bologna at 18 before moving on to Brentford; Paul Hartley came through elsewhere but blossomed at Tynecastle. Earlier eras produced Gary Mackay, the club's record appearance holder, John Robertson and Gary Locke. Dave Mackay remains the most celebrated player to emerge from the club, and Lawrence Shankland, signed from Beerschot, became the most prolific striker of the modern era.

## Identity and playing style

Hearts' identity is Tynecastle: a tight, steep, noisy ground where the crowd is close enough to intimidate visiting teams, and a club culture built on physical, hard-running football. Modern Hearts under Robbie Neilson and Steven Naismith played a 3-4-3 or 4-2-3-1 with Shankland as the focal point and a strong emphasis on [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking). As of November 2026 the team is led by Wouter Vrancken, the Belgian coach who won the Belgian Cup with Mechelen, bringing a more possession-oriented approach.

## Rivalries

The Edinburgh derby against [Hibernian](/clubs/hibernian) is one of the oldest fixtures in world football, first played in 1875, and the 2012 Scottish Cup final remains the most important version of it. Matches against [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) and [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) are the measure of Hearts' ambition to break the Glasgow duopoly.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Hearts are third in the Scottish Premiership after eleven rounds, eight points behind Celtic and four behind Rangers, and well placed for the European spots. The club's aim under Vrancken is to consolidate as the third force, build on the 2025-26 campaign and mount a serious cup run; the squad retains Shankland and a core of experienced Scottish internationals.
