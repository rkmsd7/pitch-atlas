## History

Yokohama Marinos were founded in 1972 as Nissan Motors' works team and became one of the strongest sides of the late amateur era, winning the Japan Soccer League in 1989 and 1990 and the Emperor's Cup three years running, with Masami Ihara and Tetsuji Hashiratani in defence and the Argentine Ramón Díaz, the former River Plate striker, scoring the goals that made them the first J.League top scorer in 1993. The club was one of the "Original Ten" of 1993 and won the Asian Cup Winners' Cup in 1992 and 1993.

In 1999 the club absorbed the bankrupt city rival Yokohama Flügels, a merger forced by Flügels' sponsors and resented by both sets of fans; the F in the name is the only trace, and Yokohama FC was founded by Flügels supporters in protest. The merged club won the first stage of 2000 and, under Takeshi Okada, the future Japan coach, the titles of 2003 and 2004, winning both stages in 2003 with Shunsuke Nakamura's predecessors Daisuke Oku and Yuji Nakazawa, the great centre-back who played 593 league games. Nakamura, the club's academy product and Celtic's free-kick artist, returned in 2010 and nearly won the 2013 title, lost on the final day.

## Trajectory

In 2014 the City Football Group bought a minority stake, making Marinos a sister club of [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city), and in 2018 it appointed Ange Postecoglou. His 2019 champions played the most radical football the league had seen: a 4-3-3 with a goalkeeper 30 yards off his line, inverted full-backs, a high line and relentless attacking, winning the title with 68 goals and the Brazilian Marcos Júnior as top scorer. Postecoglou left for [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) in 2021 and his successor Kevin Muscat won the 2022 title in the same style, with Anderson Lopes and Élber the finishers.

Harry Kewell took the club to the 2024 AFC Champions League final, lost 6-3 on aggregate to Al Ain after a 2-1 first-leg win, and was sacked in the summer; the 2025 season was a crisis, with Steve Holland dismissed early and the club surviving relegation only in the final weeks under the interim Patrick Kisnorbo and then Steve Corica, the former Sydney FC coach. Corica is listed as head coach in the club's current squad data as of November 2026, and Marinos are seventh in the 2026-27 J1 League with 22 points from 14 games.

## Players the club produced

The Marinos academy produced Nakamura, Ihara, Shoji Jo, Yoshikatsu Kawaguchi, the goalkeeper of the 1998 and 2002 World Cups, Shunsuke Nakamura's contemporaries Daisuke Nasu and Koji Yamase, and more recently Kota Watanabe, Ryo Miyaichi's generation and the forward Asahi Uenaka. The imports who became legends were Díaz, the Spanish striker Julio Salinas, the Argentine David Bisconti, Marcos Júnior and the Brazilian trio of Anderson Lopes, Élber and Yan Matheus.

## Identity and playing style

Postecoglou's "attacking football" is the identity the club chose and has struggled to maintain: a 4-3-3 built on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs), a sweeper-keeper and a [high line](/tactics/high-line), the City Football Group model adapted to Japanese technical players. It produced two titles and some of the most thrilling matches in J.League history, and it also produced the 2025 collapse when the players could no longer sustain it. Corica has compromised, keeping the possession structure while dropping the defensive line and pressing more selectively, with Anderson Lopes and Yan Matheus the counter-attacking threats.

## Rivalries

The Kanagawa derby with [Kawasaki Frontale](/clubs/kawasaki-frontale), the dominant club of the late 2010s against the 2019 and 2022 champions, is the biggest in the prefecture, and the Yokohama derby with Yokohama FC carries the bitterness of the 1999 merger. [Urawa Red Diamonds](/clubs/urawa-red-diamonds) and [Kashima Antlers](/clubs/kashima-antlers) are the historic national rivals.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Marinos are seventh in the 2026-27 J1 League under Corica, four points off the top six, with Anderson Lopes, Yan Matheus, Watanabe, Uenaka and Élber as the core. The 2025 relegation scare shook the City Football Group's confidence in the project, and the club's task in the new autumn-spring calendar is to prove that Postecoglou's legacy can be a sustainable identity rather than a memory. The Nissan Stadium, the 2002 World Cup final venue, remains the largest in the league.
