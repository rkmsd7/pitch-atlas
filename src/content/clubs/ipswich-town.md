## History

Ipswich Town were founded in 1878, turned professional in 1936 and joined the Football League in 1938, which makes them one of the younger League clubs despite their age. The club's two great managers both went on to manage England. Alf Ramsey, appointed in 1955, won the Third Division South in 1957, the Second Division in 1961 and then, in 1961-62, the First Division at the first attempt, with Ray Crawford and Ted Phillips scoring 61 goals between them and a withdrawn-winger system that nobody in England had worked out how to play against. Ramsey left for the England job and the 1966 World Cup.

Bobby Robson arrived in 1969 and stayed 13 years. His Ipswich, built from the youth system and two Dutch midfielders, Arnold Mühren and Frans Thijssen, finished in the top six ten times, won the FA Cup in 1978 against Arsenal with Roger Osborne's goal, and the UEFA Cup in 1981 against AZ Alkmaar, with John Wark scoring 14 goals in the competition. They were runners-up in the league in 1981 and 1982 and would, Robson always said, have won it with a bigger squad. He too left for England. Mick Mills, Kevin Beattie, Terry Butcher, Paul Mariner, George Burley and Alan Brazil were the core.

Relegation in 1986 ended that era. John Lyall won the Second Division in 1992 and Ipswich were founder members of the Premier League, going down in 1995 with a 9-0 defeat at Manchester United on the way. George Burley's team, with Matt Holland, Marcus Stewart and Richard Wright, was promoted through the play-offs in 2000 and finished fifth in 2001, qualifying for the UEFA Cup, before relegation and administration in 2003. Marcus Evans bought the club in 2007 and spent 14 years going nowhere, including relegation to League One in 2019.

The American Gamechanger 20 group bought the club in 2021 and appointed Kieran McKenna in December of that year. McKenna won back-to-back promotions from League One and the Championship in 2023 and 2024 with a possession style that made Ipswich the most admired club outside the top flight. The Premier League season of 2024-25 ended in relegation in 19th. McKenna stepped down in June 2026 after winning promotion again at the first attempt, second behind Coventry on 84 points, with a 3-0 final-day win over QPR; [Gary O'Neil](/managers/gary-oneil), the former Bournemouth and Wolves manager, was appointed on a three-year contract.

## Trajectory

Ipswich's modern arc is 25 years of waiting between two promotions: fifth and Europe in 2001, relegation in 2002, then 17 seasons in the second tier with four play-off failures and finishes between third and 16th, before the collapse into League One in 2019. McKenna's three promotions in four seasons, interrupted by one Premier League relegation, have given the club a new generation of supporters and an owner willing to spend: the summer 2026 outlay was reported at around £153m.

## Players the club produced

Ipswich's academy is the foundation of the club's history: Mick Mills, Kevin Beattie, Terry Butcher, George Burley, Eric Gates, Jason Dozzell, who debuted at 16 in 1984, Kieron Dyer, Richard Wright, Titus Bramble, Darren Bent, Connor Wickham and, more recently, Andre Dozzell and Liam Gibbs. John Wark, Paul Mariner, Arnold Mühren and Frans Thijssen were signings who became legends; Matt Holland captained the 2000-01 side; Conor Chaplin and Leif Davis were the heart of the McKenna promotions.

## Identity and playing style

[Gary O'Neil](/managers/gary-oneil) kept Bournemouth up in 2023 and took Wolves to 14th in 2024 with a flexible back three or back four and an emphasis on structured pressing and set pieces; his teams score more from [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) than most. At Ipswich he has inherited McKenna's possession principles and added directness: Julio Enciso, the club-record signing from Strasbourg for around £26m, and Emersonn, from Toulouse for a fee reported between £24m and £26.5m, provide pace; Daizen Maeda, signed from Celtic, and Fatawu Issahaku add width; Chuba Akpom and Zian Flemming lead the line.

The midfield was rebuilt with Exequiel Palacios, Sasa Lukić from Fulham for about £9m, Florentino from Burnley, Azor Matusiwa and Jack Taylor; the defence with Issa Diop, Dara O'Shea and Cedric Kipré alongside Davis and Jacob Greaves; and Alex Palmer, Christian Walton and Kjell Scherpen compete in goal.

## Rivalries

The Old Farm derby with [Norwich City](/clubs/norwich-city) is one of the fiercest in England despite the 40 miles of Suffolk and Norfolk between the clubs; Ipswich's two seasons in the Premier League without Norwich have kept it on hold. [Colchester United](/clubs/colchester-united) are the smaller Essex neighbour.

## Current situation

Ipswich are 14th in early November 2026, with three wins, two draws and four defeats from nine: 14 goals scored and 18 conceded, 11 points. The run includes a 3-2 defeat at Manchester City on 17 October, 1-1 at home to Nottingham Forest on 23 October and 2-2 at Hull on 31 October. The attack has been productive and the defence porous, which is roughly what O'Neil's recruitment suggested.

The ambition, after 2024-25, is to stay up this time; with Coventry and Hull also promoted, the bottom of the table has a familiar shape and Ipswich's results against the division's middle class will decide the season.
