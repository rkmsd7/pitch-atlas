## History

Eintracht Frankfurt were founded in 1899 and took their current form through mergers in 1911 and 1920. Their one German championship came in 1959, a 5-3 win over Kickers Offenbach in Berlin, and it qualified them for the 1960 European Cup, where they beat Rangers 12-4 on aggregate in the semi-final before losing the Hampden final 7-3 to Real Madrid, the most celebrated final in the competition's history. Founder members of the Bundesliga, they finished third in its first season.

The 1970s brought the club's finest Bundesliga side: Jürgen Grabowski and Bernd Hölzenbein, World Cup winners in 1974, with cup wins in 1974, 1975 and 1981 and the 1980 UEFA Cup, won on away goals against Borussia Mönchengladbach in an all-German final. The 1992 "Fußball 2000" team of Uwe Bein, Andreas Möller and Tony Yeboah lost the title on the final day at Rostock and is still mourned. Jay-Jay Okocha's dribbles and Yeboah's goals lit up the mid-1990s before the first relegation in 1996.

The next two decades were spent on the yo-yo: relegated in 1996, 2001, 2004 and 2011, promoted in 1998, 2003, 2005 and 2012. Alexander Meier, the Bundesliga's top scorer in 2015 from midfield, was the constant. Niko Kovač won the 2018 DFB-Pokal, beating Bayern 3-1 in Berlin, the club's first trophy in 30 years. [Adi Hütter](/managers/adi-hutter)'s side, with the "Büffelherde" of Luka Jović, Sébastien Haller and Ante Rebić, reached the 2019 Europa League semi-final, losing on penalties to Chelsea, and [Oliver Glasner](/managers/oliver-glasner) went one better: the 2022 Europa League, beating Barcelona at the Camp Nou in front of 30,000 travelling fans, West Ham in the semi and Rangers on penalties in Seville on 18 May 2022, which brought the club into the Champions League for the first time.

Dino Toppmöller took over in 2023, sold Randal Kolo Muani to PSG for 95 million euros and Omar Marmoush to Manchester City for around 75 million, and finished third in 2024-25. The 2025-26 season unravelled: Toppmöller was sacked in January 2026 with the club seventh, Albert Riera lasted 14 games and left after the final-day draw with Stuttgart, and the board brought Hütter back from 1 July 2026 on a contract to 2029.

## Trajectory

Frankfurt's trajectory since 2012 is the most upward in the league outside Leipzig: from the 2. Bundesliga to a cup win in 2018, European semi-final in 2019, European trophy in 2022, Champions League in 2022-23 and a third-place finish in 2025. The club's turnover has tripled and the Waldstadion, expanded to 58,000, sells out. The 2025-26 collapse, from third to eighth with two coaches gone and the squad hollowed out by sales, was the first serious setback of the era, and Hütter's return is both a rescue mission and a bet on familiarity.

## Players the club produced

Grabowski and Hölzenbein, the 1974 World Cup winners, were products of the Frankfurt region; Andreas Möller was an academy graduate who left for Dortmund and returned. In recent years the club has been a developer-trader of the highest class: Jović (sold to Real Madrid), Haller (West Ham), Rebić (Milan), Evan Ndicka (Roma), Kolo Muani (PSG), Willian Pacho (PSG), Marmoush (City) and Hugo Ekitiké (Liverpool, summer 2025) all passed through in four seasons or fewer. Filip Kostić and Sebastian Rode were the heart of the Europa League-winning side; Kevin Trapp, in goal since 2018 for his second spell, is the captain.

The academy has produced Marcel Sobottka, Marco Russ, Ansgar Knauff (via Dortmund) and, latterly, Nathaniel Brown, Hugo Larsson and Can Uzun, the young core Hütter inherited.

## Identity and playing style

Frankfurt's football under Hütter and Glasner was [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) from a [back three](/tactics/back-three): a 3-4-2-1 with wing-backs who defend like full-backs and attack like wingers, a [high line](/tactics/high-line), and transitions of two or three passes into the forwards. Toppmöller moved to a more possession-based 4-2-3-1 and sold the club's goalscorers each summer. [Adi Hütter](/managers/adi-hutter) has restored the counter-pressing culture and the three-man defence the Waldstadion wants to see, while rebuilding the attack around younger players after the 2025 sales.

The identity beyond tactics is the support: the "Eintracht invasion" of Barcelona in 2022 and the 50,000 who travelled to Seville made the club's away following the most famous in Europe.

Sporting director Markus Krösche, appointed in 2021, built the trading model that funded the Europa League win and its aftermath, and the club's net transfer profit over the five years to 2025 was among the highest in Europe. The criticism, heard loudly in 2025-26, is that the model sells the goalscorers before the team can build on a good season, and the Hütter appointment was partly an answer to supporters who wanted stability over trading.

## Rivalries

The Rhine-Main derby against [Mainz](/clubs/mainz) is the most frequent, Kaiserslautern the most historic (the "Südwest-Derby" from the 1970s and 1980s), Darmstadt the closest geographically, and Kickers Offenbach the oldest grudge, now rarely played. [VfB Stuttgart](/clubs/vfb-stuttgart) have become the competitor for European places in recent seasons.

## Current situation

Hütter's second spell began in July 2026 with a three-year contract and a mandate to rebuild. As of 7 November 2026 Frankfurt are ninth in the Bundesliga on 11 points from eight games (three wins, two draws, three defeats, 13 scored, 18 conceded). They beat Köln 1-0 on 16 October, lost 1-0 at Dortmund on 24 October and beat Hamburg 3-1 at home on 1 November. The next match is at promoted Paderborn on 7 November.

The squad is younger than two years ago, with Trapp, Robin Koch and Mario Götze the senior figures and Larsson, Uzun and Brown the future. The aim for the season is a return to European football, and the mood is that the club has chosen a coach who knows exactly what the Waldstadion expects and has delivered it once already.
