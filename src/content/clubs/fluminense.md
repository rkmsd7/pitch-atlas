## History

Fluminense was founded in July 1902 by Oscar Cox, a Rio-born Anglo-Brazilian who had learned the game in Switzerland, and it was the aristocratic club of the city from the beginning: the laurel-wreathed badge, the clubhouse in Laranjeiras where the Brazil national team played its first match in 1914, and the nickname Pó de Arroz, "rice powder", from a mixed-race player who whitened his face to pass in the 1910s. The club won the first Carioca championship in 1906, and the 1911 defection of its football section created [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo), the club that has been its rival ever since.

The tricolor's great teams came in waves: Castilho, Didi and [Carlos Alberto](/players/carlos-alberto) in the 1950s and 1960s; the "Máquina Tricolor" of 1975-76 with Rivellino, Paulo César Caju, Gil and Carlos Alberto Torres, coached by Mário Travaglini and later by [Telê Santana](/managers/tele-santana), who had played for the club in the 1950s; Washington and Assis in 1984, when Fluminense won its second Brasileirão; and Fred, the club's modern icon, who scored the goals for the 2010 and 2012 titles under Muricy Ramalho and Abel Braga.

Between those peaks there were lows. In 1996 and 1997 the club was relegated twice in a row and spent 1999 in the third division, returning to the top flight in 2000 by a federation decision. In 2009 it escaped relegation from 20th with ten games left, and in 2013 it was saved only by Portuguesa's points deduction for fielding an ineligible player.

## Trajectory

The modern story begins with Fernando Diniz, appointed in 2022. Diniz's Fluminense played a style he called "apositional", which the English-speaking world named [relationism](/tactics/relationism): short passes, players clustering around the ball, Ganso and Jhon Arias improvising rather than holding positions. It was mocked, then admired, and in November 2023 it won the Copa Libertadores, 2-1 after extra time against Boca Juniors at the Maracanã, with Germán Cano and John Kennedy scoring. [Marcelo](/players/marcelo), who had returned from Real Madrid, and Felipe Melo lifted the trophy. The club lost the 2023 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) final 4-0 to Manchester City, won the 2024 Recopa against LDU Quito, then collapsed in the league and Diniz was sacked.

Mano Menezes kept the club up in 2024, and in 2025 Renato Portaluppi took the team to the United States for the expanded Club World Cup, where Thiago Silva, back at 40, anchored a defence that drew with Dortmund, beat Ulsan, drew with Mamelodi Sundowns, beat [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) 2-0 and Al Hilal 2-1, and lost the semi-final 2-0 to Chelsea. Renato left for Grêmio; the club's current squad data lists Marcão, the long-serving assistant and former centre-back, as head coach as of November 2026. Fluminense are fourth in the 2026 Brasileirão with 57 points from 34 games, in the Libertadores places.

## Players the club produced

Xerém, the academy, has produced a remarkable list: Thiago Silva (via Juventude and Porto to Milan, PSG and Chelsea, then home in 2024), Marcelo (sold to Real Madrid at 18), Fabinho (via Rio Ave to Monaco and Liverpool), Richarlison (via América Mineiro to Watford, Everton and Tottenham), Gerson, João Pedro (to Watford and Brighton), Kayky (to Manchester City), André (to Wolves), John Kennedy and the 2023 side's full-backs. [Carlos Alberto](/players/carlos-alberto), the 1970 captain, began there; Rivellino, Fred, Cano, Ganso, Washington and Assis were the great signings.

## Identity and playing style

Fluminense's self-image is elegance, "o clube das tradições", and Diniz's football, whatever its results, fitted it perfectly: possession as art, the goalkeeper Fábio playing as an outfielder, Ganso as the orchestrator. The current team retains much of that, with Marcão keeping Ganso and Arias as the creators, Cano as the poacher and Thiago Silva organising the back line, but with more pragmatism in defence. See [relationism](/tactics/relationism) and [Fernando Diniz](/managers/fernando-diniz) for the ideas the 2023 champions embodied.

## Rivalries

Fla-Flu against [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) is the most famous derby in Brazil, named by the journalist Mário Filho, played at the Maracanã since 1950 and contested with equal parts hostility and family intimacy, since both clubs share the stadium. The Clássico Vovô with [Botafogo](/clubs/botafogo) is the oldest fixture in Rio, dating from 1905, and the Clássico dos Gigantes with Vasco da Gama completes the set.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Fluminense are fourth in the Brasileirão under Marcão, on course for a Libertadores place, with Cano, Arias, Ganso, Fábio and Thiago Silva, now 42, as the senior core. The 2025 Club World Cup run restored the club's finances and reputation, and the aim for 2027 is another continental campaign and the retention of Arias, the Colombian who has been the best player in Brazil's league on several measures since 2023.
