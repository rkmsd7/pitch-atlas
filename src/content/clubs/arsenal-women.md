## History

Arsenal Ladies were founded in 1987 by kit man Vic Akers, who stayed as manager for 22 years and built the most successful club in English women's history. The peak was 2006-07: Arsenal won every competition they entered, including the UEFA Women's Cup, beating Umeå in the final with a squad featuring Kelly Smith, Faye White, Alex Scott and Jayne Ludlow. Eleven league titles came in 12 seasons between 1993 and 2012.

The professional era was harder. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women) and [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city-women) outspent Arsenal, who won just one WSL title, in 2018-19 under Joe Montemurro, with [Vivianne Miedema](/players/vivianne-miedema) scoring 22 goals. Jonas Eidevall's teams won two League Cups and reached the 2023 [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) semi-final but could not land the big trophies, and he resigned in October 2024.

Renée Slegers, his assistant, took over on an interim basis, was made permanent in January 2025 and six months later won the Champions League, beating Barcelona 1-0 in Lisbon through Stina Blackstenius's goal after a stunning semi-final comeback against [Lyon](/clubs/lyon-women). In 2025-26 Arsenal finished second to City in the league, lost just once in 22 games, were beaten by Lyon in the Champions League semi-final, and won the inaugural FIFA Women's Champions Cup. [Alessia Russo](/players/alessia-russo) scored a 30-minute hat-trick in a 5-2 north London derby in March 2026 and was named the club's player of the season.

## Trajectory

Arsenal have never finished outside the top three in the WSL era but have won the league only once since 2012. The move of all home league games to the Emirates from 2024-25, drawing crowds above 50,000, has given the club the biggest home advantage in the league and the revenues to match. Their attendance record, 60,000-plus for the 2025-26 derbies, is a league benchmark.

## Players the club produced

[Leah Williamson](/players/leah-williamson) is the ultimate Arsenal product, at the club since age nine and now England captain. Lotte Wubben-Moy and Michelle Agyemang, the Euro 2025 hero, came through the academy. Signings who became icons include Kelly Smith, Miedema, [Kim Little](/players/kim-little), Beth Mead, [Mariona Caldentey](/players/mariona-caldentey), Russo and [Chloe Kelly](/players/chloe-kelly), who began her career here before returning in 2025.

## Identity and playing style

Slegers plays a patient 4-3-3 built on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play): Little dictates from deep, Caldentey and Frida Maanum work the half-spaces, Caitlin Foord and Kelly provide the width and Russo links everything as a complete nine. Williamson steps into midfield from centre-back. The team's defining quality under Slegers is calm, with the 2025 comebacks against Real Madrid and Lyon showing a side that keeps its shape when behind. Daphne van Domselaar is a confident sweeper-keeper.

## Rivalries

Chelsea is the fixture that matters most, Tottenham the local derby, City the title rival of the moment. In Europe, Arsenal have now beaten Barcelona in a final and twice knocked out Lyon, creating a three-way rivalry at the top of the continental game.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Arsenal are chasing a first league title in eight years with largely the same group: Little, now 36, still captains, Williamson signed a two-year contract in April 2026, Olivia Smith, the first million-pound signing in women's football, is in her second season and Agyemang has broken into the first team. Slegers's contract runs through 2027.
