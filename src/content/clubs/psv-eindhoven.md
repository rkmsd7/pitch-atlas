## History

PSV were founded in 1913 as the sports club of the Philips electronics company, which explains the name, the stadium's location among the old factory buildings of Eindhoven and the club's lasting reputation as the best-run organisation in Dutch football. For decades they were the third force behind [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) and [Feyenoord](/clubs/feyenoord), winning titles in 1929, 1935, 1951 and 1963 but rarely threatening in Europe.

The breakthrough came in the 1970s under Kees Rijvers, with the Van de Kerkhof twins in midfield, and the 1978 UEFA Cup win over Bastia. Then came the great team of the late 1980s. With Philips money behind them, PSV signed [Ruud Gullit](/players/ruud-gullit) from Feyenoord in 1985, and after selling him to Milan for a world record fee two years later they still had Ronald Koeman, Eric Gerets, Søren Lerby, Wim Kieft and Hans van Breukelen. Guus Hiddink's side won the 1988 Treble, and the European Cup final against [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) in Stuttgart was a goalless draw settled on penalties, Van Breukelen saving from António Veloso. It remains the only European Cup won by a Dutch club other than Ajax or Feyenoord.

The 1990s were defined by extraordinary strikers rather than trophies: [Romário](/players/romario) scored 98 league goals in five seasons, and [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) arrived as an 18-year-old in 1994 and scored 54 in 57 games before Barcelona came calling. Hiddink returned in 2002 and built the most consistent PSV team of the modern era, with [Mark van Bommel](/managers/mark-van-bommel), [Park Ji-sung](/players/park-ji-sung), Phillip Cocu and [Arjen Robben](/players/arjen-robben), winning four titles in a row from 2005 to 2008 and reaching the 2005 Champions League semi-final, where they lost to AC Milan on away goals after a 3-1 win at the Philips Stadion.

After a quieter decade under Phillip Cocu and Mark van Bommel, in which titles in 2015, 2016 and 2018 were offset by failures in Europe, the club hired [Peter Bosz](/managers/peter-bosz) in 2023. His first season was one of the best in Dutch history: a record points total, more than 100 league goals and a title clinched with five games to spare. The championship was retained in 2025 after a late Ajax collapse and won again in 2025-26, this time by a distance.

## Trajectory

PSV have been the most consistent Dutch club of the twenty-first century. Since 2000 they have won 13 Eredivisie titles, more than Ajax's 10 in the same period, and have finished outside the top four only twice, in the curtailed 2019-20 season and in 2013-14. Their worst modern run was 2009 to 2014, five seasons without a title while Twente and Ajax took turns, but even then they never fell below fourth.

In Europe the record is thinner than the domestic one. The 2005 semi-final is the high-water mark since 1988; Bosz has taken the team into the Champions League knockout rounds but no further, and the club's business model, which relies on selling players such as [Cody Gakpo](/players/cody-gakpo), Xavi Simons and Johan Bakayoko at a profit, makes deep runs difficult to sustain. PSV finished third behind Feyenoord and Ajax in 2026-27's early-season table after eleven rounds, and are in the Champions League league phase.

## Players the club produced

PSV's academy has been productive without ever matching Ajax's volume, and the club's real gift has been spotting and developing young imports. [Romário](/players/romario) and [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) both arrived from Brazil as teenagers; [Park Ji-sung](/players/park-ji-sung) and Lee Young-pyo came from South Korea after the 2002 World Cup; [Arjen Robben](/players/arjen-robben) was bought from Groningen and sold to Chelsea two years later.

Home-grown products include Willy and René van de Kerkhof, Ronald Koeman's early years, [Ruud van Nistelrooy](/players/ruud-van-nistelrooy) (signed from Heerenveen and sold to Manchester United for a Dutch record), Ibrahim Afellay, Memphis Depay, [Cody Gakpo](/players/cody-gakpo) and Xavi Simons. Jaap Stam, Jan Heintze and Mateja Kežman all passed through the club on the way to bigger stages.

## Identity and playing style

[Peter Bosz](/managers/peter-bosz) is a Cruyff disciple who spent a career in Germany adding pressing to possession, and his PSV blend [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) and a [high line](/tactics/high-line). The team defends by attacking: the full-backs push into midfield, the wingers stay wide and high, and the two central midfielders are chosen for their ability to recover the ball quickly after it is lost. Set pieces and transitions, with Guus Til arriving late in the box, add a direct edge to a side that otherwise dominates possession.

The club's broader identity is organisational: PSV are the best-run club in the Netherlands, with technical director Earnest Stewart rewarded with a new contract after a period combining three titles, Champions League qualification and over €100 million in player sales.

The Philips connection is more than a name. The company still owns the stadium's naming rights and part of the surrounding land, and the club's stability through decades of Dutch football's financial crises owes much to that corporate patience. PSV have never been in serious financial difficulty, have never been relegated and have run one of the most reliable youth academies in the country, with De Herdgang producing a steady stream of internationals. Where Ajax sell a philosophy and Feyenoord sell passion, PSV sell competence, and in the modern Eredivisie that has often been enough.

## Rivalries

The rivalry with [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) is the defining fixture of the modern Eredivisie, a battle for championships rather than tribal hatred, played between the country's two best-resourced clubs. Matches against [Feyenoord](/clubs/feyenoord) are hostile in a different way, with Rotterdam's working-class identity set against the Philips club. Locally, the Brabant derby against Willem II is older than either.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 PSV are third in the Eredivisie, two points behind Feyenoord after eleven rounds, and sit in the lower reaches of the Champions League league phase with four points from four matches. Bosz, now in his fourth season and 63 years old, has said PSV could be where he finishes his career; a fourth consecutive title would make him the club's most decorated coach of the modern era. The question, as always in Eindhoven, is whether the squad can be regenerated quickly enough after each summer's sales to stay ahead of a resurgent Ajax under [Míchel](/managers/michel-sanchez) and a rebuilt Feyenoord under [Giovanni van Bronckhorst](/managers/giovanni-van-bronckhorst).
