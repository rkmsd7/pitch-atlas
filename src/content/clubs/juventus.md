## History

Juventus began as a group of schoolboys from the Liceo D'Azeglio who met on a bench in Corso Re Umberto in 1897 and chose a Latin name meaning "youth". The black and white stripes arrived in 1903, a gift of Notts County shirts shipped to Turin by an English member. The Agnelli family, owners of Fiat, took over in 1923 and have run the club ever since, which is the longest continuous family ownership in Italian sport and the reason Juventus has long been the club of workers who migrated to Turin from the south, following the factory and its football team.

Under Edoardo Agnelli and the coach Carlo Carcano, Juventus won five straight titles from 1931 to 1935, the Quinquennio d'Oro, with Giampiero Combi, Virginio Rosetta and Luis Monti the backbone of Italy's 1934 World Cup winners. Giampiero Boniperti, who joined in 1946, scored 182 goals, captained the club, and later served 19 years as president; the 1950s titles came with the Danish forwards John Hansen and Karl Praest, and the 1958 side had Omar Sívori and John Charles, the gentle Welsh giant whose statue stands outside the stadium.

Giovanni Trapattoni's decade, 1976 to 1986, produced six Scudetti and every European trophy. The spine was Italian and World Cup-winning: [Dino Zoff](/players/dino-zoff), Gaetano Scirea, Claudio Gentile, Antonio Cabrini, Marco Tardelli and [Paolo Rossi](/players/paolo-rossi), six of the eleven who beat West Germany in Madrid in 1982, with [Michel Platini](/players/michel-platini) arriving that summer to win three straight Ballons d'Or. The 1985 European Cup final at Heysel, won 1-0 against Liverpool with a Platini penalty, is remembered for the 39 people killed when a wall collapsed before kick-off; the trophy is the club's most uncomfortable.

[Roberto Baggio](/players/roberto-baggio), bought from Fiorentina in 1990 for a world-record fee that caused riots in Florence, won the 1993 UEFA Cup and the 1993 Ballon d'Or. Marcello Lippi's side of 1994 to 1999, with [Alessandro Del Piero](/players/alessandro-del-piero), [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane), Didier Deschamps and Edgar Davids, won the 1996 Champions League on penalties against Ajax in Rome and lost the next two finals to Dortmund and Real Madrid. Lippi returned to win titles in 2002 and 2003, the second ending with a Champions League final defeat to Milan at Old Trafford, and Fabio Capello added two more in 2005 and 2006 with [Pavel Nedvěd](/players/pavel-nedved), [Gianluigi Buffon](/players/gianluigi-buffon), Lilian Thuram and Zlatan Ibrahimović.

## Trajectory

Then came Calciopoli. In the summer of 2006 wiretaps revealed the general manager Luciano Moggi's network of influence over referee appointments. Juventus were stripped of the 2005 and 2006 titles, the 2006 one awarded to Inter, relegated to Serie B for the first time in their history and given a points deduction that started at 30 and was reduced to nine on appeal. Ibrahimović, Thuram, Cannavaro, Zambrotta and Emerson left; Buffon, Del Piero, Nedvěd, Trezeguet and Camoranesi stayed and won Serie B at a canter under Deschamps, playing in front of 20,000 at Rimini and Crotone. The club bounced back to third in 2007-08 but then drifted: seventh in both 2009-10 and 2010-11, and two coaches sacked.

Three things rebuilt the club. The Juventus Stadium, a 41,000-seat ground the club owns on the site of the old Delle Alpi, opened in September 2011 and gave Juventus a revenue advantage no Italian rival could match. Antonio Conte, the former captain, arrived as coach the same summer and went unbeaten through 2011-12 with [Andrea Pirlo](/players/andrea-pirlo), picked up free from Milan, as the deep playmaker. And Giuseppe Marotta and Fabio Paratici built squads cheaply and sold expensively. Conte won three titles, Allegri five more plus four Coppa Italias, and the run of nine straight Scudetti from 2012 to 2020 is the longest in the history of Europe's big leagues. Allegri's Juventus also reached Champions League finals in 2015 and 2017, losing both, to Barcelona in Berlin and to Real Madrid in Cardiff. [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo), signed for €100m in 2018, scored 101 goals in three seasons without bringing the European Cup the club bought him for.

The 2020s have been the club's most chaotic period since Calciopoli. Maurizio Sarri and Pirlo each lasted a season, Allegri's second spell was joyless, and the Agnelli board resigned en masse in November 2022 amid the "plusvalenze" investigation into inflated transfer accounting; Juventus were docked 10 points in 2022-23, finishing seventh, and accepted a one-year European ban. Thiago Motta's 2024-25 side finished fourth after he was sacked in March, Igor Tudor started 2025-26 and was replaced in October 2025 by [Luciano Spalletti](/managers/luciano-spalletti), the former Napoli and Italy coach, who steered the club to sixth and a Europa League place.

The ownership structure changed too. Andrea Agnelli, president since 2010 and architect of both the stadium-era revival and the failed European Super League of April 2021, resigned with the board in 2022; his cousin John Elkann's holding company Exor appointed Ferrero and then, in 2025, the French executive Damien Comolli as managing director, with Giorgio Chiellini, the former captain, as director of football strategy. Exor also injected fresh capital in 2024 and again in 2026 to cover losses that had exceeded €100m a year during the Ronaldo and post-Covid seasons. The club remains the only Italian one listed on the stock exchange and the most followed on social media, and that scale is the reason a seventh-place start is treated as a crisis in Turin and a triumph in Bergamo.

## Players the club produced

Juventus' academy has produced fewer world-class players than the club's history might suggest; the model has always been to buy the best Italians from provincial clubs. The home-grown exceptions matter: Boniperti, Scirea's partner Sergio Brio, Giorgio Chiellini's contemporary Claudio Marchisio, who played 389 games, and more recently Kenan Yildiz, who arrived from Bayern's youth sector at 17 in 2022 and inherited the number 10 shirt.

The signings tell the real story. Del Piero, bought from Padova at 18, scored 290 goals and stayed through Serie B; Buffon, bought from Parma in 2001 for a goalkeeping record fee, played 685 games across two spells. Nedvěd arrived from Lazio in 2001 and won the 2003 Ballon d'Or; Pirlo, Carlos Tevez, Paul Pogba, picked up free from Manchester United and sold back for €105m, Arturo Vidal, Leonardo Bonucci, Paulo Dybala and Federico Chiesa all came in from elsewhere. Platini, Zidane, Baggio and Ronaldo, four Ballon d'Or winners, wore the shirt. The current squad is anchored by Bremer, the Brazilian centre-back signed from Torino in 2022, and Manuel Locatelli, the captain, with Teun Koopmeiners and Khéphren Thuram in midfield.

## Identity and playing style

"Winning is not important, it is the only thing that counts," Boniperti said, and the club has built an identity on that line, on the discipline of Trapattoni and Lippi and on the counter-attacking pragmatism of Allegri, who often sat his side in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and trusted individual quality. The Conte years were the exception, a pressing, wing-back-driven 3-5-2 whose shape most Italian clubs then copied.

Spalletti is a different creature. His Napoli played the most fluent football Italy had seen in a decade, built on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), a 4-3-3 with the full-backs inverting and the winger isolated against his man. At Juventus he has used a 4-2-3-1 with Yildiz drifting in from the left, Randal Kolo Muani, signed permanently from PSG for €41m, as the central striker and Nick Woltemade, on loan from Newcastle, as an alternative target. Guglielmo Vicario, on loan from Tottenham, replaced Michele Di Gregorio in goal. The defence is organised around Bremer and Jhon Lucumí, bought from Bologna, with Andrea Cambiaso and Zeki Çelik at full-back.

## Rivalries

The Derby d'Italia with [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) is the fiercest fixture in the Italian calendar outside the city derbies, poisoned by Calciopoli and the 2006 title. The Derby della Mole with [Torino](/clubs/torino) is older and lopsided; Juventus have lost it only a handful of times since 2015 but the Granata treat each one as a war. [Fiorentina](/clubs/fiorentina) fans have hated Juventus since Baggio's transfer and the 1990 UEFA Cup final; [Napoli](/clubs/napoli) and [Roma](/clubs/roma) see Juventus as the establishment, and the matches with [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) are a contest between the two most successful Italian clubs in Europe.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Juventus are seventh in Serie A with 17 points from ten games, five wins, two draws and three defeats, seven points behind leaders Lazio, who beat them 3-0 in October in the heaviest defeat of Spalletti's tenure. The attack has been the problem: Bremer and Edon Zhegrova, the Kosovo winger, lead the league scoring with just two each, and Kolo Muani's four goals have come mostly in the Europa League, where a 5-0 win over NEC Nijmegen was the standout result of the league phase. The Coppa Italia begins at the round of 16.

The summer was expensive and uneven: Kerim Alajbegović, a 19-year-old Bosnian forward, cost €32m from Leverkusen, Dušan Vlahović left for Beşiktaş on a free at the end of his contract and Timothy Weah was sold to Marseille. Spalletti's contract runs to 2027 and the board, led by chairman Gianluca Ferrero and the Exor-appointed managing director Damien Comolli, has publicly backed him, but a fifth consecutive season without a Scudetto challenge is testing the patience of a fanbase used to nine in a row.
