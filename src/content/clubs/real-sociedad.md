## History

Real Sociedad were founded in San Sebastián in 1909, inheriting the city's football tradition from the short-lived San Sebastián Recreation Club, and were founder members of [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) in 1929. The first decades were respectable rather than glorious: a Copa final in 1910, a second-place finish in 1931, long spells in the second tier through the 1940s and 1960s. The old Atotxa ground, tight and loud, was the club's home until Anoeta opened in 1993.

The great era came at the start of the 1980s. Under Alberto Ormaetxea, with Luis Arconada in goal, Jesús María Zamora, Periko Alonso and José María Bakero, La Real won the league in 1981 (Zamora's last-minute goal at Gijón) and 1982, then added the 1982 Supercopa, and reached the 1983 European Cup semi-final, losing to Hamburg. The 1987 Copa del Rey, won on penalties against Atlético, was a third major trophy, and John Toshack's late-1980s side was the last to field only Basque players: the club abandoned its own cantera-only policy in 1989 with the signing of John Aldridge.

The next peak was 2002-03, when Raynald Denoueix's team of Darko Kovačević, Nihat Kahveci, [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) and Valeri Karpin led the league into the spring and finished second, two points behind Real Madrid, before a Champions League last-16 run the following year. The fall was swift: relegation in 2007 and three seasons in Segunda, with financial trouble and the exit of Alonso, [Antoine Griezmann](/players/antoine-griezmann) later and a generation of talent. Promotion as champions in 2010 began the modern rebuild, driven by the Zubieta academy and president Jokin Aperribay.

## Trajectory

Since 2010 La Real have become one of Spain's most reliable European contenders. Philippe Montanier's 2012-13 side finished fourth and reached the Champions League; Imanol Alguacil, a former player and youth coach appointed in 2018, led the club to the 2020 Copa del Rey (the final, against Athletic, was played in April 2021 because of the pandemic), a fourth-place finish and Champions League return in 2022-23, and six straight top-six finishes.

The last two seasons have been bumpier. Alguacil left in 2025 after seven years; Sergio Francisco lasted until December 2025 with the team 15th, and [Pellegrino Matarazzo](/managers/pellegrino-matarazzo), the New Jersey-born former Stuttgart and Hoffenheim coach, was appointed on 20 December 2025, the first American to manage in La Liga. He stabilised the league season (tenth, 46 points) and won the Copa del Rey on 18 April 2026 at La Cartuja: Ander Barrenetxea scored after 14 seconds, the fastest goal in a final, Lookman and Álvarez replied for Atlético, and goalkeeper Unai Marrero saved two penalties in the 4-3 shootout. The cup brought a Europa League place for 2026-27.

## Players the club produced

Zubieta is one of the two great Basque academies. Arconada, Zamora, Bakero and Txiki Begiristain (later Barcelona's sporting director) came through in the 1970s and 1980s; Xabi Alonso, Mikel Aranburu and Asier Illarramendi in the 2000s. The modern production line is exceptional: [Mikel Oyarzabal](/players/mikel-oyarzabal), captain and the club's all-time leading scorer among post-war players, scored Spain's winning goal in the Euro 2024 final and was part of the 2026 World Cup-winning squad; Martín Zubimendi (now Arsenal), Mikel Merino, Robin Le Normand and Ander Barrenetxea all emerged in the same five-year span.

Signed players of note include Griezmann, who arrived at 14 from France and left for Atlético in 2014, Alexander Isak (2019-22), [David Silva](/players/david-silva), whose three seasons from 2020 lifted the whole team, and Takefusa Kubo, the Japanese winger who chose San Sebastián over Real Madrid's orbit in 2022 and remains the team's most watchable player.

## Identity and playing style

La Real are the possession club of the Basque Country, a conscious counterpoint to Athletic's directness. Alguacil's 4-3-3 was built on short passing through Zubimendi, Merino and Silva, positional discipline in the [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) tradition and a coordinated high press. Matarazzo has kept the structure but added German pragmatism: a 4-2-3-1 or 3-4-2-1 depending on opponent, more direct running from Kubo and Barrenetxea, Oyarzabal used as a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine) when Orri Óskarsson is rested, and a sturdier mid-block. The 2026 Copa was won by surviving, not dominating, and the early 2026-27 league form (six wins, four defeats in 11, 20 goals for and 20 against) suggests a team that attacks well and defends unevenly.

Anoeta, renamed the Reale Arena, was rebuilt between 2017 and 2019 to remove the athletics track and bring the stands to the pitch, which transformed the atmosphere and, the club believes, its home record. The city's culture, gastronomic and relaxed, is reflected in the club's style, and the Zubieta training ground sits in the hills above the town with the first team and the academy sharing pitches. The club's model, develop and sell one star every two or three years (Griezmann, Isak, Zubimendi, Merino) while keeping the core, has produced sixteen consecutive top-flight seasons without a financial crisis. The women's team, Real Sociedad Femenino, won the 2019 Copa de la Reina and is one of the stronger sides in Liga F, and the B team's 2024-25 season in Segunda, the first for a reserve side in a decade, was a mark of the academy's depth.

## Rivalries

The Basque derby with [Athletic Club](/clubs/athletic-bilbao) is the heart of the club's calendar, two proud institutions a hundred kilometres apart; the two sets of fans famously walked out together before the 1976 derby with the then-banned ikurriña flag. [Osasuna](/clubs/osasuna) and [Alavés](/clubs/alaves) are regional rivals, and the 2002-03 title race left a lasting edge with [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid).

## Current situation

Real Sociedad are fifth in La Liga on 7 November 2026 with 19 points from 11 games, inside the Champions League places on a tight table, and are in the Europa League league phase as Copa holders. Matarazzo is contracted to 2028. The summer brought Carlos Soler, Yangel Herrera and Gonçalo Guedes alongside the core of Oyarzabal, Kubo, Barrenetxea, Zubeldia and Álex Remiro, with academy products Jon Martín, Pablo Marín and Mikel Goti pushing through. A 3-2 win over Deportivo on 11 October and a 1-0 derby defeat at Athletic on 2 October framed the autumn. The club's ambitions are a return to the Champions League and, with the Copa in the cabinet, a first European final.
