## History

Racing Club was founded in 1903 in Avellaneda, the industrial suburb south of Buenos Aires, by a group of young men who had broken away from another club and named themselves after the French side Racing Club de Paris. The sky-blue-and-white stripes came later, borrowed from the national flag. Racing won seven consecutive amateur championships between 1913 and 1919, earning the nickname "La Academia" for the quality of its football and the title "El Primer Grande", the first of the big five, which the fans still use.

The professional era's high point was the 1960s. Juan José Pizzuti's "Equipo de José" won the 1966 championship unbeaten with Roberto Perfumo in defence, Humberto Maschio in midfield and Agustín Cejas in goal, then won the 1967 Copa Libertadores against Nacional of Montevideo and the Intercontinental Cup against Celtic in a brutal three-match series that ended with a play-off in Montevideo. Racing were the first Argentine club to be world champions.

What followed is known as the drought. A Racing fan buried seven black cats at the stadium during the 1967 celebrations, the legend goes, and only six were found; the club did not win another league title until 2001, suffered relegation in 1983 and spent two years in the second division, and in 1999 was declared bankrupt and briefly ceased to exist as a legal entity. The seventh cat was found during renovations in 2001, and Reinaldo "Mostaza" Merlo's team, with Diego Milito, Claudio Úbeda and the goalkeeper Gustavo Campagnuolo, won the Apertura that December.

## Trajectory

The 2000s brought relegation play-offs in 2008 and 2009, survived both times, before the club's modern revival under the president Víctor Blanco. Milito returned from Inter in 2014 and Diego Cocca's team won the Torneo de Transición that year; Eduardo Coudet's 2018-19 Superliga champions, with Lisandro López as captain and Gabriel Arias in goal, added the 18th title. Lautaro Martínez, the academy's greatest recent product, left for Inter in 2018.

Gustavo Costas, a club legend as a player and the archetypal Racing fanatic, took charge in 2024 and ended the continental drought: the Copa Sudamericana, won 3-1 against Cruzeiro in Asunción in November 2024 with Adrián "Maravilla" Martínez scoring, was the first international title since 1988. The Recopa against Botafogo followed in early 2025, and a run to the 2025 Copa Libertadores semi-finals, lost to Flamengo, was the club's deepest in the competition since 1997. The club's current squad data lists Sebastián Romero as head coach as of November 2026, and the Torneo Clausura 2026 has been poor: 13th of 15 in Group B with 15 points from 16 games.

## Players the club produced

Racing's academy produced Perfumo, Cejas, Úbeda, Milito, Lisandro López, Lautaro Martínez, Rodrigo De Paul (sold to Valencia in 2014), Lucas Alario, Matías Zaracho, Leonardo Sigali's partners in the 2019 side and the midfielder Juan Nardoni's generation. The great imports were Maschio (returned from Italy), Rubén Paz, the Uruguayan playmaker of the 1980s, the Chilean goalkeeper Arias and the Argentine veteran Luciano Vietto.

## Identity and playing style

La Academia's identity is built on its name: the fans believe the club should play well. Coudet's 2019 champions pressed high in a 4-2-3-1 and were the most attractive team in Argentina that season; Costas' 2024-25 teams were more direct and more emotional, defending deep in a 4-3-3 and attacking on the break through Maravilla Martínez and Santiago Solari's running, with the Cilindro's noise as a twelfth man. Romero has struggled to find a balance between the two this semester. See [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) for the approach the Sudamericana winners used.

## Rivalries

The Clásico de Avellaneda against [Independiente](/clubs/independiente), whose stadium is 300 metres away across a railway line, is the most geographically intimate derby in world football and one of its most hostile. Games with [Boca Juniors](/clubs/boca-juniors), [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate) and [San Lorenzo](/clubs/san-lorenzo) are the other big-five fixtures, and the 2025 Libertadores semi-final against [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) was the most important continental tie in a generation.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Racing are 13th in Group B of the Torneo Clausura, out of the knockout places with the Argentine season in its final weeks, under Romero. Maravilla Martínez, Sosa, Arias, Nardoni and Vietto are the senior players, and the 2024-25 continental success has been followed by a sharp decline. The Cilindro will expect a response; the club's financial health under Blanco is strong enough to fund a summer rebuild.
