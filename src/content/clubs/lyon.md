## History

Olympique Lyonnais were founded in 1950, breaking away from Lyon Olympique Universitaire, and for their first half-century were a cup team: Coupe de France wins in 1964, 1967 and 1973 with Fleury Di Nallo and Serge Chiesa, a Cup Winners' Cup semi-final in 1964, and a relegation in 1983 that lasted six seasons. Jean-Michel Aulas bought the club in 1987 with a plan he called "OL Europe": promotion within four years, Europe within another four, and then dominance.

He delivered beyond the plan. Lyon were back in Division 1 in 1989, in the UEFA Cup by 1991, and, after the signings of Sonny Anderson and Juninho Pernambucano and the appointment of Jacques Santini, won their first title in 2002 on the final day against Lens. Seven in a row followed, a French record: Santini (2002), Paul Le Guen (2003-05), Gérard Houllier (2006-07) and Alain Perrin (2008, with the first double). The sides featured Grégory Coupet, Éric Abidal, Michael Essien, Mahamadou Diarra, Florent Malouda, Sidney Govou, [Karim Benzema](/players/karim-benzema) and Juninho, whose free kicks defined the era. In Europe they reached the Champions League quarter-finals in 2004, 2005 and 2006 and the semi-final in 2010, losing to Bayern.

The club built its own stadium, the Groupama Stadium in Décines, opened in January 2016, and leaned on the academy: Alexandre Lacazette, Nabil Fekir, Samuel Umtiti, Corentin Tolisso and Anthony Martial carried the second-place finishes of 2015 and 2016 and [Bruno Génésio](/managers/bruno-genesio)'s Europa League semi-final in 2017. Rudi Garcia's team reached the 2020 Champions League semi-final in Lisbon, beating Juventus and Manchester City before losing to Bayern. Aulas sold to John Textor's Eagle Football in 2022; Textor's debts brought a DNCG ruling in June 2025 that provisionally relegated Lyon to Ligue 2, overturned on appeal after Textor stepped back and the club cut costs. [Paulo Fonseca](/managers/paulo-fonseca), appointed in January 2025 after [Pierre Sage](/managers/pierre-sage) was sacked, kept Lyon in Europe through the crisis.

## Trajectory

The arc is clear: a decade of total dominance (2001-10), a decade of top-four regularity with academy-driven squads (2011-20), and a half-decade of instability under Textor in which the club finished seventh, eighth, sixth and sixth and nearly lost its place in the top flight. The 2025-26 season under Fonseca, played under strict financial control, restored Lyon to the Champions League places, and 2026-27 began with the club again among the chasers behind PSG.

## Players the club produced

Lyon's academy is the best in France. [Karim Benzema](/players/karim-benzema) debuted at 17 in 2005 and scored 66 goals before Real Madrid in 2009; Hatem Ben Arfa, Loïc Rémy, Lacazette (129 league goals over two spells), Umtiti, Tolisso, Fekir, Martial, Maxence Caqueret, Rayan Cherki (sold to Manchester City in 2025) and Malick Fofana are the headline graduates, and Hugo Lloris came through the youth ranks in Nice before becoming Lyon's goalkeeper. The signings who became legends are Brazilian and French: Sonny Anderson, Juninho (100 goals, 44 of them free kicks), Cris, Fred, Lisandro López, Coupet (the academy goalkeeper of the seven titles), Essien and Malouda. [Bradley Barcola](/players/bradley-barcola) was a Lyon academy product sold to PSG in 2023.

## Identity and playing style

The seven-title Lyon were a 4-4-2 of technical superiority and ruthless recruitment rather than a tactical school. [Paulo Fonseca](/managers/paulo-fonseca) has given the modern club a clearer identity: [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5, one [inverted full-back](/tactics/inverted-full-backs), wingers high and wide, a number ten drifting to create overloads, and a controlled counter-press. Without the ball Lyon sit in a mid-block with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the wide areas; the lesson of his Milan spell, that his teams could be open in transition, has produced a more cautious rest defence and a double pivot.

The Textor years are the story Lyon supporters would rather forget. Eagle Football, the American businessman's multi-club group that also owned Botafogo and a stake in Crystal Palace, bought Aulas out in December 2022 and loaded the club with debt; the DNCG's provisional relegation in June 2025 was lifted only after Textor stepped down as president, Michele Kang took charge and the club sold players and cut wages. The women's team, OL Lyonnes, the most successful in Europe, was separated into Kang's own group. The episode cost Lyon their best young players and much of their credibility, and Fonseca's achievement in keeping the club in Europe through it is the reason his possible departure in November 2026 matters so much.

## Rivalries

The Derby Rhône-Alpes against Saint-Étienne, 60 kilometres away, is the oldest and most heated rivalry in France: the ten-time champions of the 1960s and 1970s against the club that replaced them, with Saint-Étienne now in Ligue 2. The **Olympico** against [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) was the title rivalry of the 2000s and still produces the most volatile atmospheres in the league. [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) have been the obstacle since 2011, and [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) the competitor for podium places.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Lyon are third in Ligue 1 on 17 points from nine games (five wins, two draws, two defeats, 15 scored, seven conceded), a point behind Lille and three behind PSG. They beat Nice 2-1 on 18 October, lost 2-0 at the Parc des Princes on 26 October and beat Angers 2-0 at home on 1 November. The next fixture is at Brest on 8 November.

The uncertainty is the coach. In early November 2026 multiple reports, including beIN Sports on 3 November, said Fonseca had agreed to leave Lyon for Sporting CP, who had ended Rui Borges's contract, with Lyon to receive around 5 million euros in compensation. As of this writing neither club had confirmed the move and Lyon had not named a successor; if it goes through, the club faces a mid-season search in the middle of a European campaign. On the pitch Malick Fofana, Corentin Tolisso, Georges Mikautadze and goalkeeper Lucas Perri are the core, and the target is a Champions League finish for the second straight year.
