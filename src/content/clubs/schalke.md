## History

FC Schalke 04 were founded on 4 May 1904 by a group of teenagers in the Schalke district of Gelsenkirchen, in the heart of the Ruhr coalfield. The club's identity as the miners' team (Die Knappen) and the Schalker Kreisel, the short-passing style of Ernst Kuzorra and Fritz Szepan, made them the dominant force of 1930s German football: six championships between 1934 and 1942, the 1937 double, and a run of 189 matches without a league defeat. A seventh title came in 1958, the last.

The Bundesliga years were long on drama and short on trophies. The 1971 bribery scandal, in which Schalke players were found to have taken money to lose to Arminia Bielefeld, broke up a fine side; Klaus Fischer's bicycle kicks and the 1972 DFB-Pokal were the highlights of the decade. Relegations in 1981, 1983 and 1988 followed. The revival came under Huub Stevens with the "Eurofighters" of 1997: Jens Lehmann, Olaf Thon, Marc Wilmots and Youri Mulder beat Inter on penalties in the San Siro to win the UEFA Cup, the club's only European trophy.

On 19 May 2001 Schalke beat Unterhaching 5-3 and were champions for four minutes, until Patrik Andersson's injury-time goal at Hamburg gave Bayern the title; the "Meister der Herzen" (champions of hearts) label stuck. They won the DFB-Pokal in 2001 and 2002, finished second in 2005, 2007 and 2010 (the last under Felix Magath with [Raúl](/players/raul) arriving that summer), and reached the 2011 Champions League semi-final under Ralf Rangnick, having knocked out Inter 7-3 on aggregate, before losing to Manchester United. The 2011 cup, a 5-0 final win over Duisburg, was the last trophy.

The decline was slow, then sudden. Domenico Tedesco's second place in 2018 was a mirage; two years later the club went 30 league games without a win, were relegated in 2021 with 16 points, and were back down again in 2023 after a single season up. In the 2. Bundesliga they flirted with relegation to the 3. Liga in 2024 and 2025 under a procession of coaches and with debts of more than 150 million euros. Miron Muslić, the Bosnian-Austrian coach who had kept Plymouth competitive in the Championship, was appointed in summer 2025 and led the club to promotion in 2025-26.

## Trajectory

Schalke's post-2000 trajectory is the Bundesliga's great cautionary tale: a top-four regular from 2001 to 2018, with a Champions League semi-final and Raúl, Huntelaar, Neuer and Draxler in the side, undone by the loss of chairman Clemens Tönnies, the Gazprom sponsorship, the Covid revenue collapse and a recruitment policy that sold Sané, Goretzka, Kolašinac and Meyer for little or nothing. Two relegations in three seasons were followed by two near-relegations to the third tier. The 2026 promotion, as 2. Bundesliga champions, restored the Revierderby to the top flight, but the finances remain fragile.

## Players the club produced

The Knappenschmiede, Schalke's academy, was the best in Germany for a decade: [Manuel Neuer](/players/manuel-neuer), who left for Bayern in 2011 amid fury, Mesut Özil, Benedikt Höwedes, Julian Draxler, Joël Matip, Leon Goretzka (signed from Bochum young), Leroy Sané, Max Meyer, Thilo Kehrer, Weston McKennie and Malick Thiaw all came through. The club's failure to monetise them is a large part of why it fell.

Earlier generations produced Kuzorra and Szepan, Klaus Fischer (182 Bundesliga goals for the club), Olaf Thon and Lehmann. Among signings, Ebbe Sand, Marc Wilmots, Gerald Asamoah, Kevin Kurányi, [Raúl](/players/raul) (two seasons, 40 goals, adored) and Klaas-Jan Huntelaar (29 league goals in 2011-12) are the names on the terraces.

## Identity and playing style

Schalke's historical style is the Kreisel, the spinning-top passing game of the 1930s, and the club's self-image is of a hard-working, loyal, blue-collar institution rather than a tactical school. The best modern Schalke teams, under Stevens and Rangnick, were organised pressing sides that attacked quickly. Miron Muslić plays a high-energy 3-4-3 with a [back three](/tactics/back-three), aggressive [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) and direct wing play, the style that won promotion, though the step up has exposed a lack of goals.

The financial side of the fall is the part that outsiders find hardest to grasp. Schalke's turnover in 2019 was among the top 15 in Europe; by 2022 the club had sold its training ground to the city, taken state-guaranteed loans and cut its wage bill by two-thirds. The Veltins-Arena, with its retractable roof and sliding pitch, opened in 2001 and was once the model modern stadium in Germany; it is now the asset that keeps the club alive.

## Rivalries

The **Revierderby** against [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) is Germany's fiercest, two mining cities 30 kilometres apart whose rivalry dates to the 1920s. The 4-4 draw at Dortmund in November 2017 from 4-0 down is Schalke's favourite recent memory; the derbies of 2020 and 2021, lost during the collapse, are the least favourite. Its return to the Bundesliga in 2026-27 was the fixture of the promotion. [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) are the rival from 2001 and the title races of the 2000s, and the Ruhr-Rhine matches against [Cologne](/clubs/cologne) carry regional edge.

## Current situation

The return has been hard. As of 7 November 2026 Schalke are 17th in the Bundesliga on six points from eight games (one win, three draws, four defeats), with only four goals scored, the fewest in the league, and nine conceded, which is respectable. They lost 1-0 at home to Mainz on 17 October, drew 0-0 at Köln on 24 October and lost 2-0 at home to Leipzig on 31 October. The next match is at Hoffenheim on 8 November.

Muslić's team is organised but toothless, and the squad, assembled on a tight budget around Kenan Karaman, Moussa Sylla and Loris Karius, needs goals from somewhere. The Veltins-Arena has sold out every match regardless; the club's membership of more than 180,000 makes it the second largest in Germany. The season's aim is survival, and the Revierderby, which returns to the Bundesliga calendar for the first time since 2023, will be the emotional high point whatever the table says.
