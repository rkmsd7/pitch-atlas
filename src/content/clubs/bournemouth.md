## History

Boscombe FC were formed in 1899, became Bournemouth and Boscombe Athletic in 1923 when they joined the Football League, and were renamed AFC Bournemouth in 1971, the "AFC" chosen partly so the club would appear first in alphabetical lists. For 90 years they were a lower-division club with a 12,000-capacity ground at Dean Court and a reputation for cup shocks: the 1957 run with Ollie Norris that beat Wolves and Tottenham, Ted MacDougall's nine goals in an FA Cup tie against Margate in 1971, and Harry Redknapp's team knocking out holders Manchester United in 1984.

Redknapp won the Third Division title in 1987 and took the club to the second tier for the first time. By the 2000s Bournemouth were in financial trouble: administration in 1997, when supporters raised the money to keep the club alive, and again in 2008, when a 10-point deduction relegated them to League Two and a further 17-point penalty the next season left them bottom. Eddie Howe, a 31-year-old former player with no managerial experience, was appointed in January 2009 and kept them in the League.

What followed was the most remarkable rise of the era. Howe won promotion in 2010, left for Burnley, returned in 2012 and took Bournemouth from League One to the Premier League in three seasons, winning the Championship in 2015 with a budget a fraction of their rivals'. They stayed up five years with Callum Wilson, Josh King, Nathan Aké and David Brooks, finishing ninth in 2017, and were relegated in 2020. Scott Parker won promotion in 2022, Bill Foley's consortium bought the club the same year, and [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola), appointed in 2023, took the club to 12th, ninth and then sixth in 2025-26, the highest finish in its history and a first European qualification. Iraola left for Liverpool in June 2026 and [Marco Rose](/managers/marco-rose) was appointed on a three-year contract.

## Trajectory

Bournemouth's last 25 years run from the fourth tier to the top six. Relegation to the basement division in 2002 and the points deductions of 2008 and 2009 are the floor; the three promotions between 2010 and 2015 are the climb; and the Iraola years, with a club-record 56 points in 2024-25 and sixth place in 2025-26, are the summit. The club has been in the Premier League for ten of the last twelve seasons. Six of the Iraola-era starters, including Antoine Semenyo, Dean Huijsen, Milos Kerkez, Illia Zabarnyi and Dango Ouattara, were sold for a combined total of more than £250m between 2025 and January 2026, the club's model being to recruit well and sell at a premium.

## Players the club produced

Bournemouth's academy has not produced many first-team regulars, though Eddie Howe himself, Sam Vokes, Baily Cargill and Jack Stephens came through, and Alex Scott, signed from Bristol City at 19, is a long-term project. The club's legends are mostly signings: Ted MacDougall, Steve Fletcher, who played 726 games across two spells and scored the goal that kept the club in the League in 2009, Callum Wilson, Nathan Aké, David Brooks, Dominic Solanke, sold to Tottenham for £65m in 2024, and Antoine Semenyo, sold to Manchester City in January 2026 and the scorer of the 2026 FA Cup final winner.

## Identity and playing style

[Marco Rose](/managers/marco-rose) is a Red Bull-school coach who managed Salzburg, Borussia Mönchengladbach, Borussia Dortmund and RB Leipzig, where he won the 2023 DFB-Pokal. His football is [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) with a 4-2-3-1 or 4-4-2 diamond, aggressive defensive transitions and high sprint volume, which is why the board saw him as a natural successor to Iraola. The risk, as analysts noted in pre-season, is that the approach exposes a squad that is not fully conditioned, and Bournemouth's early weeks included a run without a win and a 5-1 defeat at Chelsea.

The squad lost Semenyo and Ouattara and rebuilt around Evanilson and Justin Kluivert in attack, Lewis Cook, Tyler Adams and Marcus Tavernier in midfield, and a defence anchored by António Silva, signed from Benfica as the headline summer purchase, with Adrien Truffert and Julián Araujo at full-back. Ben Gannon-Doak, Daniel Jebbison and Álvaro Rodríguez have had opportunities in a thinner forward line.

## Rivalries

[Southampton](/clubs/southampton), 30 miles east, are the South Coast derby of recent seasons; [Portsmouth](/clubs/portsmouth) are the older Hampshire rivalry from the lower leagues; and [Brighton & Hove Albion](/clubs/brighton), another modern south-coast club, is the fixture that has produced the most recent Premier League meetings.

## Current situation

Bournemouth are eighth in early November 2026, with three wins, three draws and three defeats from nine: 14 goals scored, 13 conceded, 12 points. After a slow start the form has turned: 2-0 at home to Sunderland on 18 October, 3-0 at Manchester United on 25 October, and 2-0 against Leeds at home on 31 October. The Europa League league phase is running alongside, the club's first European campaign, and the new stand that will take the Vitality Stadium above 11,307 is in planning.

The ambition is to establish European football as a regular thing; the immediate aim is to keep Rose's transition from Iraola on track and to survive the squad's thinness through the winter.
