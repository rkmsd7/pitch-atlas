## History

Shakhtar were founded in 1936 as Stakhanovets, the team of the Donbas coal miners, and took the name Shakhtar, meaning "miner", in 1946. In the Soviet era they were a respectable cup side, winning the Soviet Cup four times between 1961 and 1983, but never the league. The club's modern history began in 1996 when Rinat Akhmetov, the Donetsk businessman who became Ukraine's richest man, took control, and within six years Shakhtar had won their first Ukrainian title in 2002 under Nevio Scala, breaking Dynamo Kyiv's decade-long monopoly.

Mircea Lucescu, appointed in 2004, built the club into a European force over twelve years. His method was to pair a Ukrainian spine of Andriy Pyatov, Dmytro Chygrynskiy and Anatoliy Tymoshchuk with a conveyor belt of young Brazilians: Matuzalém, Elano, Brandão, Jadson, [Fernandinho](/players/fernandinho), [Willian](/players/willian), Luiz Adriano, [Douglas Costa](/players/douglas-costa), Alex Teixeira and Taison. The 2009 UEFA Cup, won 2-1 after extra time against Werder Bremen in Istanbul with Jadson scoring the winner, was the first European trophy won by a Ukrainian club since the Soviet era. The Donbas Arena, the first stadium in Eastern Europe built to UEFA's elite standard, opened that summer, and Shakhtar reached the 2011 Champions League quarter-final with [Henrikh Mkhitaryan](/players/henrikh-mkhitaryan) emerging as a star.

In 2014 the war in eastern Ukraine forced the club out of Donetsk. Shakhtar have not played a home game at the Donbas Arena since May 2014, moving first to Lviv, then Kharkiv and Kyiv, and after the full-scale Russian invasion of February 2022 their foreign players were allowed by FIFA to suspend contracts and leave. The club has kept going regardless, winning titles in 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2023 and 2024, reaching the Europa League semi-finals in 2016 and 2020, and playing its European home games in Poland and Germany. Mykhailo Mudryk's sale to Chelsea for a fee reported at up to €100 million in January 2023 was the biggest in Ukrainian history.

## Trajectory

Shakhtar have won 15 of the 25 completed Ukrainian championships since 2000-01 and have never finished outside the top two in that period, a record of dominance shared only with Dynamo Kyiv. The Lucescu era brought eight titles in twelve years; Paulo Fonseca added three consecutive doubles from 2017 to 2019; and the club has managed three more titles since the full-scale invasion despite playing every match away from home. The 2024-25 season brought second place and the Ukrainian Cup. In Europe the 2009 UEFA Cup, the 2011 Champions League quarter-final and the Europa League semi-finals of 2016 and 2020 are the peaks; Shakhtar are in the 2026-27 Champions League league phase with four points from four matches.

## Players the club produced

Shakhtar's academy produced Andriy Pyatov, Taras Stepanenko, Dmytro Chygrynskiy, Oleksandr Zinchenko (who left as a teenager), Mykhailo Mudryk, Heorhiy Sudakov and Danylo Sikan, with Sudakov the latest to attract elite interest. The club's Brazilian development model is its real signature: [Willian](/players/willian), [Fernandinho](/players/fernandinho), [Douglas Costa](/players/douglas-costa), Alex Teixeira, Luiz Adriano, Bernard and Fred were all bought young from Brazil and sold to Chelsea, Manchester City, Bayern, Jiangsu, Milan and Manchester United. Darijo Srna, the Croatian captain who played 536 games for the club, and [Henrikh Mkhitaryan](/players/henrikh-mkhitaryan) are the greatest non-Brazilian imports.

## Identity and playing style

Shakhtar's identity since Lucescu has been technical, possession-based football with Brazilian flair in a 4-2-3-1, the "Brazilian Ukrainians" as the club's supporters called them, and resilience in the face of displacement. Since 2022 the squad has been overwhelmingly Ukrainian again, with young Brazilians such as Kevin and Marlon Gomes continuing the tradition. Arda Turan, the former Galatasaray, Atlético Madrid and Barcelona midfielder, was appointed head coach in 2025 and plays a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) 4-3-3 with a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and aggressive pressing.

## Rivalries

The Klasychne derby against Dynamo Kyiv is the only rivalry that matters in Ukrainian football, east against west, new money against Soviet-era prestige, and the two clubs have shared every championship since 1993. The war has given the fixture a strange poignancy; both clubs have played it in neutral stadiums.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Shakhtar are in the Champions League league phase, sitting in the lower-middle of the table with four points from four matches, and continue to play domestic and European home games away from Donetsk. The club's objectives are the usual ones in extraordinary circumstances: the Ukrainian title, progress in Europe and the continued development and sale of players such as Sudakov and Kevin to fund a club whose home city remains under occupation.
