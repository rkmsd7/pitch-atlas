## History

Stade Brestois 29 were founded in 1950 in the naval port at the western tip of Brittany and did not turn professional until 1970. Their first spell in Division 1 came in 1979-80, and the 1980s under president François Yvinec were the club's first golden age: Julio César, the Brazilian defender, [David Ginola](/players/david-ginola), Paul Le Guen, Corentin Martins and Bernard Lama all played at Francis-Le Blé, and Brest finished eighth in 1987. The money ran out in 1991; the club was liquidated, demoted to the fourth tier and restarted.

The climb back took two decades: National (third tier) through the early 2000s, promotion in 2004, and in 2010 a return to Ligue 1 under Alex Dupont after 19 years away. Three seasons ended in relegation in 2013, and six more in Ligue 2 followed before Jean-Marc Furlan's side went up as runners-up in 2019. Olivier Dall'Oglio and Michel Der Zakarian kept them up; [Franck Ribéry](/players/franck-ribery) began his career in Brest's youth system in the late 1990s, and Bruno Grougi, the midfielder of the 2010 promotion, became the club's cult figure.

Then came Éric Roy. Appointed in January 2023 with the club 17th, the former Nice coach kept them up, and in 2023-24 produced the greatest season in the club's history: third place, behind only PSG and Monaco, with Romain Del Castillo, Pierre Lees-Melou, Steve Mounié and goalkeeper Marco Bizot, and the first European qualification. The 2024-25 Champions League, played at Guingamp's Roudourou because Francis-Le Blé did not meet UEFA standards, brought wins over Sturm Graz, Salzburg and PSV and a play-off round against PSG. Roy left in 2025 and Julien Lachuer, promoted from the staff, is head coach for 2026-27.

## Trajectory

Liquidated in 1991, in the third tier until 2004, in Ligue 1 for three seasons from 2010, and then, from promotion in 2019, a steady rise to the Champions League in 2024: Brest's trajectory is the romance of modern French football. The budget is among the smallest in the division, the stadium holds 15,000, and the new Arkéa Park has been discussed for a decade. The 2024-25 and 2025-26 seasons were mid-table, which for Brest is the new normal rather than a disappointment.

## Players the club produced

Ribéry is the famous graduate, though he left at 18 without a professional appearance. The academy has produced Grougi, Brendan Chardonnet (the captain, over 250 games), Hugo Magnetti and Kamory Doumbia; the recruitment department found Del Castillo at Nîmes, Lees-Melou at Norwich, Mounié at Huddersfield, Bizot at AZ and Mahdi Camara at Saint-Étienne. Ginola, Le Guen and Julio César were the signings of the 1980s; Ludovic Ajorque the goalscorer of the 2024-25 European season.

## Identity and playing style

Roy's Brest were a 4-3-3 that defended in a mid-block, broke at pace through Del Castillo and Jérémy Le Douaron and were superb from [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces): [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) with a Breton work ethic and a famously tight dressing room. Lachuer has continued the same framework with a slightly higher press and more possession, and the 2026-27 side has been competitive and hard to beat at home.

## Rivalries

The Breton derbies against [Stade Rennais](/clubs/rennes), [Lorient](/clubs/lorient) (the Derby de la Bretagne Sud, 130 kilometres away) and Guingamp are the fixtures that matter; the "Derby Breton" against Rennes has been the biggest since Brest's rise. Nantes, now in Ligue 2, are the historic enemy of all the Breton clubs.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Brest are 11th in Ligue 1 on 12 points from nine games (three wins, three draws, three defeats, 16 scored, 12 conceded). They drew 1-1 at Lille on 17 October, lost 2-1 at home to Nice on 23 October and won 2-0 at Lorient in the Breton derby on 2 November. Lyon visit Francis-Le Blé on 8 November, followed by a trip to Strasbourg on 21 November.

Lachuer's squad retains Del Castillo, Lees-Melou, Chardonnet, Magnetti and Ajorque, with Radosław Majecki in goal. The aim is a comfortable mid-table finish and, if the form holds, a flirtation with the European places again.
