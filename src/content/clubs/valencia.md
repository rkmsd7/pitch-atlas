## History

Valencia Club de Fútbol were founded on 18 March 1919 and moved into Mestalla four years later. The bat on the crest comes from the city's coat of arms, which is why the club are "Los Murciélagos". The first great side was post-war: with Edmundo Suárez "Mundo" and the midfielder Antonio Puchades, Valencia won three league titles in 1942, 1944 and 1947 and the Copa in 1941, 1949 and 1954. The 1960s brought two Fairs Cups (1962, 1963) and a third final, and in 1971 Alfredo Di Stéfano, as coach, delivered a fourth league title.

Mario Kempes, the Argentine who was the 1978 World Cup's top scorer while a Valencia player, led the 1979 Copa del Rey and the 1980 Cup Winners' Cup, won on penalties against Arsenal in Brussels. Then came the fall: relegation in 1986, an immediate return and a slow rebuild through the 1990s under Guus Hiddink, Luis Aragonés and Claudio Ranieri, whose 1999 Copa del Rey and Supercopa began the greatest era in the club's history.

Héctor Cúper took Valencia to consecutive [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history) finals, losing to Real Madrid in Paris in 2000 and to Bayern Munich on penalties in Milan in 2001, with Gaizka Mendieta, Santiago Cañizares, Roberto Ayala and Kily González. Rafael Benítez then won the league in 2001-02, the club's first in 31 years, and in 2003-04 the double of La Liga and UEFA Cup (2-0 against Marseille in Gothenburg), with David Albelda and Rubén Baraja in midfield, Pablo Aimar as playmaker and Mista scoring the goals. The seasons that followed produced [David Villa](/players/david-villa), [David Silva](/players/david-silva), Juan Mata and Jordi Alba, the 2008 Copa del Rey under Ronald Koeman, and three consecutive third-place finishes under Unai Emery (2010-12) while the stars were sold to pay for the half-built Nou Mestalla.

## Trajectory

Valencia's decline since the Emery years is the story of Peter Lim's ownership. The Singaporean businessman bought the club in 2014; Nuno Espírito Santo's team finished fourth in 2014-15, and Marcelino's side of 2017-19 finished fourth twice and won the 2019 Copa del Rey against Barcelona, the club's most recent trophy. Marcelino was sacked in September 2019 despite it, and the years since have been a cycle of coaches (Celades, Javi Gracia, Bordalás, Gattuso, Baraja, Corberán), sales of every saleable player and fan protests against the owner. The club finished 16th in 2022-23, two points above the relegation zone, and 12th in 2024-25.

In 2025-26 Carlos Corberán led the team to ninth with 49 points, a respectable result given the budget. The start of 2026-27 was poor enough that Corberán and the executive Ron Gourlay were dismissed around 13 September 2026, with Óscar Sánchez caretaking before the Mexican coach Javier Aguirre, who had just taken Mexico to the last 16 of the 2026 World Cup, was appointed on 23 September on a contract to June 2027 with an option for a further year.

## Players the club produced

The Paterna academy is among Spain's most productive. Fernando Gómez, the club's record appearance holder, came through in the 1980s; Albelda, Baraja (bought young), Vicente, Villa (signed from Zaragoza but made at Mestalla), Silva, Mata, Isco, Paco Alcácer, José Gayà, Carlos Soler, Ferran Torres, Lee Kang-in, Yunus Musah and the current midfielder Javi Guerra all emerged from it, and most were sold. [Ferran Torres](/players/ferran-torres), who left for Manchester City in 2020 and then Barcelona, scored Spain's winning goal in the 2026 World Cup final.

Signed legends include Kempes, Mendieta, Cañizares, Ayala, Aimar, Claudio López and Ever Banega. The Valencia-born goalkeeper Cañizares and the captain Gayà, now 31 and in his fourteenth season, represent continuity across the decline.

## Identity and playing style

Valencia's classic style, forged under Cúper and Benítez, was a compact 4-2-3-1 or 4-4-2 with a ferocious double pivot, disciplined [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) and set-piece strength: the best defensive record in Europe in 2004. Emery's teams played a quicker, wider game around Villa, Silva and Mata, and Marcelino's 4-4-2 revived the Benítez model. Corberán, a Bielsa and Leeds disciple, pressed high with a young side.

Aguirre, 67, has returned Valencia to pragmatism: a 4-4-2 that defends deep, works hard in the duels and counters through Hugo Duro and Diego López, with Pepelu and Javi Guerra in the middle and the Liverpool loanee Harvey Elliott used sparingly from the bench while his fitness improves. It is a survival plan first and a style second, and the owner's reported longer-term idea, a more ambitious project from summer 2027, depends on staying up.

The Benítez years remain the reference. The 2001-02 title was won with a defence of Ayala, Carboni, Angulo and Cañizares that conceded 27 goals in 38 games and a midfield in which Baraja scored from deep; the 2003-04 double side was better still, with Vicente and Rufete on the wings and the club's best season of all. Benítez left for Liverpool that summer after a dispute with the sporting director that produced his famous line about asking for a sofa and being sent a lamp, and Valencia have not been champions since.

## Rivalries

The Derbi de la Comunitat against [Villarreal](/clubs/villarreal) and the city derby with [Levante](/clubs/levante) are the local fixtures, both played with real feeling since Villarreal rose in the 2000s and Levante established themselves. [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) are the historic enemy, from the 2000 final to the 2008 Copa semi-final and the 2019 Supercopa, and [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) matches produced the 2019 Copa final and the Villa years. Mestalla against Madrid, on 8 November 2026, is this week's fixture.

## Current situation

Valencia are 19th in La Liga on 7 November 2026 with nine points from 11 games (two wins, three draws, six defeats), one point above bottom-placed Málaga and one behind Levante and Elche. Aguirre's first league game, against Racing Santander on matchday 8, found the team 19th on four points; results have improved only slightly. The squad is thin by Valencia's historic standards: Gayà, Dimitri Foulquier and Mouctar Diakhaby in defence, Guerra, Pepelu and Guido Rodríguez in midfield, Hugo Duro, Umar Sadiq and Arnaut Danjuma up front, with Elliott and Sergi Canós as the creative options.

The deeper story is unchanged. The Nou Mestalla, abandoned in 2009, is finally being completed, the sporting director Braulio Vázquez is planning for 2027 and beyond, and the supporters' relationship with Peter Lim remains broken. For a club with six titles and two European finals, survival in 2026-27 is the only objective that matters.
