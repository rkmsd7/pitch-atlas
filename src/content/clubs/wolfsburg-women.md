## History

Wolfsburg's women's team traces its roots to VfR Eintracht Wolfsburg in 1973 and merged into VfL in 2003. Backed by Volkswagen, the club rose rapidly in the 2010s under Ralf Kellermann: a first Bundesliga title in 2013 was followed days later by a [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) final win over [Lyon](/clubs/lyon-women) in London, and a second European title came a year later with a 4-3 comeback against Tyresö in Lisbon. Nadine Kessler, the 2014 FIFA World Player of the Year, Martina Müller and Alexandra Popp were the core.

The Wolves then ran into Lyon. Finals in 2016, 2018 and 2020 all ended in defeat to the French club, and the 2023 final in Eindhoven, in which Wolfsburg led [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona-women) 2-0 at half-time through [Ewa Pajor](/players/ewa-pajor) and Popp before losing 3-2, is the one that haunts the club. Domestically the dominance was total: six Bundesliga titles in seven seasons from 2017 to 2022 and nine consecutive DFB-Pokals from 2015 to 2023, a German record.

Tommy Stroot's departure and [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich-women)'s rise ended the run. Wolfsburg have been second four seasons running, 16 points behind Bayern in 2025-26 under Stephan Lerch, who returned to the club after a spell at Hoffenheim. Pajor left for Barcelona in 2024 and [Alexandra Popp](/players/alexandra-popp), the captain and symbol of the club for 14 years, agreed in March 2026 to join Borussia Dortmund for 2026-27.

## Trajectory

Promoted to the Bundesliga in 1997, Wolfsburg became a top-two club from 2012 and have not finished outside the top two since. The AOK Stadion, a purpose-built 5,200-seat ground, is their home, with the Volkswagen Arena used for Champions League nights.

## Players the club produced

Wolfsburg's recruitment rather than its academy built the legend: Popp (from Duisburg, 2012), Pajor (from Medyk Konin at 18), [Pernille Harder](/players/pernille-harder), [Caroline Graham Hansen](/players/caroline-graham-hansen), Almuth Schult, Kessler, Svenja Huth, Lena Oberdorf, Dominique Janssen and Jill Roord. Oberdorf's 2024 move to Bayern and Pajor's to Barcelona illustrated the new pecking order; Mara Alber, on loan from Chelsea, and Smilla Vallotto are the current prospects.

## Identity and playing style

Wolfsburg have always been a physical, vertical team: a 4-3-3 that wins second balls, crosses early and dominates [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), with Popp's heading the signature weapon. Lerch has kept the directness while building more patiently through Lena Lattwein and Janina Minge; Huth, now 35 and captain, still provides crosses from the right. The challenge is replacing the goals of Popp and Pajor, with Lara Prašnikar and Cecilie Fløe sharing the load.

## Rivalries

Bayern Munich is the rivalry of the decade, Eintracht Frankfurt the historic one, and Lyon the club that denied Wolfsburg three European titles.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Wolfsburg are chasing Bayern again in a 14-team Bundesliga and competing in the Champions League league phase. Without Popp for the first time since 2012, Lerch's side is younger and less experienced, and the club's standing as Germany's second power is under threat from Frankfurt and a rising Dortmund.
