## History

OGC Nice were founded in 1904 as a gymnastics club and became the dominant French side of the 1950s, winning the title in 1951, 1952, 1956 and 1959 and the Coupe de France in 1952 and 1954, with [Just Fontaine](/players/just-fontaine) scoring 44 goals in three seasons before Reims, Héctor Rial before Real Madrid, and Joseph Ujlaki and Victor Nurenberg as the stars. They reached the European Cup quarter-finals in 1957 and 1960, losing to Real Madrid both times.

Decline set in during the 1960s, with relegations in 1964, 1982, 1991 and 1997. Nice were second in 1973 and 1976 under Jean Snella and Vlatko Marković, and won a third Coupe de France in 1997 as a relegation-bound side, beating Guingamp on penalties. The 2000s were spent in lower mid-table at the Stade du Ray, with Frédéric Antonetti's fourth place in 2013 the exception and the move to the 36,000-seat Allianz Riviera in 2013 the turning point.

Claude Puel finished fourth in 2016 with Hatem Ben Arfa's resurrection season; Lucien Favre, with Mario Balotelli, Jean Michaël Seri, Dante and Younès Belhanda, finished third in 2017, the club's best since 1976, and beat Ajax in the Champions League qualifying round before losing to Napoli. Ineos, Jim Ratcliffe's petrochemical group, bought the club in 2019, and under Christophe Galtier (2021-22, cup final and fifth), Francesco Farioli (fifth in 2024) and Franck Haise (fourth in 2025) Nice became a European regular. Haise's 2025-26 was a step back, and Olivier Pantaloni, who had taken Lorient up as Ligue 2 champions in 2025, was appointed for 2026-27.

## Trajectory

Nice's trajectory since 2013 is one of steady consolidation into the top six: four top-five finishes in six seasons from 2020, two Champions League qualifying campaigns (2017, 2025), a Conference League quarter-final in 2023 and a 2022 cup final. Ineos's ownership, however, has been overshadowed by Ratcliffe's investment in Manchester United, and the club has been for sale or in limbo for much of 2025-26. Seventh last season was a disappointment by recent standards.

## Players the club produced

The academy produced Hugo Lloris (debut 2005, sold to Lyon in 2008), Olivier Echouafni, Jérémy Pied, Vincent Koziello, Malang Sarr, Khéphren Thuram (via Monaco's academy, sold to Juventus in 2024) and Jean-Clair Todibo, sold to West Ham. Ben Arfa, Balotelli, Seri, Dante (the Brazilian centre-back who played into his 40s and captained the club for a decade), Kasper Dolberg, Youcef Atal and Terem Moffi are the signings fans remember; Jérémie Boga and Sofiane Diop the current creators.

## Identity and playing style

Favre's Nice played a patient, possession-based 4-3-3 that could counter at speed; Galtier's a pragmatic [back three](/tactics/back-three); Farioli's 2023-24 side the tightest defence in Europe (the fewest goals conceded in the top five leagues at Christmas) in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) with sharp [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking). Haise brought his Lens 3-4-3 and high pressing. Pantaloni plays a 4-3-3 with a high press and width, the style that won Ligue 2 with Lorient, and the 2026-27 side has been among the more attacking in the league.

## Rivalries

The Derby de la Côte d'Azur against [Monaco](/clubs/monaco), 20 kilometres away, is the local fixture, and the Derby de la Méditerranée against [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) the heated one, with a history of crowd trouble. Bastia, the Corsican club, were a southern rival of the 1970s and 2010s.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Nice are eighth in Ligue 1 on 14 points from nine games (four wins, two draws, three defeats, 13 scored, 12 conceded). They lost 2-1 at Lyon on 18 October, won 2-1 at Brest on 23 October and beat Rennes 5-2 at the Allianz Riviera on 30 October. The next match is at Angers on 8 November.

Pantaloni's squad is built around Boga, Diop, Moffi, Morgan Sanson and goalkeeper Marcin Bułka, with Dante, now 43, in a coaching role. The target set by the club is a European place; the ownership question hangs over everything.
