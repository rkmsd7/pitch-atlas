## History

Lecce were founded in 1908 in the baroque city at the heel of Italy, and for seven decades were a Serie C club with occasional seasons in Serie B. The first promotion to Serie A came in 1985 under Eugenio Fascetti, and the first season ended in relegation but with the result the city still celebrates: a 3-2 win at Roma on the final day that cost Roma the 1986 title. Lecce came back in 1988 and stayed three seasons, with the Argentine striker Pedro Pasculli and the Yugoslav defender Zoran Vulić. The 1990s and 2000s were a yo-yo: six promotions and six relegations between 1991 and 2012, including a 1996-97 Serie B promotion with the 17-year-old striker Valeri Bojinov's predecessor Francesco Moriero, and the Zdeněk Zeman season of 2004-05, when a side with Mirko Vučinić, Valeri Bojinov and Javier Chevantón scored 66 goals and conceded 73, finishing 11th and delighting everyone.

Pantaleo Corvino, the sporting director from the Salento who discovered Vučinić, Chevantón, Cristian Ledesma and Graziano Pellè, is the man most responsible for the club's identity as a scouting operation, and he returned in 2020 to build the current team.

## Trajectory

The worst moment came in 2012, when Lecce were relegated from Serie A and then demoted to Lega Pro for the Calcioscommesse betting scandal involving the former president Giovanni Semeraro. Five seasons in the third tier followed, four of them ending in play-off defeats, before Fabio Liverani's side won Serie C in 2018 and then finished second in Serie B in 2019 to complete back-to-back promotions. The 2019-20 Serie A season ended in relegation on the final day. Marco Baroni's 2021-22 Serie B title, with Corvino's signings from the Balkans and beyond, brought Lecce back, and Baroni, then Roberto D'Aversa, Luca Gotti and Marco Giampaolo kept them up in 16th, 14th and 17th. The 2024-25 survival was secured with a 1-0 win at Lazio on the last day in May 2025, Lameck Banda's goal and Wladimiro Falcone's saves. [Eusebio Di Francesco](/managers/eusebio-di-francesco), who had been relegated with Frosinone and Venezia, was appointed in June 2025 and finished 17th with 38 points, the fourth straight survival.

## Players the club produced

Corvino's Lecce has been a conveyor belt. Patrick Dorgu, bought from Nordsjælland's academy, was sold to Manchester United for €35m in January 2025; Nikola Krstović went to Atalanta; Morten Hjulmand to Sporting for €18m; Pontus Almqvist, Gabriel Strefezza and Federico Baschirotto were all sold at a profit. The home-grown Antonino Gallo, a Palermo-born full-back raised in the youth system, and Francesco Camarda, the Milan striker who scored on loan in 2025-26 before Milan activated their buy-back, are the recent faces. Historically the club gave Italy Franco Causio, the Juventus and 1982 World Cup winger who was born in Lecce and played there in the 1960s, Fabrizio Miccoli, from nearby Nardò, and Marco Mancosu, the captain of the 2019 promotion.

## Identity and playing style

Lecce's football under Baroni and D'Aversa was a hard-pressing 4-3-3 of young, athletic imports; Di Francesco, a disciple of Zeman, plays the same formation with more vertical ambition and a higher defensive line. The 2026-27 squad is the usual Corvino mix: Falcone, 31, in goal; Tiago Gabriel, the Portuguese centre-back, and Kialonda Gaspar from Angola; Lassana Coulibaly and Ivan Ilić, signed from Torino, in midfield; Nikola Stulić, the Serbian striker, Santiago Pierotti and the Ivorian Konan N'Dri in attack, with Willem Geubbels and Joel Monteiro as alternatives. The Via del Mare, with 31,000 seats and a reputation as the loudest ground in the south, is the twelfth man.

## Rivalries

The Derby di Puglia against Bari is the one that matters, and it has not been played in Serie A since 2011. Taranto and Foggia are the other regional enemies. In the top flight Lecce's supporters have a friendship with Napoli's and a grudge against [Lazio](/clubs/lazio), born of the two final-day deciders in 2020 and 2025.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Lecce are 14th in Serie A with nine points from ten matches, three wins and seven defeats without a draw, having conceded 21 goals. The wins over Bologna, Udinese and Venezia keep them a point above the relegation zone; the 4-0 defeat at Roma and a 2-1 home loss to Bologna in which they led are typical of a side that scores but cannot hold on. Di Francesco's contract runs to 2027 and the president Saverio Sticchi Damiani has always backed his coaches through the autumn. The budget is the smallest in the division and the strategy is unchanged: survive, sell one star a year, and keep the ground full.
