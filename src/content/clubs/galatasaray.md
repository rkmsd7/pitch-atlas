## History

Galatasaray were founded in October 1905 by students of the Galatasaray High School, the Ottoman Empire's elite French-language lycée, led by Ali Sami Yen, and their founding aim was explicitly "to play together like Englishmen, to have a colour and a name, and to beat non-Turkish teams". The yellow and red, adopted in 1908, were chosen because of the way the colours blazed in the light.

The club won its first Istanbul league in 1909 and, after the national league was founded in 1959, its first Süper Lig in 1962 with Metin Oktay, the "Uncrowned King", scoring the goals. Galatasaray won four titles in the 1960s and 1970s but it was Fatih Terim's arrival in 1996 that made the club a European force. Terim's side won four consecutive titles from 1997 to 2000 with [Hakan Şükür](/players/hakan-sukur), Gheorghe Hagi, Claudio Taffarel, Bülent Korkmaz and Gheorghe Popescu, and in 1999-2000 went unbeaten at home in Europe, beating Borussia Dortmund, Mallorca, Leeds and Real Madrid's conquerors before beating [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) on penalties in the UEFA Cup final in Copenhagen. It was the first European trophy won by a Turkish club. Three months later, under Mircea Lucescu, they beat Real Madrid 2-1 in the UEFA Super Cup with two Mário Jardel goals.

The years after Terim's first departure were uneven: titles in 2002, 2006 and 2008, but also the ignominy of a 2010-11 season that ended eighth. Terim returned for a third spell in 2011 and won two more championships with [Wesley Sneijder](/players/wesley-sneijder) and [Didier Drogba](/players/didier-drogba), reaching the 2013 Champions League quarter-final, where they lost to Real Madrid after a 3-2 second-leg win. A fourth Terim spell brought the 2018 and 2019 titles; a thirteenth-place finish in 2021-22 was the club's worst in decades.

[Okan Buruk](/managers/okan-buruk), the club's UEFA Cup-winning midfielder, was appointed in June 2022 and has not lost a title race since. His first season was won with Mauro Icardi's goals; the second, 2023-24, with a Turkish record 102 points in a ferocious race with Fenerbahçe; the third, 2024-25, with [Victor Osimhen](/players/victor-osimhen) on loan from Napoli scoring freely and a Turkish Cup added for a double. Club records list a fourth consecutive title in 2025-26 alongside a Champions League league-phase campaign.

## Trajectory

Galatasaray's 26 Süper Lig titles are a Turkish record and their dominance since 2022 has been near-total: four straight championships, a record points total and a double. The twenty-first century as a whole has brought eleven titles, more than any rival, with the lows of 2004 (sixth), 2011 (eighth) and 2022 (thirteenth) coming between coaching changes.

In Europe the 2000 UEFA Cup remains unmatched by any Turkish club, and the 2013 Champions League quarter-final is the modern high point. Galatasaray are in the 2026-27 Champions League league phase and sit in the middle of the table with six points from four matches.

## Players the club produced

Galatasaray's academy produced Metin Oktay, Bülent Korkmaz, Okan Buruk, Emre Belözoğlu, Arda Turan and Sabri Sarıoğlu, and more recently Barış Alper Yılmaz, who came via Keçiörengücü, and Yunus Akgün. [Hakan Şükür](/players/hakan-sukur), the club's and Turkey's record scorer, arrived from Bursaspor. The club's foreign stars have been equally important: Hagi, Taffarel, Popescu and Jardel in the Terim era; Sneijder, Drogba, Felipe Melo and Fernando Muslera, the Uruguayan goalkeeper who spent 13 years at the club; and in the Buruk era Icardi, Osimhen, Dries Mertens and Leroy Sané.

## Identity and playing style

Galatasaray's identity is the "Welcome to Hell" of the old Ali Sami Yen and the sense that the club represents Istanbul's European side and Turkish football's international ambitions. [Okan Buruk](/managers/okan-buruk) plays a 4-2-3-1 built on [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) pace, [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into a dominant centre-forward, aggressive [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) in midfield and ruthless [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces). Calm authority on the touchline, in a league where coaches are routinely sacked within months, has become part of the club's brand.

The financial model behind the Buruk era is unusual. Galatasaray carry heavy debt, like every big Turkish club, but president Dursun Özbek's board has leveraged the club's size to attract players in their late twenties and early thirties on wages that Serie A and the Premier League's middle tier could not match: Icardi from PSG, Osimhen from Napoli, Sané from Bayern, Lucas Torreira from Arsenal. The strategy has delivered domestic dominance and a return to the Champions League's main draw after years in the qualifying rounds, and the club's new stadium, renamed RAMS Park in 2023, generates the highest matchday revenue in Turkey. The question that follows every title is whether the model can be sustained if European progress does not come.

The academy, Florya, has also been revitalised. Barış Alper Yılmaz's rise from the third tier to the national team and a reported €35 million valuation, and the emergence of Yunus Akgün and Kazımcan Karataş, give Buruk a Turkish core to balance the imports, something the Terim sides always had.

## Rivalries

The Intercontinental Derby against [Fenerbahçe](/clubs/fenerbahce), Europe against Asia across the Bosphorus, is the biggest match in Turkey and one of the most hostile in the world. [Beşiktaş](/clubs/besiktas) complete the Istanbul triangle, and [Trabzonspor](/clubs/trabzonspor), the only non-Istanbul club to win the league consistently, provide the fiercest provincial opposition. The Fenerbahçe fixture has been played without away supporters for long stretches since 2014 because of crowd trouble, and the 2024 Turkish Super Cup between the two clubs in Riyadh was abandoned before kick-off in a dispute over Atatürk imagery, an indication of how far the rivalry reaches beyond football. Galatasaray's derby record under Buruk, including the 2025-26 win that triggered Kartal's departure across the Bosphorus, has been a large part of his authority.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Galatasaray lead the Süper Lig after ten rounds, two points clear of Amed SFK and three ahead of Trabzonspor, with Fenerbahçe already nine points adrift after a chaotic autumn. In the Champions League league phase they have six points from four matches and a realistic chance of the knockout play-offs. A fifth consecutive title would be unprecedented in Turkish football; the harder target, and the one supporters most want, is a deep European run with Osimhen, Icardi and Sané.
