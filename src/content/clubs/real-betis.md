## History

Real Betis Balompié were born from a split: Sevilla Balompié was founded in 1907 by students, merged in 1914 with Betis Football Club (itself a breakaway from Sevilla FC by members unhappy with the older club's class exclusivity), and received royal patronage the same year. That origin gave Betis an identity as the city's people's club, with the motto "¡Viva er Betis manque pierda!" (long live Betis even when they lose) capturing a supporters' culture that prizes devotion over silverware.

The one league title came in 1934-35 under the Irish coach Patrick O'Connell, with a side built on the defence of Peral, Areso and Aedo, the last title won by a club from Andalusia for 90 years. The Civil War scattered the team, and Betis fell as far as the third tier in 1947, the only club to have won the Spanish league and then played in Tercera. The Benito Villamarín era (president from 1955) brought a return to the top flight in 1958 and the first Copa del Rey in 1977, won on penalties against Athletic with Rafael Gordillo and Julio Cardeñosa.

The 1990s were the Manuel Ruiz de Lopera years, extravagant and unstable: the world-record signing of Denílson in 1998, Alfonso's goals, Finidi George, Benjamín, a UEFA Cup place in 1995, then relegation in 2000. The best modern side, coached by Lorenzo Serra Ferrer with Joaquín, Oliveira and Edu, finished fourth in 2004-05 and won the 2005 Copa del Rey against Osasuna, then took the club into the Champions League for the first time, drawing Chelsea, Liverpool and Anderlecht. Lopera's financial collapse, two further relegations (2009, 2014) and a fan takeover followed before stability returned under president Ángel Haro.

## Trajectory

Betis have been promoted three times this century (2001, and as Segunda champions in 2011 and 2015) and relegated three times (2000, 2009, 2014), a yo-yo pattern that ended for good with [Manuel Pellegrini](/managers/manuel-pellegrini)'s appointment in 2020. Since 2020-21 they have finished sixth, fifth, sixth, seventh, sixth and fifth, won the 2022 Copa del Rey (penalties against Valencia at La Cartuja after a 1-1 draw, with Borja Iglesias and Juan Miranda), reached the 2025 Conference League final in Wrocław, lost 4-1 to Chelsea, and in 2025-26 qualified for the Champions League league phase for the second time in their history, finishing fifth with 60 points behind Villarreal and Atlético.

Pellegrini, the Chilean engineer who won the Premier League with Manchester City, became the first coach to reach 100 La Liga wins with Betis and is contracted to June 2027, the longest-serving coach in the club's modern era.

## Players the club produced

The academy produced Gordillo, the great left-sided player of the 1980s who went on to Real Madrid, Joaquín, who left for Valencia in 2006 and returned in 2015 to play until he was 41, Dani Ceballos, who returned from Real Madrid in 2025, and Fabián Ruiz, who won the 2026 World Cup with [Spain](/nations/spain) after leaving for Napoli and PSG. Rafael Alkorta, Cardeñosa and Alfonso Pérez are other names on the Villamarín's honour roll.

Pellegrini's Betis has been built on experienced signings: Nabil Fekir, Sergio Canales, Andrés Guardado, Claudio Bravo, Germán Pezzella, Isco (whose revival since 2023 has been the romantic story of the project), Giovani Lo Celso, Pablo Fornals, Marc Bartra and Héctor Bellerín. Antony, signed from Manchester United after a 2025 loan, became one of the league's most productive wingers and the Colombian striker Cucho Hernández arrived from Columbus Crew.

## Identity and playing style

Betis under Pellegrini play an attacking 4-2-3-1 with a possession bias and quick combinations through the centre, in the tradition of the Chilean's Villarreal and Málaga sides. Isco is the playmaker, Lo Celso and Fornals rotate with him, Antony and Abde Ezzalzouli supply width and dribbling, and the full-backs Bellerín and Junior Firpo push high. The approach has roots in [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) but is less structured and more improvisational than Barcelona's. Defensively the team is a mid-block that can be opened through the channels, which is why Betis draw so often (15 draws in 2025-26) and why big European nights have been a mixed bag.

The crowd is the club's other identity: the Villamarín is the loudest ground in Spain, and the Gol Sur's "Himno de Betis" before kick-off is a ritual. While the Villamarín is redeveloped, Betis are playing home matches at the Estadio La Cartuja, the city's 2022 and 2026 Copa final venue.

The 2025 Conference League final in Wrocław, lost 4-1 to Chelsea after Abde Ezzalzouli had given Betis a first-half lead, was the club's first European final and the moment the Pellegrini project became visible abroad. The following season's fifth place, achieved with a squad that had lost its best midfielder, Johnny Cardoso, to Atlético, confirmed that it was built to last. Betis also reached the Copa semi-finals in 2025-26 before losing to Atlético.

## Rivalries

El Gran Derbi against [Sevilla](/clubs/sevilla) divides the city of Seville by district, family and class, and is the most intense fixture in Spain outside the Clásico. The next one is on 21 November 2026, with Betis at home at La Cartuja. [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) are a historic foe, partly through the Lopera-era politics and the 2005 Copa; [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) matches have produced memorable Betis wins, including the 4-3 at Camp Nou in 2018.

## Current situation

Betis are fourth in La Liga on 7 November 2026 with 23 points from 11 games (seven wins, two draws, two defeats), the best start to a league season in the Pellegrini era, inside the Champions League places. The Champions League campaign is the first since 2005-06; the trip to Slovan Bratislava on 24 November follows the derby. Pellegrini, in his seventh season, has a squad that mixes the veteran core with Troy Parrott, the Irish striker, Nelson Deossa in midfield and Natan and Valentín Gómez in defence. The 5-2 home win over Osasuna on 11 October was the result that got Luis Miguel Ramis sacked the next morning.

The club's aim this year is the one it has edged towards for six seasons: a top-four finish and a knockout run in Europe. "Manque pierda" still applies, but Betis have rarely needed the excuse less.
