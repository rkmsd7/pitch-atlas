## History

Fenerbahçe were founded in 1907 in Kadıköy, on the Asian shore of the Bosphorus, at a time when the Ottoman authorities forbade Turks from forming sports clubs; the founders met in secret. The club's yellow and navy, and its association with Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, who visited the ground in 1918 and whose name adorns the club's museum, gave it a national significance that has endured. Fenerbahçe claim the largest fan base in Turkey and the ground in Kadıköy, officially the Şükrü Saracoğlu Stadium, is the most intimidating in the country.

Fenerbahçe won the first Süper Lig in 1959 and dominated the early decades of the national league, with Lefter Küçükandonyadis and then Can Bartu the stars, taking nine of the first 25 championships. The 1980s brought two titles and the arrival of a generation of imports led by Toni Schumacher, and in 1996 Christoph Daum's team won the title with Jay-Jay Okocha and inflicted Manchester United's first European home defeat at Old Trafford.

The best modern period came under Christoph Daum's second spell and then Zico. With Alex de Souza, the Brazilian playmaker who became the club's greatest foreign player, [Roberto Carlos](/players/roberto-carlos), Emre Belözoğlu and Volkan Demirel, Fenerbahçe won the title in 2001, 2004, 2005 and 2007 and in 2008 reached the Champions League quarter-final, beating Sevilla on penalties before losing to Chelsea. Aykut Kocaman's 2011 title was followed by the match-fixing scandal of that summer, which saw president Aziz Yıldırım jailed, the club banned from the Champions League and the league title it had won never fully celebrated. Ersun Yanal's 2014 title, with Dirk Kuyt and Emmanuel Emenike, is the last.

Since then the club has lived through one of the longest droughts in its history, finishing second seven times in twelve seasons, most painfully in 2023-24 when 99 points under İsmail Kartal were not enough. [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) was appointed in 2024 to end the wait and could not, leaving in 2025, and the 2026-27 season began with Kartal back for a fourth spell, his resignation after a derby defeat, an interim spell under Kuyt and, on 3 November 2026, the appointment of [Emre Belözoğlu](/managers/emre-belozoglu).

## Trajectory

Fenerbahçe's 19 titles place them second in Turkey behind Galatasaray's 26, but the distribution is telling: six since 2000, none since 2014, and ten second-place finishes in the same period. The club's ability to spend, backed by the richest membership in the country, has not translated into championships in the Okan Buruk era across the Bosphorus.

In Europe, the 2008 Champions League quarter-final and the 2013 Europa League semi-final, lost to Benfica, are the high points. The club has been a consistent presence in the Champions League qualifying rounds and league phase and sits in the lower half of the 2026-27 league phase with five points from four matches.

## Players the club produced

Fenerbahçe's academy produced Can Bartu, Volkan Demirel, Semih Şentürk and, more recently, Ferdi Kadıoğlu, who became the first-choice full-back for both the club and Turkey before moving to Brighton, and Arda Güler, sold to Real Madrid in 2023 at 18, the most gifted Turkish product of his generation. The club's great foreigners, Alex, [Roberto Carlos](/players/roberto-carlos), Pierre van Hooijdonk, Nicolas Anelka, [Robin van Persie](/players/robin-van-persie), Dirk Kuyt, Edin Džeko and Youssef En-Nesyri, have often been signed at the end of careers but given the club some of its most memorable seasons.

## Identity and playing style

Fenerbahçe's identity is grandeur and grievance: the biggest support, the deepest pockets and a conviction, hardened by the 2011 scandal, that the football establishment is against them. The playing style has changed with each coach. Mourinho's 2024-25 side was a compact, counter-attacking 4-2-3-1; Kartal's teams were more expansive. [Emre Belözoğlu](/managers/emre-belozoglu), whose Başakşehir side was praised for organised possession football, favours [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps), and inherits a squad built for a 4-2-3-1 with En-Nesyri as the central striker.

The 2011 scandal is the hinge of the modern club. Yıldırım and dozens of others were convicted of match-fixing in the 2010-11 season; Fenerbahçe were barred from the 2011-12 Champions League by the Turkish federation and later banned from Europe by UEFA for two seasons, and the club has never accepted the verdicts, which were overturned in a 2020 retrial. The episode cost Fenerbahçe a generation of European revenue and poisoned relations with the federation, and the sense of injustice shapes the way supporters interpret every refereeing decision and every lost title race. Ali Koç, elected in 2018 on a promise to end the Yıldırım era, spent heavily and clashed publicly with the federation but could not deliver the title; his successors inherited the same expectations.

The club's commercial strength remains enormous: the most members of any Turkish club, a stadium that sells out for every big match and a global diaspora. What it has lacked is the stability that Galatasaray found under Buruk, and the appointment of a former captain is an attempt to find it from within.

## Rivalries

The Intercontinental Derby against [Galatasaray](/clubs/galatasaray) is Turkey's great rivalry, Asia against Europe, Kadıköy against Beyoğlu. The derby with [Beşiktaş](/clubs/besiktas) is nearly as old, and the rivalry with [Trabzonspor](/clubs/trabzonspor) was poisoned by the disputed 2010-11 title, which Trabzonspor still claim. Derby days in Kadıköy are among the most intense in Europe, with the stadium's steep stands and the Bosphorus ferries bringing supporters across from the European side, and the club's record against Galatasaray in the Buruk years has been the single biggest source of frustration for the support.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Fenerbahçe are seventh in the Süper Lig after ten rounds, nine points behind Galatasaray, following a chaotic autumn in which Kartal resigned after a derby defeat and Dirk Kuyt steered the team to the November break. Emre Belözoğlu takes over with the league roughly a third gone and the club still in the Champions League league phase; early results after the international break will determine whether the season can be salvaged. His coaching record is mixed, his status as captain of the last title-winning side is not, and the fan base's patience with the board is thin.
