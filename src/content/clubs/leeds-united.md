## History

Leeds United were formed in October 1919 after Leeds City were expelled from the Football League for illegal payments to players during the war. The new club took over Elland Road and spent its first four decades as a yo-yo side, with John Charles, the Gentle Giant who could play centre-half or centre-forward and was sold to Juventus in 1957, its one great player.

Don Revie, appointed player-manager in 1961, changed the colours to all white in imitation of Real Madrid and built a team that finished first or second in the league seven times in ten seasons. Revie's Leeds won the title in 1969 and 1974, the FA Cup in 1972, the League Cup in 1968 and the Fairs Cup in 1968 and 1971, with Billy Bremner, [Jack Charlton](/players/jack-charlton), Norman Hunter, Johnny Giles, Peter Lorimer, Eddie Gray and Allan Clarke. They were loved and loathed in equal measure: brilliant, cynical, and runners-up far more often than the record suggests they should have been, including the 1975 European Cup final lost to Bayern Munich in Paris amid rioting. Revie left for England in 1974; Brian Clough's 44 days as his successor became a book and a film.

Relegation in 1982 was followed by eight years in the Second Division. Howard Wilkinson brought them back in 1990 and, with Gordon Strachan, Gary McAllister, David Batty, Gary Speed and the late arrival of Eric Cantona, won the last First Division title in 1992. George Graham and then David O'Leary built a young side with Harry Kewell, Alan Smith, Jonathan Woodgate, Lee Bowyer and Rio Ferdinand that reached the UEFA Cup semi-final in 2000 and the Champions League semi-final in 2001. Chairman Peter Ridsdale had borrowed against future Champions League income to pay for it; when that income stopped, the club collapsed. Relegation in 2004, administration, a 15-point deduction and the drop to League One in 2007 followed, with Ken Bates and later Massimo Cellino as owners.

Marcelo Bielsa, appointed in 2018 by Andrea Radrizzani, made Leeds relevant again: the Championship title in 2020, ninth in the Premier League in 2021, and a style of football that made Elland Road a destination. The 49ers Enterprises group completed its takeover in 2023 after relegation, and [Daniel Farke](/managers/daniel-farke) won promotion with 100 points in 2024-25, a season after losing the play-off final to Southampton.

## Trajectory

The trajectory of the last 25 years is the steepest fall and recovery in the top division. Fourth and a Champions League semi-final in 2001; relegation in 2004 with debts above £100m; the Championship play-off final defeat to Watford in 2006; relegation to League One in 2007; three seasons in the third tier, including the 2010 FA Cup win at Old Trafford against Manchester United; a decade in the Championship between seventh and 15th; then Bielsa's rise, two Premier League seasons, relegation in 2023, and Farke's promotion.

Leeds's 2025-26 season ended in 14th on 47 points, the first time Farke had kept a promoted side in the Premier League, and the first survival for the club since 2022. In early November 2026 they are 12th after nine matches, with two wins and five draws.

## Players the club produced

Leeds's academy at Thorp Arch was the engine of the O'Leary team: Kewell, Smith, Woodgate, Paul Robinson, Ian Harte, Stephen McPhail and later James Milner, who debuted at 16, Fabian Delph, Kalvin Phillips, the Yorkshire Pirlo of Bielsa's team, and Archie Gray, sold to Tottenham in 2024. Earlier graduates included Bremner's generation of Revie-era players, many of whom came through the club's youth system: Lorimer, Gray, Hunter, Paul Reaney, Paul Madeley and Terry Cooper.

The signings who became legends include John Charles, Allan Clarke, Gordon Strachan, Tony Yeboah, whose volleys against Liverpool and Wimbledon in 1995 remain the club's most replayed goals, Lucas Radebe, Mark Viduka, Pablo Hernández and Raphinha. In the current side, Ethan Ampadu is the captain, James Trafford, signed from Burnley as the new number one, is described by the club as a record signing, Dominic Calvert-Lewin, who joined from Everton in 2025, leads the line, and Anton Stach and Ao Tanaka run the midfield.

## Identity and playing style

[Daniel Farke](/managers/daniel-farke) won two Championship titles with Norwich with a possession-based 4-2-3-1 and a third with Leeds. In the Premier League he has become more pragmatic: a compact mid-block, quick transitions through Daniel James and Harry Wilson on the wings, and a strong emphasis on [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), where Joe Rodon and Jaka Bijol are the targets. Leeds's 2026-27 defensive record, with only eight goals conceded in nine matches by early November, is among the best in the league, and Trafford's form has been a large part of that.

The attacking structure is simpler than Bielsa's: Calvert-Lewin as the target, Brenden Aaronson or Wilson as the number ten, and Jayden Bogle overlapping from right-back. Farke's Leeds are a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) team at heart with good possession numbers in home games.

## Rivalries

[Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) are the Roses rivalry, revived with Leeds's returns to the top flight in 2020 and 2025; the fixture at Elland Road on 18 October 2026 ended 1-1. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) are the enmity of the 1970 FA Cup final and Revie-era battles. In Yorkshire, [Sheffield United](/clubs/sheffield-united) and [Bradford City](/clubs/bradford-city) are the local rivalries, and Huddersfield Town the oldest.

## Current situation

Leeds are 12th in early November 2026 with two wins, five draws and two defeats from nine: nine goals scored, eight conceded, 11 points. The autumn results include the 1-1 draw with Manchester United, a 0-0 at Sunderland on 25 October and a 2-0 defeat at Bournemouth on 31 October. The home game against Tottenham on 7 November was the tenth fixture.

The summer rebuild spent more than £74m: Trafford, Nico Elvedi, Tarik Muharemović, Sean Longstaff, Noah Okafor, Lukas Nmecha and Harry Wilson on a free, with Pascal Struijk sold to Brighton. With three promoted clubs in the division, the ambition is a comfortable mid-table finish and, in time, a push for Europe. Farke, in his fourth season, is the longest-serving Leeds manager since Wilkinson.
