## History

Milan were founded by Englishmen. Herbert Kilpin, a lace-maker from Nottingham, and Alfred Edwards registered the Milan Foot-Ball and Cricket Club on 16 December 1899, chose red and black "to represent the devil and the fear it strikes in opponents", and won the Italian championship in 1901, 1906 and 1907. The club has kept the English spelling of the city's name ever since; the fascist regime forced it to become Milano for a few years, but Milan it remained. The 1908 walkout that created [Internazionale](/clubs/inter-milan) came from inside this club.

The post-war decades belonged to the Gre-No-Li trio, Gunnar Gren, Gunnar Nordahl and Nils Liedholm, three Swedes who turned Milan into the dominant Italian side of the 1950s, and then to [Gianni Rivera](/players/gianni-rivera), the Golden Boy signed from Alessandria at 16 who played 658 games and in 1969 became the first Italian to win the Ballon d'Or. Nereo Rocco's Milan beat Benfica at Wembley in 1963 to become the first Italian club to win the European Cup, and added a second in 1969 against Ajax with Rivera and Pierino Prati running riot. The 1973 Cup Winners' Cup was the last trophy of that era.

Then came collapse. Milan were relegated to Serie B in 1980 for their part in the Totonero betting scandal, came straight back, and went down again in 1982 on the pitch. The club that Silvio Berlusconi bought in February 1986 was broke and mediocre. His first great decision was to appoint Arrigo Sacchi, an unknown coach from Parma, in 1987, and his second was to buy [Ruud Gullit](/players/ruud-gullit), [Marco van Basten](/players/marco-van-basten) and [Frank Rijkaard](/players/frank-rijkaard). Sacchi's Milan, with [Franco Baresi](/players/franco-baresi) at the back and a teenage [Paolo Maldini](/players/paolo-maldini) at left-back, played a zonal, high-pressing 4-4-2 that broke Italian football's defensive habits and won the 1989 and 1990 European Cups, the second a 4-0 demolition of Steaua Bucharest in Barcelona that is often called the finest final performance ever given. Fabio Capello inherited the side in 1991 and went 58 league games unbeaten, won four titles in five years, and beat Barcelona 4-0 in the 1994 Champions League final in Athens with a team missing both Baresi and Costacurta.

The second great wave came under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) between 2001 and 2009. With [Andrea Pirlo](/players/andrea-pirlo) reinvented as a deep playmaker, [Kaká](/players/kaka) sprinting between the lines, [Andriy Shevchenko](/players/andriy-shevchenko) and [Filippo Inzaghi](/players/filippo-inzaghi) finishing, and Maldini, [Alessandro Nesta](/players/alessandro-nesta) and [Cafu](/players/cafu) behind them, Milan won the 2003 Champions League on penalties against Juventus at Old Trafford, lost the 2005 final to Liverpool in Istanbul after leading 3-0 at half-time, and avenged it in Athens in 2007 with two Inzaghi goals. That seventh European Cup is the one no other Italian club has approached. Ancelotti's side also won the 2004 Scudetto and the 2007 Club World Cup.

## Trajectory

Milan have been in Serie B twice, in 1980-81 and 1982-83, and have otherwise been in the top flight since 1929. The post-Berlusconi years were the hardest in half a century. After the 2011 title under Massimiliano Allegri and a Zlatan Ibrahimović-led attack, the squad was sold off, the club finished between sixth and tenth for six straight seasons, and Berlusconi's 2017 sale to the Chinese businessman Yonghong Li ended with the hedge fund Elliott Management taking control when he defaulted in 2018. Elliott cleaned the balance sheet, hired Maldini as technical director, and Stefano Pioli's side won a 19th Scudetto in 2021-22, the first in 11 years, with [Rafael Leão](/players/rafael-leao) as its star. RedBird Capital bought the club in 2022, sacked Maldini a year later, and the second half of the decade has been bumpy: a Champions League semi-final in 2023, second in 2024, then an eighth-place 2024-25 under first Paulo Fonseca and then Sérgio Conceição, albeit with a Supercoppa win in Riyadh in January 2025.

Allegri returned for 2025-26 and delivered fifth place and 70 points, a 15-point improvement, before being sacked on 25 May 2026 when the club decided it wanted a younger, more expansive coach. [Ruben Amorim](/managers/ruben-amorim), fresh from his bruising Manchester United spell, was appointed on 16 June. The club also faces the biggest infrastructure question in its history: Milan and Inter completed the purchase of San Siro from the city in 2025 and plan a new 71,500-seat stadium on the site, with the old ground to be partly demolished.

Milan's record in finals is the club's calling card: 11 European Cup finals, seven won, more than any club but Real Madrid, and a reputation for peaking in May. The 1989 side's 5-0 semi-final win over Real Madrid at San Siro, the 1994 Athens final, the 2007 revenge in Athens and Pirlo's long-range passing in 2003 are the moments the Curva Sud sings about. The club has also been the home of a particular kind of longevity: Maldini, Costacurta, Ambrosini, Seedorf and Pirlo all played into their late 30s under Ancelotti's famous "Milan Lab" sports-science programme, which allowed the oldest squad in Europe to win the 2007 Champions League with an average age over 31.

## Players the club produced

No club has produced more one-club defenders of distinction. Franco Baresi played 719 games from 1977 to 1997, a sweeper who stepped up rather than back; Paolo Maldini, son of the 1963 captain Cesare, played 902 games over 25 seasons and retired at 41 with five European Cups; Alessandro Costacurta played 663. All three were academy graduates, as was Rivera's partner Angelo Anquilletti and later Demetrio Albertini, the metronome of the Capello years. Gianni Rivera and Gianluigi Donnarumma, who debuted at 16 in 2015 and left for Paris in 2021, are the other great home-grown names, and Francesco Camarda, who became Serie A's youngest ever player at 15 in 2023, is the current hope.

The signings are a who's who of the game. Van Basten scored 125 goals in 201 games before his ankle gave out at 28. Gullit, Rijkaard, Dejan Savićević, Zvonimir Boban, George Weah, Shevchenko, who scored 175 goals and won the 2004 Ballon d'Or, Kaká, who won it in 2007, Pirlo, Seedorf, the only man to win the Champions League with three clubs, Nesta, Cafu, Ibrahimović across two spells, and Leão, sold to Galatasaray for €38m in summer 2026 after seven seasons. [Luka Modrić](/players/luka-modric), who joined on a free from Real Madrid in 2025 at 39, is still in the squad at 41.

## Identity and playing style

Milan's football identity was forged by Sacchi: zonal defending, a compact 25-metre block, pressing as a collective act, and a belief that the team is the star. Capello made it more pragmatic and Ancelotti bent it around a diamond midfield to fit Pirlo and Kaká, but the club's self-image has always been attacking and European, in contrast to the siege mentality next door at Inter.

Amorim brought his 3-4-3 to Milan, the system that ended Sporting's title drought and failed at Old Trafford. He plays a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with Fikayo Tomori, Mario Gila, signed from Lazio for €25m, and Strahinja Pavlović, wing-backs in Pervis Estupiñán and Alexis Saelemaekers, Modrić and Adrien Rabiot as a double pivot, and two number tens, often [Christian Pulisic](/players/christian-pulisic) and Omari Hutchinson, behind Gonçalo Ramos, the €74m signing from PSG. Diego Moreira, bought from Strasbourg for €45m, gives width on the left. The early results suggest a side far better in transition than in sustained possession, with Rabiot's late runs producing five league goals and the 19-year-old Alphadjo Cissé breaking through as the top scorer across all competitions.

## Rivalries

The Derby della Madonnina with [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) is the city's permanent argument. Milan's 3-1 win at San Siro on 31 October 2026 was the first derby victory of the Amorim era and the champions' only league defeat of the season to date. The rivalry with [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) is about silverware and the 2003 Champions League final, the only all-Italian final in the competition's history. Milan fans have a historical hostility to [Genoa](/clubs/genoa) dating from the 1995 killing of a Genoa supporter, Vincenzo Spagnolo, by a Milan ultra, an event that forced a rethink of Italian fan culture. Games against [Napoli](/clubs/napoli) and [Roma](/clubs/roma) carry the weight of recent title races and the 2025 Supercoppa semi-final.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Milan sit fifth in Serie A with 21 points from ten games, six wins, three draws and one defeat, four points off the top in a congested race. The derby win over Inter and a 4-0 Europa League victory at Red Bull Salzburg on 15 October are the high points; the 0-3 at Bournemouth a week later and a 4-2 home defeat to Bologna showed the fragility of a side still learning Amorim's shape. Milan are in the Europa League league phase, their first European season since 2024-25, and enter the Coppa Italia at the round of 16 in January.

The summer business, €155m spent against €87m recovered, was the most aggressive of the RedBird era. Ramos has started slowly, Hutchinson's loan from Nottingham Forest includes an option to buy, and the return of Samuel Chukwueze and Yunus Musah from loans gives depth. The absence of Leão's dribbling is the obvious hole in the attack. The target set by Giorgio Furlani and Igli Tare is a return to the Champions League; a title tilt would be a bonus in Amorim's first season.
