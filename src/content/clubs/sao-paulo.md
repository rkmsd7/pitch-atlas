## History

São Paulo Futebol Clube was founded in 1930 by former members of Club Athletico Paulistano and Associação Atlética das Palmeiras, dissolved in 1935 and refounded in 1935-36, which is why the club counts its history from 1930 and its continuity from the refounding. From the start it was the club of the city's professional classes and its first great star was Leônidas da Silva, the "Black Diamond" who invented the bicycle kick, signed in 1942 and the driver of five Paulista titles in the 1940s. The Morumbi, a 1960 bowl built with the club's own money and once holding 140,000, gave São Paulo a stadium larger than any rival's.

The 1970s and 1980s brought the first national titles, in 1977 and 1986, with Serginho Chulapa, Careca, Dario Pereyra and Oscar. The greatest era came when [Telê Santana](/managers/tele-santana), the coach of Brazil's beloved 1982 side, took charge in 1990. His São Paulo won the Brasileirão in 1991, the Copa Libertadores in 1992 and 1993, and the Intercontinental Cup twice: 2-1 against [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in Tokyo in 1992, with Raí scoring twice against Cruyff's Dream Team, and 3-2 against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) in 1993. Raí, [Cafu](/players/cafu), Müller, Zetti, Leonardo and Palhinha were the core, and Telê's insistence on technical, attacking football made the side the reference point for a generation.

## Trajectory

The 2000s were the club's second golden age, built on the goalkeeper Rogério Ceni, who played 1,237 games, scored 131 goals from free kicks and penalties, and captained the team for 15 years. Under Paulo Autuori, São Paulo won the 2005 Libertadores against Athletico Paranaense and then the first FIFA [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) in its modern format, beating Liverpool 1-0 in Yokohama with Mineiro's goal and a Ceni masterclass. Muricy Ramalho's teams then won three straight Brasileirão titles in 2006, 2007 and 2008, a feat matched only by Pelé's Santos.

Since 2008 the club has not won the league. A 2012 Copa Sudamericana, a 2021 Paulista under Hernán Crespo that ended a nine-year wait for any trophy, and the 2023 Copa do Brasil under Dorival Júnior, with Lucas Moura's return from Tottenham the catalyst, have been the highlights. The Morumbi was renamed MorumBIS under a naming-rights deal in 2024, and Oscar, the former Chelsea midfielder, came home in 2025 for the club's tricolor finale to his career. Crespo's second spell in 2025 ended mid-season; the head coach for 2026 is not confirmed in the club's current squad data as of November 2026, so none is listed here. São Paulo are tenth in the 2026 Brasileirão with 44 points from 34 games.

## Players the club produced

Cotia, the club's academy, is among the most productive in Brazil. [Kaká](/players/kaka) made his debut in 2001 and was sold to AC Milan in 2003; Casemiro went to [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) in 2013; Lucas Moura to PSG in 2013; Oscar left as a teenager in 2010; Hernanes, Antony (via Ajax to Manchester United), Éder Militão (via Porto to Real Madrid), Rodrigo Caio, Brenner, Igor Gomes and Lucas Beraldo (to PSG in 2024) followed. Of the older generation, Cafu, Müller, Zetti and Denílson came through the club. Ceni, Raí, Luís Fabiano, Dario Pereyra and the Argentine Jonathan Calleri were the great signings.

## Identity and playing style

São Paulo's self-image is "soberano", sovereign, the aristocrat among São Paulo clubs, and its football has traditionally been technical and organised: Telê's 4-4-2 with wingers and a creative Raí, Muricy's compact 3-5-2 with Ceni's long passes, and in the 2020s a possession game under Crespo, Rogério Ceni as a coach, and Dorival. The club's great tradition of goalkeepers, Zetti, Ceni, Rafael, is part of the identity, as is the academy's role in funding the club through sales. The 2026 team has been built around Oscar's passing, Lucas Moura's dribbling and Calleri's finishing in a 4-2-3-1, defensively solid, offensively dependent on individual moments.

## Rivalries

The Majestoso with [Corinthians](/clubs/corinthians), the elite against the people, is the rivalry that most defines São Paulo's identity, and the Choque-Rei with [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) has produced several of the biggest Paulista and Libertadores ties. The San-São with [Santos](/clubs/santos) is older and was at its best in the Pelé and Telê eras. Internationally, the club's three Intercontinental wins against Barcelona, Milan and Liverpool are the games the fans still sing about.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 São Paulo are tenth in the Brasileirão, chasing a Sudamericana place, with no head coach confirmed in the current squad data after a season of instability. Oscar, 35, Lucas Moura, Calleri, Luciano and the goalkeeper Rafael are the senior players, and the club's debt, among the largest in Brazil, limits every window. The academy at Cotia remains the engine: the next Kaká or Casemiro is the most reliable route back to the top.
