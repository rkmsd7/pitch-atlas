## History

Club Atlético Osasuna were founded in Pamplona on 24 October 1920; the name is Basque for "health" and the club remains one of the four in Spain owned by its members. Osasuna first reached [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) in 1935 and spent the next half-century bouncing between divisions, with the move to El Sadar in 1967 and a period of stability from 1980 under Pedro Mari Zabalza. The 1980s and early 1990s were the first golden era: fourth in 1990-91 with Jan Urban's goals, a UEFA Cup run in 1991-92 that included a win at Stuttgart, and the arrival of Michael Robinson, who became a Spanish television icon.

Relegation in 1994 led to six seasons in Segunda, with promotion in 2000 under Miguel Ángel Lotina. The twenty-first century's best spells came under Javier Aguirre, the Mexican who took Osasuna to the 2005 Copa del Rey final (lost 2-1 to Betis in extra time) and a fourth-place finish in 2005-06, the club's highest, with César Cruchaga, Patxi Puñal and Javier Flaño; and then, in 2006-07 under Ziganda, to the UEFA Cup semi-final, where Sevilla won 3-1 on aggregate. Osasuna were relegated in 2014 and 2017 and promoted in 2016 and, as champions, in 2019 under Jagoba Arrasate, whose six seasons produced the 2023 Copa final, lost 2-1 to Real Madrid, and the club's first European qualification in 16 years (a Conference League play-off in 2023, lost to Club Brugge).

## Trajectory

Osasuna have spent 17 of the last 20 seasons in La Liga, with 2014-16 and 2017-19 in Segunda. Arrasate's team finished between seventh and 11th every year from 2019-20 to 2023-24; Vicente Moreno's single season produced ninth in 2024-25. The 2025-26 campaign under Alessio Lisci was a struggle that ended in 17th on 42 points, saved on head-to-head records above Mallorca. Luis Miguel Ramis was appointed in June 2026, lasted eight league matches and was sacked on 12 October after a 5-2 defeat at Betis with the team 16th on eight points. Arrasate, sacked by Mallorca in February 2026, returned the same day on a contract to June 2029.

## Players the club produced

Tajonar, the academy, is central to Osasuna's identity and economics. Patxi Puñal (the record appearance holder), Cruchaga, the Flaño twins, Roberto Torres, Oier Sanjurjo, David García, Jon Moncayola, Aimar Oroz and Iker Muñoz all came through it, as did Nacho Monreal (Arsenal), Javi Martínez (Athletic, Bayern) and Mikel Merino's father Ángel. Signed heroes include Urban, Robinson, Rubén García, the Argentine forward Chimy Ávila and the Croatian striker Ante Budimir, the club's leading scorer of the 2020s.

## Identity and playing style

Osasuna's football reflects the Navarrese temperament and El Sadar's noise: aggressive, direct, physically committed, built on [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into Budimir, pressing in packs and [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces). Arrasate's first spell refined that into a 4-4-2 or 4-3-3 with real quality on the ball from Oroz and Moncayola and width from Rubén García and Moi Gómez. His return has restored the same approach, with Lucas Torró and Moncayola as the midfield engine and Budimir, Raúl Moro and Enrique Barja ahead of them. The first match back, a 2-0 home win over Racing Santander with a Budimir penalty and an Asier Osambela goal, ended a six-week winless run.

## Rivalries

The Basque-Navarrese derbies against [Athletic](/clubs/athletic-bilbao), [Real Sociedad](/clubs/real-sociedad) and [Alavés](/clubs/alaves) are the fixtures with the most feeling, Athletic's visit on 8 November 2026 among them. Real Zaragoza, the nearest big club to the south, are the other historic rival.

## Current situation

Osasuna are 15th on 7 November 2026 with 11 points from 11 games (three wins, two draws, six defeats), a point above the relegation zone, with 22 goals conceded the worst defensive record outside the bottom three. Arrasate's immediate job is to restore the defensive solidity that defined his earlier side, with Sergio Herrera and Aitor Fernández sharing the goalkeeping, Alejandro Catena, Enzo Boyomo and Jorge Herrando in the centre and Valentin Rosier and Abel Bretones at full-back.

Osasuna's structure, member-owned and debt-free with a renovated El Sadar voted the best stadium in Europe by fans in 2021, has always allowed patience. The target this year is survival with Arrasate, and then a return to the mid-table comfort the club enjoyed in his first spell.
