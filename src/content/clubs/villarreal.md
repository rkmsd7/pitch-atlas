## History

Villarreal Club de Fútbol were founded on 10 March 1923 in a ceramics-industry town of about 50,000 people in the province of Castellón, and spent most of their first 75 years in the regional and third divisions. The transformation began when Fernando Roig, a local businessman whose family owns the Mercadona supermarket chain, bought the club in 1997. Villarreal reached [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) for the first time in 1998, went straight back down, and returned in 2000 to stay.

The first great side was Manuel Pellegrini's. Between 2004 and 2009 the Chilean built a team around Juan Román Riquelme, [Diego Forlán](/players/diego-forlan), Marcos Senna, Robert Pirès, Santi Cazorla and Giuseppe Rossi that won the Intertoto Cup twice, finished third in 2004-05, and reached the 2006 Champions League semi-final, where Riquelme's late penalty was saved by Jens Lehmann and Arsenal went through 1-0 on aggregate. In 2007-08 Villarreal finished second, their best ever placing, behind Real Madrid.

The club's lowest moment came in 2011-12, a season that began with a Champions League group and ended, after the sacking of Juan Carlos Garrido and the death of the club's founder's generation, in relegation on the final day. They were back a year later. Marcelino's team of 2013-16 finished fourth and reached the 2016 Europa League semi-final; Unai Emery's arrived in 2020 and won the club's first major trophy, the 2021 Europa League in Gdańsk, beating Manchester United 11-10 on penalties after a 1-1 draw, Gerard Moreno scoring and Gerónimo Rulli saving David de Gea's kick. The following spring Villarreal beat Juventus 3-0 in Turin and Bayern Munich over two legs to reach the Champions League semi-final, where Liverpool came from 2-0 down at the Cerámica.

## Trajectory

Villarreal have been a top-half club in every season since 2013-14 except 2018-19 (14th, saved late by Javier Calleja), and a European regular: Champions League in 2005-06, 2008-09, 2011-12, 2021-22, 2024-25, 2025-26 and 2026-27, with Europa League or Conference League places in most other years. Marcelino's second spell, from November 2023, produced a fifth-place finish in 2024-25 and third place in 2025-26 with 72 points and 72 goals, the best league campaign in the club's history, which qualified the team directly for the Champions League league phase.

Marcelino left in summer 2026 and the club hired [Iñigo Pérez](/managers/inigo-perez), the 38-year-old Navarrese coach who had taken [Rayo Vallecano](/clubs/rayo-vallecano) to the 2026 Conference League final, on a three-year deal in June 2026.

## Players the club produced

The academy, long overshadowed by the signings, has become one of the best in Spain. Bruno Soriano captained the club for a decade; Pau Torres and Yeremi Pino were Euro 2024 winners with [Spain](/nations/spain); [Álex Baena](/players/alex-baena), who came through to become the league's leading creator before his 2025 move to Atlético, was in Spain's 2026 World Cup-winning squad; Alberto Moleiro, signed from Las Palmas, and the teenagers Carlos Macià and Pau Navarro are the next line.

The signings define the eras: Riquelme and Forlán (Pichichi and European Golden Shoe in 2005), Senna, Cazorla, Rossi, Nilmar, Cédric Bakambu, Carlos Bacca, Samuel Chukwueze, Arnaut Danjuma, Rulli, and Gerard Moreno, who returned from Espanyol in 2018 and is the club's all-time top scorer. Nicolas Pépé, Ayoze Pérez and the Georgian striker Georges Mikautadze lead the current attack, with Péter Gulácsi in goal and Juan Foyth and Logan Costa in defence.

## Identity and playing style

Villarreal have a consistent football identity for a club of their size: technical, patient and built on good midfielders. Pellegrini's 4-2-2-2 gave Riquelme freedom between the lines; Marcelino's 4-4-2 was more compact and faster in transition, with wingers who defended and strikers who pressed; Emery's teams were tactically flexible, often a [back three](/tactics/back-three) in Europe, with [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) rehearsed to the inch.

Iñigo Pérez's Rayo were a brave, pressing 4-2-3-1 with aggressive full-backs and a willingness to play through pressure, and he has brought the same ideas to the Cerámica with a three-man midfield of Santi Comesaña, Pape Gueye and Moleiro. The early Champions League results, 2-3 at Dortmund, defeats to Napoli and a 0-6 home loss to Paris Saint-Germain on matchday four, exposed the risk of that approach against elite opposition.

The Gdańsk final of 26 May 2021 was decided by the longest shootout in a major European final: 22 penalties, every outfield player scoring, until Rulli saved De Gea's kick after scoring his own. It was also Emery's fourth Europa League as a coach. The Cerámica, rebuilt in 2023-24 with a new roof and corner stands, is one of the smallest grounds in the Champions League and one of the most atmospheric, and the Ciutat Esportiva, with its 11 pitches, is the reason the club's academy keeps producing internationals.

## Rivalries

The Derbi de la Comunitat against [Valencia](/clubs/valencia) is the fixture that matters most, a rivalry in which the small town has often beaten the big city since 2000. The provincial derby against Castellón, dormant for decades, returned to relevance with Castellón's rise to Segunda, and [Levante](/clubs/levante) are the other regional opponent. There is no deep enmity with the big two; Villarreal's great European ties with Arsenal, Manchester United and Liverpool carry more memory than most domestic fixtures.

## Current situation

The 2026-27 season has been difficult. On 7 November 2026 Villarreal are tenth in La Liga with 12 points from 11 games (three wins, three draws, five defeats), and in the Champions League they have three points and 14 goals conceded after four matchdays. Spanish reports in early November said the board was debating Iñigo Pérez's position after the PSG defeat; nothing has been announced and the coach remains in charge as of this writing.

The squad is strong on paper: Gulácsi, Foyth, Costa, Renato Veiga and Sergi Cardona in defence, Comesaña, Gueye, Moleiro and Nathan Saliba in midfield, Gerard Moreno, Ayoze, Pépé, Mikautadze, Tajon Buchanan and Tani Oluwaseyi in attack. The gap between that squad and the results is the story of the autumn. Roig's Villarreal have been patient with coaches before; the next month will show whether that patience holds.
