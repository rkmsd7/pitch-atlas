## History

Naples had football clubs from 1904, when the English sailor William Poths founded Naples Foot-Ball Club, but the modern Associazione Calcio Napoli dates from 1 August 1926 and the merger engineered by Giorgio Ascarelli, a Jewish textile industrialist whose name was later scrubbed from the stadium by the fascist regime. The club's early decades were a story of near misses, two third-place finishes in the 1930s under the Paraguayan striker Attila Sallustro, a first Coppa Italia in 1962 won from Serie B, and the arrival of Omar Sívori and José Altafini in 1965 that made the Stadio San Paolo the loudest ground in Italy without ever quite producing a title.

Everything changed on 5 July 1984, when a Fiat Panda drove into the San Paolo and 75,000 people welcomed [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona), bought from Barcelona for a world-record fee the club could barely afford. Within three years Napoli were champions, the first side from the Italian south ever to win Serie A, and the city held a mock funeral for Juventus. The 1987 side, with Maradona, Bruno Giordano, Ciro Ferrara and the midfielder Fernando De Napoli, won the double; the 1989 UEFA Cup came with the Brazilian Careca alongside Maradona and a two-legged final win over Stuttgart; the second Scudetto in 1990, won by two points from Milan, was the peak. Maradona's suspension for cocaine use in 1991 and his departure ended the era, and the club has never settled the argument about whether he or the city made the other.

## Trajectory

The years after Maradona were a long slide. Napoli sold Careca, Gianfranco Zola and Ferrara to pay debts, were relegated in 1998 and again in 2001, and in August 2004, with liabilities of around €70m, were declared bankrupt and expelled from the professional leagues. The film producer Aurelio De Laurentiis bought the sporting title for about €30m and re-entered the club as Napoli Soccer in Serie C1, where 50,000 people turned up to watch third-tier football. Two promotions in three seasons, under Edy Reja with Emanuele Calaiò and Roberto Sosa up front, took the club back to Serie A in 2007, the historic name restored.

De Laurentiis built slowly and without debt. Walter Mazzarri's side with Marek Hamšík, Ezequiel Lavezzi and Edinson Cavani reached the Champions League in 2011 and won the 2012 Coppa Italia; Rafa Benítez added another in 2014 and signed [Gonzalo Higuaín](/players/gonzalo-higuain), who broke the Serie A scoring record with 36 goals in 2015-16 and then joined Juventus. [Maurizio Sarri](/managers/maurizio-sarri)'s Napoli of 2015 to 2018, with Lorenzo Insigne, Dries Mertens as a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine), Jorginho and Kalidou Koulibaly, played the most beautiful football in Europe and took 91 points in 2017-18 without winning the title, undone by Juventus and the Inter-Juventus game that Napoli supporters still cite.

The third Scudetto, in 2022-23 under [Luciano Spalletti](/managers/luciano-spalletti), was won by 16 points with [Khvicha Kvaratskhelia](/players/khvicha-kvaratskhelia), bought unknown from Dinamo Batumi, and Victor Osimhen, who scored 26, as the stars of a side that also reached the Champions League quarter-finals. The hangover was brutal: Spalletti left, three coaches were used in a tenth-place 2023-24, and Osimhen's relationship with the club collapsed. Antonio Conte arrived in June 2024, sold Kvaratskhelia to PSG in January 2025 and still won the 2024-25 title by a point from Inter, with [Scott McTominay](/players/scott-mctominay), signed from Manchester United for €30m, voted the league's best player. Conte's 2025-26 side added the Supercoppa Italiana in Riyadh in December 2025, beating Milan and then Bologna 2-0 with two David Neres goals, and finished second to Inter on 76 points, but Conte and De Laurentiis parted by mutual consent on 24 May 2026, the latest in a line of coaches the president has worn down. [Massimiliano Allegri](/managers/massimiliano-allegri), sacked by Milan the same week, was appointed on 3 July.

The stadium is part of the story. The Stadio San Paolo, built for the 1960 Olympics in the Fuorigrotta district, was renamed for Maradona four days after his death in November 2020 and holds 54,000 after the 2019 Universiade renovation; De Laurentiis has argued for years with the city over ownership and has threatened to build elsewhere, most recently at Bagnoli, so that Naples could host matches at Euro 2032, which Italy will co-host with Turkey. The club's finances are the healthiest among the Italian big five, with no debt, a wage bill a third of Juventus' and a transfer profit every season since 2015, which is why the president is both the most reviled and the most quietly envied owner in Serie A.

The Scudetto of 2023 was celebrated for a month, with murals of Kvaratskhelia and Osimhen appearing in the Quartieri Spagnoli next to the famous Maradona shrine, and the title of 2025 was received more soberly, with Conte already hinting that the club could not keep him. Napoli has always been a place where football is both religion and grievance, and the transition from Conte to Allegri, a coach whose caution the city instinctively distrusts, has reopened the argument about what the club is for.

## Players the club produced

Napoli's youth system has rarely been the point; the club's genius under De Laurentiis has been scouting. Kvaratskhelia for €10m, Kim Min-jae from Fenerbahçe, Koulibaly from Genk, Fabián Ruiz from Betis, Hamšík from Brescia at 20, Cavani from Palermo, Lavezzi from San Lorenzo, and Osimhen from Lille for a club-record €70m, all were bought cheaply or at least before their peak and sold for multiples. The home-grown exceptions are the ones the city loves most: Ciro Ferrara, the Neapolitan defender who won both Maradona titles and then went to Juventus; Fabio Cannavaro, who grew up as a ball boy at the San Paolo before leaving for Parma; Gianluca Grava; and Lorenzo Insigne, from Frattamaggiore, who scored 122 goals and lifted the 2020 Coppa Italia as captain before leaving for Toronto. Giovanni Di Lorenzo, bought from Empoli in 2019, is now the captain and the longest-serving player of the modern squad, and Alessandro Buongiorno, signed from Torino in 2024, is the defensive leader.

## Identity and playing style

Napoli's football identity has been rewritten by each of its great coaches. Ottavio Bianchi's Maradona side was Italian in structure and Argentine in inspiration; Sarri's 4-3-3 was built on quick, short, automated combinations down the left, a style that gave the language the word "Sarrismo"; Spalletti's side pressed higher and played through Lobotka and Anguissa; Conte used a 3-4-2-1 or 4-3-3 with McTominay crashing the box and Kevin De Bruyne, who joined on a free from Manchester City in 2025 at 34, pulling the strings.

Allegri has inherited a squad built for Conte and has, characteristically, made it more cautious. He plays a 4-3-3 that becomes a 4-4-1-1 without the ball, a [low block](/tactics/low-block) that counters through David Neres and Noa Lang, with Rasmus Højlund, signed permanently from Manchester United for €44m, as the striker and Lorenzo Lucca in reserve. Vanja Milinković-Savić, bought from Torino after a loan, has taken the goalkeeping shirt from Alex Meret. The problem, as the early results show, is that a squad built to press and dominate possession has not adapted well to a coach who prefers to concede it.

## Rivalries

Napoli against [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) is the match that carries the north-south divide of Italy: Turin's industrial establishment against the city that fed it workers and had never won anything until Maradona. Games against [Roma](/clubs/roma) are the Derby del Sole, a clash of the two biggest cities south of Florence and, since the 2014 shooting of a Napoli fan before the Coppa Italia final, one with heavy policing. [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) have decided the last three title races with Napoli and the 3-2 defeat at San Siro in September 2026 was a reminder of the gap. There is an old cross-regional friendship with Genoa, from a 1982 relegation decider, and an enmity with Atalanta and Verona built on decades of racist chanting from the stands of the north.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Napoli are eighth in Serie A with 14 points from ten matches, four wins, two draws and four defeats, 14 points behind leaders Lazio and already out of the title conversation by any realistic reading. The results have been erratic: a 4-1 win at Monza and 3-0 against Frosinone sit beside home defeats to Como and a 1-0 loss at promoted Venezia, and a 3-3 draw at home to Roma. In the Champions League the champions of two seasons ago have one win and three defeats from four league-phase games, the heaviest a 4-1 at Porto on 4 November. McTominay leads all competitions with five goals and De Bruyne has four in the league at 35.

Allegri's position is the obvious story. De Laurentiis gave him a three-year deal in July, but the president has sacked coaches for less, and the squad, which lost Miguel Gutiérrez to Leverkusen and Romelu Lukaku to Fenerbahçe in the summer, looks older and slower than the 2025 champions. The Coppa Italia round of 16 in December and a top-four recovery are the targets; a Champions League exit before the knockout play-offs would make Allegri's winter very cold.
