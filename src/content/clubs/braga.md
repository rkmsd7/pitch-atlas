## History

Sporting Clube de Braga were founded in 1921 in the ancient cathedral city of the Minho, and their red-and-white kit dates from 1930, when a club official returned from a trip to London so impressed by Arsenal that he adopted their colours. The "Arsenalistas" nickname has stuck. For most of their history Braga were a mid-table club at best, with a first Taça de Portugal in 1966 their only trophy of note for half a century.

The modern Braga were built around the Estádio Municipal, the extraordinary two-sided stadium carved out of a quarry by Eduardo Souto de Moura for Euro 2004, and the presidency of António Salvador, who took over in 2003 and professionalised the club. Under Jesualdo Ferreira, Carlos Carvalhal and above all Domingos Paciência, Braga went from making up the numbers to breaking into the big three's territory. The 2009-10 season was the summit: second in the league behind Benfica and ahead of Porto, Champions League qualification, and a victory over Sevilla in the play-off that put Braga in the group stage with Arsenal and Shakhtar.

Dropping into the Europa League, Paciência's team beat Liverpool, Dynamo Kyiv and Porto's rivals Benfica in the semi-final to reach the 2011 final in Dublin, where [Porto](/clubs/porto) won 1-0 with a Radamel Falcao header. It remains the only European final reached by a Portuguese club outside the big three. The years since have brought two more Taça de Portugal wins, in 2016 on penalties against Porto and in 2021 against Benfica, three Taça da Liga titles and an almost permanent place in fourth.

## Trajectory

Braga have finished fourth in the Primeira Liga eleven times since 2000, a statistic that captures both their consistency and the ceiling imposed by the big three. The best finishes were second in 2010 and third in 2012, 2020 and 2023, the latter earning direct Champions League qualification under Artur Jorge, whose side also reached the league's European places with a club-record points total. The worst was ninth in 2014. Since 2015 they have never been out of the top four, and Europa League or Conference League group stages have become routine.

## Players the club produced

Braga have been a superb developer and seller. Éder, who scored the winner in the Euro 2016 final, spent four years at the club; [João Palhinha](/players/joao-palhinha) and Rafa Silva both broke through at Braga before moving to Sporting and Benfica; Francisco Trincão, Vitinha, Raúl Silva and David Carmo were sold for large fees. Alan, the Brazilian forward, is the club's record scorer, and Ricardo Horta, captain since 2017 and the club's all-time top scorer in the Primeira Liga, turned down moves to Benfica to stay. Custódio, Alberto Salgado and Hugo Viana are among the Portugal internationals who passed through.

## Identity and playing style

Braga's identity under Salvador has been that of a club punching above its weight through good coaching appointments and clever recruitment, usually in a 4-2-3-1 or 4-3-3 that presses with energy and attacks through quick wingers and Horta's movement off the striker. Carlos Carvalhal's 2020-21 side was the most expansive version; Artur Jorge's 2022-23 team the most efficient. The club has also been a launching pad for coaches: [Rúben Amorim](/managers/ruben-amorim) won the 2020 Taça da Liga at Braga before Sporting paid a record fee for him. As of November 2026 the coaching position has changed during the season and should be checked against the club's latest announcements.

## Rivalries

The Minho derby against [Vitória de Guimarães](/clubs/vitoria-guimaraes), thirteen miles away, is one of the most heated matches in Portugal, two proud cities with a rivalry that long predates football. Matches against [Porto](/clubs/porto) carry the sting of the 2011 final and of two decades spent trying to break into the big three.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Braga are eighth in the Primeira Liga after ten rounds, well off their usual pace, with 14 points. The season began with the familiar ambitions of a top-four finish and European progress, but a poor autumn has put the club under pressure to recover quickly; the squad, led by Horta and the young winger Roger Fernandes, remains strong enough to climb.
