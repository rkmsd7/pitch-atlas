## History

Barcelona's women's section was founded in 1988 as Club Femení Barcelona and became an official part of FC Barcelona in 2002, but for most of its first three decades it was a modest operation overshadowed by Levante, Athletic Bilbao and Rayo Vallecano. The club won its first league title in 2011-12 under Xavi Llorens and turned fully professional in 2015, the first Spanish women's side to do so, and the decision changed everything.

A first [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) final in 2019 ended in a 4-1 defeat to [Lyon](/clubs/lyon-women). Two years later, under Lluís Cortés, Barça beat [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women) 4-0 in Gothenburg to win the trophy for the first time, with [Alexia Putellas](/players/alexia-putellas) winning the Ballon d'Or. The 2021-22 side under Jonatan Giráldez won every one of its 30 league games and set a world attendance record of 91,648 at Camp Nou against Wolfsburg, then lost the final to Lyon. Barcelona won the Champions League again in 2023, coming from 2-0 down against [Wolfsburg](/clubs/wolfsburg-women) in Eindhoven, and in 2024 against Lyon in Bilbao, and [Aitana Bonmatí](/players/aitana-bonmati) collected three consecutive Ballons d'Or.

Pere Romeu, Giráldez's assistant, took over in 2024. His first season ended with a 1-0 defeat to [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal-women) in the 2025 Lisbon final, but 2025-26 was a clean sweep: Liga F by 15 points, Copa de la Reina, Supercopa and a fourth Champions League, beating OL Lyonnes 4-0 in Oslo on 23 May 2026 with two goals each from [Ewa Pajor](/players/ewa-pajor) and [Salma Paralluelo](/players/salma-paralluelo). Putellas, captain, played her 500th game for the club in April 2026 and left in the summer after 14 years.

## Trajectory

Three straight second places behind Atlético Madrid from 2017 to 2019 preceded seven consecutive titles from 2019-20. In Europe the club has reached seven of the last eight Champions League finals and won four. The 2026 summer exodus of Putellas (London City Lionesses), Paralluelo (Lyon) and Fridolina Rolfö ([Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united-women)) was the first serious loss of talent since the dynasty began.

## Players the club produced

La Masia's female wing is the best academy in the world. [Aitana Bonmatí](/players/aitana-bonmati), [Clàudia Pina](/players/claudia-pina), Patri Guijarro, Cata Coll, Jana Fernández and Bruna Vilamala all came through; [Vicky López](/players/vicky-lopez), signed from Madrid CFF at 16, is the current prodigy and 2026 Women's Kopa Trophy winner. Among signings, [Mariona Caldentey](/players/mariona-caldentey), [Lieke Martens](/players/lieke-martens), [Caroline Graham Hansen](/players/caroline-graham-hansen), [Lucy Bronze](/players/lucy-bronze), Keira Walsh, [Asisat Oshoala](/players/asisat-oshoala) and Pajor all became or remained world-class in blaugrana.

## Identity and playing style

Barça Femení is [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in its purest form: a 4-3-3 with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs), Guijarro as the single pivot, Bonmatí and a second interior making late runs, wingers held high and wide, and a counter-press that wins the ball back within five seconds. Romeu kept Giráldez's system and added more direct running from Pajor and Paralluelo. Pina's 21 league goals in 2025-26, often from the left, showed how the attack has become less reliant on the wide one-v-one and more on third-man combinations.

## Rivalries

The Clásico against [Real Madrid Femenino](/clubs/real-madrid-women) is lopsided (Barcelona have won almost every meeting, including a 6-0 Champions League quarter-final in April 2026) but draws the biggest crowds in Spain. Lyon are the European rival of the era: four finals between the clubs since 2019. Chelsea and Arsenal are the English benchmarks, and the 2025 final defeat to Arsenal still stings.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Romeu's side leads Liga F again and is through the Champions League league phase. Bonmatí, back from the broken leg suffered in November 2025, Pajor, the 2026 Ballon d'Or runner-up, Pina, López and Graham Hansen form the core, with Ona Batlle and Irene Paredes anchoring the defence. The questions are whether the departures finally bring the league closer and whether the club can keep paying its stars as English wages rise, a concern Bonmatí voiced publicly in September 2026.
