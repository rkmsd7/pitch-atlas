## History

Racing Club de Lens were founded in 1906 in a coal-mining town in the Pas-de-Calais, and the mines gave the club its identity: the Sang et Or (blood and gold) colours, the Stade Félix-Bollaert built by the mining company in 1933 (it holds more people than the town has inhabitants), and "Les Corons", the Pierre Bachelet song about miners' cottages that the crowd sings at half-time. Promoted to the top flight in 1937, Lens were a mid-table club for decades, with a Coupe de France final in 1948 and a UEFA Cup quarter-final in 1978.

The great season was 1997-98. Daniel Leclercq, a former Lens defender, built a side of Tony Vairelles, Vladimír Šmicer, Marc-Vivien Foé, Éric Sikora and Guillaume Warmuz that won the title on the final day at Auxerre, the club's only championship, and added the 1999 Coupe de la Ligue. The 1998-99 Champions League brought a 1-0 win at Wembley over Arsenal. Joël Muller's side lost the 2002 title on the final day to Lyon; Lens were third in 2004 and fourth in 2006 with Seydou Keïta and Olivier Monterrubio, and reached the 2000 UEFA Cup semi-final.

Then came the yo-yo years: relegated in 2008 and 2011, promoted as Ligue 2 champions in 2009 and as runners-up in 2014, relegated again in 2015 amid financial problems that nearly ended the club. Six seasons in Ligue 2 ended with promotion in 2020 under Franck Haise, who built the modern Lens: seventh in 2021 and 2022, then second in 2022-23 with 84 points, one behind PSG, with Seko Fofana, Loïs Openda (21 goals), Brice Samba and Florian Sotoca, and a Champions League group that included a 2-1 win over Arsenal.

Haise left for Nice in 2024; Will Still finished eighth; [Pierre Sage](/managers/pierre-sage) produced a top-half, attractive 2025-26 before moving to Crystal Palace; Dino Toppmöller's subsequent spell ended during 2026, and Yannick Cahuzac, the club's former captain, is in charge for 2026-27.

## Trajectory

Lens have had two relegations and three promotions this century, and a six-year spell in Ligue 2 (2015-20) during which the club was close to losing its professional status. Since 2020 they have been a top-eight side every season, with the 2023 runners-up finish the high point and a Champions League campaign the reward. The club's budget is mid-range, its stadium full every week, and its model is to recruit well in Africa and the lower French divisions and sell on (Openda to Leipzig, Fofana to Al Nassr, Kevin Danso to Tottenham).

The 2015 crisis is the hinge of the modern club. Hafiz Mammadov, an Azerbaijani businessman, bought Lens in 2013 and promised investment that never arrived; the DNCG, French football's financial regulator, initially refused to allow the club into Ligue 1 in 2014 and then imposed a transfer embargo, and the 2014-15 season was played with a skeleton squad and home matches at Amiens while the Bollaert was rebuilt for Euro 2016. Relegation followed. Joseph Oughourlian, a French-Armenian fund manager, took control in 2018, cleared the debts and appointed Haise, and the club's rise since is attributed to that stability. The Bollaert's "Marek" stand and the pre-match rendition of "Les Corons" became known across Europe during the 2023-24 Champions League campaign.

## Players the club produced

Lens's academy, La Gaillette, produced [Raphaël Varane](/players/raphael-varane), who debuted at 17 and was sold to Real Madrid in 2011 for 10 million euros, Geoffrey Kondogbia, Serge Aurier (via Toulouse), Benjamin Bourigeaud and, in the 2020s, Andy Diouf and a stream of young defenders. The club made careers for Foé, Keïta, Šmicer, Eric Carrière, Vairelles and, more recently, Fofana, Openda, Samba and Facundo Medina. Florian Sotoca, signed from Grenoble, has been the long-serving forward; Florian Thauvin returned to France with Lens in 2025.

## Identity and playing style

Haise's Lens played a 3-4-3 that pressed high and attacked at speed through the wing-backs (Przemysław Frankowski and Deiver Machado), with Fofana driving from midfield: [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) from a [back three](/tactics/back-three), and the most entertaining football in France outside Paris in 2022-23. Sage used the same base with structured [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) and more patient build-up. Cahuzac, a combative midfielder who captained the promotion side, has kept the three-man defence and the intensity the Bollaert expects.

## Rivalries

The **Derby du Nord** against [Lille](/clubs/lille), 40 kilometres away, is the great rivalry of northern France: the mining town against the regional capital, the working-class club against the bourgeois one, and a fixture that has been played in both divisions. Lille's 1-0 win on 30 October 2026 was the most recent. Valenciennes are the other Nord-Pas-de-Calais rival. [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) became a rival of sorts in 2022-23, when Lens beat them 3-1 on New Year's Day and finished one point behind.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Lens are ninth in Ligue 1 on 13 points from nine games (four wins, one draw, four defeats, 16 scored, 13 conceded). They won 3-1 at Troyes on 17 October and 2-1 at home to Paris FC on 24 October, then lost the derby 1-0 at Lille on 30 October. Marseille visit the Bollaert on 8 November, followed by Toulouse at home on 21 November.

Cahuzac's squad is built around Thauvin, Adrien Thomasson, Frankowski, Jonathan Gradit, Medina and Diouf. The season began with a coaching change and the target is a top-six finish and a return to Europe; the Bollaert, with its 38,000 and its Corons, remains the club's greatest asset.
