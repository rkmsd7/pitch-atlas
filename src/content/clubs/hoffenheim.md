## History

TSG 1899 Hoffenheim are from a village of 3,000 people in the Kraichgau region near Heidelberg. For ninety years they were an amateur club, and they would have stayed one had Dietmar Hopp, the co-founder of the software company SAP, not played for them as a boy. Hopp began funding the club in 1989, and from the Oberliga in 2000 the money and planning took Hoffenheim up the pyramid: Regionalliga Süd from 2001, the 2. Bundesliga in 2007, and the Bundesliga in 2008 under [Ralf Rangnick](/managers/ralf-rangnick), who had been appointed in 2006 with the brief of building a top-flight club from the third tier.

The first Bundesliga season was a sensation. Rangnick's young, pressing side, with Vedad Ibišević (18 goals before Christmas), Demba Ba, Luiz Gustavo, Carlos Eduardo and Andreas Beck, were Herbstmeister, top at the winter break, and moved into the new 30,000-seat Rhein-Neckar-Arena in Sinsheim in January 2009. They faded to seventh when Ibišević was injured, but the blueprint for the Red Bull school had been written. Rangnick left in 2011 after a dispute over the sale of Luiz Gustavo to Bayern; three seasons of mid-table and a 2013 play-off survival against Kaiserslautern followed, with Roberto Firmino the one bright light before his sale to Liverpool.

Julian Nagelsmann was appointed in February 2016 at 28, the youngest coach in Bundesliga history, with the club 17th. He kept them up, then finished fourth in 2017 and third in 2018, taking Hoffenheim into the Champions League with Niklas Süle, Sebastian Rudy, Kerem Demirbay, Andrej Kramarić and Serge Gnabry. He left for Leipzig in 2019; Alfred Schreuder, Sebastian Hoeneß (sixth in 2020 and a Europa League last-16 run), [Pellegrino Matarazzo](/managers/pellegrino-matarazzo) (seventh in 2024) and Christian Ilzer have followed, with the 2024-25 season a brush with relegation.

## Trajectory

Eight promotions-worth of divisions in eight years, then 18 consecutive Bundesliga seasons with two European qualifications under Nagelsmann and Hoeneß and one under Matarazzo. The club's trajectory flattened after 2018 as the Hopp model (he reduced his involvement after 2020 and transferred his majority voting rights back to the members in 2023) was questioned and recruitment lost its edge. The 2025-26 season brought a return to the top half, and the 2026-27 side, with Kramarić still scoring at 35, has started unevenly.

## Players the club produced

Hoffenheim's academy, built by Hopp with the same ambition as the first team, has produced Niklas Süle, Nadiem Amiri, Dennis Geiger, Maximilian Beier (sold to Dortmund), Tom Bischof (sold to Bayern in 2025) and Tim Drexler. The club's transfer record is the real story: Roberto Firmino (bought for 4 million euros, sold to Liverpool for 41), Luiz Gustavo, Süle, Rudy, Demirbay, Gnabry, Joelinton (Newcastle), David Raum (Leipzig) and Beier all left for large fees. Andrej Kramarić, signed from Leicester in 2016, is the club's record scorer and the one star who stayed. Oliver Baumann has been the goalkeeper since 2014 and became Germany's number one in his late 30s. Sandro Wagner's 2016-17 season under Nagelsmann earned him a Germany recall.

## Identity and playing style

Hoffenheim are the laboratory of modern German pressing. Rangnick's 4-3-3 with [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) and vertical attacking was the prototype for Leipzig and Salzburg; Nagelsmann's flexible [back three](/tactics/back-three), with constant in-game shape changes and video-wall training, was the next step. Hoeneß added a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and inverted full-backs, and Matarazzo a more cautious block. The 2026-27 side plays a 3-4-2-1 with an emphasis on quick transitions through Adam Hložek and Bazoumana Touré and Kramarić as the finisher.

## Rivalries

Hoffenheim are disliked rather than rivalled: the Hopp-funded model made them the target of protests from Dortmund, Bayern and Freiburg supporters for a decade. Regionally, Karlsruher SC, 40 kilometres away, were the club whose place Hoffenheim took; [VfB Stuttgart](/clubs/vfb-stuttgart) are the Baden-Württemberg rival in the Bundesliga, and [SC Freiburg](/clubs/freiburg) the south-western fixture with the sharpest fan hostility.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Hoffenheim are 13th in the Bundesliga on nine points from eight games (three wins, five defeats, no draws, 14 scored, 16 conceded). They won 2-1 at Gladbach on 18 October and 3-0 at home to Leverkusen on 25 October before losing 2-0 at Werder on 31 October. Schalke visit the PreZero Arena on 8 November.

The club's head coach for 2026-27 could not be verified at the time of writing. Kramarić, Baumann and Anton Stach are the senior figures, Drexler and Touré the young players the club expects to sell for profit. The aim is a top-half finish and, in a league where Hoffenheim have twice reached Europe in the last seven seasons, a return to European competition.
