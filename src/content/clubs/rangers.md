## History

Rangers were founded in 1872 by four teenagers on Glasgow Green, and by the turn of the century had become, with Celtic, one half of the Old Firm that has dominated Scottish football ever since. Bill Struth, manager from 1920 to 1954, won 18 titles and set the club's culture of discipline and smartness, insisting players wore bowler hats on the way to matches; Alan Morton, the "Wee Blue Devil", was the first great Ibrox star.

The 1960s produced Jim Baxter, the most gifted Scottish player of his generation, and two Cup Winners' Cup finals lost in 1961 and 1967. The third, in Barcelona in 1972, was won 3-2 against Dynamo Moscow, with Willie Johnston scoring twice and John Greig lifting the trophy; it remains the club's only European title. The Ibrox disaster of January 1971, when 66 supporters died in a crush on Stairway 13, is the club's deepest tragedy and led to the rebuilding of the stadium.

Graeme Souness's arrival as player-manager in 1986, funded by David Murray's purchase of the club in 1988, began the modern era. Souness signed England internationals such as Terry Butcher and Chris Woods, broke the club's unwritten ban on signing Catholics with Mo Johnston in 1989 and won three titles. Walter Smith then took the run to nine in a row from 1989 to 1997, with Ally McCoist scoring 355 goals in all, Brian Laudrup and Paul Gascoigne providing the brilliance and Richard Gough the captaincy. Dick Advocaat's expensively assembled team won the Treble in 1999, Alex McLeish won it again in 2003 and the title on the final day of 2005, and Smith returned to win three more championships from 2009 to 2011 and reach the 2008 UEFA Cup final in Manchester, lost 2-0 to Zenit.

Then the club collapsed. Years of overspending, a tax dispute and Craig Whyte's disastrous takeover led to administration in February 2012 and liquidation of the operating company that summer. Rangers were placed in the Third Division and climbed back through three promotions, returning to the top flight in 2016. Steven Gerrard's 2020-21 side went through the league season unbeaten to win the 55th title and stop Celtic's ten in a row, and [Giovanni van Bronckhorst](/managers/giovanni-van-bronckhorst) took the club to the 2022 Europa League final in Seville, beating Dortmund, Braga and RB Leipzig before losing on penalties to Eintracht Frankfurt. The years since have been turbulent: Michael Beale, Philippe Clement, Russell Martin and Danny Röhl all came and went, and in June 2026 the club turned to [Derek McInnes](/managers/derek-mcinnes).

## Trajectory

Rangers' 55 titles are a world record for a top-flight club, but the twenty-first century has been Celtic's. Rangers won six championships between 2000 and 2011 and one since, in 2021, with four consecutive second-place finishes from 2022 to 2025. The 2012 liquidation is the fault line: four seasons in the lower divisions, two third-place finishes on return and then the Gerrard rebuild.

Europe has been a relative strength. The 2008 UEFA Cup final and the 2022 Europa League final are the only two European finals reached by any Scottish club this century, and Rangers' 2022 run, which included a 3-1 win at Leipzig and a semi-final second leg at Ibrox that supporters still talk about, is among the great Scottish European nights.

## Players the club produced

Rangers' academy produced John Greig, Sandy Jardine, Derek Johnstone, Barry Ferguson, Alan Hutton, Charlie Adam and, more recently, Nathan Patterson and Billy Gilmour, who left for Chelsea at 15. The club's greatest players have more often been signings: Jim Baxter from Raith, [Ally McCoist](/players/ally-mccoist) from Sunderland, Brian Laudrup from Fiorentina, Paul Gascoigne from Lazio, Davie Cooper from Clydebank and Alfredo Morelos from HJK. Nacho Novo, Steven Davis, Allan McGregor and James Tavernier, the long-serving captain of the post-liquidation era, are the names supporters of the last 20 years hold closest. Alan Hutton, Ryan Kent and Calvin Bassey were all sold on for sizeable fees.

## Identity and playing style

Rangers' identity is Struth's: dignity, discipline and the expectation of winning. The playing style has varied by manager. Gerrard's title-winners played a controlled 4-3-3 with aggressive full-backs; Van Bronckhorst's European side was a compact [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) team that pressed in a mid-block and struck through Joe Aribo and Ryan Kent.

[Derek McInnes](/managers/derek-mcinnes), confirmed on 17 June 2026 on a three-year contract, favours an organised 4-2-3-1 or 4-3-3, a [low block](/tactics/low-block) against stronger opponents, [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and a direct approach to the final third. His Aberdeen teams were the most consistent challengers to Celtic for a generation without ever beating them when it mattered most, and that is the record he must now improve.

The ownership story is central to the modern club. After liquidation, Rangers were owned by a succession of investors whose conduct ended in litigation, until Dave King's consortium took control in 2015 and stabilised the club through years of loans. The 2025 takeover by an American group, led by the healthcare entrepreneur Andrew Cavenagh with 49ers Enterprises as a partner, brought new capital, a new chief executive and a recruitment structure modelled on Leeds United, where the 49ers' football arm had overseen promotion. Ibrox itself remains the club's greatest asset, a listed Archibald Leitch stand and a ground that sells out every home league match.

## Rivalries

The Old Firm against [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) is the fixture that defines the club: 140 years old, sectarian in its history, enormous in its stakes. Rangers also have a long-standing rivalry with [Aberdeen](/clubs/aberdeen), sharpened in the 1980s by the Alex Ferguson years and by a series of ill-tempered matches, and with [Hearts](/clubs/hearts), the pre-eminent club of Edinburgh.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Rangers are second in the Scottish Premiership after eleven rounds, four points behind Celtic with a game in hand, and are not in the Champions League league phase. McInnes inherited a squad reshaped by the club's American ownership group and their recruitment department, and the early Old Firm meetings have been tight. The season's objectives are to close the gap on O'Neill's Celtic, restore respectability in Europe and give the club a settled period after cycling through Clement, Martin and Röhl in a year. Progress so far is steady rather than spectacular, which at Ibrox counts as a start.
