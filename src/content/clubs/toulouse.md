## History

Toulouse FC, in their current form, were founded in 1970 after an earlier club of the same name had sold its professional licence to Red Star in 1967. The new club rose through the divisions to reach Division 1 in 1982 and spent the 1980s as a respectable top-flight side, eliminated Napoli with Diego Maradona from the 1986-87 UEFA Cup on penalties, and finished third in 1987. The Stadium de Toulouse, built for the 1938 World Cup and rebuilt for 1998, has been home throughout.

Relegation in 1994 and again in 1999 and 2001 brought the crisis: in 2001, after finishing 17th, the club was demoted to the National for financial reasons. Erick Mombaerts took them straight back up twice, National champions in 2002 and Ligue 2 champions in 2003. Élie Baup's side finished third in 2007 behind Lyon and Marseille, and reached the Champions League qualifying round, losing to Liverpool; Alain Casanova's finished fourth in 2009 with André-Pierre Gignac the league's top scorer with 24. Wissam Ben Yedder (71 league goals, 2010-16) and Moussa Sissoko were the next generation.

The decline through the late 2010s ended with relegation in 2020, when the curtailed season left Toulouse bottom with 13 points. RedBird Capital, the American investment firm, bought the club that summer, installed Damien Comolli as president and built a data-driven recruitment model. Philippe Montanier's side won Ligue 2 in 2022 with Branco van den Boomen's 12 goals and 20 assists, and Carles Martínez Novell won the 2023 Coupe de France, beating Nantes 5-1 at the Stade de France, the club's first major trophy, which brought a Europa League campaign and a round-of-16 tie against Benfica. RedBird's purchase of AC Milan led to UEFA scrutiny over multi-club ownership in 2023, resolved with governance changes.

## Trajectory

Twenty-three of the last twenty-four seasons in Ligue 1, with the single 2020-21 exception, and a trophy in 2023: for a club that was in the third tier in 2001, Toulouse's trajectory is one of durable mid-table stability with two peaks (2007 and 2009) and two relegation scares survived (the 2018 play-off against Ajaccio, 2015 and 2016). The RedBird era has made the club a reference for data-led recruitment and sales, with Thijs Dallinga, van den Boomen, Zinedine Machach and Alban Lafont (sold to Fiorentina, bought back from Nantes) among the trades.

## Players the club produced

The academy produced Lafont, who debuted at 16 in 2015, Issiaga Sylla, Moussa Sissoko (sold to Newcastle in 2013), Kévin Rimane and, in the 2020s, Guillaume Restes, the goalkeeper who became France's under-21 number one. Gignac, signed from Lorient, Ben Yedder from Alfortville, Max Gradel, Daniel Moreira, Bryan Bertrand and Achille Emaná were the signings of the Ligue 1 years; van den Boomen, Dallinga, Zakaria Aboukhlal and Vincent Sierro those of the RedBird era.

## Identity and playing style

Toulouse's identity since 2020 is the model: data, youth, sales and a possession-based style. Montanier's promotion side played a 3-4-3 with van den Boomen as the deep creator; Martínez Novell's cup winners a 4-3-3 with [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in build-up and a high press. The 2026-27 team plays a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with wing-backs, pressing in midfield and attacking through Aboukhlal and Aron Dønnum, and has been one of the harder sides in the league to beat.

## Rivalries

The Derby de la Garonne against Bordeaux, 250 kilometres away, is the historic rivalry of the south-west, suspended by Bordeaux's fall into the amateur leagues in 2024. Montpellier are the Occitanie rival, now in Ligue 2, and [Nice](/clubs/nice) the other southern club Toulouse have competed with for European places. In the city itself, the rivalry is with rugby: Stade Toulousain, the European champions, draw the bigger crowds.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Toulouse are tenth in Ligue 1 on 13 points from ten games (three wins, four draws, three defeats, 15 scored, 14 conceded). They drew 0-0 with Troyes on 25 October, lost 2-0 at Marseille on 1 November and beat Strasbourg 3-1 at the Stadium on 6 November. The next fixture is at Lens on 21 November, and the season ends with a trip to the Parc des Princes on 29 May 2027.

The club's head coach for 2026-27 could not be verified at the time of writing. Restes, Aboukhlal, Dønnum, Sierro and the centre-backs Mark McKenzie and Charlie Cresswell are the core, and the target is a top-half finish and, with the right winter window, a push for Europe.
