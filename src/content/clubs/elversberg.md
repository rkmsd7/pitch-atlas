## History

SV Elversberg come from Spiesen-Elversberg, a municipality of around 13,000 people in the Saarland, the smallest state in mainland Germany. Founded in 1907, the club spent a century in the regional leagues, and the ground, named after a linden tree planted for the Kaiser, held a few thousand. The first flicker of ambition came in the 2000s, when local businessman Frank Holzer and the pharmaceutical company Ursapharm began to fund the club, and Elversberg became a regular in the Oberliga and then Regionalliga Südwest, winning the Regionalliga in 2013 and spending a single season in the 3. Liga before being relegated.

The transformation came under Horst Steffen, appointed in 2018. His side won the Regionalliga Südwest in 2022, finished second in the 3. Liga in 2023 at the first attempt, and in 2023-24 settled into the 2. Bundesliga in 11th. The 2024-25 season was the breakthrough: third place, behind Köln and Hamburg, and a promotion play-off against Heidenheim that they lost on aggregate after winning the first leg, a result that left the club a goal short of the Bundesliga. Steffen left for Werder Bremen that summer, and Vincent Wagner, promoted from the under-19s, carried on the project. In 2025-26 Elversberg finished third again and this time won the play-off, taking a village club into the Bundesliga for the first time.

## Trajectory

The rise is almost unprecedented in Germany: fourth tier in 2022, third tier in 2023, second tier in 2024, Bundesliga in 2026. The club's budget remains among the smallest in professional football, and the stadium, expanded to around 10,000 for the top flight, is the smallest in the Bundesliga by a distance. The model is simple: a sporting director (Ole Book) who finds overlooked players in the lower leagues and on loan from bigger clubs, coaches who promote from within, and a style of play that does not change with the division. The first months of 2026-27 have confounded expectations, with the club in the top half in November.

## Players the club produced

Elversberg are a club of developed rather than produced players. Kevin Koffi scored the goals in the Regionalliga years; Luca Schnellbacher and Robin Fellhauer have been the constants through three promotions; Nicolas Kristof has been the goalkeeper throughout; Carlo Sickinger the midfield organiser. Loan signings have been crucial: Fisnik Asllani, on loan from Hoffenheim, and Paul Wanner, on loan from Bayern, were the creative stars of 2024-25, and the club has continued to use loans from Bundesliga academies to fill the attack. Lukas Pinckert and Bambasé Conté have been the recent breakout players.

## Identity and playing style

Steffen's Elversberg played a brave, possession-based 4-3-3 with a high defensive line, unusual for a promoted club and the reason the style has travelled across divisions. Vincent Wagner, who coached the youth teams in the same system, has kept it: build from the back, [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in the opponent's half, a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and counter-pressing when the ball is lost. It produces open games; the 4-3 win over Augsburg and the 4-1 win over Mainz in October 2026 showed both the attacking quality and the exposure at the back.

## Rivalries

The Saarland derby against 1. FC Saarbrücken is the one that matters, though the clubs have rarely been in the same division; FC Homburg is the other local rival. Kaiserslautern, 60 kilometres away in the Palatinate, are the regional giant Elversberg have overtaken in the pyramid. In the Bundesliga the club has no natural rival, and its supporters treat every fixture as a bonus.

## Current situation

Elversberg have made a remarkable start. As of 7 November 2026 they are seventh in the Bundesliga on 13 points from eight games (four wins, one draw, three defeats, 16 scored, 16 conceded), in the Conference League qualifying place. They beat Augsburg 4-3 on 17 October, lost 4-0 at Leipzig on 24 October and beat Mainz 4-1 at home on 30 October. The next fixture is at Dortmund on 7 November.

Wagner's side has already won four of eight, which is more than most predicted for the whole season. Kristof, Fellhauer, Schnellbacher and Marcel Correia provide the experience; the loans and young signings the goals. The aim remains survival, and the Ursapharm-Arena, sold out for every match, has become the Bundesliga's novelty venue of the season.
