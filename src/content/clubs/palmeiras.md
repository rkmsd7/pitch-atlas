## History

Palestra Italia was founded in 1914 by Italian immigrants in São Paulo, and its name, colours and identity were Italian until 1942, when Brazil's entry into the Second World War forced the club to rename itself Sociedade Esportiva Palmeiras. It won the São Paulo championship that same year under its new name and never looked back. The club's social base, the Italian-descended communities of the city's eastern and western districts, gave it a self-image of pride and endurance that persists in the "Porco" nickname, an insult turned into a chant.

The first great team was the Academia of the 1960s and early 1970s: Ademir da Guia, "O Divino", the most elegant midfielder of his era, Djalma Santos at right-back, Dudu, Leivinha and César, winning the Taça Brasil of 1960 and 1967, the Roberto Gomes Pedrosa of 1967 and 1969, and the Brasileirão of 1972 and 1973. Palmeiras supplied much of the Brazil squad of 1965 that beat Uruguay with the club's own eleven.

The 1990s were the Parmalat era. Backed by the Italian dairy group, Palmeiras signed [Rivaldo](/players/rivaldo), Edmundo, Evair, [Roberto Carlos](/players/roberto-carlos), [Cafu](/players/cafu) and Zinho, won the Brasileirão in 1993 and 1994 under Vanderlei Luxemburgo, and under Luiz Felipe Scolari won the 1998 Copa do Brasil, the 1998 Copa Mercosur and the 1999 Copa Libertadores, beating Deportivo Cali on penalties with the goalkeeper Marcos the hero. The club lost the 2000 Libertadores final to Boca Juniors and then, with Parmalat gone, collapsed: relegation in 2002, promotion in 2003, relegation again in 2012 (while winning the Copa do Brasil), promotion in 2013.

## Trajectory

The rebuild began with the Allianz Parque, opened in 2014 on the site of the old Palestra Itália stadium, and with the arrival of the Crefisa financial group's sponsorship under president Leila Pereira, who later became the first woman to run a major Brazilian club. Cuca's 2016 champions, led by Gabriel Jesus before his sale to Manchester City, ended a 22-year league drought; Scolari returned to win the 2018 title.

The appointment of [Abel Ferreira](/managers/abel-ferreira) in November 2020 made Palmeiras the dominant club of the decade. Within three months he had won the Copa Libertadores, Breno Lopes heading the winner in the 99th minute against Santos at the Maracanã in January 2021, and the Copa do Brasil. The Libertadores was retained in November 2021 with a 2-1 extra-time win over Flamengo in Montevideo, Deyverson scoring after a Flamengo error, and the club took Chelsea to extra time in the 2021 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) final. Back-to-back Brasileirão titles in 2022 (with Endrick, 16, scoring in the run-in) and 2023 (overtaking Botafogo's 13-point lead in the last two months) took the record to twelve.

Palmeiras lost the 2024 title to Botafogo and the 2025 title and Libertadores final, 1-0 in Lima, to [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo), having reached the quarter-finals of the 2025 Club World Cup, where they beat Botafogo and lost 2-1 to Chelsea. Ferreira, under contract to 2027, is listed as head coach in the club's current squad data as of November 2026; in the 2026 Brasileirão the club is second with 73 points from 34 games, three behind Flamengo with four rounds to play.

## Players the club produced

The academy at the Academia de Futebol has become one of the biggest exporters in world football. Gabriel Jesus (to Manchester City, 2017), Endrick (to [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), 2024), Estêvão Willian (to [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea), 2025), Vitor Reis (to Manchester City, 2025), Luis Guilherme (to West Ham), Danilo (to Nottingham Forest), Gabriel Menino and Gabriel Veron were all produced in the 2010s and 2020s, generating more than €300m in fees. Earlier generations gave Brazil Ademir da Guia, Marcos, Alex, Mauricio and Vanderlei Luxemburgo's crop of the 1990s. The signings who became legends were Rivaldo, Edmundo, Evair, Dudu and Raphael Veiga, and the Paraguayan captain Gustavo Gómez and goalkeeper Weverton are the longest-serving members of the Ferreira sides.

## Identity and playing style

Abel Ferreira's Palmeiras is the most clearly defined tactical project in Brazilian football: a 4-2-3-1 or 3-4-2-1 that defends in a compact mid-block, presses selectively in the middle third, and attacks in rapid transitions with a target forward (Flaco López, now Vitor Roque) and runners from the second line (Veiga, Estêvão before his sale, Andreas Pereira). It is less a possession team than a control team: Palmeiras concede fewer goals than anyone and are lethal from [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), which have decided several finals. The identity, physical, organised and ruthless, is sometimes derided as "European" by rivals and embraced by the fans as the reason for twelve stars. See [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) for the structure the club has perfected.

## Rivalries

The Derby Paulista with [Corinthians](/clubs/corinthians) is the biggest rivalry in São Paulo and arguably Brazil, dating from 1917 and contested across two of the largest fan bases in the country; the Choque-Rei with [São Paulo](/clubs/sao-paulo) and the Clássico da Saudade with [Santos](/clubs/santos), which produced the 2021 Libertadores final, are the other city fixtures. Nationally, [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) is the rival that matters: the two have met in Libertadores finals in 2021 and 2025 and finished first and second in the league in 2025 and, as it stands, 2026.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Palmeiras are second in the Brasileirão, three points behind Flamengo with four games left, in a title race that has become the defining fixture of Brazilian football. Ferreira, in his sixth year, has Weverton, Gómez, Veiga, Vitor Roque, Flaco López and Andreas Pereira as the core and a new generation of academy graduates waiting. The club's financial strength, with Leila Pereira's backing and the sale of Estêvão, makes it the best-run big club in Brazil, and its target for 2027 is a fourth Libertadores and a record thirteenth title.
