## History

Borussia Mönchengladbach were founded on 1 August 1900 in a small textile town on the Lower Rhine and spent their first six decades in regional football, winning the DFB-Pokal in 1960 as a second-tier club. Promoted to the Bundesliga in 1965 alongside Bayern, they produced the most romantic team in German history under Hennes Weisweiler: the Fohlenelf, the Foals, a side of young, fast attackers led by Günter Netzer, Berti Vogts, Jupp Heynckes, Herbert Wimmer and later Rainer Bonhof, Uli Stielike and Allan Simonsen.

Between 1970 and 1977 they won five titles, the first club to win three in a row (1975-77), and the UEFA Cup in 1975 and 1979. The 1971 "Büchsenwurfspiel", when a 7-1 win over Werder was annulled after a can hit an opponent, and the 1977 European Cup final, lost 3-1 to Liverpool in Rome, are the two near-misses that haunt the era, and the Bökelberg, the tiny sloping ground, was its home. Netzer's self-substitution to score the winner in the 1973 cup final is the club's defining image. Weisweiler left for Barcelona in 1975, Udo Lattek won the last two titles, and the team was sold off piece by piece: Netzer to Real Madrid, Bonhof to Valencia, Simonsen to Barcelona.

[Lothar Matthäus](/players/lothar-matthaus) came through in the early 1980s before leaving for Bayern; Stefan Effenberg's side won the 1995 DFB-Pokal. Relegation came in 1999 and again in 2007, and the club moved to the 54,000-capacity Borussia-Park in 2004. Lucien Favre, appointed in February 2011 with the club bottom, saved them through the play-off against Bochum and built the modern Gladbach: fourth in 2012 with Marco Reus, Marc-André ter Stegen and Dante, third in 2015, Champions League football in 2015-16 and 2020-21, when Marco Rose's team reached the round of 16.

## Trajectory

Gladbach's trajectory since promotion in 2008 rose steadily to a peak between 2012 and 2020, with four top-four finishes and the Raffael-Stindl-Thorgan Hazard attack, and has fallen since. Rose left for Dortmund in 2021, [Adi Hütter](/managers/adi-hutter) lasted one season, Daniel Farke finished tenth, and Gerardo Seoane's three years brought 14th, 10th and a mid-table 2025-26 before the club turned to Alexander Blessin, the former St Pauli coach, for 2026-27. The sales of ter Stegen, Dante, Granit Xhaka, Thorgan Hazard, Denis Zakaria and Manu Koné funded the stadium debt but drained the squad, and the club has not been in Europe since 2021.

The stadium move explains much of the modern club. The Bökelberg held 34,000 and could not be expanded; Borussia-Park, opened in 2004 on the edge of town, cost around 87 million euros and was financed by debt that the club spent a decade repaying through player sales. The ground has one of the largest standing terraces in Germany, the Nordkurve, and the club's membership has grown past 100,000, but the gap in revenue to Dortmund and Bayern has widened, and Gladbach's modern ceiling has been the Champions League group stage rather than the trophies of the 1970s. Max Eberl, sporting director from 2008 to 2022, was the architect of the Favre-to-Rose years before leaving, and his departure coincided with the decline.

## Players the club produced

The Foals era produced a generation of West Germany's 1972 European champions and 1974 World Cup winners: Netzer, Vogts, Heynckes, Wimmer, Bonhof and Stielike all came through Weisweiler's academy or arrived as teenagers. [Lothar Matthäus](/players/lothar-matthaus) signed from Herzogenaurach as a 18-year-old in 1979 and left for Bayern in 1984. Later graduates include ter Stegen, the Barcelona goalkeeper, Patrick Herrmann (over 400 games), Marco Reus, who left for Dortmund in 2012 after two seasons as the Bundesliga's most exciting young player, Mahmoud Dahoud and Rocco Reitz.

Among signings, Raffael, Lars Stindl, Yann Sommer and Christoph Kramer defined the Favre-Hecking-Rose decade. Tim Kleindienst, signed from Heidenheim in 2024, has been the recent goalscorer.

## Identity and playing style

Gladbach's identity was set by Weisweiler: attacking, youthful, fast, the opposite of the pragmatic Bayern of the same era. Favre gave the modern club a disciplined [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) style with a compact mid-block, and Rose brought Red Bull-school [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing). Seoane's sides were more possession-based and less convincing. Alexander Blessin, who built St Pauli's promotion side on a [back three](/tactics/back-three) and an aggressive press, has been asked to restore the intensity, but the early results of 2026-27 have been poor.

## Rivalries

The Rhine derby against [Cologne](/clubs/cologne), 30 kilometres away, is the fixture that matters most in the region and one of the most hostile in Germany. [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) were the rival of the 1970s, when the two clubs shared every title between 1969 and 1977. The "Borussia derby" with [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) is friendlier, and Fortuna Düsseldorf and [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) complete the Rhineland set.

## Current situation

Gladbach are in trouble. As of 7 November 2026 they are bottom of the Bundesliga with four points from nine games (one win, one draw, seven defeats, 12 scored, 25 conceded). A 3-2 win at Stuttgart on 23 October was the only victory; it was followed by a 0-0 home draw with Paderborn on 31 October and a 3-1 defeat at Hamburg on 6 November. The next fixture after the international break is Dortmund at home on 21 November.

Blessin's position is under scrutiny after a start that has produced the worst defensive record in the league. Julian Weigl, Nico Elvedi and Kleindienst are the senior players; Reitz is the academy hope. The club's aim at the start of the season was a return to the top half; by November it is survival, and the Borussia-Park crowd, which has been patient through five mediocre seasons, is restless.
