## History

Al Hilal, "the Crescent", was founded in Riyadh in 1957 as Olympic Club and renamed by royal decree a year later. It won its first league title in 1977 and became the dominant force in Saudi football through the 1980s and 1990s, with Yousef Al-Thunayan's playmaking, Sami Al-Jaber's 1990s goals (he scored at four World Cups for [Saudi Arabia](/nations/saudi-arabia)), Mohammed Al-Deayea in goal and Nawaf Al-Temyat, the 2000 Asian Footballer of the Year. The club won the Asian Club Championship in 1991 and 2000, the Asian Cup Winners' Cup in 1997 and 2002 and the Asian Super Cup twice, and its nickname "Al-Zaeem", the boss, reflects a record of 19 league titles and more than 60 official trophies, the most of any Asian club.

The 2000s and 2010s were competitive rather than dominant, with Al Ittihad and later Al Ahli taking titles, and the club lost AFC Champions League finals in 2014 and 2017 before winning in 2019 (against Urawa Red Diamonds) and 2021 (2-0 against Pohang Steelers), with Salem Al-Dawsari, the winger who scored against Argentina at the 2022 World Cup, as the club's modern icon.

## Trajectory

The Public Investment Fund's takeover in June 2023 turned Al Hilal into the league's super-club. Jorge Jesus's 2023-24 team, with [Neymar](/players/neymar) (injured for most of his two years), Aleksandar Mitrović, Rúben Neves, Kalidou Koulibaly, Sergej Milinković-Savić and Malcom, won 34 consecutive matches across all competitions, a world record, and took the league by 14 points with the King Cup. The 2024-25 title went to [Al Ittihad](/clubs/al-ittihad), Jesus left, and in June 2025 the club appointed Simone Inzaghi, days after his Inter had lost the Champions League final.

Inzaghi's first weeks produced the most famous result in Saudi club history: a 4-3 extra-time win over [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) in the round of 16 of the 2025 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) in Orlando, Marcos Leonardo scoring twice, after a goalless draw with Real Madrid in the group. Fluminense ended the run in the quarter-finals. Darwin Núñez from Liverpool and Theo Hernández from Milan arrived that summer, and the club's current squad data lists Inzaghi as head coach as of November 2026, with Al Hilal top of the 2026-27 Saudi Pro League with 30 points from 11 games.

## Players the club produced

Al Hilal's youth system is the most productive in the Gulf. Al-Jaber, Al-Deayea's successor goalkeepers, Al-Temyat, Mohammad Al-Shalhoub, Yasser Al-Qahtani, Al-Dawsari, Mohammed Kanno, Saud Abdulhamid (sold to Roma in 2024) and the midfielder Nasser Al-Dawsari all came through, and the Saudi national team's 2022 World Cup win over Argentina was built on a Hilal core. The imports who became legends are Mitrović, Neves, Koulibaly, Milinković-Savić, the Emirati playmaker Omar Abdulrahman, the Moroccan goalkeeper Bono and, for his name if not his minutes, Neymar.

## Identity and playing style

Al Hilal's self-image is of the establishment club, and its football under Jesus was a dominant, high-pressing 4-3-3 with Neves dictating and Mitrović finishing. Inzaghi has imposed his Inter template: a 3-5-2 with wing-backs (Theo Hernández and João Cancelo), Milinković-Savić and Neves in a midfield that circulates patiently, and two strikers in Núñez and Marcos Leonardo running in behind, with Al-Dawsari drifting in from the left. It is a possession team that is most dangerous in transition, as City discovered. See [back three](/tactics/back-three) and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking).

## Rivalries

The Riyadh derby against [Al Nassr](/clubs/al-nassr) is the biggest match in Saudi football, and since Ronaldo's arrival it has been the most-watched club fixture in the Arab world; Al Hilal's run of results in it since 2023 has been a point of pride. The games against [Al Ittihad](/clubs/al-ittihad), the Jeddah champions of 2024-25, and [Al Ahli](/clubs/al-ahli), the Asian champions of 2025, are the other fixtures between the four PIF clubs.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Al Hilal lead the Saudi Pro League under Inzaghi with 30 points from 11 games, two ahead of Al Qadsiah and Al Nassr, with Al-Dawsari, Neves, Koulibaly, Núñez, Theo Hernández and Milinković-Savić as the core. The club's ambition is a 20th title, a fifth AFC Champions League Elite and another Club World Cup statement in 2029; the Kingdom Arena, its home since the King Fahd Stadium closed for renovation, will be replaced by a new stadium for the 2034 World Cup.
