## History

Hibernian were founded in 1875 by Irish immigrants in Edinburgh's Cowgate, under the auspices of the Catholic Young Men's Society, and the name comes from the Latin for Ireland. The club was initially barred from the Scottish FA on the grounds that it was for Irishmen rather than Scots, and its early history is bound up with the same immigrant story that produced Celtic a decade later; Celtic's founders consulted Hibs before setting up.

The great era was the late 1940s and early 1950s, when the "Famous Five" forward line of Gordon Smith, Bobby Johnstone, Lawrie Reilly, Eddie Turnbull and Willie Ormond won three titles in 1948, 1951 and 1952 and made Hibs the best team in Scotland. In 1955 Hibs became the first British club to enter the European Cup, Scotland's champions Aberdeen having declined, and reached the semi-final. The club was also a pioneer of floodlit football and, in 1972-73 under Eddie Turnbull, won the League Cup and beat Hearts 7-0 at Tynecastle on New Year's Day, the result Hibs supporters have cherished longest.

The 1980s and 1990s brought a relegation in 1980 (with George Best briefly on the books), a takeover crisis in 1990 when Hearts' chairman Wallace Mercer tried to buy and merge the club, which was defeated by the "Hands off Hibs" campaign, and the 1991 League Cup. Alex McLeish's side of the late 1990s, with Franck Sauzée and Russell Latapy, was the most entertaining Hibs team in a generation and reached the 2001 Scottish Cup final, and Tony Mowbray's young side of 2005 to 2006, with Scott Brown, Kevin Thomson, Derek Riordan and Steven Whittaker, finished third and won the 2007 League Cup under John Collins.

The Scottish Cup, though, had become a curse: Hibs had not won it since 1902, and lost finals in 2001, 2012 (5-1 to Hearts) and 2013. On 21 May 2016, as a Championship club under Alan Stubbs, they beat Rangers 3-2 with David Gray's header in the 92nd minute, ending the 114-year wait and prompting a pitch invasion. Neil Lennon took them back up as champions in 2017 and to fourth in 2018; David Gray, the cup-final hero, later managed the club to third place in 2024-25.

## Trajectory

Hibs have spent the twenty-first century as a mid-table club with upward spikes: third in 2001, 2005, 2021 and 2025, fourth in 2006, 2010 and 2018, and the relegation of 2014 via a play-off against Hamilton. Three seasons in the Championship brought the cup and the 2017 title. Europe has been intermittent, with qualifying-round exits more common than group stages. The 2024-25 third-place finish under Gray was the club's best for two decades and the 2025-26 season was a step back.

## Players the club produced

Hibs' academy has produced Scotland internationals in every era: Pat Stanton, the club's greatest captain, in the 1960s and 1970s; John Collins and Gordon Hunter in the 1980s; and in the 2000s a remarkable generation of Scott Brown (sold to Celtic for a Scottish record fee), Kevin Thomson, Derek Riordan, Steven Whittaker, Garry O'Connor and Steven Fletcher, all of whom were sold for large fees. Leigh Griffiths, Jason Cummings and Ryan Porteous followed; Josh Doig was sold to Hellas Verona in 2022. Franck Sauzée and Russell Latapy were signings, but no Hibs list is complete without them.

## Identity and playing style

Hibs' identity is attacking football in the Famous Five tradition, and supporters have been quick to turn on managers who abandon it. Recent teams have played a 3-5-2 or 4-2-3-1 with Martin Boyle's pace as the main threat on the break and an emphasis on quick transitions. As of November 2026 the team is led by Marink Reedijk, a Dutch coach appointed during the season after a poor start, with the task of restoring the structure of the Gray era.

## Rivalries

The Edinburgh derby against [Hearts](/clubs/hearts) is the fixture that matters: first played in 1875, laden with the 7-0 of 1973, the 5-1 of 2012 and 140 years of neighbourhood rivalry between Leith and Gorgie. Matches with [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) carry the shared Irish heritage; those with [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) the memory of the 2016 final.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Hibs are bottom of the Scottish Premiership after eleven rounds with eight points, a crisis that has already cost one manager his job and left the club fighting relegation rather than chasing Europe. Reedijk's immediate task is survival; the squad retains Boyle and Newell but has lost the balance of the side that finished third in 2025, and the Easter Road support is restless.
