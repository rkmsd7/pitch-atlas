## History

América was founded on 12 October 1916 by students in Mexico City, took its name from the date (Columbus Day, the "discovery of America") and spent its first four decades as a respectable but rarely dominant club. The transformation came in 1959, when Emilio Azcárraga Milmo, the owner of the Telesistema television empire that became Televisa, bought the club and set out to make it the most glamorous team in the country. He succeeded, and in doing so created the most-hated club in Mexico: América became the team of money, television and the capital, and every other fan base defined itself against it.

The 1960s and 1970s brought the first league titles (1966, 1971, 1976) with Enrique Borja and the Chilean playmaker Carlos Reinoso, and the 1980s were the golden decade: five championships in six seasons between 1984 and 1989, with Luis Roberto Alves "Zague", Cristóbal Ortega and the goalkeeper Héctor Miguel Zelada. The Estadio Azteca, where the club has played since 1966, hosted two [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) finals and will host its third in 2026, and América's identity is bound up with the stadium's scale.

The 1990s and 2000s produced fewer titles but bigger names: [Hugo Sánchez](/players/hugo-sanchez) for a season in 1992-93, Iván Zamorano, Claudio "Piojo" López, Pável Pardo and, above all, Cuauhtémoc Blanco, the street footballer from Tepito who became the club's defining player over three spells and invented the "cuauhtemiña" hop at the 1998 World Cup. Salvador Cabañas was the best striker in Mexico until he was shot in a Mexico City bar in January 2010 and never played again.

## Trajectory

The modern era began with the Clausura 2013 final, won on penalties against Cruz Azul after goalkeeper Moisés Muñoz scored a 93rd-minute equaliser with a diving header, one of the most famous goals in Mexican football. Titles followed in Apertura 2014, Apertura 2018 and, under the Brazilian André Jardine, three in a row: Apertura 2023, Clausura 2024 and Apertura 2024, the first tricampeonato of the short-tournament era, taking the record to 16 and clear of [Guadalajara](/clubs/guadalajara)'s twelve. Guillermo Ochoa's return in 2019 and Henry Martín's goals anchored those teams.

In CONCACAF the club's seven Champions Cup titles are a record, the last in 2016; it has been a regular at the [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) but missed the 2025 edition after losing a play-in to LAFC. The Clausura 2025 ended with a final defeat by Toluca, and Apertura 2025 with a quarter-final exit. While the Azteca was rebuilt for the World Cup the club played its 2024-25 home games at the Estadio Ciudad de los Deportes; it returned to the renamed Estadio Banorte in 2026.

## Players the club produced

América's academy produced Blanco, Pardo, Raúl Jiménez (sold to Atlético Madrid in 2014), Diego Lainez, Edson Álvarez (sold to Ajax and then West Ham) and Guillermo Ochoa, who made his debut at 18 in 2004. The club has also been a magnet for the best foreign players in the Mexican league: Reinoso, Zamorano, López, the Paraguayan Cabañas, the Colombian Mateus Uribe and, in the 2020s, the Spanish midfielder Álvaro Fidalgo, who became the brain of the three-peat team.

## Identity and playing style

Jardine's América played a controlled, possession-heavy 4-2-3-1 built around Fidalgo's tempo and Martín's link play, pressing high and using the wingers Alejandro Zendejas and Brian Rodríguez to attack the box. It was the most European-looking team in the league and the deepest squad. The club's broader identity is one of expectation: the coach is sacked after one bad tournament, the president Emilio Azcárraga Jean is a constant presence, and the fans, the largest following in Mexico and a huge diaspora in the United States, expect to win every semester. The head coach for the current Apertura 2026 is not confirmed in the club's squad data as of November 2026, so none is listed here.

## Rivalries

The Clásico Nacional against [Guadalajara](/clubs/guadalajara) is the biggest match in Mexican football, capital versus Jalisco, Televisa's money versus Chivas' Mexican-only purity, and it has been played in the United States for sold-out crowds as often as at home. The Clásico Joven with [Cruz Azul](/clubs/cruz-azul), a cross-town rivalry sharpened by the 2013 final and by the two clubs sharing stadiums during the Azteca works, and the Clásico Capitalino with [Pumas UNAM](/clubs/pumas-unam) complete the set.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 América sit top of the Apertura 2026 table on goal difference from Guadalajara and Cruz Azul with 29 points from 15 games, and they will be among the favourites for the Liguilla in December. Martín, Fidalgo, Zendejas and the goalkeeper Luis Malagón remain the core. The World Cup final was played in their stadium in July and the club has returned to a modernised Azteca; the aim is a 17th title and a return to the CONCACAF summit.
