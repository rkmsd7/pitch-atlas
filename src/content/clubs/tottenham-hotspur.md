## History

Tottenham Hotspur were formed in 1882 by schoolboys from the Hotspur Cricket Club, named after Harry Hotspur of the Percy family, who owned land in the area. They were the first and only non-League club to win the FA Cup, in 1901 as a Southern League side, and joined the Football League in 1908. Arthur Rowe's push-and-run team won the Second Division in 1950 and the First Division in 1951, playing a quick, short-passing style that was ahead of its time.

Bill Nicholson, a player in that side, became manager in 1958 and built the club's greatest team. In 1960-61 Spurs won the Double, the first of the 20th century, with Danny Blanchflower as captain, Dave Mackay as the engine, John White as the creator and Bobby Smith and Les Allen scoring. [Jimmy Greaves](/players/jimmy-greaves) arrived from Milan in December 1961 and scored 266 goals, still the club record. Spurs retained the FA Cup in 1962 and in 1963 became the first British club to win a European trophy, beating [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) 5-1 in the Cup Winners' Cup final. Nicholson added the FA Cup in 1967, League Cups in 1971 and 1973 and the UEFA Cup in 1972. Blanchflower's line, that the game is about glory, about doing things in style, became the club motto.

Keith Burkinshaw's team of the early 1980s, with Glenn Hoddle, Ossie Ardiles and Ricky Villa, won the FA Cup in 1981 and 1982 and the UEFA Cup in 1984. Terry Venables won the 1991 FA Cup with [Paul Gascoigne](/players/paul-gascoigne) and [Gary Lineker](/players/gary-lineker), the last major trophy of the century. The Premier League years brought [Jürgen Klinsmann](/players/jurgen-klinsmann), David Ginola, Ledley King and a long stretch of mid-table, until Harry Redknapp took the club into the Champions League in 2010 with [Gareth Bale](/players/gareth-bale) announcing himself against [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan).

Mauricio Pochettino's team of 2014 to 2019, with [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane), Son Heung-min, Dele Alli, Christian Eriksen and Hugo Lloris, was the best Spurs side in 50 years: second in 2017, the move into the new 62,850-seat stadium in April 2019, and a Champions League final in Madrid two months later, lost 2-0 to Liverpool. No trophy came. Kane left for [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) in 2023 as the club's record scorer with 280 goals. [Ange Postecoglou](/managers/ange-postecoglou) then ended the 17-year wait, winning the Europa League in Bilbao in May 2025 against Manchester United while finishing 17th in the league, and was sacked 16 days later. Thomas Frank's season also ended in 17th, and [Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi) was appointed for 2026-27.

## Trajectory

For most of the Premier League era Spurs sat between fifth and twelfth. The Redknapp years lifted them into the top four for the first time in 2010; the Pochettino years made that habitual, with five straight top-four finishes from 2016 to 2019 and a second place in 2017, the highest since 1963.

The post-Pochettino drift has been steep. Mourinho, Nuno Espírito Santo, Antonio Conte (fourth in 2022) and Postecoglou (fifth in 2024) followed in quick succession. Then came the paradox of 2024-25: a European trophy, qualification for the Champions League, and 17th in the league with 22 defeats, the club's worst finish since 1977. The 2025-26 season under Frank was no better in the league, another 17th on 41 points, one place above the relegation zone, with an early Champions League exit.

The pattern across those two seasons was the same: a squad capable of beating the best on a good night, as it did to Manchester City in the 2025 Carabao Cup and to Eintracht Frankfurt and Bodø/Glimt on the road to Bilbao, and incapable of grinding out league points against mid-table opponents. Injuries to Maddison, Van de Ven, Romero and Kulusevski recurred through both campaigns, and the club's heavy investment in players aged 18 to 22, among them Archie Gray, Lucas Bergvall, Wilson Odobert and Mathys Tel, left it short of experience when results turned.

The response was the largest rebuild in the club's history. De Zerbi was appointed in summer 2026, [Sandro Tonali](/players/sandro-tonali) was signed from [Newcastle United](/clubs/newcastle-united) for a club-record fee of around £100m, and eight senior signings arrived for more than £230m. Luka Vušković was sold to Brighton for a fee rising to £50m.

## Players the club produced

Tottenham's academy has produced some of England's best. [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane), the club's record scorer, 2026 Ballon d'Or winner and England captain, came through from the age of 11 via loans at Leyton Orient, Millwall, Norwich and Leicester. Ledley King, Sol Campbell, Glenn Hoddle and Steve Perryman, who holds the appearance record with 854, were earlier graduates. More recently, Harry Winks, Oliver Skipp and Mikey Moore came through, and Archie Gray and Lucas Bergvall, signed as teenagers, are products of the club's development model.

The signings are the club's folklore: [Jimmy Greaves](/players/jimmy-greaves), Dave Mackay from Hearts, Ardiles and Villa from Argentina after the 1978 World Cup, [Gary Lineker](/players/gary-lineker), [Paul Gascoigne](/players/paul-gascoigne), [Jürgen Klinsmann](/players/jurgen-klinsmann), David Ginola, Dimitar Berbatov, Luka Modrić, [Gareth Bale](/players/gareth-bale), who became the world's most expensive player when he left for [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) in 2013, Son Heung-min, the Premier League's most prolific Asian player and the Europa League-winning captain, and Hugo Lloris.

## Identity and playing style

[Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi) is one of the most distinctive coaches in Europe. At Sassuolo, Shakhtar, [Brighton & Hove Albion](/clubs/brighton) and [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) his teams played a possession game built on baiting the press: centre-backs standing on the ball, inviting opponents forward, then playing through them with vertical passes into midfielders who receive on the half-turn. It is [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with a provocateur's edge, and it needs technically secure players everywhere.

At Tottenham he has a 4-2-3-1 with Tonali and Rodrigo Bentancur as the double pivot, [James Maddison](/players/james-maddison) or [Xavi Simons](/players/xavi-simons) at ten, Mohammed Kudus and Sávio wide, and Omar Marmoush, signed from Manchester City, as a mobile centre-forward with Dominic Solanke and Richarlison as alternatives. The defence was rebuilt around Jan Paul van Hecke, signed from Brighton for £52m, Marcos Senesi from Bournemouth and Andy Robertson from Liverpool, with Micky van de Ven and Pedro Porro retained and Martin Dúbravka arriving as an experienced goalkeeper.

De Zerbi's teams are vulnerable to a well-timed high press, and Spurs have conceded goals from build-up errors this season, but they are also capable of dominating possession against anyone.

## Rivalries

[Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) are the rivalry that defines the club: the 1913 move to Highbury, the 1919 election, Campbell's defection, and a derby that Spurs have not won often enough in the Arteta era. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) are the second London enmity, sharpened by the Battle of the Bridge in 2016; Spurs won 2-0 at Stamford Bridge on 24 October 2026. [West Ham United](/clubs/west-ham-united), relegated in 2026, are the east-north London rivalry born of the 1980s and the 2006 lasagne affair.

## Current situation

Spurs are ninth in early November 2026: three wins, three draws and three defeats from nine, 10 goals scored and 10 conceded, 12 points. The autumn has been encouraging: a 3-1 home win over Coventry on 19 October, the 2-0 win at Chelsea on 24 October, and a 2-0 home win against Crystal Palace on 31 October. The trip to Leeds on 7 November was the tenth fixture.

The summer brought Tonali, Marmoush, Sávio, Simons, Van Hecke, Senesi, Robertson, Dúbravka, Conor Gallagher and a loan for Mykhailo Mudryk, with De Zerbi saying in August that the club had "not finished yet". The departures included Vušković, Cristian Romero and Guglielmo Vicario. Spurs are not in Europe in 2026-27 after the 17th-place finish, which gives the new coach a clear week between matches to install his system.

The playing squad is now one of the deepest in the league: Dúbravka, Antonín Kinský and Brandon Austin in goal; Van de Ven, Van Hecke, Senesi, Tosin Adarabioyo, Porro, Destiny Udogie, Robertson and Ben Davies in defence; Tonali, Bentancur, Gallagher, Maddison, Simons, Kudus, Kulusevski, Mudryk, Gray, Bergvall and Mateus Fernandes in midfield; Marmoush, Solanke, Richarlison, Sávio, Tel and Odobert in attack. De Zerbi's challenge is to turn that depth into a settled first eleven.

The ambition is a return to the top six and a cup. Daniel Levy's long chairmanship, the stadium's commercial success and the squad's cost mean that another finish in the bottom half would be a crisis; the first ten weeks suggest a team that is improving but not yet consistent.
