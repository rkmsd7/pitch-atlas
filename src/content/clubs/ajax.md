## History

Ajax were founded on 18 March 1900 in an Amsterdam café by a group of young men who named their club after the Greek hero, and for their first half-century they were a respected but not exceptional Dutch side. The transformation began in 1964 when [Rinus Michels](/managers/rinus-michels), a former Ajax forward, took charge of a team that was flirting with relegation. Michels had something nobody else had: a slight, argumentative teenager from the Betondorp neighbourhood called [Johan Cruyff](/players/johan-cruyff). Together they built the most influential club side of the twentieth century.

The Ajax of the late 1960s and early 1970s played what the world came to call [total football](/tactics/total-football): defenders attacked, forwards defended, every player was expected to understand every position, and the ball was moved with a speed and intelligence that made the European Cup look easy. They lost the 1969 final to AC Milan, then won three in a row in 1971 (2-0 against Panathinaikos at Wembley), 1972 (2-0 against Inter in Rotterdam, both goals from Cruyff) and 1973 (1-0 against Juventus in Belgrade). Michels left for Barcelona in 1971 and Ștefan Kovács took the team to the second and third of those trophies, a sign that the system was bigger than any one coach. Cruyff followed Michels to Catalonia in 1973 and the great side broke apart.

The club fell back to being merely very good domestically for a decade before a second golden generation arrived. Cruyff returned as manager in 1985 and promoted a group of academy players who would define the next era: [Marco van Basten](/players/marco-van-basten), [Frank Rijkaard](/players/frank-rijkaard), [Dennis Bergkamp](/players/dennis-bergkamp) and, slightly later, the De Boer twins. The 1987 Cup Winners' Cup was won with a Van Basten header against Lokomotive Leipzig, and in 1992 Ajax won the UEFA Cup against Torino on away goals.

The third and most recent peak was [Louis van Gaal](/managers/louis-van-gaal)'s side of 1994 to 1996. Built around Danny Blind, Frank Rijkaard in his second spell, [Edgar Davids](/players/edgar-davids), [Clarence Seedorf](/players/clarence-seedorf), Jari Litmanen, Finidi George and the teenage [Patrick Kluivert](/players/patrick-kluivert), they went through the 1994-95 Eredivisie unbeaten and beat AC Milan 1-0 in the Champions League final in Vienna with a Kluivert goal in the 85th minute. They reached the final again in 1996 and lost on penalties to Juventus. The Bosman ruling then did to that team what Barcelona's chequebook had done to the 1973 side, and within three years most of them had gone.

The twenty-first century has been more uneven. Ajax won the league in 2002 and 2004, then endured a six-year drought before Frank de Boer's four consecutive titles from 2011 to 2014. [Erik ten Hag](/managers/erik-ten-hag) built the side that reached the 2019 Champions League semi-final, beating Real Madrid at the Bernabéu and Juventus in Turin before Lucas Moura's hat-trick for Tottenham ended the dream in stoppage time. Ten Hag won three titles and left for Manchester United in 2022; the years since have been the most chaotic in the club's modern history, with sporting directors sacked, a 2023-24 season that began at the bottom of the table and a 2025-26 campaign in which three different men occupied the dugout.

## Trajectory

Ajax have never been relegated and have finished in the top three of the Eredivisie in the vast majority of seasons since the league's foundation in 1956. Their 36 championships are a Dutch record, ten clear of [PSV](/clubs/psv-eindhoven), and their four European Cups place them alongside Bayern Munich and behind only Real Madrid, AC Milan and Liverpool in the competition's history.

The pattern of the last 25 years is one of peaks and troughs rather than steady dominance. From 2000 to 2010 they won two titles and finished second five times, usually behind PSV. The De Boer era (2011 to 2014) brought four straight championships but no European progress of note. The Ten Hag years were the modern high point: titles in 2019, 2021 and 2022, a domestic double in 2019 and 2021, and the 2019 Champions League run that revived the club's reputation across Europe. Since 2022 there has been one second-place finish under Francesco Farioli in 2024-25, when a late collapse handed the title to PSV, surrounded by seasons that would have been unthinkable a decade earlier: fifth in 2023-24 and a 2025-26 campaign in which John Heitinga was sacked in November after a dismal Champions League league phase, Fred Grim and then Óscar García served as caretakers, and the team finished some way behind PSV.

The club's European record since 1996 tells a similar story: the 2017 Europa League final under [Peter Bosz](/managers/peter-bosz), lost 2-0 to Manchester United, and the 2019 semi-final are the only two deep runs in nearly three decades.

## Players the club produced

No club in the world has a stronger claim to the phrase "talent factory". De Toekomst, the academy complex next to the stadium, has produced generation after generation of players who defined Dutch and European football.

The first wave was [Johan Cruyff](/players/johan-cruyff), the greatest Dutch player of all time and the thinker behind everything that followed, alongside Johan Neeskens, Piet Keizer, Ruud Krol and Sjaak Swart. The 1980s academy gave the world [Marco van Basten](/players/marco-van-basten), [Frank Rijkaard](/players/frank-rijkaard), [Dennis Bergkamp](/players/dennis-bergkamp), Aron Winter and the De Boer brothers; [Ruud Gullit](/players/ruud-gullit) was not an academy product but spent two seasons at the club. The 1990s produced [Clarence Seedorf](/players/clarence-seedorf), [Edgar Davids](/players/edgar-davids), [Patrick Kluivert](/players/patrick-kluivert), Edwin van der Sar and Michael Reiziger, the core of the 1995 side.

The twenty-first century has been just as rich: [Wesley Sneijder](/players/wesley-sneijder), Rafael van der Vaart, John Heitinga and Nigel de Jong in the early 2000s; Christian Eriksen, Daley Blind and Toby Alderweireld in the De Boer years; and the Ten Hag generation of [Frenkie de Jong](/players/frenkie-de-jong), [Matthijs de Ligt](/players/matthijs-de-ligt), Donny van de Beek and Ryan Gravenberch, with Hakim Ziyech and Dušan Tadić as the senior signings who made it work. Zlatan Ibrahimović and Luis Suárez both spent formative years in Amsterdam before becoming global stars.

## Identity and playing style

Ajax's identity is the clearest of any club in Europe: 4-3-3, possession, width, pressing, youth. The principles set down by [Rinus Michels](/managers/rinus-michels) and refined by [Johan Cruyff](/managers/johan-cruyff-manager) and [Louis van Gaal](/managers/louis-van-gaal) are written into the academy's coaching manuals, and every first-team coach is judged on whether he honours them. The modern descendant of total football is [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), and Ten Hag's title-winning sides were a textbook example: a goalkeeper comfortable on the ball, full-backs pushed high, two eights who rotated with the wingers, and a centre-forward who dropped in to create overloads.

[Míchel](/managers/michel-sanchez), appointed ahead of 2026-27 on a contract to 2028, was chosen precisely because his Girona teams played that way with cheaper players. His early months have been about restoring structure after a season in which the team's shape disintegrated under three coaches, and about reconnecting the first team to the academy pipeline. The arrival of Jordi Cruyff in a football leadership role in early 2026 reinforced the sense of a club returning to its own principles.

The club's institutional story matters here too. Ajax are a listed company, the only one of its kind in the Netherlands, and the turmoil since 2022 has been as much in the boardroom as on the pitch: sporting director Sven Mislintat was dismissed within months in 2023 after a chaotic transfer window, Alex Kroes was suspended and reinstated in 2024, and the supervisory board changed repeatedly. Jordi Cruyff's arrival and Míchel's appointment were intended to end that cycle and to put the football decisions back in the hands of people who understand the Ajax way.

## Rivalries

De Klassieker against [Feyenoord](/clubs/feyenoord) is the biggest match in Dutch football, Amsterdam against Rotterdam, the capital's sophistication against the port city's work ethic. The fixture has been played without away supporters since 2009 after years of violence, which says something about how deep the feeling runs. The rivalry with [PSV](/clubs/psv-eindhoven) is less visceral but has been the more important title race for most of the last three decades, with the two clubs trading championships since the early 1990s.

## Current situation

As of November 2026, Ajax sit second in the Eredivisie after eleven rounds, a point behind Feyenoord and a point ahead of PSV, in a three-way race that has become the story of the Dutch season. Míchel's team is still being assembled; the squad was turned over heavily in the summer and the coach has asked for patience while the positional structure beds in. Ajax are not in the Champions League league phase this season, which gives Míchel a lighter calendar than his rivals and the board a strong incentive to finish in the top two. The longer-term question is whether a club that has cycled through sporting directors and coaches since 2022 can give its new manager the stability that the 2019 side enjoyed.
