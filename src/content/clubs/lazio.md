## History

Lazio are the oldest club in Rome, founded on 9 January 1900 by nine young men led by Luigi Bigiarelli, a runner, in Piazza della Libertà, as a multi-sport society whose colours were the white and sky blue of Greece in honour of the Olympic ideal. The eagle came from the Roman legions. The club refused to be absorbed into the 1927 merger that created Roma and has defined itself in opposition ever since. The first Scudetto took 74 years: Tommaso Maestrelli's 1973-74 side, led by the Welsh-born striker Giorgio Chinaglia, who scored 24 goals that season, and the captain Pino Wilson, was famous for its internal feuds, its players carrying guns and its brilliant, direct football. Maestrelli died of cancer in 1976, Chinaglia left for New York, and the midfielder Luciano Re Cecconi was shot dead in a jeweller's shop in a prank gone wrong in 1977, a tragedy that broke the side.

The second golden age came under Sergio Cragnotti, the food magnate who bought the club in 1992 and spent like no one in Italy. Sven-Göran Eriksson's Lazio won the Cup Winners' Cup in 1999, the last edition, beating Mallorca at Villa Park, the UEFA Super Cup against Manchester United, and in 1999-2000 the double, the title clinched on the last day when Juventus lost in a Perugia downpour. [Alessandro Nesta](/players/alessandro-nesta), the Roman centre-back from the academy, captained a side with [Pavel Nedvěd](/players/pavel-nedved), Juan Sebastián Verón, Siniša Mihajlović, Marcelo Salas, Diego Simeone and Hernán Crespo, bought from Parma for a then world-record fee. Cragnotti's Cirio empire collapsed in 2002, the stars were sold, and the club came close to bankruptcy before Claudio Lotito, a cleaning-services businessman, took control in 2004 and has run it with a tight fist ever since.

## Trajectory

Lazio have not been in Serie B since 1988, when a nine-point Totonero penalty kept them down for a second year, and they have finished in the top half of Serie A in all but a handful of seasons this century. The Calciopoli penalties of 2006, originally relegation, reduced on appeal to a points deduction, left them 16th in the revised 2005-06 table. Under Lotito the club has been frugal and steady: Coppa Italias in 2009, 2013, the derby final, and 2019 under [Simone Inzaghi](/managers/simone-inzaghi), who also finished second in 2022-23 under [Maurizio Sarri](/managers/maurizio-sarri)'s guidance. Ciro Immobile, signed from Sevilla in 2016, scored 207 goals in eight seasons and set the Serie A single-season record of 36 in 2019-20 to win the European Golden Shoe. Sarri's second spell, 2025-26, ended with a ninth-place finish, a Coppa Italia final defeat to Inter and a mutual-consent exit on 27 May 2026. [Gennaro Gattuso](/managers/gennaro-gattuso), who had resigned as Italy coach after the failed 2026 World Cup qualification, was appointed on 23 June.

## Players the club produced

Lazio's academy has given Italy its greatest modern centre-back, Nesta, who left in tears for Milan in 2002 when the club's debts demanded it, and the midfielder Giuseppe Favalli's contemporary Marco Di Vaio. Earlier it produced Pino Wilson and the goalkeeper Felice Pulici. The signings are what the fans remember: Chinaglia, bought from Massese; Nedvěd from Sparta Prague, who won nothing bigger than the 2000 title in Rome but became a Ballon d'Or winner at Juventus; Verón, Mihajlović, [Dino Zoff](/players/dino-zoff), who kept goal and later coached the club, Miroslav Klose, who scored 64 goals in five seasons after 2011, Sergej Milinković-Savić, bought from Genk for €18m and sold to Al-Hilal for €40m in 2023, and Immobile. Mattia Zaccagni, the current captain, and Nicolò Rovella, the midfield anchor signed from Juventus, are the backbone of the present side.

## Identity and playing style

Eriksson's Lazio played a 4-4-2 of great technical players with Nedvěd and Verón as creative wide midfielders; Inzaghi's played a 3-5-2 with Luis Alberto and Milinković-Savić feeding Immobile; Sarri wanted his 4-3-3 of short passing and pressing triggers. Gattuso, who was a tough, direct coach at Milan and Napoli and a more pragmatic one with Italy, has turned Lazio into a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) side built on a compact 4-2-3-1 and set pieces. Davide Frattesi, on loan from Inter, has been the revelation of the autumn as a box-crashing number eight, Gustav Isaksen and Tijjani Noslin share the league's top scorer position on four goals each, Nuno Tavares provides the left-sided thrust and Christos Mandas has become one of the best young goalkeepers in Italy. The summer signings were cheap and clever: Alfonso Pedraza and Danilho Doekhi on free transfers, Andrea Pinamonti from Sassuolo for €3m, Albert Guðmundsson and Josip Šutalo on loan.

## Rivalries

The Derby della Capitale with [Roma](/clubs/roma) is the city's fault line, and in 2026-27, with Lazio first and Roma third, the November fixture at the Olimpico is the most anticipated derby in a generation. The supporters' other hostility is to [Napoli](/clubs/napoli), a grudge dating from the 1980s terraces, and to [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), the 2000 title rival whom Lazio beat 3-0 in October 2026. There is a longstanding friendship with Inter's ultras, which gives matches between the two a strange warmth despite Inter having beaten Lazio in the 2026 Coppa Italia final.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Lazio are top of Serie A with 28 points from ten matches, nine wins and one draw, 24 goals scored and eight conceded, three points clear of champions Inter. The 6-2 win at Sassuolo on 27 October and a 2-2 draw with Milan in September are the only games in which the defence has looked porous; otherwise Gattuso's side has ground out 1-0 wins at Bologna and against Genoa, and the coach was named Serie A's coach of the month for October. The season's one embarrassment came early, a 2-0 home defeat to Serie B side Mantova in the Coppa Italia first round in August, and there is no European football after last season's ninth place, which has left the squad fresh for a one-competition campaign.

Whether Lazio can sustain a title challenge with a squad assembled for a fraction of Inter's or Milan's wage bill is the question of the Serie A season. Lotito has never spent in January; the hope is that Frattesi's loan is made permanent, that Pinamonti and Isaksen keep scoring, and that Gattuso, in his first Serie A job since Napoli in 2021, can keep a side of good professionals believing in a first Scudetto since 2000.
