## History

Paris Saint-Germain are young by the standards of the great European clubs. They were founded on 12 August 1970 by the merger of Paris FC and Stade Saint-Germain, after a supporter campaign to give the capital a top-flight team, and were promoted to Division 1 in their first season. A political split in 1972 separated the two founding halves: [Paris FC](/clubs/paris-fc) kept the professional licence and the place in the top division, while PSG dropped to the third tier. Within two years the roles had reversed, PSG were back in Division 1 and took up residence at the Parc des Princes in 1974.

The first golden age came under president Francis Borelli with Safet Sušić, Luis Fernández and Dominique Rocheteau: the Coupe de France in 1982 and 1983 and a first league title in 1986. Canal+ bought the club in 1991 and the 1990s were the club's most glamorous era before Qatar: George Weah, David Ginola, Raí and Youri Djorkaeff; the 1994 title; a Champions League semi-final in 1995; and the 1996 Cup Winners' Cup, won 1-0 against Rapid Vienna in Brussels with a Bruno N'Gotty free kick, the club's first European trophy. They lost the same final to Barcelona in 1997.

The 2000s were a decade of drift. Ronaldinho spent two seasons in Paris (2001-03) in a team that could not finish above ninth, Pauleta scored 109 goals as a lone star, and the club flirted with relegation in 2007 and 2008, finishing 16th in 2007-08 and surviving only on the final day. Three Coupe de France wins (2004, 2006, 2010) were the consolation.

Qatar Sports Investments bought the club in 2011 and everything changed. [Zlatan Ibrahimović](/players/zlatan-ibrahimovic), Thiago Silva, Edinson Cavani and Marco Verratti arrived; Laurent Blanc won three straight domestic trebles; Unai Emery signed Neymar for 222 million euros and [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe) for 180 million in 2017. Eleven titles in thirteen seasons followed, but the Champions League kept escaping: the 6-1 collapse at Barcelona in 2017, the 2020 final lost 1-0 to Bayern in Lisbon, the 2021 semi-final against Manchester City. [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) came and went (2021-23) without a European trophy.

[Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique) was appointed in July 2023 and, after Mbappé left for Real Madrid in 2024, built a young, pressing collective around [Ousmane Dembélé](/players/ousmane-dembele), [Vitinha](/players/vitinha), [Achraf Hakimi](/players/achraf-hakimi), [João Neves](/players/joao-neves), [Désiré Doué](/players/desire-doue) and [Bradley Barcola](/players/bradley-barcola). On 31 May 2025 they beat Inter 5-0 in Munich, the biggest winning margin in a European Cup final, to complete a treble. Dembélé won the 2025 Ballon d'Or. On 30 May 2026 in Budapest they retained the trophy, drawing 1-1 with Arsenal (Havertz 6', Dembélé penalty) and winning 4-3 on penalties, the first club to defend the Champions League since Real Madrid in 2018.

## Trajectory

PSG's trajectory is the sharpest upward curve in modern football. From 16th in 2008 and 13th in 2010 they rose to fourth in 2011, second in 2012 and then thirteen seasons in which they finished first or second every year. Under Luis Enrique the 2024-25 title was the fourth in a row and the 2025-26 title, the club's 14th and fifth straight, made them the most decorated club in French history ahead of Saint-Étienne.

In Europe the arc is the more telling one: quarter-finalists four years running (2013-16), the 2020 final, the 2021 semi-final, then early exits in 2022, 2023 and 2024, the 2025 win and the 2026 defence. The club also won the 2025 UEFA Super Cup against Tottenham and reached the 2025 Club World Cup final, losing 3-0 to Chelsea in New Jersey.

The stadium question runs alongside the sporting one. The Parc des Princes, owned by the city of Paris and holding under 48,000, is the smallest home of any recent European champion, and the club's attempts to buy it have been refused repeatedly; by 2026 PSG had identified sites in the Paris suburbs for a new stadium of 60,000 or more and begun planning in earnest. The Camp des Loges training ground was replaced in 2023 by a 74-hectare campus at Poissy, the most expensive training complex in football, which houses the academy that Luis Enrique's project depends on.

## Players the club produced

PSG's academy, long accused of letting Paris's talent go to other clubs, has been reclaimed in the Luis Enrique years. Warren Zaïre-Emery, Senny Mayulu and Ibrahim Mbaye are academy products who played in Champions League-winning sides; earlier graduates include Mamadou Sakho, Adrien Rabiot, Kingsley Coman, Moussa Diaby and Christopher Nkunku, most of whom left early. Presnel Kimpembe spent his whole career at the club.

The signings who became legends are longer: Weah, Raí, Ginola, Pauleta, Ibrahimović (156 goals in four years), Cavani (the club's record scorer until Mbappé passed him), Thiago Silva, Verratti and [Marquinhos](/players/marquinhos), who signed in 2013 at 19 and captained the European champions. [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe) scored 256 goals in 308 games between 2017 and 2024, the record that will stand longest. [Ousmane Dembélé](/players/ousmane-dembele), converted into a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine) by Luis Enrique, won the Ballon d'Or in 2025; [Bradley Barcola](/players/bradley-barcola), signed from Lyon in 2023, left for Liverpool in summer 2026 for around 125 million euros.

## Identity and playing style

The Luis Enrique PSG are the most clearly defined team in Europe. The shape is a 4-3-3 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in the Barcelona tradition with Vitinha as the pivot who controls tempo, Neves and Fabián Ruiz as the interiors, Hakimi as a wide forward from right-back, and interchangeable forwards with no fixed striker. Without the ball it is [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) with coordinated [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the opponent's first pass; the 2025 final against Inter was a demonstration of a team winning the ball 30 metres from goal again and again.

The deeper identity shift was cultural. Luis Enrique insisted on a collective without a superstar, and the departure of Mbappé, which looked like a loss, became the enabling event. The club's recruitment under Luís Campos now targets players under 23 with elite technical and running profiles rather than finished stars.

The 2025 and 2026 Champions League runs illustrate the method. In 2024-25 PSG finished 15th in the league phase and had to come through the play-off round against Brest, then beat Liverpool on penalties at Anfield after a 1-0 defeat at home, dismantled Aston Villa and Arsenal, and produced in Munich the most complete final performance in the competition's history, with two goals from Doué and one each from Hakimi, Kvaratskhelia and Mayulu. In 2025-26 the path was harder: a 5-4 first-leg win over Bayern in the semi-final, an away leg survived, and a final against Arsenal that was decided by the nerve of a young team from the penalty spot. Luis Enrique joined the small group of coaches with three European Cups.

## Rivalries

**Le Classique** against [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) is France's biggest match: Paris against the south, the Canal+ club against Bernard Tapie's European champions, and in the QSI era a fixture PSG have dominated but never defused. [Lyon](/clubs/lyon) were the rival of the 2000s, when OL won seven straight titles while PSG struggled, and [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) the challenger in 2017. [Paris FC](/clubs/paris-fc), the club PSG once were half of, returned to Ligue 1 in 2025 with Red Bull and Arnault money and have become a city rivalry for the first time since 1972.

## Current situation

PSG defend both titles in 2026-27 with the same coach and almost the same squad. The summer window, which closed on 1 September 2026, saw Barcola sold to Liverpool, but Dembélé, Kvaratskhelia, Doué, Vitinha, Neves, Hakimi and Marquinhos all stayed, and Luis Enrique's contract runs on. As of 7 November 2026 they lead Ligue 1 with 20 points from nine games (six wins, two draws, one defeat), two clear of [Lille](/clubs/lille). Recent results include a 3-1 win at Strasbourg on 17 October, a 2-0 home win over Lyon on 26 October and a 5-1 win at Le Havre on 30 October. Promoted Troyes visit the Parc on 7 November.

In the Champions League league phase they travelled to Villarreal on 3 November and host Roma on 25 November; the final is at Atlético Madrid's Metropolitano on 5 June 2027, and a third straight European Cup, something no club has done since Bayern in 1976, is the stated target. Mbappé, now at Real Madrid, won the 2026 World Cup Golden Boot and was Ballon d'Or runner-up to [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane); his shadow over the club has receded, but the standard he set has not.
