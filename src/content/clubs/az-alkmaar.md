## History

AZ were formed in 1967 by the merger of Alkmaar '54 and FC Zaanstreek, and their first golden age arrived quickly. Funded by the Molenaar brothers, who ran a local building firm, AZ '67 of the late 1970s and early 1980s were the best team in the Netherlands outside the big three. Kees Kist won the European Golden Boot in 1979 with 34 goals, and in 1980-81 Georg Kessler's side won the Eredivisie with a 101-goal attack, took the KNVB Cup for a third time in four years and reached the UEFA Cup final, losing 5-4 on aggregate to Bobby Robson's Ipswich Town.

When the Molenaars withdrew their money the club collapsed, sliding into the second division in 1988 and staying there for a decade. The rescue came from another local businessman, Dirk Scheringa, whose DSB Bank funded a new stadium in 2006 and a serious squad. Co Adriaanse took AZ to a UEFA Cup semi-final in 2005, and [Louis van Gaal](/managers/louis-van-gaal), who had initially resigned after a poor 2007-08 before the players asked him to stay, won the 2008-09 title with a side built around Mounir El Hamdaoui, Moussa Dembélé and goalkeeper Sergio Romero. They lost only twice all season.

Scheringa's bank failed in 2009 and the club again had to live within its means. It has done so remarkably well, finishing in the top five in all but two seasons since 2014 and reaching the 2023 Conference League semi-final under Pascal Jansen, where Lazio were beaten in the quarter-final before West Ham ended the run.

## Trajectory

AZ's modern trajectory is a story of consistency without a title. Since the 2009 championship they have finished between third and fifth in twelve of fifteen completed seasons, and in the curtailed 2019-20 campaign they were level on points with Ajax at the top when the league was abandoned, losing the title on goal difference in effect. Four KNVB Cups, the last in 2013, and regular European group stages have followed. The club's model, developing players at one of Europe's best academies and selling them to Serie A and the Premier League, keeps them competitive but caps their ceiling.

## Players the club produced

The AZ academy has been rated the best in the Netherlands in recent years, and its output shows why: Teun Koopmeiners (to Atalanta and Juventus), Calvin Stengs, Myron Boadu, Owen Wijndal, Tijjani Reijnders (to AC Milan and Manchester City), Jesper Karlsson and Sven Mijnans all came through or broke through at Alkmaar. Earlier eras produced Barry van Galen, the club's long-serving captain, and saw Moussa Dembélé, Jozy Altidore and Vincent Janssen launch careers in the Eredivisie before moving to England.

## Identity and playing style

AZ play possession football with a young side, generally in a 4-3-3 or 4-2-3-1, with a philosophy set at academy level and carried through the age groups. The club's technical direction has favoured coaches who develop rather than dominate, and the team is characteristically good at building through midfield and vulnerable to physical opponents. As of November 2026 the first team is led by Lee-Roy Echteld, a former AZ youth coach, after Maarten Martens' three-year spell, with the familiar emphasis on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and promoting from within.

## Rivalries

AZ's nearest rivals are [Ajax](/clubs/ajax), thirty miles south in Amsterdam, and the matches between the two have gained edge since AZ became a consistent top-four side. There is also a long-standing sense of competition with [FC Twente](/clubs/fc-twente) and Utrecht as the clubs fighting for the places behind the big three.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 AZ are fifth in the Eredivisie after eleven rounds, three points off the top four. The squad has the usual AZ shape, a core of academy graduates and a handful of senior signings, and the ambition is the familiar one of a top-four finish and a cup run while the club prepares for the next wave of sales.
