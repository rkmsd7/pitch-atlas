## History

Le Mans, the city of the 24-hour race, had a professional football club in Stade Olympique du Maine from the 1930s, but the modern club, Le Mans Union Club 72, was formed in 1985 by a merger and spent its first years in the lower divisions. Promotion to Division 2 came in 1987, and for the next fifteen years the club was a second-tier side with an academy that produced more than its results suggested: [Didier Drogba](/players/didier-drogba) signed as a 20-year-old from Levallois in 1998, took four seasons to become a regular, and was sold to Guingamp in 2002.

Thierry Goudet's side went up to Ligue 1 in 2003 as runners-up, came straight down, and under Frédéric Hantz went up again in 2005. This time they stayed five seasons: 11th, 12th, ninth and 13th, with a Coupe de la Ligue semi-final in 2006 and a team that included Daniel Cousin, Romaric, Stéphane Sessègnon, Gervinho and Grafite, and the club was renamed Le Mans FC in 2010 to coincide with the move to the new 25,000-seat MMArena in 2011. Relegation in 2010, a stadium the club could not afford and a 2013 relegation to the National on the pitch led to liquidation that summer; the club was dissolved and refounded in the fifth tier.

The rebuild, driven by president Thierry Gomez, went through CFA 2, CFA and National 2, with back-to-back promotions in 2017 and 2018 that took the club into Ligue 2 for 2018-19, when they were relegated again. Six seasons in the National followed, until Patrick Videira's side won the division in 2024-25 and then, remarkably, finished second in Ligue 2 in 2025-26 to reach Ligue 1 for the first time since 2010.

## Trajectory

Liquidated and sent to the fifth tier in 2013, in Ligue 1 thirteen years later: Le Mans's trajectory is one of the fastest climbs in French football, achieved with back-to-back promotions in 2017 and 2018 and again in 2025 and 2026. The club's budget is the smallest in Ligue 1 by some distance and the MMArena, built for a bigger club, is finally being filled.

## Players the club produced

[Didier Drogba](/players/didier-drogba) is the name: 64 goals in 139 games across all competitions between 1998 and 2002, most of them in the second division, before Guingamp, Marseille and Chelsea. Gervinho (2005-07) and Sessègnon were sold to Lille and PSG; Daniel Cousin and Romaric were the Ligue 1-era favourites; Anthony Le Tallec and Marko Baša passed through; Yohann Pelé was the goalkeeper of the top-flight years. The refounded club's heroes are more modest: the promotion sides of 2025 and 2026 were built around Dylan Durivaux, Patrick Burner, Gaëtan Weissbeck and Ali Abdi.

## Identity and playing style

Le Mans's identity is the rebirth. Videira, who played for the club before coaching it, has built a team on organisation, work rate and [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), typically a 3-5-2 that sits in a mid-to-[low block](/tactics/low-block) and attacks on the [counter](/tactics/counter-attacking). It got the club up twice in two years and is being asked to keep them up against far better-resourced opponents.

## Rivalries

[Angers](/clubs/angers), 95 kilometres away, are the Pays de la Loire derby opponent, and the two clubs' promotion in 2026 restored the fixture to Ligue 1. Laval is the Mayenne neighbour from the lower leagues, and Nantes the regional giant, now in Ligue 2.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Le Mans are 17th in Ligue 1 on seven points from nine games (one win, four draws, four defeats, 11 scored, 18 conceded), in the automatic relegation places. They lost 3-0 at home to Toulouse on 16 October, drew 1-1 at Auxerre on 25 October and lost 2-0 at promoted rivals Troyes on 31 October. Monaco visit the MMArena on 8 November.

Videira's side has drawn four of nine and won once, which is roughly what was expected of a club that was in the National eighteen months earlier. The aim is survival; the realistic hope is the play-off place, two points away, and the club has already achieved more than anyone outside Le Mans believed possible.
