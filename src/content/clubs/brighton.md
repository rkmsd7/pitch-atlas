## History

Brighton & Hove Albion were founded in 1901, joined the Football League in 1920 and spent most of the next 60 years in the Third Division, winning the Charity Shield in 1910 as Southern League champions and little else. The club's first golden period came under Alan Mullery, who took them from the Third Division to the First in three seasons, reaching the top flight in 1979 with Peter Ward and Brian Horton. They lasted four years, and the 1983 FA Cup final against Manchester United, drawn 2-2 after Gordon Smith's late miss ("and Smith must score"), was lost 4-0 in the replay. Relegation came the same week.

The decline that followed nearly ended the club. By 1996-97 Brighton were bottom of the Fourth Division, their owners had sold the Goldstone Ground to developers, and supporters were protesting at every match. A final-day draw at Hereford kept them in the League; they spent two seasons playing at Gillingham, 70 miles away, and then at the Withdean athletics track. Dick Knight's consortium saved the club, and Micky Adams won back-to-back promotions in 2001 and 2002, with Bobby Zamora scoring 83 goals in two seasons.

Tony Bloom, a Brighton supporter and professional gambler, bought the club in 2009 and funded the £93m stadium at Falmer, opened in 2011 as Gus Poyet's team won League One. Chris Hughton's side was promoted to the Premier League in 2017 after play-off heartbreak in 2013, 2014 and 2016, and survived three seasons. Graham Potter brought a possession style and a ninth-place finish; [Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi) took that to sixth in 2023 and a first European campaign, reaching the Europa League last 16. [Fabian Hürzeler](/managers/fabian-hurzeler), appointed at 31 in 2024 as the youngest permanent manager in Premier League history, has delivered two eighth-place finishes and, in 2026-27, the best start in the club's history.

## Trajectory

From the Third Division title in 2001 to the Premier League in 2017 is a straight line interrupted by one crash: relegation to League One in 2006, five seasons in the third tier, and the League One title in 2011 that coincided with the stadium's opening. Since 2017 Brighton have never been relegated and have moved from survival (15th, 17th, 15th, 16th) to the top half every season since 2021-22: ninth, sixth, 11th, eighth, eighth.

The eighth place in 2025-26, on 53 points, brought a Conference League place through the cascade of European qualification, since Crystal Palace won the competition and Manchester City won both domestic cups. The club's business model, buying young players identified by Bloom's data company Starlizard and selling them at a premium, continued: Moisés Caicedo (£115m to Chelsea), Alexis Mac Allister (£35m to Liverpool), Marc Cucurella, Yves Bissouma, Ben White, Leandro Trossard, Simon Adingra, [João Pedro](/players/joao-pedro) (£60m to Chelsea in 2025) and Carlos Baleba (to Manchester United in summer 2026) were all sold for large profits.

## Players the club produced

Brighton's academy produced Lewis Dunk, the captain since 2019 and the club's record Premier League appearance-maker, Solly March, Evan Ferguson and Jack Hinshelwood. The club's reputation rests on recruitment rather than the academy: Caicedo from Independiente del Valle for £4m, Mac Allister from Argentinos Juniors, [Kaoru Mitoma](/players/kaoru-mitoma) from Kawasaki Frontale for £2.5m, Pascal Gross from Ingolstadt for £3m, who left in 2024 as the club's greatest Premier League player, and Bart Verbruggen from Anderlecht. Earlier eras had Peter Ward, Bobby Zamora, Glenn Murray and Bruno Saltor.

Hürzeler's squad has its own development successes: Yankuba Minteh, Georginio Rutter, Diego Gómez, Matt O'Riley and Mats Wieffer, all signed in 2024, and the Croatian centre-back Luka Vušković, 19, signed from Tottenham in summer 2026 for a club-record £46m rising to £50m, a direct replacement for Jan Paul van Hecke, who went the other way for £52m.

## Identity and playing style

[Fabian Hürzeler](/managers/fabian-hurzeler) inherited De Zerbi's possession principles and made them more direct. Brighton still build from the back with the goalkeeper and bait the press, but the ball moves forward faster, the wingers (Mitoma and Minteh) are told to isolate full-backs, and the pressing is a man-oriented [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) that has been the most intense in the league in 2026-27. The base shape is a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession with one full-back inverting.

The defence has been the story of the autumn: with Vušković, Pascal Struijk (signed from Leeds) and Olivier Boscagli ahead of Verbruggen, Brighton had conceded only seven goals in nine matches by early November, the fewest in the division alongside Arsenal, and had scored 24, the most. Rutter as the number ten and Charalambos Kostoulas and Stefanos Tzimas as young strikers give them goals from several sources. The 5-0 win over Crystal Palace on 18 October and the 1-0 win at Manchester City on 31 October were the two results that made the rest of the league take notice.

## Rivalries

The rivalry with [Crystal Palace](/clubs/crystal-palace), 45 miles away, dates from the 1970s when Mullery and Terry Venables managed the two clubs and has been a Premier League fixture since 2017; the M23 derby is one of the odder enmities in English football but one of the most heated. [Portsmouth](/clubs/portsmouth), the other south-coast club, were the historic rival before the Premier League years separated them.

## Current situation

Brighton are third in early November 2026: six wins, one draw and two defeats from nine, 24 goals scored, seven conceded, 19 points, five behind Arsenal and two behind Manchester City. The defeats were a 2-0 loss at Liverpool on 25 October and one earlier in the season. Commentators have begun asking whether Brighton can sustain a title challenge; Hürzeler has said it is too early to talk about one, and the Conference League fixtures add to the load on a squad that was described as thin in pre-season.

The summer saw Vušković and Struijk arrive, Van Hecke, Baleba and Danny Welbeck leave, and the core of Mitoma, Dunk, Verbruggen, Rutter and Minteh retained. The club's ambition is stated as European football every season and a first major trophy; the FA Cup semi-finals of 2019 and 2023 remain the closest it has come.
