## History

Espanyol were founded on 28 October 1900 by Ángel Rodríguez, a university student, as Sociedad Española de Football, the name chosen to distinguish a club of Spanish-born members from the foreigner-founded FC Barcelona. That origin, and the royal title granted in 1912, gave the club a distinct political identity in the city, and the two Barcelona clubs have coexisted uneasily ever since. The legendary goalkeeper Ricardo Zamora, "El Divino", was an Espanyol product; the club won its first Copa del Rey in 1929 and was a founder member of [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) the same year.

Espanyol's history is one of near-misses rather than titles. A second Copa came in 1940, a third-place league finish in 1973 under José Santamaría, and then in 1988 a UEFA Cup final against Bayer Leverkusen, won 3-0 at Sarrià in the first leg and lost 3-0 and on penalties in Germany. The club was relegated in 1989 and 1993, moved from the beloved Sarrià to the Olympic stadium on Montjuïc in 1997 and won the Copa del Rey in 2000 against Atlético (with Raúl Tamudo scoring) and 2006 against Zaragoza.

The 2006-07 season was both glorious and heartbreaking: Ernesto Valverde's side reached the UEFA Cup final in Glasgow, took Sevilla to penalties after a 2-2 draw, and lost; Tamudo's late equaliser at Camp Nou that June, the "Tamudazo", cost Barcelona the league. Captain Dani Jarque died of a heart attack in August 2009, weeks before the club moved into the new Cornellà-El Prat stadium; Andrés Iniesta's World Cup-final shirt dedication, "Dani Jarque siempre con nosotros", belongs to Espanyol's story as much as Spain's.

## Trajectory

Espanyol were a stable mid-table club from 1994 to 2019, with Mauricio Pochettino's 2009-12 spell and Rubi's 2018-19 seventh place (the first European qualification since 2007) as the best stretches. Chinese ownership under Chen Yansheng, from 2016, promised a push for Europe and delivered volatility instead: relegation in 2020, the Segunda title in 2021, relegation again in 2023, and promotion via the play-offs in 2024 under Manolo González. González stabilised the side in 14th in 2024-25 and 11th in 2025-26, with 46 points, before a poor start to 2026-27 (18th after ten games, eight points) cost him his job on 28 October 2026. Eder Sarabia, the former Barcelona assistant who had taken [Elche](/clubs/elche) up and kept them up, was appointed the same day on a contract to June 2028.

## Players the club produced

Espanyol's academy is one of the most underrated in Spain. Zamora in the 1920s, Tamudo (the club's all-time top scorer, 140 goals), Iván de la Peña (a Barcelona graduate who found his home at Espanyol), Jarque, Sergio García, Javi Puado (the current captain), Marc Roca, Adrià Pedrosa and, most notably, Joan García, the goalkeeper who was the best in the league in 2024-25 and was sold to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in 2025 for his €25m release clause, to the fury of the Espanyol fan base. [Gerard Moreno](/players/gerard-moreno) came through the youth system before his years at Villarreal.

Thomas N'Kono, the Cameroonian goalkeeper of 1982-91 and still the goalkeeping coach, and Pochettino, who played 275 games before coaching the club, are the adopted sons.

## Identity and playing style

Espanyol's tradition is combative and organised rather than expansive: tight 4-4-2 or 4-2-3-1 shapes, strong goalkeepers (Zamora, N'Kono, Diego López, García, Marko Dmitrović), and a reliance on a prolific striker (Tamudo, García, Moreno). Manolo González's side was a pragmatic [low block](/tactics/low-block) that drew heavily on Puado's running and set pieces. Sarabia is a different animal: a possession coach in the Barcelona school whose Elche played a brave 3-4-3 from the back, with high full-backs and numerical superiority in midfield. His stated plan is to change the team's style gradually, and he has already asked sporting director Monchi for a striker and a winger in January.

## Rivalries

The Derbi Barceloní against Barcelona is the rivalry that defines the club: the small side of the city against the global one, decided in Barcelona's favour far more often than not but with the Tamudazo, the 2009 win at Camp Nou and the 2025 relegation escape against them as Espanyol's treasured chapters. [Girona](/clubs/girona) have become a regional rival as Catalonia's third club, and [Sevilla](/clubs/sevilla) carry the memory of 2007.

## Current situation

Espanyol are 14th on 7 November 2026 with 11 points from 11 games (three wins, two draws, six defeats), two points above the relegation zone. Sarabia's first match, at home to [Deportivo](/clubs/deportivo-la-coruna) on 7 November, was watched from the stands because of a touchline suspension carried over from his time at Elche; his official presentation was on 3 November at the RCDE Stadium.

The squad is modest: Dmitrović in goal, Leandro Cabrera, Unai Núñez and Omar El Hilali in defence, Edu Expósito and Pol Lozano in midfield, Puado, Bryan Zaragoza and Roberto Fernández in attack. Monchi, hired as sporting director after his Aston Villa spell, is overseeing a longer rebuild under the club's ownership, which passed from Chen Yansheng in 2025. The aim this season is survival with a better style; the Derbi is the match every Periquito circles.
