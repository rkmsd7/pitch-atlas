## History

AS Monaco were founded in 1924 in the principality on the Côte d'Azur, play in the French league system by special arrangement, and have been owned or underwritten by the Grimaldi family's state for most of their history. The first title came in 1961 under Lucien Leduc, with the red-and-white diagonal shirt designed by Princess Grace, and the club won again in 1963 and 1978. The Stade Louis II, rebuilt in 1985 with its arches and a 16,000 capacity in a state of 38,000 people, is the smallest great ground in Europe.

Arsène Wenger arrived in 1987 and won the title in his first season with Glenn Hoddle, Mark Hateley and the young Emmanuel Petit; he signed George Weah from Cameroon, reached the 1992 Cup Winners' Cup final (lost to Werder) and left for Japan in 1994. Jean Tigana won the 1997 title with [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry), David Trezeguet, Fabien Barthez and Emmanuel Petit, reached a Champions League semi-final in 1998, and Claude Puel added the 2000 title. Didier Deschamps took a side of Ludovic Giuly, Fernando Morientes, Jérôme Rothen and Dado Pršo to the 2004 Champions League final, beating Real Madrid on away goals and Chelsea 5-3 on aggregate before losing 3-0 to Porto in Gelsenkirchen.

The decline was swift: financial trouble, mid-table seasons and relegation in 2011. The Russian billionaire Dmitry Rybolovlev bought the club in December 2011, won Ligue 2 in 2013 and signed Radamel Falcao and James Rodríguez for 100 million euros that summer. The strategy then shifted to youth under Leonardo Jardim and sporting director Luis Campos: [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe), Bernardo Silva, Fabinho, Thomas Lemar, Benjamin Mendy and Tiémoué Bakayoko won the 2017 title with 107 goals and reached the Champions League semi-final, beating Manchester City and Dortmund. The team was sold within a year for more than 350 million euros.

Thierry Henry's spell as coach (2018-19) nearly ended in relegation. Niko Kovač finished third in 2021, Philippe Clement third in 2022, [Adi Hütter](/managers/adi-hutter) second in 2024 and third in 2025, with Champions League football restored. Hütter was sacked in November 2025, [Sébastien Pocognoli](/managers/sebastien-pocognoli) lasted under eight months, and Filipe Luís, the former Atlético Madrid full-back who had won the Libertadores with Flamengo, was appointed on 7 June 2026.

## Trajectory

Monaco's century is a series of peaks (1988, 1997, 2000, 2004, 2017) and troughs (relegation in 2011, 17th in 2019) with little in between. Since 2020 they have been consistently in the top three or near it: third in 2021, 2022 and 2025, second in 2024, with Champions League league-phase campaigns in 2024-25 and 2025-26. The 2025-26 season, with two coaching changes, was below that standard, and the club's trajectory under Filipe Luís is intended to be about identity rather than quick fixes.

## Players the club produced

Monaco's academy at La Turbie is the most productive in France after Lyon's and PSG's: [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry), Lilian Thuram, Emmanuel Petit, David Trezeguet, [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe) (debut at 16, 2016-17 champion at 18, sold to PSG for 180 million euros), Layvin Kurzawa, Benoît Badiashile, [Aurélien Tchouaméni](/players/aurelien-tchouameni) (developed after signing from Bordeaux, sold to Real Madrid for around 80 million in 2022), Eliesse Ben Seghir and Maghnes Akliouche, the current star. Among signings who became legends: Weah, [Jürgen Klinsmann](/players/jurgen-klinsmann) (1992-94), Hoddle, Djorkaeff, Giuly, Morientes, Falcao, Bernardo Silva, Fabinho and Wissam Ben Yedder, the club's leading scorer of the 2020s.

## Identity and playing style

Monaco's identity is youth, pace and the sale of both. The best Monaco sides, Wenger's, Deschamps', Jardim's, were [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) teams built around fast forwards; Jardim's 2017 side scored 107 league goals with a 4-4-2 of relentless running. Hütter brought [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) from a [back three](/tactics/back-three) and a [high line](/tactics/high-line).

Filipe Luís is a different kind of coach: a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) believer whose Flamengo averaged the most possession in Brazil, with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) stepping into midfield and sustained pressure in the opponent's half. His early months at Monaco have been about installing that game with a slightly more conservative rest defence, and the squad's young, athletic profile suits a coach who built his reputation with Flamengo's academy.

## Rivalries

The Côte d'Azur derby against [Nice](/clubs/nice), 20 kilometres along the coast, is the local fixture, and Nice fans have long treated Monaco as the artificial rich neighbour. [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) are the southern rival for Champions League places. [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) were the title rivals of 2017, when Monaco's young side beat them to the championship, and [Lyon](/clubs/lyon) the rivals for podium places through the 2000s and 2020s.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Monaco are seventh in Ligue 1 on 15 points from nine games (four wins, three draws, two defeats, 12 scored, ten conceded). The recent form has been poor: a 1-0 defeat at Lorient on 18 October, a 1-1 home draw with Lille on 25 October and a 3-1 defeat at Paris FC on 1 November. The next fixture is at promoted Le Mans on 8 November.

Filipe Luís is five months into the job, with Denis Zakaria as captain, Akliouche and Lamine Camara as the creative midfielders, Breel Embolo up front and Thilo Kehrer in defence. The target set by the board is a Champions League place, the competition for second behind PSG is open, and the Flamengo experience, where a Libertadores title was followed by a sacking within months, is a reminder of how quickly things can turn. The Monaco board has historically been patient, and the coach's credibility with players is his greatest asset.
