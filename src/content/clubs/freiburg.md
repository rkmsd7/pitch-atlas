## History

SC Freiburg were founded in 1904 in the university town at the edge of the Black Forest and were an amateur club until the 1970s. Promotion to the 2. Bundesliga came in 1978, and the club's identity was created by one man: Volker Finke, appointed in 1991, who took Freiburg into the Bundesliga for the first time in 1993 and stayed until 2007. His 1994-95 side, playing an attacking, passing game that earned the nickname "Breisgau-Brasilianer", finished third and qualified for the UEFA Cup, with Rodolfo Cardoso, Jörg Schmadtke and a young [Joachim Löw](/managers/joachim-low) in the squad a few seasons earlier.

Finke's Freiburg were relegated three times (1997, 2002, 2005) and promoted three times, which the club accepted as the price of its principles: no debt, a famous academy (the Freiburger Fußballschule, opened in 2001), and coaches given time. Robin Dutt took them up in 2009 as champions; Christian Streich, the academy coach, took over in December 2011 and stayed 12 and a half years, the longest-serving coach in the league. His sides finished fifth in 2013, seventh in 2017, sixth in 2022 (reaching the DFB-Pokal final, lost on penalties to Leipzig) and fifth in 2023 (a Europa League last-16 run, eliminated by Juventus), and the club moved from the Dreisamstadion to the new Europa-Park Stadion in 2021.

Julian Schuster, a former Freiburg captain, replaced Streich in summer 2024 and finished fifth in his first season, qualifying for the Europa League again, before a mid-table 2025-26 while managing European midweeks.

## Trajectory

Freiburg have been in the Bundesliga for all but one of the last 17 seasons and have not finished lower than 15th since 2018. The trajectory under Streich and Schuster is an unusually smooth upward curve for a club with the fourth-smallest budget in the division: four European qualifications in 2013, 2017, 2022-23 and 2025, a cup final, and no relegation since 2015, which was the one blemish of the Streich era and was reversed within a season.

## Players the club produced

The Freiburger Fußballschule has produced a remarkable list for a town of 230,000: Matthias Ginter (returned in 2022 after Dortmund and Gladbach), Maximilian Philipp, Christian Günter (over 400 games, a one-club captain), Nico Schlotterbeck (sold to Dortmund), Kevin Schade, Noah Atubolu and Merlin Röhl. Earlier generations included Levan Kobiashvili, Sebastian Kehl, Andreas Zeyer and Ali Güneş. Papiss Cissé scored 22 goals in 2010-11 before Newcastle; Nils Petersen, the club's record Bundesliga scorer, and Vincenzo Grifo, who returned for a third spell and became the set-piece specialist of the Streich years, are the terraces' favourites. Oliver Baumann, Roman Bürki and Alexander Schwolow were Freiburg goalkeepers before moving on.

## Identity and playing style

Freiburg's identity is a philosophy before a formation: patience, youth, sustainability, and coaches who are teachers. Streich's football was a compact 3-4-3 or 4-4-2 with an aggressive press, outstanding [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) (Grifo's deliveries and Ginter's headers made Freiburg the most dangerous dead-ball side in the league for years) and quick [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) transitions. Julian Schuster has kept the [back three](/tactics/back-three) and the pressing and added more possession in build-up, with Ritsu Doan and Grifo as the creators and Lucas Höler as the pressing forward.

## Rivalries

The Baden derby against Karlsruher SC is the historic rivalry, rarely played since Karlsruhe's decline; [VfB Stuttgart](/clubs/vfb-stuttgart) are the Baden-Württemberg rivals in the Bundesliga, and the games with Hoffenheim carry a south-west edge. Freiburg's wins over [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), such as the 2-1 in 2015 and the 2-1 in 2022, are treated as landmark days.

## Current situation

Freiburg have started 2026-27 strongly. As of 7 November 2026 they are third in the Bundesliga on 16 points from eight games (five wins, one draw, two defeats, 18 scored, ten conceded), in the Champions League places. They lost 2-1 at Leverkusen on 18 October and 3-1 at home to Bayern on 25 October, then won 2-1 at Augsburg on 31 October. Union Berlin visit the Europa-Park Stadion on 8 November.

Schuster's squad is largely homegrown or long-serving: Günter, Ginter, Grifo, Atubolu, Höler and Doan. The club's stated aim is European qualification; a Champions League place would be a first, and the start has made it a realistic conversation.
