## History

Inter Miami exists because of a clause. When [David Beckham](/players/david-beckham) joined the [LA Galaxy](/clubs/la-galaxy) in 2007 his contract contained an option to buy an MLS expansion franchise at a fixed price, and after years of stadium wrangling the club was formally unveiled in January 2018 with Jorge and José Mas as co-owners. The crest, a pair of herons with locked legs and the Latin motto "Libertas, Unitas, Fortuna", and the pink-and-black palette were designed to feel Mediterranean rather than American, and the club has leaned into that identity ever since.

The first two seasons, 2020 and 2021, were a mess. Diego Alonso and then Phil Neville coached teams built around big names past their peak, among them Gonzalo Higuaín and Blaise Matuidi, and the club was fined and sanctioned in 2021 for breaching MLS roster and salary rules in the Matuidi signing. Attendances at the temporary stadium in Fort Lauderdale were loyal but modest and the football was forgettable.

Then came 15 July 2023. [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) chose Miami over a return to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) and a lucrative move to the [Saudi Pro League](/leagues/saudi-pro-league), and within weeks he was joined by Sergio Busquets and Jordi Alba under their old Barcelona coach Gerardo "Tata" Martino. The transformation was immediate: Messi scored a stoppage-time free kick on his debut against Cruz Azul, and the club won the Leagues Cup in August 2023, beating Nashville on penalties after Messi had scored in every round. [Luis Suárez](/players/luis-suarez) followed in 2024.

## Trajectory

The arc is unusual: a club that finished 14th of 15 in the Eastern Conference in 2023 (Messi arrived too late and too injured to rescue the league campaign) and then won the Supporters' Shield in 2024 with a record 74 points. That 2024 side was eliminated in the first round of the play-offs by [Atlanta United](/clubs/atlanta-united), a reminder that MLS rewards the knockout rounds more than the regular season.

2025 was the breakthrough and also a year of extremes. Miami lost the Leagues Cup final 3-0 to the [Seattle Sounders](/clubs/seattle-sounders), reached the group stage of the expanded [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) on home soil (a goalless draw with Al Ahly, a Messi free-kick win over Porto, then a 4-0 round-of-16 defeat by [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain)), and finished third in the East. In the play-offs, with Javier Mascherano now in charge, the club finally clicked: a 5-1 Eastern Conference final win over [New York City FC](/clubs/new-york-city-fc) and then a 3-1 MLS Cup victory over Vancouver Whitecaps at Chase Stadium on 6 December 2025, the farewell match for Busquets and Alba, who both retired. Messi was MLS MVP in 2024 and again in 2025, and he extended his contract through 2028.

In 2026 the club finished second in the East behind Nashville's runaway regular season. The best-of-three first-round series are under way as of early November 2026; the long-promised Miami Freedom Park stadium project has been the other story of the year.

## Players the club produced

Inter Miami is a buying club, not yet a producing one, though the academy and the second team (Inter Miami II in MLS Next Pro) have started to feed players into the first team, Benjamin Cremaschi being the first homegrown to become a regular before his 2025 move to Parma. The club's historical importance will instead be measured by the players it hosted: [Messi](/players/lionel-messi), [Suárez](/players/luis-suarez), Busquets, Alba, and more recently Rodrigo De Paul, who arrived from [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) in 2025 as a World Cup winner in his prime. The best American talent to pass through has been goalkeeper Drake Callender and defender Noah Allen.

## Identity and playing style

Under Martino and then Mascherano, Miami played a possession game built entirely around Messi's freedom: a 4-3-3 that became a 3-2-5 in possession, Busquets setting the tempo from deep, Alba overlapping on the left, and Messi drifting from the right half-space into the pockets where he could combine with Suárez. It was never a pressing team; its weakness was always transition defending with an ageing spine, which is how [Atlanta](/clubs/atlanta-united) and later PSG and Seattle punished it.

Kily González, listed as head coach in the club's current squad data as of November 2026, inherited a squad being rebuilt around De Paul and younger legs after the retirements of Busquets and Alba. The underlying idea, Messi as the hub and everything else arranged to give him the ball in the final third, has not changed. See [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) for the Barcelona-rooted framework the club has imitated.

## Rivalries

The Florida derby with Orlando City is the fixture that matters most to supporters, a rivalry that predates Messi and turned nasty in a 2024 Leagues Cup fixture. The games with [Atlanta United](/clubs/atlanta-united) have been loaded since Atlanta knocked the Shield winners out in 2024, and [NYCFC](/clubs/new-york-city-fc) became a conference rival through the 2025 play-offs. Internationally the club's most-watched matches have been against Mexican sides in the Leagues Cup.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Inter Miami are the reigning MLS Cup holders, second in the East in the 2026 regular season with 59 points from 33 games, and in the play-offs. Messi, 39, remains the focal point and is under contract to 2028; Suárez and De Paul are the other senior names, with Tadeo Allende the main goal threat alongside them. Kily González is head coach per the squad data; the club's ambition is to become the first team to retain MLS Cup since the [LA Galaxy](/clubs/la-galaxy) in 2011-12, and to open Miami Freedom Park. Watch for [Argentina](/nations/argentina) internationals continuing to gravitate to South Florida.
