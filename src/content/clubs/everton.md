## History

Everton were founded in 1878 as St Domingo's, a Methodist church team, and were founder members of the Football League in 1888. They played at Anfield until a rent dispute in 1892 drove them out and led the landlord to create [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool). Everton moved to Goodison Park, the first purpose-built football stadium in England, and won the league there in 1891, 1915, 1928, 1932 and 1939. [Dixie Dean](/players/dixie-dean) scored 60 league goals in 1927-28, a record that will never be broken, and 383 for the club in all.

Harry Catterick's teams of the 1960s won the title in 1963 and 1970, the latter with the midfield of Alan Ball, Howard Kendall and Colin Harvey, the Holy Trinity, and the FA Cup in 1966. Kendall returned as manager in 1981 and, after nearly being sacked in 1983, built the best Everton side of all: the FA Cup in 1984, the title and the Cup Winners' Cup in 1985 (3-1 against Rapid Vienna in Rotterdam), and the title again in 1987, with Neville Southall in goal, Kevin Ratcliffe as captain, Peter Reid in midfield and Graeme Sharp and Andy Gray, then Gary Lineker, scoring. The post-Heysel European ban denied that team the European Cup, and Kendall left for Athletic Bilbao.

Joe Royle's Dogs of War won the FA Cup in 1995, the last trophy. The Premier League years under Walter Smith were a struggle; [David Moyes](/managers/david-moyes), appointed in March 2002 from Preston, called Everton "the People's Club" and gave the club 11 seasons of stability on a small budget, including fourth place in 2005, the 2009 FA Cup final and a generation of players built around Tim Cahill, Leighton Baines, Phil Jagielka and Mikel Arteta.

Farhad Moshiri bought the club in 2016 and spent more than £500m on players to little effect. Carlo Ancelotti brought a brief glow in 2020-21; then came Rafael Benítez, Frank Lampard, two consecutive escapes from relegation under Sean Dyche, and points deductions for breaching profit and sustainability rules in 2023-24. The Friedkin Group completed its takeover in December 2024, Moyes returned in January 2025 with the club 16th, and in 2025-26 Everton played their first season at the Hill Dickinson Stadium at Bramley-Moore Dock, leaving Goodison after 133 years.

## Trajectory

Everton's claim is longevity: more top-flight seasons than any other English club, and only four spent outside it since 1888. In the Premier League era the shape is a long mid-table plateau with a rise in the Moyes years (fourth in 2005, fifth in 2008 and 2009, sixth in 2013) and a slide under Moshiri's managers: from seventh in 2017 to 16th, 17th and 15th between 2022 and 2024, with the 2023-24 finish depressed by an eight-point deduction.

Thirteenth in 2024-25 and 13th again in 2025-26, the first season in the new stadium, suggested stabilisation. The 2026-27 start has been better than anyone expected: fifth in early November after ten matches, with four wins and four draws.

## Players the club produced

Everton's academy produced [Wayne Rooney](/players/wayne-rooney), who scored against Arsenal at 16 in October 2002 and returned for a season in 2017, Ross Barkley, Tom Davies, Anthony Gordon, sold to Newcastle for £45m in 2023, and Jarrad Branthwaite, the current centre-back who was bought from Carlisle at 17 and developed at Finch Farm. Earlier graduates include Colin Harvey and, in the Kendall era, Kevin Ratcliffe and Gary Stevens.

The signings who became legends include [Dixie Dean](/players/dixie-dean) from Tranmere, Alan Ball from Blackpool after the 1966 World Cup, Neville Southall from Bury, Andy Gray, Graeme Sharp, Duncan Ferguson, Tim Cahill, Leighton Baines, Romelu Lukaku, the club's leading Premier League scorer, and [Jordan Pickford](/players/jordan-pickford), England's goalkeeper since 2018. [Jack Grealish](/players/jack-grealish), who joined on loan from Manchester City in 2025 and then permanently, has been the creative centre of the Moyes team.

## Identity and playing style

[David Moyes](/managers/david-moyes) has never pretended to be a stylist. His Everton defend in a compact 4-2-3-1 that drops into a [low block](/tactics/low-block) when needed, attack with pace in wide areas and are dangerous from [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), where James Tarkowski and Branthwaite are the targets. The 2026-27 version has more quality in possession than his earlier sides: Grealish drifting in from the left, Kiernan Dewsbury-Hall and Hayden Hackney, signed from Middlesbrough, running the midfield, Christian Nørgaard, bought from Arsenal for around £6.8m, as the screen, and Brennan Johnson, signed from Crystal Palace for a fee reported between £22m and £30m, providing speed on the right.

Thierno Barry and Tyrique George, who returned from Chelsea permanently, share the striker's role, with Tyler Dibling, 20, the young wide player the club is building around. Merlin Röhl, from Freiburg, has added a ball-carrier to the midfield. Everton's game is still counter-attacking at heart, with a settled, organised identity that the new stadium's atmosphere has helped.

## Rivalries

The Merseyside derby with [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) is the oldest continuous top-flight derby in England and has the most red cards in Premier League history. The last derby at Goodison, 2-2 in February 2025 with Tarkowski's injury-time equaliser, was one of the great nights there. [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) are a secondary rivalry from the 1985 FA Cup final and the 1995 one that Everton won through Paul Rideout.

## Current situation

Everton are fifth in early November 2026, with four wins, four draws and two defeats from ten matches, 12 goals scored and 10 conceded, 16 points, level with Chelsea. October included a 2-2 draw at Hull on 11 October, a 2-0 home defeat to Chelsea on 17 October, a 2-0 loss at Arsenal on 24 October, then a 1-0 win at Newcastle on 2 November and a 3-1 home win over Coventry on 6 November.

The summer business was modest and sensible: Johnson, Röhl, Hackney, George, Nørgaard, with Dwight McNeil sold to Crystal Palace and Idrissa Gueye leaving on a free. Moyes, 63, is in the final year of his contract and no extension talks had been reported as of August. The ambition for 2026-27 is a top-half finish and, if the autumn form holds, the first European qualification since 2017; the realistic aim is to make the new stadium a home and the club a stable one again.
