## History

Sport Club Corinthians Paulista was founded on 1 September 1910 by five workers from the Bom Retiro district of São Paulo, inspired by a touring English amateur side, Corinthian FC, and it has carried the identity of a workers' club ever since. In a city whose other giants were founded by Italian immigrants (Palmeiras) or by the elite (São Paulo), Corinthians belonged to the periphery, and the Fiel, "the faithful", grew into the largest fan base in the state and the second largest in Brazil. The club won its first Paulista title in 1914 and dominated the state championship for decades, with the goalkeeper Gilmar and the attacker Luizinho in the 1950s, before a 23-year drought that ended in 1977 with Basílio's goal against Ponte Preta.

The 1980s belonged to the Democracia Corinthiana. Under the president Waldemar Pires and the sociologist Adilson Monteiro Alves, players led by [Sócrates](/players/socrates), Wladimir and Casagrande ran the club by vote, from training times to signings, during Brazil's military dictatorship, and won the Paulista titles of 1982 and 1983 with "Democracia" on their shirts. Sócrates, the chain-smoking doctor with the back-heel, is the club's philosopher-king; Rivellino, who played there from 1965 to 1974, is its artist; and [Garrincha](/players/garrincha) played a season in 1966.

The first Brasileirão title came in 1990 under Nelsinho Baptista, with Neto's free kicks and the striker Tupãzinho. The 1990s and 2000s added titles in 1998 and 1999 under Vanderlei Luxemburgo and Oswaldo de Oliveira, with Marcelinho Carioca, Rincón and Edílson, and the first FIFA Club World Championship in January 2000, won on penalties against Vasco da Gama at the Maracanã. The 2005 title under the Media Sports Investment partnership, with Carlos Tevez and Javier Mascherano, was followed by a scandal-ridden collapse and relegation in 2007.

## Trajectory

The response to relegation defined the modern club. Promoted at once in 2008, Corinthians signed [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) in 2009, won the Copa do Brasil and the Paulista with him, and in 2011 won the Brasileirão under Tite. The 2012 season was the peak: an unbeaten Copa Libertadores campaign ending with Emerson Sheik's two goals against Boca Juniors in the final, and then the Club World Cup in Japan, where Paolo Guerrero's header beat Chelsea 1-0 in front of 30,000 travelling Corinthians fans in Yokohama. Cássio, the goalkeeper, was the player of the tournament and stayed until 2024.

The Neo Química Arena, built for the opening match of the 2014 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), gave the club a home of its own after decades at the Pacaembu. Tite won the 2015 title, Fábio Carille the 2017 one, and then the club entered a long period of debt, with stadium financing and salary arrears constraining every window. Memphis Depay's arrival in 2024 was a surprise that produced a Sudamericana semi-final and the 2025 Paulista and Copa do Brasil titles, the latter won on penalties against Vasco in November 2025. The head coach for the 2026 season is not confirmed in the club's current squad data as of November 2026, so none is listed here; Corinthians are 17th in the Brasileirão with 37 points from 34 games, one place above the relegation zone on goal difference.

## Players the club produced

The Terrão academy produced Rivellino, Casagrande, Wladimir, Marcelinho Paraíba, Paulinho (sold to Tottenham, returned, sold to Barcelona), Malcom (to Bordeaux and Barcelona), Pedrinho, Fagner and, more recently, the midfielder Gabriel Moscardo (to PSG) and the forward Wesley. The great imports were Sócrates, Neto, Tevez, Mascherano, Ronaldo, Guerrero, Jô, the Peruvian playmaker André Carrillo and Memphis.

## Identity and playing style

Corinthians' identity is "raça", grit, and its most successful coaches, Tite above all, have built teams that defend as a unit in a 4-1-4-1 or 4-2-3-1, press in the middle third and win through organisation, set pieces and a reliable goalkeeper. The 2012 Libertadores winners conceded four goals in 14 games. The fans demand effort before beauty, which is why Sócrates' team, which offered both, remains the romantic ideal. The club's 2025-26 teams have been organised around Memphis as a false nine and Rodrigo Garro's set-piece delivery, with Hugo Souza in goal, but the squad is thin and ageing. See [low block](/tactics/low-block) for the defensive framework the club has traditionally mastered.

## Rivalries

The Derby Paulista with [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) is the fiercest in São Paulo, the workers' club against the Italians' club, and the Majestoso with [São Paulo](/clubs/sao-paulo), the elite club, is almost as intense. The Clássico Alvinegro with [Santos](/clubs/santos), two black-and-white clubs from the same state, has included some of the best matches in Paulista history. Nationally, Corinthians versus [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) is the "clash of the two biggest fan bases".

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Corinthians are in a relegation fight, 17th in the Brasileirão with four rounds to play and no confirmed head coach in the current squad data. Memphis, Yuri Alberto, Hugo Souza, Garro and Carrillo are the players who must keep the club up; the 2025 Copa do Brasil win gives a Libertadores place regardless, which would be a strange prize for a relegated club. The financial restructuring that began in 2025 continues, and the Fiel, 40,000 strong at every home game, are as loud as ever.
