## History

Liverpool exist because of an argument about rent. In 1892 [Everton](/clubs/everton) left Anfield after a dispute with the landowner John Houlding, who responded by founding a new club to play in the empty ground. Liverpool were in the First Division by 1894 and champions by 1901, with further titles in 1906, 1922 and 1923. Then came a long, ordinary middle: a title in 1947, relegation in 1954, and eight seasons in the Second Division.

Bill Shankly arrived in December 1959 and built everything the modern club rests on. He won promotion in 1962, the league in 1964 and 1966 and the FA Cup in 1965, created the Boot Room culture of coaches who learned on the job, and told the supporters they were the twelfth man. His successor Bob Paisley, a Boot Room graduate, was even more successful: six titles, three European Cups (1977, 1978, 1981) and the UEFA Cup in nine years, with [Kevin Keegan](/players/kevin-keegan), then [Kenny Dalglish](/players/kenny-dalglish), Graeme Souness, Alan Hansen and [Ian Rush](/players/ian-rush) as the spine. Joe Fagan won a treble in 1984, including the European Cup in Rome, and Dalglish as player-manager won the Double in 1986 and the title again in 1988 and 1990 with John Barnes and Peter Beardsley.

The Heysel disaster of 1985, in which 39 Juventus supporters died before the European Cup final, led to a ban on English clubs in Europe. The Hillsborough disaster of 15 April 1989, in which 97 Liverpool supporters were unlawfully killed at an FA Cup semi-final, changed the club and the city for ever; the campaign for justice lasted until 2016. Dalglish resigned in 1991. The 1990 title was the last for 30 years.

The Premier League era brought cups rather than championships: Gérard Houllier's treble of League Cup, FA Cup and UEFA Cup in 2001, and Rafael Benítez's Champions League in 2005, when Liverpool came from 3-0 down against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) in Istanbul to win on penalties. [Steven Gerrard](/players/steven-gerrard) was the captain and symbol of those years. Ownership chaos under Tom Hicks and George Gillett nearly bankrupted the club in 2010; Fenway Sports Group bought it from the brink.

[Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), appointed in October 2015, turned doubters into believers. With [Mohamed Salah](/players/mohamed-salah), [Sadio Mané](/players/sadio-mane), Roberto Firmino, [Virgil van Dijk](/players/virgil-van-dijk) and [Alisson Becker](/players/alisson-becker), Liverpool reached three Champions League finals in five years, won the sixth European Cup in Madrid in 2019, and in 2019-20 won the league with 99 points, the first title in three decades. Klopp left in 2024 and [Arne Slot](/managers/arne-slot) won the title in his first season, 2024-25, the club's 20th, level with Manchester United. A year later, after a fifth-place finish, Slot was sacked and [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola) took over.

## Trajectory

Liverpool's position for a century has been near the top with occasional unexplained dips. Between 1973 and 1990 they finished first or second in 17 of 18 seasons. From 1991 to 2019 they were top-four regulars but champions never: second in 2002, 2009, 2014 (the Gerrard slip against Chelsea) and 2019 (97 points, one behind City).

The Klopp years changed the ceiling. From 2017-18 to 2021-22 Liverpool finished fourth, second, first, third and second, won the Champions League, the Club World Cup, the FA Cup and two League Cups, and in 2021-22 came within a goal of a quadruple. The 2022-23 slump to fifth was followed by third in Klopp's farewell season and then, under Slot, the 2024-25 title by ten points.

The 2025-26 title defence stalled. [Alexander Isak](/players/alexander-isak), signed from Newcastle for a British-record £125m on deadline day after a summer of standing off, arrived short of fitness; [Florian Wirtz](/players/florian-wirtz), the £116m signing from [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen), took time to settle; and Liverpool finished fifth on 60 points, 25 behind [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal). Slot was dismissed on 30 May 2026. Note that some club profiles on this site refer to a third-place finish; the final table lists Liverpool fifth, behind Arsenal, Manchester City, Manchester United and Aston Villa.

In Europe, Liverpool's six European Cups are more than any other English club, and their 2019-20 and 2024-25 league wins are the only two of the Premier League era.

## Players the club produced

The academy at Kirkby has produced the club's two most important captains of the modern era. [Steven Gerrard](/players/steven-gerrard) played 710 games, scored in the finals of the FA Cup, League Cup, UEFA Cup and Champions League, and was the complete midfielder of his generation. Trent Alexander-Arnold, from West Derby, reinvented the right-back as a playmaker before leaving for [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) in 2025. Before them, Robbie Fowler, Steve McManaman, Michael Owen (the 2001 Ballon d'Or winner) and Jamie Carragher all came through. Curtis Jones, Conor Bradley and Rio Ngumoha are the current academy representatives in the first team.

The signings who became legends are the heart of the club's folklore. [Kevin Keegan](/players/kevin-keegan) from Scunthorpe for £33,000 in 1971; [Kenny Dalglish](/players/kenny-dalglish) from [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) to replace him in 1977, the greatest player in the club's history; [Ian Rush](/players/ian-rush) from Chester, the record scorer with 346 goals; Graeme Souness, John Barnes, Luis Suárez, Fernando Torres, Xabi Alonso. [Mohamed Salah](/players/mohamed-salah), signed from [Roma](/clubs/roma) for £36.9m in 2017, is the Premier League's most prolific overseas scorer and the club's leading scorer of the modern era. [Virgil van Dijk](/players/virgil-van-dijk) and [Alisson Becker](/players/alisson-becker) were the two signings that turned Klopp's team into champions.

## Identity and playing style

[Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola), appointed in June 2026 after three seasons at [AFC Bournemouth](/clubs/bournemouth), has brought Anfield back to something close to the Klopp template. His teams press high, defend man-to-man rather than zonally, and look to attack within seconds of winning the ball: a [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) philosophy with a Spanish accent, built on a 4-2-3-1 in which the full-backs push very high and the double pivot covers enormous ground.

In 2026-27 that means [Ryan Gravenberch](/players/ryan-gravenberch) and [Dominik Szoboszlai](/players/dominik-szoboszlai) as the pivot, [Florian Wirtz](/players/florian-wirtz) as the number ten, [Bradley Barcola](/players/bradley-barcola), signed from [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) for around €125m, running the left, [Mohamed Salah](/players/mohamed-salah) on the right and [Alexander Isak](/players/alexander-isak) as the reference point. Ronald Araújo arrived from Barcelona to add aggression alongside Van Dijk, and Jérémy Jacquet came from Rennes as a long-term centre-back.

The difference from Slot's more patient possession side is visible in the numbers: more sprints, more turnovers in the final third, a higher line, and more goals conceded on the break while the squad adapts. Iraola's Bournemouth were the most intense pressing side in the league; the question at Liverpool is whether a squad built for control can be re-wired for chaos.

## Rivalries

[Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) are the great rival, two cities 35 miles apart that competed for the Industrial Revolution's trade and then for English football's crown. United's 20 titles were matched by Liverpool's 20th in 2025. The fixtures that define it include the 1977 FA Cup final, the 1996 final decided by Eric Cantona, and Liverpool's 7-0 at Anfield in March 2023.

The Merseyside derby with [Everton](/clubs/everton) is the oldest continuous top-flight derby in England, the friendly derby of the 1980s when families split across both sides shared Wembley, and the fixture with the most red cards in Premier League history. Everton's move to the Hill Dickinson Stadium in 2025 ended the Goodison chapter. [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) became the modern rival through the Klopp-Guardiola title races of 2019 and 2022, with 97 and 92 points not enough to win the league. The 2026-27 version began badly: City won 3-0 at Anfield on 11 October.

## Current situation

Liverpool are sixth in early November 2026, with four wins, three draws and two defeats from nine matches and 15 points, nine behind the leaders Arsenal. The autumn has been uneven. They beat Brentford 2-1 away on 17 October and Brighton 2-0 at home on 25 October, but lost 3-0 at home to Manchester City on 11 October and 1-0 to Arsenal on 1 November. Wirtz began the season without a goal in his first six league appearances and the attack has relied on Isak and Salah.

The summer rebuild was significant: Iraola replacing Slot, Barcola from PSG, Araújo and Jacquet in defence, Víctor Muñoz and Federico Chiesa in the wide rotation, and the sale of several Slot-era squad players. Iraola said in August that the club was not finished recruiting, and the January window is expected to bring a defensive midfielder.

The ambition for 2026-27 is realistic rather than romantic: a return to the Champions League places, a cup run, and establishing the pressing identity. Salah is 34 and Van Dijk 35, and how long the two pillars of the Klopp and Slot sides can keep playing at this level is the question hanging over Iraola's first season.
