## History

Botafogo Football Club was founded in 1904 in the bayside neighbourhood of the same name and merged in 1942 with the rowing club Botafogo de Regatas, from which it inherited the lone-star badge. The club's identity is tied to the Brazil team of the late 1950s and 1960s: the 1958 and 1962 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) winners were built around Botafogo's spine of [Garrincha](/players/garrincha), Nílton Santos, Didi, Zagallo and Amarildo, and the 1970 team around [Jairzinho](/players/jairzinho), Gérson and Paulo César Caju. Garrincha, the bow-legged right winger from Pau Grande, is the club's eternal symbol; the stadium's nickname, "the Garrincha", honours him, and Nílton Santos, the "Encyclopaedia of Football" who invented the attacking full-back, gave the ground its official name.

Botafogo won the Carioca championship repeatedly in that era and the Rio-São Paulo tournaments of 1962 and 1964, but the national titles were harder to come by: the Taça Brasil of 1968 and then nothing until 1995, when Túlio Maravilha's goals won the Brasileirão in a two-legged final against Santos. The club spent most of the 1990s and 2000s in decline, relegated in 2002, promoted in 2003, with Clarence Seedorf's 2012-14 spell and the Uruguayan striker Loco Abreu's goals among the few highlights, and fell into Série B twice more, in 2014 and 2020.

## Trajectory

The 2020 relegation, with a chaotic season of 20 wins in a calendar year of mismanagement, led to the decision that transformed the club. In 2022 the American businessman John Textor's Eagle Football group bought the football department under Brazil's new SAF company law, making Botafogo a sister club of [Lyon](/clubs/lyon) and Crystal Palace, and the investment was immediate: Tiquinho Soares, Marçal, Eduardo and, in 2023, a 13-point lead at the top of the Brasileirão under Luís Castro that was thrown away after Castro left for Al Nassr, one of the most notorious collapses in Brazilian football history.

Artur Jorge, the Portuguese coach, was appointed for 2024 and delivered the greatest season in club history. Botafogo signed Thiago Almada from Atlanta United, Luiz Henrique from Betis, Igor Jesus, Alex Telles and Gregore, won the Copa Libertadores on 30 November 2024, beating Atlético Mineiro 3-1 in Buenos Aires despite Gregore's red card in the first minute, and won the Brasileirão a week later. A club that had never won either now had both, and qualified for the 2025 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup), where it beat the European champions [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) 1-0 in Pasadena through Igor Jesus before losing to Palmeiras in the round of 16.

Artur Jorge left for Qatar in early 2025 and the club went through Renato Paiva and then [Davide Ancelotti](/managers/davide-ancelotti), Carlo's son, who took his first head-coaching job in July 2025. The club's current squad data lists Tite, the former Brazil and Flamengo coach, as head coach as of November 2026; Botafogo are 11th in the 2026 Brasileirão with 42 points from 34 games, and Textor's financial tangle with Lyon has been the year's constant background noise.

## Players the club produced

The club's Marechal Hermes academy produced Garrincha's generation and, more recently, Matheus Nascimento, the goalkeeper Gatito Fernández's understudies, and the striker Igor Jesus, who joined as a free agent from Shabab Al Ahli in 2024 and was sold to Nottingham Forest in 2025. The historic legends were home-grown or arrived young: Heleno de Freitas, the troubled genius of the 1940s, Quarentinha, Nílton Santos, Didi, Gérson, Jairzinho and Zagallo. The modern era's great signings were Túlio, Abreu, Seedorf, Almada, Luiz Henrique (sold to Zenit in 2025) and the Venezuelan Jefferson Savarino.

## Identity and playing style

The Lone Star stands for elegance and bad luck in equal measure, the club of artists that always seems to lose something at the last moment, a reputation the 2023 collapse confirmed and the 2024 double finally contradicted. Artur Jorge's champions were a disciplined 4-3-3 that pressed high, defended its box ferociously and attacked with pace through Luiz Henrique and Savarino, with Almada as the creator; it was the most "European" title-winning side the league had seen, built on [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) principles and set-piece strength. Tite, by contrast, prefers a controlled 4-2-3-1 with patient possession, the approach he used with [Brazil](/nations/brazil) and Corinthians, and the 2026 team has been solid rather than spectacular.

## Rivalries

The Clássico da Rivalidade with [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) is the big one in the Textor era, sharpened by the 2023-24 title races and public feuding between the owner and Flamengo's directors. The Clássico Vovô with [Fluminense](/clubs/fluminense), the oldest derby in Rio, and the games against Vasco da Gama complete the Rio set, with the Maracanã or the Nilton Santos hosting all of them.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Botafogo are 11th in the Brasileirão, out of the title race and fighting for a Sudamericana place, with Tite listed as head coach and Savarino, Alex Telles, Gregore and the goalkeeper John as the senior figures. The 2024 double guaranteed the club's place in the 2025 Club World Cup and a lasting place in Brazilian history, but the squad has been sold down since and Textor's Eagle Football is restructuring. The lone star, as ever, is both glorious and precarious.
