## History

New York City FC was announced in May 2013 as a joint venture between the City Football Group, owners of [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city), and the New York Yankees, who hold a minority stake and whose stadium in the Bronx has been the club's awkward home ever since. The first season in 2015 was a celebrity exercise: David Villa as the first signing, then [Frank Lampard](/players/frank-lampard-player) and Andrea Pirlo, with Jason Kreis as coach. Villa was magnificent for four years, scoring 80 league goals and winning the 2016 MVP, while the Lampard and Pirlo signings were more about the brand than the standings, and the baseball-field pitch dimensions made the club a punchline.

Patrick Vieira replaced Kreis in 2016 and gave the club its footballing identity, a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) model copied from the parent club, and NYCFC finished second in the East in 2016 and 2017. Domènec Torrent, Pep Guardiola's former assistant, took the club to the top of the conference in 2019.

## Trajectory

The breakthrough came under Ronny Deila in 2021. With Valentín "Taty" Castellanos winning the Golden Boot and Maxi Moralez pulling the strings, NYCFC beat Portland on penalties at Providence Park in the MLS Cup final on 11 December 2021, the first major trophy in the club's history, and followed it with the 2022 Campeones Cup against Atlas. Castellanos was sold to Girona and then [Lazio](/clubs/lazio), Deila left for Standard Liège, and a 2023 season without either ended outside the play-offs.

Pascal Jansen, the Dutch coach who had taken AZ Alkmaar into Europe, was appointed in January 2025 and made an immediate impact: a fifth-place finish and a play-off run that beat Charlotte and Philadelphia before a 5-1 Eastern Conference final defeat at [Inter Miami](/clubs/inter-miami). The 2026 regular season was a disappointment, 11th in the East with 39 points, outside the play-offs. Jansen is listed as head coach in the club's current squad data as of November 2026.

Off the pitch, the long-awaited stadium is finally real. Etihad Park, a 25,000-seat privately financed ground at Willets Point in Queens next to the Mets' Citi Field, broke ground in late 2024 and is scheduled to open for the 2027 season, ending more than a decade of Yankee Stadium baseball diamonds.

## Players the club produced

NYCFC's academy is the most productive in the Northeast. James Sands, the first homegrown signing, went to [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) on loan and captained the club; Joe Scally was sold to Borussia Mönchengladbach as a 17-year-old and became a Germany-based United States international; Tayvon Gray, Justin Haak and Jonathan Shore followed. The City Football Group network also brought Castellanos (from Montevideo City Torque), Santiago Rodríguez and Talles Magno through, and the club has been a seller as much as a buyer.

## Identity and playing style

From Vieira onwards the club has played the City Football Group's 4-3-3 possession game, with a deep-lying playmaker (Alexander Ring, then Keaton Parks), wide forwards who tuck in and full-backs who provide width. Jansen's version is more vertical, pressing higher and attacking quickly through Alonso Martínez, the Costa Rica striker who became the main scorer in 2025, and Hannes Wolf. The constraint has always been the pitch: Yankee Stadium's narrow dimensions reward directness, and the club has been better on the road than it should be.

## Rivalries

The Hudson River Derby with the New York Red Bulls is the defining fixture, played in front of hostile crowds at both venues since 2015 and settled more often than not in the Red Bulls' favour early on, with NYCFC redressing the balance in the 2020s. The 2025 conference final created a rivalry with [Inter Miami](/clubs/inter-miami), and Philadelphia Union have been a regular play-off opponent.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 NYCFC have missed the play-offs under Jansen after an 11th-place finish, with Martínez, Moralez, in his late thirties and still the creative heart, and goalkeeper Matt Freese as the key names. The real event is the move to Queens in 2027: the club expects the new stadium to transform both its attendances and its ability to attract players, and the summer of 2026, with World Cup matches at MetLife across the river, was the warm-up.
