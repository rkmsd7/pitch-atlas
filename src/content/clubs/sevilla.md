## History

Sevilla Fútbol Club date their founding to 25 January 1890, which makes them the oldest club in Spain devoted solely to football, formed by a mix of Scottish and British workers and young Sevillians. They won their first Copa del Rey in 1935, reached the top flight in 1934 and, with the forward line of Campanal, Pepillo and Juan Arza, won their only league title in 1945-46, a single point ahead of Barcelona. Two more Copas (1939, 1948) and the move to the Ramón Sánchez-Pizjuán in 1958 fixed the club in the Spanish elite for a generation, before a slow decline through the 1970s and 1980s brought relegations in 1968 and 1973.

The modern club was shaped by chaos and then by Monchi. In 1992 Sevilla briefly had Diego Maradona under Carlos Bilardo; in 1995 they were administratively relegated and reinstated after fan protests; in 1997 and 2000 they went down for real. The return in 2001 under Joaquín Caparrós, with a youth-first policy that produced [José Antonio Reyes](/players/jose-antonio-reyes), [Sergio Ramos](/players/sergio-ramos) and Jesús Navas, and the sporting direction of Ramón Rodríguez "Monchi" from 2000, built the most successful period in the club's history.

Juande Ramos won the UEFA Cup in 2006 (4-0 against Middlesbrough) and 2007 (penalties against Espanyol), the 2006 UEFA Super Cup against Barcelona, the 2007 Copa del Rey and Supercopa, with a team of [Dani Alves](/players/dani-alves), Freddie Kanouté, Luís Fabiano, Adriano and the goalkeeper Andrés Palop. The death of Antonio Puerta in August 2007, at 22, after collapsing on the pitch, remains the club's deepest wound. Unai Emery then won three consecutive Europa Leagues (2014, 2015, 2016), Julen Lopetegui a sixth in Cologne in 2020, and José Luis Mendilibar a seventh in Budapest in 2023, beating Roma on penalties.

## Trajectory

Sevilla were in Segunda as recently as 2000-01, won it, and within six years were the best cup side in Europe. From 2005-06 to 2021-22 they finished in the top seven in every season but two, including four consecutive fourth-place finishes from 2019-20 to 2021-22 under Lopetegui, who was sacked in October 2022.

The decline since has been steep. The 2023 Europa League triumph came in a season of 12th place, Monchi left for Aston Villa, and the club's debt, salary-limit problems and a revolving door of coaches (Mendilibar, Diego Alonso, Quique Sánchez Flores, García Pimienta, Caparrós as caretaker, Matías Almeyda) produced 14th in 2023-24 and 17th in 2024-25, the lowest finish in more than two decades. Almeyda was sacked in March 2026 with the team 15th, and [Luis García Plaza](/managers/luis-garcia-plaza), a survival specialist, was hired on 24 March 2026; he kept Sevilla up in 13th on 43 points, three clear of the drop, and was given a contract to June 2027.

## Players the club produced

The academy's modern graduates are among the best in Spain: Ramos, Reyes, Navas (who returned in 2017 and became the club's all-time appearance leader, retiring in 2024 as a European champion with [Spain](/nations/spain)), Alberto Moreno, Carlos Marchena, Diego Capel and Bryan Gil. Juan Arza was the club's greatest home-grown striker of the 1940s and 1950s.

Monchi's signings were the other engine: Alves, Kanouté, Júlio Baptista, Adriano, Ivan Rakitić (captain of the 2014 winners, later back from Barcelona), Carlos Bacca, Kevin Gameiro, Éver Banega, Clément Lenglet, Jules Koundé, Diego Carlos and Lucas Ocampos were all bought cheaply and sold for profit while winning trophies.

## Identity and playing style

Sevilla's tradition is one of intensity and wide attacking: fast full-backs, strong wingers, a big striker, the Sánchez-Pizjuán's "Himno del Centenario" at kick-off. Ramos' sides pressed and counter-attacked; Emery's were pragmatic and brilliant in knockout football; Lopetegui's were a disciplined [low block](/tactics/low-block) with Koundé, Diego Carlos and Bono conceding fewer than a goal a game. Mendilibar's 2023 run was pure [direct play](/tactics/direct-play).

García Plaza's Sevilla are organised and economical: a 4-4-2 or 4-2-3-1 built around Youssouf Fofana and Lucien Agoumé in midfield, Rubén Vargas and Chidera Ejuke wide, and a striker rotation of Isaac Romero, the Scottish signing Robbie Ure and the young Belgian Lucas Stassin. The summer saw 11 arrivals, 10 departures and seven contract terminations on a tight salary limit, with Jon Guridi and Juan Iglesias among the free transfers. It is a team designed to be hard to beat rather than to entertain.

The seven Europa League finals are worth listing because no other club has anything like them: 2006, 4-0 against Middlesbrough in Eindhoven; 2007, 2-2 and penalties against Espanyol in Glasgow; 2014, 0-0 and penalties against Benfica in Turin; 2015, 3-2 against Dnipro in Warsaw; 2016, 3-1 against Liverpool in Basel after trailing at half-time; 2020, 3-2 against Inter in Cologne with a Diego Carlos overhead kick; 2023, 1-1 and penalties against Roma in Budapest. Sevilla have never lost a final in the competition.

## Rivalries

El Gran Derbi against [Real Betis](/clubs/real-betis) is the fiercest city rivalry in Spain, a divide of class and neighbourhood (Nervión against Heliópolis) that goes back to Betis splitting from Sevilla in 1907. The 22-0 of 1918, the 2007 Juande Ramos bottle incident and the 2022 Copa derby abandoned after a bar was thrown at Joan Jordán are the infamous chapters; the next edition is on 21 November 2026. [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) have been the Europa League and Copa rival of the 2010s, and the 2006-07 season produced genuine tension with [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid).

## Current situation

Sevilla are sixth in La Liga on 7 November 2026 with 18 points from 12 games (five wins, three draws, four defeats), a much better start than the previous two seasons promised. García Plaza's first full season began with a 3-1 win at Athletic on the opening weekend, and the team has stayed in the top half since. The club's financial situation remains the backdrop: the salary limit is among the tightest of any historic side in Spain, the interim sporting leadership under José Ignacio Navarro sold Akor Adams to Venezia to fund signings, and the board has been under pressure from supporters for several seasons.

For now the targets are modest and realistic: mid-table security first, then a push for the Europa League places that would restore the club to the competition it owns. The derby on 21 November is the match the city is waiting for.
