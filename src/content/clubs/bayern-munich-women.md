## History

Bayern's women's section was founded in 1970 and won a German championship in 1976, before the Bundesliga existed, but spent decades as a secondary concern. The modern rise began under Thomas Wörle, who delivered back-to-back Bundesliga titles in 2015 and 2016 with [Vivianne Miedema](/players/vivianne-miedema), then a teenager signed from Heerenveen, leading the attack. Four second places followed as [Wolfsburg](/clubs/wolfsburg-women) dominated, with Champions League semi-finals in 2019 and 2021.

Jens Scheuer's 2021 title, decided in a late-season head-to-head with Wolfsburg, was a one-off until Alexander Straus arrived from Norway in 2022. His teams won the league in 2023, 2024 and 2025, adding a first DFB-Pokal in 2025 to end Wolfsburg's nine-year hold on the cup. José Barcala, the Spanish coach promoted for 2025-26, delivered a fourth straight title with 74 points, clinched on 22 April 2026 at Union Berlin, a second DFB-Pokal, and the club's deepest European run: a Champions League semi-final lost 4-2 to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona-women) in May 2026, with [Alexia Putellas](/players/alexia-putellas) scoring twice.

## Trajectory

Second in five seasons between 2017 and 2022, first in five of the last six. Bayern now operate from the FC Bayern Campus, with the 15,000-seat Sportpark Unterhaching and occasionally the Allianz Arena, where the club set a German women's attendance record, for big games. The recruitment of Germany's best young players, Lena Oberdorf from Wolfsburg in 2024 and [Giulia Gwinn](/players/giulia-gwinn) from Freiburg before her, mirrors the men's team's strategy.

## Players the club produced

Sydney Lohmann, Lina Magull, Melanie Behringer and Klara Bühl, signed young from Freiburg, became Germany internationals at Bayern. Miedema scored 35 league goals in three seasons before leaving for Arsenal. The current squad is a who's who of northern European football: Gwinn (captain of Germany), Oberdorf, Bühl, [Pernille Harder](/players/pernille-harder), Magdalena Eriksson, Glódís Perla Viggósdóttir (club captain), Sarah Zadrazil and Vanessa Gilles. Lea Schüller, the club's long-time striker, left for Manchester United in summer 2026.

## Identity and playing style

Barcala plays a 4-2-3-1 with [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) principles: Oberdorf and Zadrazil as the double pivot, Harder as the creator, Bühl attacking from the left and Gwinn overlapping on the right. The press is aggressive and the back line, with Viggósdóttir and Eriksson, holds a [high line](/tactics/high-line). It is a more technical version of the German tradition and the semi-final against Barcelona showed both its ceiling and the gap that remains.

## Rivalries

Wolfsburg is the defining rivalry of the 2010s and 2020s, the fixture that has decided most German titles since 2013. Eintracht Frankfurt are the historic power of the league. In Europe, Barcelona have eliminated Bayern in two semi-finals.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Bayern lead the Bundesliga again under Barcala and are in the Champions League league phase. With Gwinn, Oberdorf, Bühl and Harder, the stated target is a first European final; the question is whether the club can hold on to its stars as English and Spanish clubs come calling.
