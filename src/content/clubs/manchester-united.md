## History

Manchester United were formed in 1878 as Newton Heath LYR, the works team of the Lancashire and Yorkshire Railway's carriage department. Bankrupt by 1902, the club was rescued by a local brewer, John Henry Davies, and renamed Manchester United. Under Ernest Mangnall they won the league in 1908 and 1911 and the FA Cup in 1909, and in 1910 moved into Old Trafford, built for Davies on the edge of the Trafford Park industrial estate.

The club's modern identity was created by Matt Busby, appointed in 1945. Busby insisted on control of team affairs, built a side from the youth team and won the title in 1952, 1956 and 1957 with the Busby Babes, a team whose average age was barely 22 and whose best player, Duncan Edwards, was regarded as the finest English footballer of his generation. On 6 February 1958, returning from a European Cup tie in Belgrade, the team's plane crashed on take-off at Munich. Twenty-three people died, including eight players. Edwards died 15 days later. Busby, critically injured, rebuilt: [Bobby Charlton](/players/bobby-charlton), a Munich survivor, [Denis Law](/players/denis-law) and [George Best](/players/george-best) won the league in 1965 and 1967, and on 29 May 1968 United beat [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) 4-1 at Wembley to become the first English club to win the European Cup, ten years after the disaster.

The following two decades were a long hangover, including relegation in 1974 under Tommy Docherty. Alex Ferguson arrived from Aberdeen in November 1986, nearly lost his job in 1990 before Mark Robins's FA Cup goal at Nottingham Forest, and then won everything. The signing of [Eric Cantona](/players/eric-cantona) from Leeds in November 1992 unlocked the first title in 26 years; the Class of '92, [Ryan Giggs](/players/ryan-giggs), [Paul Scholes](/players/paul-scholes), [David Beckham](/players/david-beckham), Gary Neville, Phil Neville and Nicky Butt, gave the club its soul; and the treble of 1999, completed by Teddy Sheringham and Ole Gunnar Solskjær's injury-time goals against [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) in Barcelona, was the summit. [Roy Keane](/players/roy-keane), [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) and [Wayne Rooney](/players/wayne-rooney) carried the second great Ferguson side to the 2008 Champions League in Moscow and three titles in a row. Ferguson retired in 2013 with 13 Premier Leagues, five FA Cups and two Champions Leagues.

Since then United have had nine permanent managers and no league title. David Moyes, Louis van Gaal (FA Cup 2016), José Mourinho (Europa League and League Cup 2017), Ole Gunnar Solskjær, Ralf Rangnick, Erik ten Hag (League Cup 2023, FA Cup 2024), and Ruben Amorim, whose 3-4-3 produced a 15th-place finish in 2024-25, the club's worst since 1974. Sir Jim Ratcliffe's INEOS took control of football operations in 2024. Amorim was sacked in January 2026 and [Michael Carrick](/managers/michael-carrick), the former midfielder, was appointed head coach on 13 January.

## Trajectory

For the 26 years of Ferguson, United's trajectory was a flat line at the top: never lower than third from 1991-92 to 2012-13, champions 13 times. The post-Ferguson decade is a jagged line: seventh in Moyes's only season, second under Mourinho in 2018 and Solskjær in 2021, third in Ten Hag's first season, eighth in his second, and the collapse to 15th in 2024-25, when the only consolation was reaching the Europa League final in Bilbao and losing it to [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur).

Carrick's arrival in January 2026 produced the sharpest turn in years. United won 11 of their last 17 league matches, climbed from mid-table to third on 71 points, and returned to the Champions League for the first time since 2023-24. The summer of 2025 recruitment, Matheus Cunha from Wolves, Bryan Mbeumo from Brentford, Benjamin Sesko from RB Leipzig and goalkeeper Senne Lammens, belatedly looked coherent once Carrick switched to a back four.

Old Trafford itself is the next chapter: the club has announced plans for a new 100,000-seat stadium beside the current ground, with a timetable that should be checked against current reporting.

## Players the club produced

No English club has produced more. The Busby Babes came almost entirely from the youth system; Duncan Edwards, Eddie Colman and [Bobby Charlton](/players/bobby-charlton) were products of it, and Charlton went on to 249 goals and the 1966 World Cup. [George Best](/players/george-best), spotted in Belfast at 15, became the first footballer treated like a pop star. The Class of '92 won the FA Youth Cup and then everything else: [Ryan Giggs](/players/ryan-giggs) holds the club appearance record with 963, [Paul Scholes](/players/paul-scholes) was the midfielder Xavi and Zidane said they admired most, [David Beckham](/players/david-beckham) became the most famous player in the world. Marcus Rashford, Kobbie Mainoo and Alejandro Garnacho are recent graduates; Rashford remains at the club in 2026-27 and Mainoo, 21, is a regular in Carrick's midfield.

The signings are just as storied. [Denis Law](/players/denis-law), the King of the Stretford End; Bryan Robson, Captain Marvel; [Eric Cantona](/players/eric-cantona), the catalyst; Peter Schmeichel, [Roy Keane](/players/roy-keane), Ruud van Nistelrooy, Rio Ferdinand, [Nemanja Vidić](/players/nemanja-vidic), [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo), who arrived at 18 and left as a Ballon d'Or winner, and [Wayne Rooney](/players/wayne-rooney), the club's record scorer with 253 goals until Ronaldo's second spell. [Bruno Fernandes](/players/bruno-fernandes), signed from [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) in January 2020, is the captain and the most productive United player of the post-Ferguson years.

## Identity and playing style

[Michael Carrick](/managers/michael-carrick) was the most elegant passer of Ferguson's last great side and his coaching reflects that: a 4-2-3-1 or 4-3-3 built on control of the ball through midfield, a patient build-up that invites pressure before playing through it, and full-backs who overlap rather than invert. He had impressed at Middlesbrough, taking a mid-table Championship side to the play-offs, and at United he simplified Amorim's system by returning to a back four and giving [Bruno Fernandes](/players/bruno-fernandes) a free role from the right side of midfield.

The 2026 summer was about the midfield. Carlos Baleba arrived from [Brighton & Hove Albion](/clubs/brighton) for a fee reported between £65m and £70m but was injured on arrival and did not feature until the autumn; [Andrey Santos](/players/andrey-santos) was signed from Chelsea for £48m as a box-to-box option; Youri Tielemans came from Aston Villa on a free transfer and has been the most consistent of the three. With Manuel Ugarte and Mainoo, United now have a midfield of five senior options for two or three places.

In attack, Carrick prefers Cunha as a second striker drifting off the left, Mbeumo on the right and Sesko through the middle, with Rashford and Amad Diallo as alternatives. The press is moderate, not a [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) team, and the defensive block drops into a 4-4-2 that has been vulnerable on the counter in 2026-27. The aim is a side closer to the 2008 template of control and transition than to the chaos of the Amorim year.

## Rivalries

[Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) are the historic rival: two industrial cities, the two most successful clubs in England, level at 20 titles each since 2025. [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) are the local rival and, since 2008, the dominant one; the derby went 1-1 at Old Trafford in September 2026 and City's 6-1 of 2011 is still the result United supporters would most like to forget. [Leeds United](/clubs/leeds-united) are the Roses rivalry, revived by Leeds's return to the Premier League in 2025; the sides drew 1-1 at Elland Road on 18 October 2026. [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) were the rivalry of the Wenger-Ferguson years, from the 1999 FA Cup semi-final to the Battle of the Buffet in 2004.

## Current situation

The 2026-27 season has started badly. In early November United are 17th, with one win, four draws and four defeats from nine league matches, 10 goals scored and 15 conceded. October brought a 1-1 draw at Leeds, a 3-0 home defeat to Bournemouth on 25 October and a 2-0 loss at Chelsea on 31 October. The Champions League group stage has gone better, with Santos and Baleba deployed together in Europe, but the league form has put Carrick, who was hailed as the club's saviour in May, under pressure within four months.

The squad is deep: Lammens in goal, Leny Yoro and Matthijs de Ligt as the centre-back pairing, [Bruno Fernandes](/players/bruno-fernandes), Mainoo, Baleba, Santos, Tielemans and Mount in midfield, Cunha, Mbeumo, Sesko, Rashford and Amad in attack. The problem has been structure rather than talent: slow starts, goals conceded in transition, and a reliance on Fernandes that opponents have learned to exploit. Reports in October described a "stuttering start" and a midfield still finding its connections.

The ambition remains a return to the top four and a run in the Champions League. The stadium project, the INEOS restructuring and the question of whether Carrick is a long-term head coach or a successful caretaker promoted too soon will define the rest of the season.
