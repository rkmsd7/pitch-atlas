## History

Aberdeen were formed in 1903 from the merger of three city clubs and spent their first half-century as a solid but largely unsuccessful side, winning a first Scottish Cup in 1947 and a first title in 1955 under Dave Halliday. Eddie Turnbull's 1970 Scottish Cup, won against Celtic, and Ally MacLeod's 1976 League Cup kept the trophy cabinet ticking over, but nothing prepared Pittodrie for what came next.

[Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) arrived in 1978 and built the greatest provincial side in Scottish history. With Willie Miller and Alex McLeish at the back, Jim Leighton in goal, Gordon Strachan on the right and Mark McGhee and John Hewitt up front, Aberdeen won the title in 1980, the first by a club outside the Old Firm since 1965, and again in 1984 and 1985. They won the Scottish Cup four times in a row from 1982 to 1986. And on 11 May 1983 in Gothenburg, in pouring rain, they beat Real Madrid 2-1 after extra time in the Cup Winners' Cup final, Hewitt heading the winner, before beating Hamburg over two legs for the UEFA Super Cup. Ferguson left for Manchester United in 1986; the "New Firm" era, in which Aberdeen and Dundee United had broken the Glasgow duopoly, ended with him.

The years since have been a long attempt to live up to that. Alex Smith won both cups in 1990, with the Scottish Cup final decided on penalties against Celtic; Willie Miller's teams finished second in 1991 and 1993; and then the club declined badly, finishing bottom in 2000 and surviving only because the league was expanding. Jimmy Calderwood's side reached the UEFA Cup last 32 in 2008, beating Copenhagen and drawing with Bayern Munich. [Derek McInnes](/managers/derek-mcinnes), appointed in 2013, won the 2014 League Cup, the club's first trophy in 19 years, and finished second four times in a row from 2015 to 2018 behind Celtic. Jimmy Thelin's team won the 2025 Scottish Cup on penalties against Celtic, ending a 35-year wait for the trophy and sending the Dons into the Europa League.

## Trajectory

Aberdeen's modern league record swings between the McInnes years, when they were consistently the best of the rest, and periods of mediocrity on either side: eleventh in 2004, tenth in 2010, tenth in 2022. Third-place finishes in 2007, 2014 and 2023 and the four second places of 2015 to 2018 were the highs. Europe has brought a UEFA Cup last 32 in 2008, Europa League group football in 2023-24 under Barry Robson and 2025-26 after the cup win, and the 2026-27 season has begun poorly.

## Players the club produced

Aberdeen's youth system produced most of the Gothenburg side: Willie Miller, Alex McLeish, Neale Cooper, Neil Simpson, John Hewitt and Eric Black. Later graduates include Eoin Jess, Stephen Glass, Scott McKenna (sold to Nottingham Forest), Ryan Jack, Lewis Ferguson (sold to Bologna, now Serie A's most reliable midfielder), Calvin Ramsay (to Liverpool), Dean Campbell and Connor Barron. Gordon Strachan, Jim Leighton and Mark McGhee were signings who became club legends, and Duncan Shearer, Eoin Jess and Scott Booth defined the 1990s.

## Identity and playing style

Aberdeen's identity is Ferguson's inheritance: a club that expects to compete with the Old Firm rather than merely survive them, and a crowd at Pittodrie that remembers Gothenburg. Recent teams have played an energetic 4-2-3-1 pressing side under Thelin, with [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) and strong [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) the main weapons against Glasgow. As of November 2026 the club has changed coach during the season and the current head coach should be checked against the club's latest announcements.

## Rivalries

The rivalry with [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) is the most bitter, forged in the 1980s and inflamed by a series of violent matches and by Neil Simpson's 1988 tackle on Ian Durrant. [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) have been Aberdeen's most frequent cup-final opponents, and the New Firm derby against Dundee United recalls the era when the two clubs were Scotland's best.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Aberdeen are tenth in the Scottish Premiership after eleven rounds with eleven points, a troubling start that has put the club closer to the relegation play-off place than to Europe. The 2025 cup win raised expectations and the Europa League campaign that followed stretched a thin squad; the immediate task is to climb the table, with the January window likely to be decisive.
