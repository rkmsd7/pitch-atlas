## History

RB Leipzig are the newest major club in Germany and the most argued about. In 2009 Red Bull, having failed to buy into clubs in Munich, Düsseldorf and elsewhere, purchased the playing licence of SSV Markranstädt, a fifth-tier club outside Leipzig, and renamed it RasenBallsport Leipzig, "lawn ball sport" being a way around the ban on sponsor names. The city, home of the first German champions VfB Leipzig in 1903 and of the former East German clubs Lokomotive and Chemie, had not had a top-flight team since 1994. The stadium, the rebuilt Zentralstadion from the 2006 World Cup, was renamed the Red Bull Arena.

The rise was planned and fast: Oberliga champions in the first season, Regionalliga champions in 2013, promotion from the 3. Liga in 2014 and from the 2. Bundesliga in 2016. [Ralf Rangnick](/managers/ralf-rangnick), sporting director from 2012 and twice interim coach, set the philosophy, recruiting players under 24 with pace and pressing capacity. In their first Bundesliga season, 2016-17, Ralph Hasenhüttl's side finished second behind Bayern and qualified for the Champions League.

Julian Nagelsmann (2019-21) took the club to a Champions League semi-final in 2020, beating Tottenham and Atlético Madrid before losing to PSG in Lisbon, and to second place in 2021. Jesse Marsch lasted half a season; Domenico Tedesco won the club's first trophy, the 2022 DFB-Pokal, on penalties against Freiburg; [Marco Rose](/managers/marco-rose) retained it in 2023 against Frankfurt and added the Supercup against Bayern. The 2024-25 season under Rose was the club's worst since promotion, seventh and outside Europe, and [Ole Werner](/managers/ole-werner) was appointed for 2025-26 to reconnect the club with its pressing identity.

## Trajectory

Seven promotions and divisions in seven years, then nine seasons in the Bundesliga with eight top-four finishes: no club in Europe has risen faster. The 2025-26 campaign under Werner was one of transition behind Kompany's Bayern, but the club returned to the Champions League places. The summer of 2026 brought the sale of [Yan Diomande](/players/yan-diomande), the Ivorian winger whose breakthrough season had made him the most coveted young player in the league, to Real Madrid for around 125 million euros, the biggest transfer in the club's history and proof that the Red Bull model of develop-and-sell still functions.

## Players the club produced

Leipzig's business is development. Timo Werner, signed from Stuttgart at 20, scored 95 goals before his 2020 move to Chelsea and returned in 2022. Naby Keïta went to Liverpool, Dayot Upamecano and Ibrahima Konaté to Bayern and Liverpool, Christopher Nkunku to Chelsea, Joško Gvardiol to Manchester City, [Dani Olmo](/players/dani-olmo) to Barcelona, Dominik Szoboszlai to Liverpool and [Yan Diomande](/players/yan-diomande) to Real Madrid. Each sale financed the next wave; Benjamin Šeško, Loïs Openda and Xavi Simons were the recent ones.

The club's own identity rests on a smaller group of long-servers: Yussuf Poulsen, who joined in the third tier in 2013 and stayed more than a decade; Willi Orbán, captain since 2017; goalkeeper Péter Gulácsi; and Emil Forsberg, the Swedish playmaker of the first Bundesliga years. Marcel Sabitzer was the midfield engine of the Nagelsmann side.

## Identity and playing style

Leipzig are the purest expression of the Red Bull school: [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) with vertical passing, [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) in midfield, and a 4-2-2-2 with narrow attacking midfielders and two strikers running the channels. Rangnick's rules (win the ball back within eight seconds, shoot within ten) were the founding text, and Nagelsmann added positional flexibility and a [back three](/tactics/back-three).

[Ole Werner](/managers/ole-werner), who built Werder Bremen's promotion side on a back three and two strikers, was hired to bring the club back to those roots after the drift of the Rose years. He alternates between a 3-4-2-1 and the classic 4-2-2-2, asks for a patient first phase and a fast second, and makes the team dangerous in [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) transitions. [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), who ran Red Bull's global football operation before becoming Germany coach in July 2026, remains the reference for what Leipzig claim to be.

The 50+1 question has never gone away. Leipzig satisfy the rule technically, with the club association controlling the voting shares, but the association has only a handful of voting members, all connected to Red Bull, which is why opposing fans regard the arrangement as a circumvention. The DFB accepted it in 2014 after changes to the badge and the membership structure, and the debate has been a constant of German football politics since.

## Rivalries

Leipzig's rivalries are mostly with the idea of Leipzig. Supporters across Germany, from Dortmund's Südtribüne to Union Berlin's Alte Försterei, have boycotted or protested the club as a marketing vehicle that circumvents the 50+1 rule. [Union Berlin](/clubs/union-berlin), the fan-owned East German club, are the natural East derby and the ideological opposite. Dynamo Dresden are the historic Saxon rivals, met only in cups. On the pitch [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) and [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) have been the opponents in title races and cup finals, and Bayern's 6-1 win on 17 October 2026 was a reminder of the gap at the top.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Leipzig are fourth in the Bundesliga on 15 points from eight games (five wins, no draws, three defeats), with the second-best attack in the league (22 goals). After the 6-1 defeat in Munich they responded with a 4-0 home win over Elversberg on 24 October and a 2-0 win at Schalke on 31 October. Augsburg visit the Red Bull Arena on 7 November.

The squad has been reshaped since the Diomande sale, with Šeško, Openda and Simons the senior attackers and Orbán, Gulácsi and David Raum the spine. Werner's project is about proving the club can be a pressing team again and a developer of the players the Red Bull network produces; the Champions League places, which they currently occupy, are the measure of a successful season.
