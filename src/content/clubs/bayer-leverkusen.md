## History

Bayer 04 Leverkusen were founded on 1 July 1904 by employees of the Bayer pharmaceutical company, and the company has owned the club ever since, one of the two exemptions (with Wolfsburg) to the Bundesliga's 50+1 rule. For most of the twentieth century they were a modest works team; promotion to the Bundesliga came only in 1979. The breakthrough was the 1988 UEFA Cup, won on penalties against Espanyol after losing the first leg 3-0, and the 1993 DFB-Pokal under Reiner Calmund's management, the two trophies that defined the club until 2024.

The 1990s and early 2000s produced the team that gave Leverkusen their nickname "Neverkusen": Rudi Völler, Ulf Kirsten, Bernd Schneider, Michael Ballack, Lúcio, Zé Roberto and Emerson. They lost the 2000 title on the final day at Unterhaching, then in 2002 finished second in the league, lost the cup final to Schalke and the Champions League final 2-1 to Real Madrid in Glasgow, the match of Zinedine Zidane's volley, all within three weeks. Klaus Toppmöller's side had beaten Liverpool and Manchester United on the way. The following season they nearly went down, finishing 15th.

A decade of top-four finishes under Michael Skibbe, Jupp Heynckes, Sami Hyypiä and Roger Schmidt followed, with Stefan Kießling's 25 goals in 2012-13 and Schmidt's high-pressing side reaching the Champions League regularly without threatening the title. Peter Bosz brought attacking football and [Kai Havertz](/players/kai-havertz); Gerardo Seoane a third-place finish in 2022.

[Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) was appointed in October 2022 with the club 17th. Eighteen months later Leverkusen were champions, the first club to go through a Bundesliga season unbeaten (28 wins, six draws, 90 points), with [Florian Wirtz](/players/florian-wirtz), Alejandro Grimaldo, Granit Xhaka, Jeremie Frimpong and Victor Boniface. They added the DFB-Pokal, lost only once all season, the Europa League final against Atalanta, and won the 2024 Supercup. Second place in 2024-25 was followed by the departures of Alonso to Real Madrid and Wirtz to Liverpool in the summer of 2025.

## Trajectory

Leverkusen's position in German football has been remarkably stable: fourth or better in 16 of the last 26 seasons and only twice outside the top half (15th in 2002-03, 12th in 2016-17). They have been Bayern's most consistent irritant without winning anything until 2024, which is why that title, and the manner of it, mattered so much. The transition since has been managed carefully. Erik ten Hag's spell in summer 2025 was short, and sporting director Simon Rolfes turned to [Kasper Hjulmand](/managers/kasper-hjulmand), the former Denmark coach, to rebuild without drama. By the end of 2025-26 the team had re-established itself in the upper tier, finishing sixth and qualifying for the Europa League, but Hjulmand was sacked and [Carles Martínez Novell](/managers/carles-martinez-novell), the former Toulouse coach, took over on 1 July 2026 on a contract to June 2028.

## Players the club produced

Leverkusen's academy and scouting have produced or made a long list of Germany internationals: Bernd Schneider, Michael Ballack (signed from Kaiserslautern, sold to Bayern in 2002), Simon Rolfes, Stefan Kießling, Lars Bender, [Kai Havertz](/players/kai-havertz), who debuted at 17 and left for Chelsea in 2020 for around 80 million euros, and [Florian Wirtz](/players/florian-wirtz), the Köln academy product who signed at 16 in 2020 and became the best young player in Germany before his record move to Liverpool in 2025. Jonathan Tah spent a decade at the club before joining Bayern in 2025.

The Brazilian imports of the 2000s, Lúcio, Zé Roberto, Emerson and Juan, were sold on to Bayern, Real Madrid and Roma; Arturo Vidal, Toni Kroos (on loan), Son Heung-min and Leon Bailey were later profit-makers. Alejandro Grimaldo, signed on a free transfer from Benfica in 2023, was the signing of the title season.

## Identity and playing style

Alonso's Leverkusen gave the club a tactical identity it had never had: a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with Frimpong and Grimaldo as attacking wing-backs, Xhaka and Palacios as a controlling double pivot, Wirtz free behind the striker, and [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) that could switch to a devastating counter. The team's habit of scoring late (they rescued points after the 90th minute more than a dozen times in 2023-24) became its signature.

[Kasper Hjulmand](/managers/kasper-hjulmand) kept the back three as the base in 2025-26 and added Scandinavian pragmatism, and his man-management is the reason the dressing room held together after Alonso's departure. [Carles Martínez Novell](/managers/carles-martinez-novell) has inherited that structure and brought the positional ideas he used at Toulouse: a patient build-up with the goalkeeper involved, a 3-4-2-1 whose inside forwards form a box with the double pivot, structured [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) that steer the ball wide rather than an all-out [high line](/tactics/high-line), and a sharper focus on set pieces.

## Rivalries

The nearest rival is [Cologne](/clubs/cologne), 20 kilometres away: the Rhine derby is a contrast between the big-city club with the long history and the works team with the modern trophies, and Köln's promotion in 2025 restored the fixture. [Borussia Mönchengladbach](/clubs/borussia-monchengladbach) complete the Rhineland set. The relationship with [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) is one of decades of near misses, the 2000 and 2002 title races above all, and the 2024 title was savoured as much for ending Bayern's eleven-year run as for itself.

## Current situation

Martínez Novell's first season has started unevenly. As of 7 November 2026 Leverkusen are sixth in the Bundesliga on 13 points from eight games (four wins, one draw, three defeats, 18 scored, 16 conceded), having beaten Freiburg 2-1 on 18 October before losing 3-0 at Hoffenheim on 25 October and 4-2 at home to Stuttgart on 31 October. The next fixture is the Rhine derby at Köln on 8 November.

The squad still includes Grimaldo, Hradecky, Andrich and Boniface from the title team, with younger signings being given time. The club is in Europe again, and the ambition stated by Rolfes is to challenge Bayern rather than simply hold a Champions League place. [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), Germany's coach since July 2026, has leaned on Leverkusen's German players in his first squads.
