## History

Borussia Dortmund were founded on 19 December 1909 by eighteen young men from the Trinity parish in Dortmund's Borsigplatz district, in defiance of a local priest who disapproved of football. The name came from a nearby brewery. For decades they were a working-class club of the Ruhr coalfield, overshadowed by [Schalke](/clubs/schalke) until the 1950s, when they won three German championships (1956, 1957, 1963) and became the first German club to win a European trophy, the 1966 Cup Winners' Cup against Liverpool at Hampden Park.

The 1970s brought relegation (1972) and near bankruptcy, but the 1974 opening of the Westfalenstadion for the World Cup gave the club a home that would define it. Promoted in 1976, Dortmund rebuilt slowly through the 1980s before Ottmar Hitzfeld's side of the 1990s, financed by Michael Meier and built around Matthias Sammer, Jürgen Kohler, Andreas Möller and Karl-Heinz Riedle, won back-to-back titles in 1995 and 1996 and then the 1997 Champions League, beating Juventus 3-1 in Munich with two Riedle goals and Lars Ricken's famous lob 16 seconds after coming on. The Intercontinental Cup followed against Cruzeiro.

Matthias Sammer, by now coach, won a sixth title in 2002 with Tomáš Rosický, Jan Koller and Márcio Amoroso, and the club reached the UEFA Cup final the same season. Then came the crash. A stock-market flotation in 2000 and reckless spending left Dortmund close to insolvency in 2005, saved only by creditors accepting deferred payments and the sale and lease-back of the stadium. Mid-table finishes followed, with 13th place in 2007-08 the low.

[Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) arrived in 2008 and built the team that revived the club: Mats Hummels, Neven Subotić, Nuri Şahin, Shinji Kagawa, Mario Götze and [Robert Lewandowski](/players/robert-lewandowski), with Marco Reus added in 2012. They won the Bundesliga in 2011 and 2012 (the second with a record 81 points and a 5-2 cup-final win over Bayern) and reached the 2013 Champions League final at Wembley, lost 2-1 to Bayern. Thomas Tuchel won the 2017 DFB-Pokal; Lucien Favre finished second twice; [Edin Terzić](/managers/edin-terzic) won the 2021 cup and took the club to the 2024 Champions League final, lost 2-0 to Real Madrid at Wembley. The 2022-23 title was lost on the final day with a 2-2 draw against Mainz, a wound that has not fully healed.

## Trajectory

Dortmund's 21st century has three phases: the Sammer peak and financial collapse (2002-08), the Klopp revival (2008-15), and a decade of consistent top-four finishes that has brought one cup and no league title. They have finished second in the Bundesliga seven times since 2013. The 2014-15 season, when they were bottom at Christmas and finished seventh, was the only serious wobble in that period, and the 2024-25 campaign under Nuri Şahin, who was sacked in January with the club 11th, was the next scare. [Niko Kovač](/managers/niko-kovac) took over in February 2025 and secured fourth on the final day, then reached the Club World Cup quarter-finals that summer.

In Europe the record is one Champions League win (1997), two more finals (2013, 2024), a UEFA Cup final (1993) and a 2002 UEFA Cup final. The club's business model, buying young and selling high (Dembélé, Pulisic, Sancho, Haaland, Bellingham), funds the stadium and the wage bill without external owners.

The stadium is central to the story. The Westfalenstadion, expanded in stages to 81,365 for league matches, has the highest average attendance in world football, and the Südtribüne, the largest standing terrace in Europe, is the Yellow Wall that every visiting team is warned about. The club's membership passed 200,000 in the 2020s, and its match-day revenue, together with the sales model, is the reason Dortmund can compete with Bayern on a budget half the size. The 2005 insolvency crisis, when the club had to be bailed out by a bond holder vote, remains the cautionary tale that shapes its conservative finances.

## Players the club produced

Dortmund's academy has produced Lars Ricken, Marco Reus (a boyhood fan who returned from Gladbach in 2012 and stayed until 2024), Nuri Şahin, Mario Götze, Christian Pulisic, Youssoufa Moukoko and Jamie Gittens. More than the academy, though, the club is known as the finishing school of Europe: Shinji Kagawa, [Robert Lewandowski](/players/robert-lewandowski), [Ousmane Dembélé](/players/ousmane-dembele) (one season, sold to Barcelona for up to 140 million euros), Jadon Sancho, [Erling Haaland](/players/erling-haaland) (86 goals in 89 games between January 2020 and 2022) and [Jude Bellingham](/players/jude-bellingham), who arrived from Birmingham at 17 and left for Real Madrid at 19 as the Bundesliga's player of the season.

Hummels, Mats and Jürgen Kohler among the defenders, Roman Weidenfeller and Gregor Kobel in goal, Michael Zorc (572 games, later sporting director) and Sammer in midfield make up the club's hall of fame. [Serhou Guirassy](/players/serhou-guirassy), signed from Stuttgart in 2024, has been the goalscorer of the Kovač era, and [Karim Adeyemi](/players/karim-adeyemi) the fastest runner in transition.

## Identity and playing style

Dortmund's identity was forged by Klopp's [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing): win the ball back within seconds, attack before the opponent can reset, and let the Südtribüne's 25,000 standing fans provide the energy. Every coach since has been measured against that template, and the club's best seasons have been the ones with transition speed and a clear structure.

[Niko Kovač](/managers/niko-kovac) has returned the team to that register. He plays a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with wing-backs, presses in a compact 5-2-3 and attacks with [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into Guirassy and runners from the inside forwards, a disciplined rather than chaotic version of the pressing game. The result is a side that concedes little (six goals in the first eight league games of 2026-27) and scores in bursts. Kovač's pragmatism was unfashionable when he arrived, but it has made Dortmund serious again.

The most memorable Dortmund matches of the modern era share a pattern: a home crowd that lifts the team to something beyond its level. The 4-1 win over Real Madrid in the 2013 semi-final, with four Lewandowski goals, the 3-2 comeback against Málaga in the quarter-final the same spring with two goals in stoppage time, the 2-1 defeat of Manchester City in 2016-17 and the 2024 semi-final win over PSG at a sold-out Westfalenstadion are the nights the Yellow Wall is remembered for. The club's weakness, equally consistent, has been the failure to close out domestic title races: 2019 (lost a nine-point lead), 2023 (a 2-2 draw on the final day when a win would have been enough) and the near-misses under Favre and Terzić.

## Rivalries

The **Revierderby** against [Schalke](/clubs/schalke) is the fiercest rivalry in Germany, two coal-mining cities 30 kilometres apart whose supporters have been at odds since the 1920s. Dortmund's 4-4 draw from 4-0 down in November 2017 and Schalke's relegations in 2021 and 2023 are the recent chapters; Schalke's promotion for 2026-27 brought the fixture back to the Bundesliga after three seasons.

**Der Klassiker** against [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) became a rivalry in the Klopp years and has stayed one: the 2012 double, the 2013 Wembley final, the transfer of Götze, Lewandowski and Hummels to Munich, and the near-annual title race. The 31 October 2026 meeting ended 2-2 in Munich. The "Borussia derby" with [Borussia Mönchengladbach](/clubs/borussia-monchengladbach) is a friendlier affair rooted in the 1970s.

## Current situation

Dortmund are Bayern's closest challengers in 2026-27. As of 7 November 2026 they are second on 18 points from eight games, level with Bayern, unbeaten with five wins and three draws, and with the best defence in the league (six conceded). They drew 0-0 at Union Berlin on 17 October, beat Frankfurt 1-0 on 24 October and drew 2-2 at the Allianz Arena on 31 October. Promoted Elversberg visit on 7 November and the next fixture after the international break is at Gladbach on 21 November.

Kovač is in his second full season, with Guirassy leading the line, Julian Brandt and Adeyemi behind him, Nico Schlotterbeck organising the back three and Kobel in goal. Jobe Bellingham, Jude's younger brother, signed from Sunderland in 2025, has become a regular in midfield. The club is in the Champions League league phase again, and the question Kovač is asked at every press conference is whether this is the year the gap to Bayern closes. [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s appointment as Germany coach on 24 July 2026 has put the club's most famous former manager back in the national conversation and made comparisons unavoidable.
