## History

Beşiktaş were founded in 1903 as a gymnastics club in the Beşiktaş district on the European shore of the Bosphorus, making them the oldest sports club in Turkey, and football followed in 1911. The black and white stripes and the eagle crest arrived in the 1940s, when a supporter's cry of "Black Eagles" during a run of victories gave the club its nickname. Hakkı Yeten, the first great Turkish striker, led the club through the Istanbul league era.

Beşiktaş won their first Süper Lig in 1957 and 1958, then waited until 1966 and 1967 for more. The 1980s and early 1990s were the club's best era: under Gordon Milne, with Metin Tekin, Feyyaz Uçar and Ali Gültiken, the "Metin-Ali-Feyyaz" forward line, Beşiktaş won three titles in a row from 1990 to 1992, the 1992 season completed unbeaten, and added championships in 1995 under Christoph Daum. The centenary season of 2002-03 under Mircea Lucescu brought a title with Sergen Yalçın, the club's most gifted modern player, and İlhan Mansız.

Jean Tigana's 2007 side and Mustafa Denizli's 2009 double followed, and after the demolition of the old İnönü Stadium the club spent two years playing at the Atatürk Olympic Stadium before opening the Vodafone Park (now Tüpraş Stadyumu) in 2016. Şenol Güneş's teams won back-to-back titles in 2016 and 2017 with Ricardo Quaresma, Mario Gómez, Cenk Tosun and Oğuzhan Özyakup, and in 2017-18 Beşiktaş topped a Champions League group containing Porto, RB Leipzig and Monaco without losing a game, before Bayern Munich ended the run in the last 16. Sergen Yalçın returned as coach and won the 2021 double, clinched on goal difference from Galatasaray on the final day.

## Trajectory

Beşiktaş's 16 titles place them third in Turkey, and the twenty-first century has brought five: 2003, 2009, 2016, 2017 and 2021. The club has never finished below sixth since 2000 but has struggled for consistency, with fourth, sixth and third-place finishes rotating around brief title runs. The years since 2021 have been marked by financial strain, frequent coaching changes and Galatasaray's dominance, with sixth in 2022 and 2024 and fourth in 2025. Europe has brought the 2017-18 Champions League last 16 and regular Europa League and Conference League group football.

## Players the club produced

Beşiktaş's academy produced Sergen Yalçın, Rıza Çalımbay, Metin Tekin, Feyyaz Uçar, İbrahim Üzülmez and more recently Oğuzhan Özyakup (via Arsenal's academy and back), Semih Kılıçsoy and the goalkeeper Ersin Destanoğlu. Cenk Tosun, Burak Yılmaz and Necip Uysal made their names at the club, and the foreign signings who became icons include Pascal Nouma, Ricardo Quaresma, Demba Ba, Vincent Aboubakar, Anderson Talisca and Ciro Immobile.

## Identity and playing style

Beşiktaş's identity is the Çarşı supporters' group, the most politically engaged and loudest fan culture in Turkey, and the stadium that set a world record for crowd noise in 2013. The club's teams have traditionally been combative, built on a strong midfield and a powerful centre-forward, usually in a 4-2-3-1 or 4-1-4-1 that presses in the opponent's half and attacks with [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking). The coaching position has changed frequently in recent seasons and the head coach as of November 2026 should be checked against the club's latest announcements.

## Rivalries

The derbies against [Galatasaray](/clubs/galatasaray) and [Fenerbahçe](/clubs/fenerbahce) are the two biggest fixtures in a Beşiktaş season, with the Galatasaray match the more geographically immediate on the European side. [Trabzonspor](/clubs/trabzonspor) are the main provincial rival.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Beşiktaş are fifth in the Süper Lig after ten rounds, six points behind Galatasaray and in the chasing pack with Trabzonspor and Kasımpaşa. The club's ambitions remain a return to the title race and a European group stage; the squad, led by Immobile and Rafa Silva, has quality but the club's finances and coaching churn have made sustained challenges difficult.
