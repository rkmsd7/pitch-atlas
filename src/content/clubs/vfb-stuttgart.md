## History

VfB Stuttgart were formed in 1893 as a rugby club, took up football in 1912 and merged with Kronen-Club Cannstatt to form the modern VfB. They won their first German championships in 1950 and 1952 under Georg Wurzer, with Robert Schlienz, the one-armed captain, as the symbol of the side, and the DFB-Pokal in 1954 and 1958. Founder members of the Bundesliga in 1963, they spent the first decade in mid-table and were relegated in 1975, returning two years later under Jürgen Sundermann with a young side that finished fourth in its first season back.

Helmut Benthaus won the 1984 title with Karlheinz Förster, Guido Buchwald and Karl Allgöwer. Christoph Daum's 1992 champions, with [Jürgen Klinsmann](/players/jurgen-klinsmann) gone but Fritz Walter and Matthias Sammer in the team, took the title on the final day at Leverkusen, then famously fielded four foreigners in a Champions League tie against Leeds and were thrown out. Joachim Löw's "magic triangle" of Krasimir Balakov, Giovane Élber and Fredi Bobić won the 1997 cup and reached the 1998 Cup Winners' Cup final, lost to Chelsea.

Felix Magath's young side of Timo Hildebrand, Andreas Hinkel, Kevin Kurányi and Alexander Hleb finished second in 2003 and reached the Champions League. Armin Veh's 2007 champions, with Mario Gómez, Cacau, Sami Khedira and Pável Pardo, won the title on the final day against Cottbus after a run of eight straight wins, the club's fifth and most recent. Decline followed: three coaches in 2010-11, a cup final in 2013, and relegation in 2016 after 39 years in the top flight. Promoted at once under Hannes Wolf, they went down again via the play-off in 2019, came back in 2020 under Pellegrino Matarazzo and survived two more play-off scares.

[Sebastian Hoeneß](/managers/sebastian-hoeness) arrived in April 2023 with the club 16th, won the relegation play-off against Hamburg and then produced the best season since 2007: second place in 2023-24 with 73 points and [Serhou Guirassy](/players/serhou-guirassy)'s 28 goals. The 2025 DFB-Pokal, a 4-2 win over Arminia Bielefeld in Berlin on 24 May 2025, was the first trophy since 2007.

## Trajectory

Stuttgart's century is a sine wave: second in 2003, champions in 2007, third in 2009, then a long slide to relegation in 2016, a yo-yo period with two relegations and two promotions, and the Hoeneß revival. The club's problem in the 2010s was structural, with frequent changes of sporting director and coach, and the solution since 2023 has been continuity: Hoeneß signed an extension in March 2025, and Fabian Wohlgemuth's recruitment has turned the squad over profitably (Guirassy to Dortmund, Hiroki Ito to Bayern, Nick Woltemade's rise) without losing quality. The 2024-25 season was spent balancing a first Champions League campaign in 15 years with the league, and finished ninth; 2025-26 brought a return to the European places behind Bayern.

## Players the club produced

Stuttgart's academy is one of Germany's best. [Jürgen Klinsmann](/players/jurgen-klinsmann) came through in the early 1980s and scored 79 goals before leaving for Inter; Guido Buchwald, Karlheinz and Bernd Förster were earlier graduates. The 2007 side was built on homegrown players: Hildebrand, Hinkel, Gómez, Khedira, Serdar Taşçı, Christian Gentner. [Joshua Kimmich](/players/joshua-kimmich) was a Stuttgart youth player who left for Leipzig at 18 before Bayern, Timo Werner was the club's youngest ever debutant at 17, Antonio Rüdiger and Bernd Leno came through in the same years, and Maximilian Mittelstädt and Chris Führich are recent internationals developed at the club.

Among signings, Balakov, Élber, Bobić, Cacau and [Serhou Guirassy](/players/serhou-guirassy), who scored 28 league goals in 2023-24 after years of injury, are the names supporters remember. Deniz Undav, Angelo Stiller and Alexander Nübel have been the core of the Hoeneß side.

## Identity and playing style

Under [Sebastian Hoeneß](/managers/sebastian-hoeness) Stuttgart play a brave 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, with one full-back inverting into midfield ([inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs)), the goalkeeper and centre-backs expected to play through the first line, and attacking midfielders occupying the half-spaces. It is [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with a German pressing edge: a [high line](/tactics/high-line), aggressive counter-pressing in the [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) tradition and a high volume of crosses and cut-backs that explains why their strikers score so freely. The weakness is pace in behind, mitigated by recruiting quicker centre-backs.

The club's historic identity is attacking too; the 1990s Löw sides and the 2007 champions were counter-attacking teams with fast forwards, and the MHPArena, rebuilt for Euro 2024, has become one of the louder stadiums in the league.

## Rivalries

The Baden-Württemberg derby against Karlsruher SC is the oldest and fiercest, though the clubs have met only in cups since 2009. [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) are the southern neighbour and the regular Supercup opponent (Stuttgart lost the 2025 Supercup to them). [Eintracht Frankfurt](/clubs/eintracht-frankfurt) have been the rival for European places in recent seasons, and the final-day draw between the clubs in May 2026 decided Frankfurt's season rather than Stuttgart's. Hoffenheim, 80 kilometres away, are the regional opponent fans like least.

## Current situation

The 2026-27 season has started below expectations. As of 7 November 2026 Stuttgart are 12th on nine points from eight games (three wins, five defeats, no draws), having lost 1-0 at Hamburg on 17 October and 3-2 at home to Gladbach on 23 October before a 4-2 win at Leverkusen on 31 October that showed the side's attacking quality. Werder Bremen visit on 7 November.

Hoeneß remains in charge, with the squad built around Stiller, Undav, Führich, Mittelstädt and Nübel. The club's aim is to stay in the European places behind Bayern and alongside Dortmund, Leverkusen and Leipzig, and the November results will decide whether the slow start is a blip. Hoeneß is linked with bigger jobs every summer, and the appointment of [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) as Germany coach in July 2026 has kept the question of the next generation of German coaches in the air.
