## History

SC Paderborn 07 are a young club in an old city. The current entity dates from a 1985 merger (TuS Schloss Neuhaus and 1. FC Paderborn), with the 1907 founding date inherited from a predecessor, and for most of their history they were an amateur side in the Westphalian leagues. The first national attention came in 2004, when Paderborn, then a Regionalliga club, beat Hamburg 4-2 in the DFB-Pokal; it emerged that referee Robert Hoyzer had fixed the match as part of a betting scandal, through no fault of the club. Promotion to the 2. Bundesliga followed in 2005.

A relegation in 2008 was reversed the next season, and under André Breitenreiter the club finished second in the 2. Bundesliga in 2014 and reached the Bundesliga for the first time, a 15,000-seat stadium hosting Bayern and Dortmund. They were top of the table after four games, Moritz Stoppelkamp scored from 83 metres against Hannover, and then reality arrived: last place, and relegation. The fall continued: last in the 2. Bundesliga in 2016, and 18th in the 3. Liga in 2017, which should have meant the fourth tier, except that 1860 Munich failed to obtain a licence and Paderborn were reprieved.

Steffen Baumgart, appointed in April 2017, turned the reprieve into the most remarkable sequence in recent German football: second in the 3. Liga in 2018, second in the 2. Bundesliga in 2019, and a second Bundesliga season in 2019-20, which ended in relegation but with an attacking style that won admirers. Lukas Kwasniok kept the club in the top half of the second division for four years, and after a fourth-place finish in 2024-25 the club went up again in 2025-26 as runners-up, the third promotion to the top flight in twelve years.

## Trajectory

Few clubs have seen so many divisions in so short a time: the fourth tier in 2001, the Bundesliga in 2014, the third tier in 2017, the Bundesliga in 2019, and again in 2026. Paderborn's model, a small budget, young coaches and a willingness to sell (Ron Schallenberg, Sven Michel, Dennis Srbeny, Robert Leipertz all moved on for profit), has made them the archetype of the overachieving regional club. The 2026-27 season under Ralf Kettemann, promoted from the coaching staff, is the first time they have started a Bundesliga season looking organised rather than overwhelmed.

## Players the club produced

Paderborn are not a nursery of stars, but a number of players made their careers there: Uwe Hünemeier, the captain of the first promotion; Alban Meha, the Kosovan playmaker; Stoppelkamp; Sven Michel, who scored freely under Baumgart before Union Berlin; Dennis Srbeny, Ron Schallenberg (sold to Schalke) and Christian Strohdiek, who played more than 300 games. Marvin Mehlem and Filip Bilbija have been the creators of the Kettemann side, Pelle Boevink the goalkeeper, and Felix Götze, brother of Mario, the defensive leader.

## Identity and playing style

Baumgart's Paderborn were the most reckless team in Germany, a 4-4-2 that pressed from the front and attacked with everyone; it was wonderful and it got them relegated. The club has since become more measured. Kwasniok's sides combined a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with possession and high pressing, and Ralf Kettemann has kept that structure, with an emphasis on a compact mid-block, [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) transitions, the pragmatism of a club that knows it must defend to survive.

## Rivalries

The Ostwestfalen derby against Arminia Bielefeld, 50 kilometres away, is the one that matters locally, and the two clubs have met in all three professional divisions. Preußen Münster are the other Westphalian neighbour. Nationally, Paderborn have no rival of note, which is part of their charm.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Paderborn are 15th in the Bundesliga on nine points from eight games (two wins, three draws, three defeats), with six goals scored, the second fewest in the league, and nine conceded, among the best defensive records. They drew 2-2 at Werder on 17 October, beat Hamburg 1-0 at home on 24 October and drew 0-0 at Gladbach on 31 October. Frankfurt visit the Home Deluxe Arena on 7 November.

Kettemann's team is hard to break down and short of goals, which is the usual profile of a promoted side built to survive. Mehlem, Bilbija and Michel are the attacking hopes; the aim is a first ever Bundesliga season without relegation, and the early defensive numbers give it a chance.
