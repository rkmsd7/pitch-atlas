## History

LAFC was awarded to a star-studded ownership group in October 2014, a few months after Chivas USA folded, and spent three years building before kicking a ball. The founders included Magic Johnson, Will Ferrell, Mia Hamm and the venture capitalist Larry Berg, and the plan was explicit: an urban stadium on the site of the old Los Angeles Memorial Sports Arena, a supporters' culture modelled on South American and European curvas, and a playing style to match. The 3252 supporters' section was organised before the team existed.

Bob Bradley was the first head coach, Carlos Vela the first designated player, and the first season (2018) produced a play-off place and the opening of what was then Banc of California Stadium, the first open-air stadium built in Los Angeles since 1962. 2019 was the masterpiece: Vela scored 34 goals, an MLS record at the time, and LAFC won the Supporters' Shield with 72 points, another record, before losing the Western Conference final to [Seattle](/clubs/seattle-sounders).

## Trajectory

The 2020 pandemic season brought a run to the CONCACAF Champions League final, lost 2-1 to Tigres UANL, and 2021 brought a rare miss of the play-offs. Steve Cherundolo replaced Bradley in 2022 and the club immediately won the double: a second Shield and, after [Gareth Bale](/players/gareth-bale)'s 128th-minute header had forced penalties against Philadelphia, a first MLS Cup. Bale and Giorgio Chiellini, both signed that summer, retired within months.

Since then LAFC has been the perennial nearly team: MLS Cup finals lost in 2023 (to Columbus) and 2024 (to the Galaxy), a 2023 Champions League final lost to León, and a U.S. Open Cup win in 2024 as consolation. The club represented MLS at the 2025 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) after winning a play-in against Club América for the place vacated by León, drawing with Flamengo and losing to Chelsea and Espérance. In August 2025 Son Heung-min arrived from [Tottenham](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) and scored a hat-trick within weeks. Cherundolo left at the end of 2025; the identity of the current head coach is not confirmed in the club's squad data as of November 2026, so no manager is listed here.

## Players the club produced

LAFC's record in developing talent is stronger than its age suggests. Diego Rossi, signed from Peñarol as a teenager, became the 2020 Golden Boot winner before moving to Fenerbahçe. The academy produced defender Erik Dueñas and midfielder Nathan Ordaz, and the club sold Mamadou Fall and Kellyn Acosta on. Its real production line is of icons: Vela, the leading scorer in club history; [Bale](/players/gareth-bale); Hugo Lloris, who joined from Tottenham in 2024 and was the best goalkeeper in the league in 2025; Denis Bouanga, the Gabon forward who scored 20 or more league goals in three straight seasons from 2023; and Son.

## Identity and playing style

Under Bradley the club was a high-pressing, high-possession side, one of the first in MLS to play a genuinely modern game built on [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) triggers and quick combinations around Vela as a false right winger. Cherundolo kept the pressing but made the team more direct, using Bouanga's pace in transition and defending in a tight 4-3-3 with the full-backs tucked in. Son's arrival in 2025 added a second elite finisher, and the 2026 team has been at its best as a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) side rather than a dominant one, which explains a mid-table 47-point regular season.

## Rivalries

El Tráfico against the [LA Galaxy](/clubs/la-galaxy) is the rivalry: the first meeting in 2018 was a 4-3 Galaxy win, the 2019 edition was a 5-3 LAFC win, and the fixture has produced more goals per game than any other derby in the league. Seattle became a play-off rival through the 2019 and 2023 Western finals. [San Diego FC](/clubs/san-diego-fc), 120 miles south and in the same conference since 2025, is the newest derby.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 LAFC finished sixth in the West with 47 points, in the play-offs, with Son, Bouanga and Lloris as the core. The club has not named a manager in its current squad data, and the priority after the play-offs is settling the coaching position and the long-term contract of Bouanga. The World Cup brought matches to BMO Stadium's neighbourhood in the summer; the supporters would trade all of it for a second MLS Cup.
