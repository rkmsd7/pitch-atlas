## History

Atlético were founded on 26 April 1903 by Basque students in Madrid as a branch of Athletic Club of Bilbao, which explains the red and white stripes and the long mutual affection between the two. The club became independent in 1921, merged with the air force's team after the Civil War (hence Atlético Aviación, which won the first two post-war league titles in 1940 and 1941) and settled into its identity as Madrid's working-class club, playing at the Estadio Metropolitano and then, from 1966 to 2017, at the Vicente Calderón on the banks of the Manzanares.

The 1960s and 1970s were a golden age. Under Luis Aragonés as player and then coach, Atlético won the Cup Winners' Cup in 1962, league titles in 1966, 1970, 1973 and 1977, and reached the 1974 European Cup final, where Luis's free kick in extra time against Bayern Munich was cancelled out by Georg Schwarzenbeck's long-range equaliser in the last minute; Bayern won the replay 4-0. Atlético took the Intercontinental Cup that winter in Bayern's absence. The Jesús Gil presidency (1987-2003) was chaotic and compelling: 39 coaches in 16 years, Paulo Futre, Radomir Antić's 1996 double with a young [Diego Simeone](/managers/diego-simeone) in midfield, and then the humiliation of relegation in 2000, "un añito en el infierno" that became two.

The return in 2002 brought [Fernando Torres](/players/fernando-torres), the home-grown Niño, and a slow climb back through mid-table. [Diego Forlán](/players/diego-forlan) and [Sergio Agüero](/players/sergio-aguero) won the 2010 Europa League, Radamel Falcao the 2012 edition, and Simeone, appointed in December 2011, turned that momentum into the modern Atlético: the 2013 Copa del Rey at the Bernabéu, the 2014 league title sealed with a draw at Camp Nou, and Champions League finals in 2014 (lost in extra time) and 2016 (lost on penalties), both to [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid).

The two lost finals deserve their own paragraph because they define the club's modern psychology. In Lisbon in 2014 Atlético led from Diego Godín's 36th-minute header until the 93rd, when Sergio Ramos equalised from a corner; Madrid scored three more in extra time. In Milan in 2016 Griezmann missed a penalty with the score at 1-0 to Madrid, Yannick Carrasco equalised, and Juanfran hit the post in the shootout before Cristiano Ronaldo scored the winning kick. Simeone has said the 2014 final is the one that still visits him. Atlético responded by winning the 2018 Europa League in Lyon, 3-0 against Marseille with two from Griezmann, and the UEFA Super Cup against Madrid that August, 4-2 in Tallinn.

## Trajectory

Atlético's century was shaped by the fall and the rise. Relegated in 2000 with a team that included Hasselbaink and Rubén Baraja, they finished fourth in Segunda in 2000-01 and won promotion as champions in 2001-02 under Luis Aragonés. The next decade was one of mid-table drift, with a 2008-09 return to the Champions League under Javier Aguirre and Quique Sánchez Flores' 2010 Europa League the high points.

Since 2012-13 Atlético have finished in the top four in every season but one, a run no other club outside Madrid and Barcelona can claim. Simeone's second league title in 2020-21, built on Jan Oblak, [Koke](/players/koke) and [Luis Suárez](/players/luis-suarez), was won on the final day at Valladolid. In 2025-26 they finished fourth with 69 points, reached the Copa del Rey final (lost 4-3 on penalties to [Real Sociedad](/clubs/real-sociedad) after a 2-2 draw in Seville on 18 April 2026, with Álvarez and Sørloth missing from the spot) and went further in the Champions League than at any point since 2017: a quarter-final elimination of Barcelona, 3-2 on aggregate, then a 2-1 aggregate semi-final defeat to Arsenal.

The move from the Calderón to the Metropolitano in September 2017 changed the club's economics. The new ground, on the eastern edge of the city, holds 70,000, hosted the 2019 Champions League final and allowed Atlético to spend at a level unthinkable in Gil's day: João Félix for €126m in 2019, Álvarez for €75m in 2024, and a wage bill that, while still well below Madrid's and Barcelona's, is the third in Spain. The ownership of Miguel Ángel Gil Marín and Enrique Cerezo, with the investment of Ares Management and Apollo in recent years, has been stable in a way the club's history was not.

## Players the club produced

The academy produced Torres, Koke, Gabi, Saúl Ñíguez, Lucas Hernández, Marcos Llorente's father's generation and, most recently, Pablo Barrios and Giuliano Simeone. Adelardo, the club's all-time appearance leader, played 17 seasons; José Eulogio Gárate was the great striker of the 1970s.

Signed stars include Futre, Forlán, [Agüero](/players/sergio-aguero), Falcao, Diego Godín, [Antoine Griezmann](/players/antoine-griezmann), who returned from Barcelona and became the club's all-time top scorer, [Jan Oblak](/players/jan-oblak), and [Julián Álvarez](/players/julian-alvarez), signed from Manchester City in 2024 and the team's centrepiece since. Álvarez and Cristian Romero were in Argentina's side beaten by Spain in the 2026 World Cup final; [Álex Baena](/players/alex-baena), signed from Villarreal in 2025, and Robin Le Normand were in the winners' squad.

The 2025-26 Champions League run showed how far the club has come. In the quarter-final first leg at Camp Nou Barcelona led 2-0 inside half an hour before Atlético pulled a goal back; at the Metropolitano Barcelona won 2-1 on the night but Atlético went through 3-2 on aggregate in a match with two red cards and the loudest atmosphere the ground has known. The semi-final against Arsenal was tighter still: 1-1 in Madrid, then a single goal at the Emirates from Piero Hincapié. Simeone said afterwards he was "proud and at peace", which from him counts as a statement. It was the club's first semi-final since 2017 and the fourth of his tenure.

The Copa del Rey final a month earlier was the other side of the same coin: Atlético twice came from behind, Álvarez equalising with seven minutes left, and then lost the shootout. The combination of those two April-May weeks, so close in both competitions, is why the mood around the club in autumn 2026 is impatient rather than content.

## Identity and playing style

Atlético under [Simeone](/managers/diego-simeone) are the definitive modern [low block](/tactics/low-block): a 4-4-2 with the lines 25 metres apart, strikers who defend, wingers who become full-backs, and a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) threat that has beaten better-resourced teams for fifteen years. [Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) have long supplied a third of the goals and [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the touchline are the preferred way to win the ball. Since 2020 the coach has often used a [back three](/tactics/back-three) and 5-3-2, and the Álvarez-era team presses higher and keeps more of the ball than the caricature allows.

In 2026-27 the shape is a 4-4-2 or 4-2-3-1 with Morten Hjulmand and Johnny Cardoso in midfield, Baena drifting inside from the left, Ademola Lookman (signed from Atalanta in February 2026) on the right and Álvarez partnered by Jonathan David, a deadline-day loan from Juventus with a €25m option. The defence has been rebuilt around Romero, Le Normand, Dávid Hancko and Alejandro Grimaldo. After 11 games they have conceded only eight goals, the best record in the league, and Oblak, now 33 and in his twelfth season, remains the goalkeeper most Spanish coaches would pick first. Pablo Barrios and Rodrigo Mendoza, both academy products, give the midfield a younger edge than it has had for years.

The Metropolitano crowd is the other half of the identity. The "Atleti, Atleti" chant, the clenched-fist gesture Simeone makes to the stands, the club's self-image as the team of effort rather than entitlement: these are not marketing, they are how the club was rescued from the Gil years. Players who arrive from richer clubs tend to describe the same thing, that at Atlético you are asked to run before you are asked to play, and that the stands notice when you do.

## Rivalries

The Madrid derby is the central one, more painful than hateful from Atlético's side given the 2014 and 2016 finals and a Bernabéu winless run that lasted from 1999 to 2013. The rivalry with [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) grew through title races (2014, 2021) and Champions League ties (2014, 2016, 2026). [Athletic Bilbao](/clubs/athletic-bilbao) are the parent club, and the two sets of fans historically got on; the fixture is affectionate rather than fierce. [Sevilla](/clubs/sevilla) have been the Europa League and Copa rival of the 2010s.

## Current situation

Atlético are second in La Liga on 7 November 2026, with 28 points from 11 games (nine wins, one draw, one defeat), two behind Barcelona and one ahead of [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s Real Madrid. Simeone, in his fifteenth season and contracted to June 2027, has the best defence in the division and a strike pairing of Álvarez and David that has adapted quickly.

Questions hang over the future. Sporting director Andrea Berta's departure prompted reports that the new football leadership is thinking about long-term succession, and Simeone has given no indication of his plans beyond 2027. The summer window brought Romero, Grimaldo, Hjulmand and Lee Kang-in alongside David, with Thomas Lemar loaned to Elche and Sørloth retained. The Champions League, the one prize that has escaped Cholismo, and a third league title under Simeone remain the measures of this team. As of November 2026 both look genuinely possible.
