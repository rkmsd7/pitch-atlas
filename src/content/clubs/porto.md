## History

FC Porto were founded in 1893 by António Nicolau de Almeida, a port wine merchant who had discovered football in England, and the club's identity has always carried something of the northern city's self-image: industrious, proud, suspicious of Lisbon. For the first eight decades Porto were the junior partner in Portuguese football, winning titles in 1939, 1940, 1956 and 1959 but watching [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) and [Sporting](/clubs/sporting-cp) dominate.

The transformation began with the election of Jorge Nuno Pinto da Costa as president in 1982. Pinto da Costa ran the club for 42 years, and within five of them Porto were European champions. Artur Jorge's team beat Bayern Munich 2-1 in Vienna in 1987, a final remembered for Rabah Madjer's backheeled equaliser and Juary's winner, with Fernando Gomes, twice European Golden Boot winner, up front. The Intercontinental Cup and UEFA Super Cup followed, and the 1990s brought five consecutive titles under Bobby Robson and António Oliveira, the "penta" from 1995 to 1999.

The second European peak belongs to [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho). Appointed in January 2002 with the club in mid-table, he promised the title the following year and delivered a Treble: the league, the cup and the UEFA Cup, won 3-2 against Celtic in Seville. In 2004 Porto beat Manchester United (Costinha's late goal at Old Trafford, Mourinho's sprint down the touchline), Lyon and Deportivo before beating Monaco 3-0 in the Champions League final in Gelsenkirchen with goals from Carlos Alberto, [Deco](/players/deco) and Dmitri Alenichev. It remains the last Champions League won by a club from outside Europe's big five leagues.

Mourinho left for Chelsea and took the model with him, but Porto kept winning. Jesualdo Ferreira won three titles, and André Villas-Boas, aged 33, went through the 2010-11 league season unbeaten and won the Europa League with a [Radamel Falcao](/players/radamel-falcao) goal against Braga in Dublin, Falcao having scored 17 times in the competition. Vítor Pereira added two more titles, and Sérgio Conceição, in seven years from 2017, won three championships and reached a Champions League quarter-final in 2021 with a side that beat Juventus. The 2024 presidential election ended Pinto da Costa's reign; André Villas-Boas returned as president, Conceição left, and after a difficult year under Vítor Bruno and Martín Anselmi the club turned to [Francesco Farioli](/managers/francesco-farioli) in July 2025.

Pinto da Costa's legacy is contested but immense. Under him Porto won 68 trophies, built the Estádio do Dragão for Euro 2004 and turned a regional club into a global brand, while his tenure was also marked by the "Apito Dourado" refereeing scandal of 2004, repeated conflicts with the Portuguese federation and, at the end, financial strain that left the club heavily indebted. Villas-Boas won the 2024 election on a promise of transparency and renewal and inherited a squad that needed selling and a balance sheet that needed repair. The 2024-25 season, which brought a third-place finish and the Club World Cup group stage, was a transition; 2025-26, Farioli's first, was the beginning of something new.

## Trajectory

Porto have won 30 Portuguese titles, 25 of them since 1978, and in the twenty-first century they have been the most successful club in the country: 13 championships between 2003 and 2022 against Benfica's eight and Sporting's four. The Mourinho and Ferreira years, 2003 to 2009, brought six titles in seven seasons; the Villas-Boas and Pereira years added three straight from 2011 to 2013; Conceição's three came in 2018, 2020 and 2022.

The pattern since 2022 has been slippage: third in 2024 and 2025, outside the title race for the first time in a generation while Sporting won two championships, and the loss of the Pinto da Costa infrastructure that had made Porto Europe's best-run selling club. The club's record in Europe since 2004 includes the 2011 Europa League, Champions League quarter-finals in 2009, 2015, 2019 and 2021, and a place in the 2026-27 league phase, where Porto sit in the lower half with three points from four matches.

## Players the club produced

Porto's genius has been less in producing players than in finding them. The scouting network built under Pinto da Costa turned Brazil, Argentina and Colombia into a conveyor belt: [Deco](/players/deco), [Hulk](/players/hulk), [Radamel Falcao](/players/radamel-falcao), [James Rodríguez](/players/james-rodriguez), Jackson Martínez, Lucho González, Lisandro López, Alex Sandro, Danilo, Éder Militão, Luis Díaz and Alan Varela were all bought cheaply and sold for enormous profits. [Pepe](/players/pepe) arrived from Marítimo and left for Real Madrid; [Ricardo Carvalho](/players/ricardo-carvalho) and Paulo Ferreira followed Mourinho to Chelsea.

The academy has produced some of Portugal's finest too: Vítor Baía, the goalkeeper of the 1990s and 2004; Rúben Neves, who captained the side at 18; Diogo Dalot, Fábio Vieira, Diogo Costa and, most recently, Rodrigo Mora. Fernando Gomes, the great striker of the 1980s, was a Porto boy from the start. [Luís Figo](/players/luis-figo) is a Sporting product, but the 2026 Portugal squad's spine owed much to the Dragão's development work.

The numbers behind the model are remarkable. Between 2004 and 2024 Porto's net transfer income was the highest of any club in Europe over that period, with sales including [James Rodríguez](/players/james-rodriguez) to Monaco for €45 million, [Hulk](/players/hulk) to Zenit for €55 million, [Radamel Falcao](/players/radamel-falcao) to Atlético for €40 million, Éder Militão to Real Madrid for €50 million and Luis Díaz to Liverpool for up to €60 million. The academy's recent output, with Diogo Costa established as Portugal's goalkeeper and Rodrigo Mora regarded as one of the most gifted teenagers in Europe, suggests the pipeline survives the change of president. Deco, the Brazilian-born playmaker who became a Portugal international, remains the symbol of the Mourinho side, and his return to Barcelona as sporting director in 2023 keeps the Porto network alive at the highest level.

## Identity and playing style

For most of the Pinto da Costa era Porto's identity was intensity: Mourinho's compact 4-3-1-2 with Deco behind two strikers, Conceição's aggressive 4-4-2 that pressed in the opponent's half and attacked through pacey wingers, all underpinned by the sense of a club at war with Lisbon and with the referees. That mentality, the "mística" of the Dragão, is still what supporters demand.

[Francesco Farioli](/managers/francesco-farioli) has given it a different tactical form. A former goalkeeping coach and disciple of [Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi), he plays a possession-dominant [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) built from the goalkeeper, with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) and a patient, short-passing build-up that his Nice side used to produce the best defence in France. The board extended his contract to 2028 after one season, a vote of confidence in a club that has rarely given young coaches time.

The transition from Conceição's intensity to Farioli's control was not instant. The 2025-26 season saw Porto dominate possession in most matches but drop points in games they would once have ground out, and the coach's insistence on building from the back drew criticism from a crowd used to directness. By the spring the pattern had settled: a 4-3-3 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, with Alan Varela as the pivot, the full-backs stepping inside and Samu Aghehowa as a mobile centre-forward who stretches defences for the inside forwards. The Dragão, which had been restless in the autumn, became an asset again.

## Rivalries

O Clássico against [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) is Portugal's biggest match and the most bitter: Porto's rise under Pinto da Costa was built on an explicit grievance against the Lisbon establishment, and the two clubs' presidents spent decades trading accusations. Matches against [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) have gained weight since Sporting's revival under [Rúben Amorim](/managers/ruben-amorim). The city derby against Boavista, the Invicta derby, is friendlier and now rarer after Boavista's financial collapse. Braga, forty miles to the north, have become a regular irritant since their rise in the 2010s, and the 2011 Europa League final between the two northern clubs remains the only all-Portuguese European final in history.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Porto lead the Primeira Liga after ten rounds, five points clear of Benfica and nine ahead of Sporting, with Portuguese coverage framing Sporting's expected appointment of [Paulo Fonseca](/managers/paulo-fonseca) as an attempt to "catch Porto". In the Champions League the start has been harder, with three points from four league-phase matches. Farioli's squad has been refreshed with younger, saleable talent in the Porto tradition, and president André Villas-Boas has staked his second term on restoring both the trophy habit and the transfer model. A 31st title would be the first of the post-Pinto da Costa era and the first since 2022, and the early-season lead has given the Dragão a sense of momentum it has not felt for four years; the test, as every Porto supporter knows, comes in the spring when the Clássicos and the European knockouts arrive together.
