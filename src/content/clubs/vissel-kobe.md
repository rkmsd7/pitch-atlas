## History

Vissel Kobe began in 1966 as Kawasaki Steel's works team in Kurashiki, moved to Kobe in 1995 as part of the city's plan to rebuild after the Great Hanshin earthquake of that January, and joined the J.League in 1997. The name is a portmanteau of "victory" and "vessel", a reference to the port city. For two decades the club was a byword for underachievement: relegated in 2005 and 2012, rescued from bankruptcy in 2004 by the e-commerce billionaire Hiroshi Mikitani, whose Rakuten group has owned it since, and rarely above mid-table even with the Japanese internationals Hotaru Yamaguchi and Gotoku Sakai.

Mikitani's ambitions were global. In 2017 he signed Lukas Podolski from Galatasaray; in May 2018 Andrés Iniesta, days after his Barcelona farewell, on a contract that made him the highest-paid player in Asia; and in 2018-19 David Villa, Sergi Samper and Thomas Vermaelen. The "Barcelonisation" of Kobe was derided as a vanity project until December 2019, when Iniesta captained the club to its first trophy, the Emperor's Cup, beating Kashima 2-0 at the new National Stadium in Tokyo with Villa in his final match. The 2020 Super Cup and an AFC Champions League semi-final followed.

## Trajectory

Iniesta's influence faded as his body did, and the club flirted with relegation in 2022, finishing 13th. The turning point was the appointment of Takayuki Yoshida, a former Vissel player, in 2022, and the decision to build around Japanese internationals rather than foreign stars: Yuya Osako, the striker who had played for Werder Bremen and Cologne, Yoshinori Muto back from Mainz and Newcastle, Yamaguchi and Sakai. Iniesta left in July 2023. That December Vissel won the J1 League for the first time, with Osako the top scorer, and in 2024 they retained it and added the Emperor's Cup for a first double, the first club to win back-to-back titles since Kawasaki in 2020-21.

Yoshida stepped down after the 2025 season; the club's current squad data lists Michael Skibbe, the German who had coached Sanfrecce Hiroshima, as head coach as of November 2026, and Vissel are fifth in the 2026-27 J1 League with 26 points from 13 games, in the chasing pack behind FC Tokyo and Kashiwa Reysol.

## Players the club produced

Vissel's academy produced the midfielder Daiju Sasaki, the full-back Ryo Hatsuse, the winger Yuta Goke (sold to Celtic in 2025) and the goalkeeper Daiya Maekawa, and the club's reputation now rests on its ability to rebuild Japanese internationals' careers: Osako, Muto, Yamaguchi and Sakai all came home to Kobe and won their first titles there. Iniesta, Villa, Podolski, Vermaelen and the Brazilian striker Leandro were the imported names of the Mikitani era.

## Identity and playing style

The Iniesta years were an attempt at [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) in Kansai, with Iniesta and Samper in a possession-based 4-3-3 under the Spanish coaches Juan Manuel Lillo and Thorsten Fink's successors; it produced beauty and inconsistency. Yoshida's champions were the opposite, a direct, physical 4-3-3 built on Osako's hold-up play, long balls from the goalkeeper and relentless pressing from Muto and Taisei Miyashiro, the most "un-Japanese" title-winning side in league history and the subject of a national debate about whether efficiency had beaten artistry. Skibbe has kept the pressing and added more build-up structure. See [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) and [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing).

## Rivalries

The Kansai derbies against Gamba Osaka and Cerezo Osaka are the fixtures that matter, Kobe's rise having disturbed Osaka's old order, and Sanfrecce Hiroshima has been the main title rival in the 2023-25 seasons. The Emperor's Cup final and the 2016 play-off history have given the games with [Kashima Antlers](/clubs/kashima-antlers) an edge.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Vissel Kobe are fifth in the 2026-27 J1 League under Skibbe, with Osako, now 36, Yamaguchi, Muto, Sasaki, Miyashiro and Hatsuse as the core. The club's two titles and the 2024 double have made it a regular in the AFC Champions League Elite, and Rakuten's ambition is now continental: an Asian title would complete the transformation from relegation fodder to the model club of the J.League's second generation.
