## History

San Diego had been promised an MLS team several times, and several times the plans collapsed, usually over a stadium. The breakthrough came with Snapdragon Stadium, built by San Diego State University and opened in 2022, and with an unusual ownership group: Mohamed Mansour, the Egyptian-British billionaire whose Man Capital owns the Right to Dream academies in Ghana, Egypt and Denmark, and the Sycuan Band of the Kumeyaay Nation, the first Native American tribe to own a share of a major American professional team. The franchise was announced in May 2023 as the league's 30th club and started play in February 2025.

The design was deliberately different. Rather than sign an ageing star, the club appointed Mikey Varas, a former United States youth coach, built a squad from Scandinavian and Mexican markets, and opened a Right to Dream academy in the county with the expectation of developing rather than buying its best players. The one headline signing was Hirving "Chucky" Lozano, the [Mexico](/nations/mexico) winger from PSV, who gave the club an immediate connection to the border city's huge Mexican-American population.

## Trajectory

The debut season was the best by any expansion team in MLS history. Anders Dreyer, a Danish winger signed from Anderlecht, was Newcomer of the Year and among the league leaders in goal contributions, Jeppe Tverskov ran midfield, and the team finished top of the Western Conference with 63 points, ahead of Vancouver and Minnesota. The play-off run ended in the Western Conference final against Vancouver Whitecaps. For a club that had existed for seven months, it was extraordinary, and Snapdragon's average crowd of over 28,000 made it one of the best-attended in the league.

The 2026 season was the correction. Losing the element of surprise and with Lozano's form and fitness fluctuating, San Diego finished 13th in the West with 38 points and missed the play-offs. Varas remains head coach per the club's current squad data as of November 2026.

## Players the club produced

Two seasons is too short to have produced anyone, but the model is the point. Right to Dream's Danish arm, FC Nordsjælland, has sold players to the Premier League for years, and San Diego's academy is designed to do the same for MLS. The Ghanaian midfielder Emmanuel Boateng and the teenager Luca Bombino were the first academy-linked players to see first-team minutes in 2025. The club's reputation so far rests on Dreyer, Lozano, Tverskov and the goalkeeper CJ dos Santos.

## Identity and playing style

Varas built a possession-based 4-3-3 that was the clearest example of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) among the 2025 expansion generation: a deep-lying midfield trio, inverted full-backs and wingers who held width until the moment to cut inside. Dreyer's left foot from the right and Lozano's pace from the left gave the team a pattern that opponents found hard to stop in year one and easier to prepare for in year two. The club's self-image, "built by the community, for the community", is tied to the Sycuan ownership and the border location; the home kit's chrome and azure colours nod to the Pacific and the city's bay.

## Rivalries

The 120-mile derby with [LAFC](/clubs/lafc) and the games with the [LA Galaxy](/clubs/la-galaxy) are the obvious fixtures, and the league scheduled them prominently in 2025. The more natural rivalry is with Club Tijuana of [Liga MX](/leagues/liga-mx), 25 miles away across the border, in the Leagues Cup and in pre-season friendlies.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 San Diego are out of the play-offs after a 13th-place finish in the West. Varas is listed as head coach, Dreyer and Tverskov are the core, and Lozano is approaching the end of his initial contract. The 2026 World Cup, with its Mexico-based group matches a short drive away, was a marketing bonus; the second-season slump is the first real test of whether the Right to Dream model can produce consistency in MLS.
