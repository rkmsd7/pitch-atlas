## History

Real Madrid were the last of Europe's superclubs to field a women's team. After decades of refusing, the club announced in 2019 that it would absorb CD Tacón, a Madrid side that had just won promotion, and Real Madrid Femenino played its first game in October 2020. The squad was built fast: Olga Carmona and Teresa Abelleira, both future World Cup winners with Spain, Kosovare Asllani, Caroline Weir, Esther González, Athenea del Castillo and goalkeeper Misa Rodríguez.

Second place in the first season under David Aznar earned a [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) place, and Alberto Toril, appointed in 2021, kept Madrid in Europe every year: a quarter-final in 2023-24, and group-stage runs that produced wins over Chelsea and PSG. The problem was always [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona-women). Madrid lost the first 19 Clásicos, including a 2023 Copa de la Reina final and the 2025 Supercopa final, and have finished second to Barça in five of their six seasons. In 2025-26 the margin was 15 points and the Champions League quarter-final ended 6-0 on aggregate in April 2026.

Pau Quesada, the former Spain Under-19 coach, replaced Toril for 2025-26 and has been tasked with turning a consistent runner-up into a trophy winner.

## Trajectory

Second, third, second, second, second, second. No club in Europe has been so consistently the second-best team in its country while winning nothing. The Alfredo Di Stéfano stadium at Valdebebas is the home ground, with the Bernabéu used once, for a 2024 Clásico that drew over 50,000.

## Players the club produced

Madrid's academy is young, but the first team has become a launchpad: Carmona scored the winning goal in the 2023 World Cup final while a Madrid player, Abelleira anchored Spain's midfield, and [Linda Caicedo](/players/linda-caicedo), signed from Deportivo Cali at 17, became one of the world's best wingers here. Athenea, the captain, is the longest-serving player. Signe Bruun, Sandie Toletti and Sara Däbritz provide the experience.

## Identity and playing style

Toril's Madrid played a counter-attacking 4-3-3 with Caicedo and Athenea on the wings; Quesada has moved towards a more possession-based approach in the Spanish tradition of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), with Toletti and Däbritz controlling and Bruun as the target. Merle Frohms, the former Germany goalkeeper, is a confident sweeper. Against Barcelona the plan remains a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block) and transitions, and it has not yet worked.

## Rivalries

The Clásico against Barcelona is the biggest fixture in Spanish women's football and the most one-sided; Madrid's first ever win over Barça, in the 2025 Liga F, remains a rare high. The derby against Atlético Madrid, the dominant Spanish club of the late 2010s, is the second rivalry.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Quesada's side is second in Liga F again and in the Champions League league phase. Caicedo, 21, is the star; Athenea the leader; and a first trophy, probably in the Copa de la Reina or Supercopa, is the most realistic way to end the wait.
