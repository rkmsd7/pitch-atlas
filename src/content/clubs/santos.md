## History

Santos Futebol Clube was founded in 1912 in the port city of Santos, 70 kilometres from São Paulo, and for its first four decades it was a respectable state club with a small stadium, the Vila Belmiro, that still holds only 16,000. Then, in 1956, a 15-year-old from Bauru called Edson Arantes do Nascimento arrived for a trial. [Pelé](/players/pele) scored on his debut and over the next 18 years turned Santos into the most famous club on earth.

The Santos of 1958 to 1965 was arguably the greatest club side in history. With Pelé, Coutinho, Pepe, Zito, Gilmar, Mauro, Mengálvio and later [Carlos Alberto](/players/carlos-alberto) and Clodoaldo, under the coach Lula, it won five consecutive Brazilian championships (the Taça Brasil of 1961 to 1965), the Copa Libertadores in 1962 and 1963, and the Intercontinental Cup both years: 5-2 and 2-1 against [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) in 1962, Pelé scoring a hat-trick in Lisbon, and 2-4, 4-2 against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) in 1963. The club toured the world constantly, playing hundreds of friendlies to pay the wages, and the 1969 war in Nigeria is said to have paused for a Santos exhibition. Pelé's 1,000th goal came at the Maracanã in November 1969 and he left in 1974.

The decades after Pelé were quieter: a Brasileirão title in 1968 with him, then nothing nationally until 2002, when Vanderlei Luxemburgo's "Meninos da Vila" of Diego, Robinho, Elano, Alex and Renato won the title as the youngest champions in memory, beating Corinthians in the final with Robinho's pedaladas. A second title followed in 2004.

## Trajectory

The next generation was even more spectacular. [Neymar](/players/neymar) made his debut at 17 in 2009, and with Paulo Henrique Ganso, Elano, Arouca and the coach Muricy Ramalho the club won the 2010 Copa do Brasil (Neymar's goal against Flamengo that year won the FIFA Puskás award) and the 2011 Copa Libertadores, 2-1 against Peñarol with Neymar and Danilo scoring, the first since Pelé. The 2011 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) final, lost 4-0 to Guardiola's Barcelona, was Neymar's audition; he moved to Barcelona in 2013, and [Rodrygo](/players/rodrygo), sold to Real Madrid in 2019, was the next Vila product.

Jorge Sampaoli's 2019 side finished second and Cuca's 2020 team reached the Libertadores final, losing to Palmeiras at the Maracanã, but the club's debts, mismanagement and an endless cycle of coaches caught up with it. In December 2023 Santos were relegated for the first time in their history, a night that ended with riots outside the Vila Belmiro. They won Série B in 2024, and in January 2025 Neymar came home from Al Hilal on a short contract that was extended into 2026, scoring his first goals back in the shirt that made him. Cuca is listed as head coach in the club's current squad data as of November 2026, and Santos are ninth in the 2026 Brasileirão with 47 points from 34 games. Neymar's contract status beyond the 2026 season and his fitness have been the year's running story and are not verifiable here; he remains listed in the squad.

## Players the club produced

No academy in the world has a better claim to the word "produced". [Pelé](/players/pele), Pepe, Coutinho, Clodoaldo, Carlos Alberto (signed from Fluminense but made at Santos), Robinho, Diego, Alex, Elano, Ganso, [Neymar](/players/neymar), [Rodrygo](/players/rodrygo), Gabriel Barbosa, Lucas Lima, Kaio Jorge, Ângelo (sold to Chelsea) and Marcos Leonardo (sold to Benfica) all came through the Vila. The sales of Neymar, Rodrygo and Marcos Leonardo kept the club alive; the failure to replace them explains the relegation.

## Identity and playing style

Santos' identity is attacking football and young players, the "Meninos da Vila" ideal, and every successful Santos team has been built around a teenage star: Pelé, Robinho, Neymar. The club's best modern coaches, Luxemburgo in 2002 and Muricy in 2010-11, played a 4-2-3-1 with a free number ten and wide dribblers. Cuca's 2026 team is pragmatic by necessity, a 4-3-3 that defends deep and relies on Neymar's set pieces and the midfielder João Schmidt's organisation, while the goalkeeper Gabriel Brazão has been the season's best performer. The Vila Belmiro, tiny and intimate, is part of the identity; the club has discussed a new stadium for a decade.

## Rivalries

The Clássico Alvinegro with [Corinthians](/clubs/corinthians) and the Clássico da Saudade with [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras), the latter a nostalgic name for the Pelé-Ademir da Guia era and the fixture of the 2020 Libertadores final, are the two biggest. The San-São with [São Paulo](/clubs/sao-paulo) completes the set of São Paulo state derbies, and the relegation season's defeats to all three made it worse.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Santos are ninth in the Brasileirão, safe from relegation and outside the continental places, with Cuca listed as head coach and Neymar, João Schmidt, Guilherme, Tiquinho Soares and Brazão as the key players. Marcelo Teixeira's board, elected after relegation, has restructured the club's governance and the search for an SAF investor continues. The ambition is a return to the Libertadores and, for Neymar, a dignified final chapter where it began.
