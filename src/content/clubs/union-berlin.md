## History

1. FC Union Berlin were founded in their current form on 20 January 1966 in Köpenick, in the south-east of East Berlin, though the club's roots go back to 1906. In the GDR they were the workers' alternative to Dynamo Berlin, the Stasi-backed club that won ten straight titles; Union's one trophy, the 1968 FDGB-Pokal, and their reputation as a club of dissent ("Eisern Union", iron Union) were the foundation of a fiercely loyal identity. The Stadion An der Alten Försterei, in a forest by the Spree, has been home since 1920.

After reunification the club nearly went bankrupt several times. The 2000-01 season under Georgi Vasilev was a breakthrough: promotion from the Regionalliga and a DFB-Pokal final, lost 2-0 to Schalke. Relegation to the fourth tier followed in 2005, and in 2008 the supporters, with the club unable to afford a stadium that met licensing standards, rebuilt the ground themselves: more than 2,000 volunteers gave 140,000 hours of labour. Some fans also donated blood to raise money. Promotion to the 2. Bundesliga as 3. Liga champions came in 2009.

Urs Fischer, appointed in 2018, won promotion through the 2019 play-off against Stuttgart on away goals, the club's first season in the top flight at 53. What followed was one of the Bundesliga's great overachievements: 11th, 7th, 5th and then 4th in 2022-23, bringing Champions League football to a 22,000-capacity ground (home games were moved to the Olympiastadion). The 2023-24 season turned sour, with Fischer sacked in November during a 16-match winless run, and the club survived only on the final day.

## Trajectory

From the Oberliga in 2006 to the Champions League in 2023 in 17 years, almost entirely through continuity, a sporting director (Oliver Ruhnert) who found undervalued players and a coach who stayed five years. The fall since has been gentler than it might have been: 15th in 2024, 13th in 2025 under Steffen Baumgart, and a mid-table 2025-26. Mauro Lustrinelli, the Swiss coach, was appointed for 2026-27, and the early months have been difficult.

## Players the club produced

Union are not a producer of stars but a maker of careers. Torsten Mattuschka, the cult hero of the 2. Bundesliga years, Christopher Trimmel, the Austrian right-back and captain since 2014, Rani Khedira, Robin Knoche and Grischa Prömel were the backbone of the Fischer side; Max Kruse, Taiwo Awoniyi (sold to Nottingham Forest for a record fee) and Sheraldo Becker provided the goals. Goalkeeper Frederik Rønnow has been the most consistent performer of recent seasons. Young players like Ilyas Ansah represent the club's attempt to develop its own.

## Identity and playing style

Union's identity is the terraces: 18,000 standing places, the annual Christmas carol singing in the stadium, and the sense of a community club in a city of 3.7 million. Fischer's football was a compact [back three](/tactics/back-three), a deep mid-block, [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking), the most efficient low-possession team in the league. Lustrinelli has kept the three-man defence but asked for more possession and a higher press, with mixed results so far.

## Rivalries

Hertha BSC, the West Berlin club, are the city rival, and Union have won most of the recent derbies; Hertha's relegation in 2023 means the fixture is now a cup-only affair. Dynamo Berlin were the GDR-era enemy. [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig), the corporate East German club, are the ideological opposite, and Union fans famously sat in silence for the first 15 minutes of their first Bundesliga meeting in protest.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Union are 16th in the Bundesliga, in the relegation play-off place, with seven points from eight games (one win, four draws, three defeats, seven scored, 19 conceded). They drew 0-0 at home to Dortmund on 17 October, 1-1 at Augsburg on 24 October and 1-1 at home to Köln on 1 November, a run of draws that keeps them out of the bottom two without lifting them clear. The next match is at Freiburg on 8 November.

Lustrinelli's side has the league's second-worst goal difference and an attack that has scored seven times in eight games. Trimmel, Khedira, Rønnow and Danilho Doekhi are the experienced core; the aim for the season has shrunk to staying up, and the club's famous patience with coaches will be tested over the winter.
