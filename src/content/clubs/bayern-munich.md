## History

FC Bayern München were founded on 27 February 1900 by Franz John and a group of members who had walked out of a gymnastics club that refused to let them join the German football federation. For their first six decades they were a respected but not dominant Munich club, winning a first German championship in 1932 under the Jewish president Kurt Landauer and coach Richard Dombi, then suffering under the Nazi years and the post-war reconstruction. When the Bundesliga was founded in 1963 Bayern were not even included; city rivals 1860 Munich were.

Everything changed with the generation that came up together in the mid-1960s: [Franz Beckenbauer](/players/franz-beckenbauer), [Gerd Müller](/players/gerd-muller) and Sepp Maier. Promoted in 1965, they won the DFB-Pokal in 1966 and 1967, the Cup Winners' Cup in 1967, and a first Bundesliga title in 1969 under Branko Zebec. Udo Lattek's side then won three straight league titles (1972-74) and three straight European Cups (1974, 1975, 1976), the first after a replayed final against Atlético Madrid, the second against Leeds in Paris, the third against Saint-Étienne in Glasgow. Müller scored 365 Bundesliga goals in 427 games, a record that stood for half a century.

The 1980s belonged to Karl-Heinz Rummenigge, Klaus Augenthaler and later [Lothar Matthäus](/players/lothar-matthaus), with six titles in nine years but two lost European Cup finals (1982 against Aston Villa, 1987 against Porto). Uli Hoeneß, a player in the 1970s side, became general manager in 1979 at 27 and turned Bayern into the best-run club in Germany, debt-free and commercially dominant. The 1990s brought the "FC Hollywood" era of dressing-room drama, [Jürgen Klinsmann](/players/jurgen-klinsmann), Matthäus and Mario Basler, plus a UEFA Cup in 1996 and the heartbreak of the 1999 Champions League final, lost in injury time to Manchester United.

[Oliver Kahn](/players/oliver-kahn), Stefan Effenberg and Ottmar Hitzfeld avenged that in 2001, beating Valencia on penalties in Milan four days after winning the Bundesliga with a last-minute Patrik Andersson goal at Hamburg. Bayern moved into the Allianz Arena in 2005. The modern dynasty began with [Philipp Lahm](/players/philipp-lahm), [Bastian Schweinsteiger](/players/bastian-schweinsteiger), [Arjen Robben](/players/arjen-robben), [Franck Ribéry](/players/franck-ribery), [Manuel Neuer](/players/manuel-neuer) and Thomas Müller: finalists in 2010 and 2012 (lost on penalties to Chelsea in their own stadium), then the 2013 treble under Jupp Heynckes, sealed with Robben's 89th-minute winner against Dortmund at Wembley.

[Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) (2013-16) won three titles and reshaped the club's thinking; Niko Kovač, then [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick), delivered the 2020 treble behind closed doors, including an 8-2 demolition of Barcelona and a 1-0 final win over PSG in Lisbon. [Robert Lewandowski](/players/robert-lewandowski) broke Müller's single-season record with 41 goals in 2020-21. Bayern won eleven straight titles from 2013 to 2023, lost the 2024 crown to Leverkusen, and have won the last two under [Vincent Kompany](/managers/vincent-kompany).

## Trajectory

Bayern's post-2000 trajectory is a flat line at the top with a handful of blips. They have finished outside the top three only twice this century: fourth in 2006-07, which cost Felix Magath his job and brought Hitzfeld back, and third in 2023-24, when Thomas Tuchel's second season unravelled and Leverkusen went unbeaten. Second-place finishes (2003-04, 2008-09, 2011-12) were treated as crises; the 2011-12 one, when they lost the league to Klopp's Dortmund, the cup final 5-2 to the same opponent and the Champions League final at home, produced the signings of Javi Martínez and Mario Mandžukić and the treble a year later.

In Europe the club has reached eleven European Cup finals, winning six (1974, 1975, 1976, 2001, 2013, 2020). Since 2020 they have been eliminated by the eventual winners or finalists more often than not: PSG in 2021, Villarreal in 2022, Manchester City in 2023, Real Madrid in a 2024 semi-final, Inter in 2025, and in 2025-26 a semi-final against PSG that began with a 5-4 defeat in Paris, a match in which [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) scored and the two sides traded nine goals. PSG went on to retain the trophy.

## Players the club produced

Bayern's academy at Säbener Strasse has produced more German internationals than any other. Beckenbauer, Maier and Müller formed the spine of West Germany's 1972 and 1974 winners. Later generations gave Germany [Lothar Matthäus](/players/lothar-matthaus) (signed from Gladbach, the 1990 World Cup captain), [Philipp Lahm](/players/philipp-lahm) and [Bastian Schweinsteiger](/players/bastian-schweinsteiger), both academy graduates who lifted the 2014 World Cup, Thomas Müller, David Alaba and [Jamal Musiala](/players/jamal-musiala), who joined from Chelsea's academy at 16 and became the Bundesliga's most watched dribbler.

The club has also been a destination for the best of the rest of Germany: [Oliver Kahn](/players/oliver-kahn) from Karlsruhe, [Manuel Neuer](/players/manuel-neuer) from Schalke, [Joshua Kimmich](/players/joshua-kimmich) from Stuttgart via Leipzig, Mario Götze and Robert Lewandowski from Dortmund. The imports who became legends are a shorter but brighter list: Giovane Élber, Ribéry and Robben, Lewandowski (344 goals in 375 games), and [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane), signed from Tottenham in August 2023 for around 100 million euros, who won the Bundesliga Golden Boot in his first two seasons, scored 73 goals in all competitions in 2025-26 and became the first English Ballon d'Or winner since Michael Owen on 26 October 2026.

## Identity and playing style

Bayern's football has always been a combination of technical superiority and relentless pressure, the "Mia san mia" conviction that the club will find a way. Tactically the modern identity was set by Guardiola's [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) in Lahm and Alaba, and sharpened by Flick's extreme [high line](/tactics/high-line) and [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) in 2020.

[Vincent Kompany](/managers/vincent-kompany), appointed in May 2024 after a long search, has blended the two. His Bayern play a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 2-3-5 in possession, with Kimmich dictating from the base, Musiala and [Michael Olise](/players/michael-olise) in the half-spaces, and Kane as a classic target nine who also drops to link play. Without the ball the line is high and the counter-press immediate, and the 2025-26 team was the most prolific in the division by a distance. Kompany's man-management, notably his handling of Kane and Neuer, has been as important as the structure.

The club's structure is as much a part of the identity as the football. Bayern are majority-owned by their 400,000 members, with Adidas, Audi and Allianz holding minority stakes, have reported a profit for more than thirty consecutive years, and have never carried significant debt. The "Mia san mia" culture, the Säbener Strasse training ground where fans can watch sessions, and the Oktoberfest photo shoots are the softer side of a club that is ruthless in the transfer market and in its boardroom, where Hoeneß and Rummenigge remain influential long after their formal retirements.

## Rivalries

**Der Klassiker** with [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) is the defining fixture of the modern Bundesliga: the 2013 Champions League final at Wembley, the title duels of 2011-12 and 2018-19, and the regular transfer traffic from Dortmund to Munich (Götze, Lewandowski, Hummels) that Dortmund fans resent. The 2026-27 meeting on 31 October ended 2-2 at the Allianz Arena with both sides unbeaten.

Within Bavaria, 1860 Munich were the historic rival until their decline. [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) became a true rival in 2024 by taking the title, and [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig) were the designated challengers of the late 2010s. Nationally, the "Bayern-Dusel" (Bayern luck) narrative and the club's habit of signing rivals' best players make them the team everyone else measures themselves against.

## Current situation

Bayern began 2026-27 as champions for the second year running under Kompany, with Kane holding the Ballon d'Or and the Bundesliga's early scoring lead. As of 7 November 2026 they lead the table with 18 points from eight games (five wins, three draws, 27 scored, eight conceded), level on points with Dortmund after the 2-2 Klassiker draw on 31 October. Recent results include a 6-1 win over RB Leipzig on 17 October and a 3-1 win at Freiburg on 25 October. They visit Mainz on 7 November, host Köln on 21 November and travel to Hamburg on 28 November, with a Champions League trip to [Lille](/clubs/lille) on 25 November in between.

The squad is largely the one that won the 2025-26 title, with Kimmich as captain, Musiala and Olise as the creative pair and Luis Díaz, signed from Liverpool in 2025, on the left. The missing piece remains the Champions League, which [PSG](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) retained in May 2026 after that 5-4 semi-final epic in Paris; Kompany's contract and his standing with the board are secure, and the club's stated aim for the season is a seventh European Cup.
