## History

Kawasaki Frontale began in 1955 as the works team of Fujitsu, the electronics company, and for most of its history it was a second-tier club in a city overshadowed by Tokyo and Yokohama on either side. The club joined the J.League's second division in 1999, was promoted for a single season in 2000 and relegated immediately, and returned in 2005 as J2 champions. The name combines "frontier" with the Italian "frontale", and the colours and the dolphin mascot are the product of a 1990s rebrand.

The first good Frontale side was built by Takashi Sekizuka around 2006-09: the Brazilian striker Juninho, the Korean-Japanese forward Jong Tae-se, Kazuki Ganaha and the playmaker Kengo Nakamura, an academy-adjacent signing who stayed 18 years, scored 74 goals from midfield and became the club's symbol. The team finished second in 2006, 2008 and 2009 and reached the 2009 AFC Champions League quarter-finals without winning anything, a reputation for beautiful losing that lasted a decade. Yahiro Kazama's 2012-16 teams, obsessed with possession and technique, refined the style and topped the 2016 aggregate table, only to lose in the play-offs.

## Trajectory

Toru Oniki, Kazama's assistant, was promoted in 2017 and turned the style into titles. Kawasaki won the J1 League in 2017 and 2018, the League Cup in 2019, and then produced the two best seasons in J.League history: the 2020 title with 88 points and 88 goals, and the 2021 title with 92 points, both won by double-digit margins with Yu Kobayashi, Akihiro Ienaga, Ao Tanaka, Kaoru Mitoma and Reo Hatate in the side. Mitoma scored 13 goals in his debut season straight from university and was sold to Brighton in 2021; Tanaka went to Fortuna Düsseldorf and Leeds, Hatate to Celtic, Hidemasa Morita to Santa Clara and Sporting.

The sales thinned the squad and the 2022 and 2023 seasons produced second and eighth place, with the 2023 Emperor's Cup as consolation. Oniki left after 2023 for Kashima Antlers; Shigetoshi Hasebe took the club to the first AFC Champions League Elite final in 2025, beating Al Sadd and Al Nassr in the Jeddah finals week before losing 2-0 to [Al Ahli](/clubs/al-ahli). The club's current squad data does not confirm the head coach for the 2026-27 season, so none is listed here. Kawasaki are eighth in the 2026-27 J1 League with 22 points from 14 games.

## Players the club produced

Kawasaki's academy and its partnership with the University of Tsukuba produced Mitoma, Tanaka, Morita, Hatate, Ryota Oshima, Shintaro Kurumaya, Kaoru Mitoma's youth team-mate Koki Tsukagawa and the goalkeeper Sota Nakamura's successors; the club's youth system is ranked alongside Kashima's and Gamba Osaka's as the best in Japan. Kengo Nakamura, Kobayashi and Ienaga were signed young and became legends, and the Brazilians Juninho, Eduardo, Leandro Damião and Marcinho, and briefly a young Hulk on loan in 2006, were the imports.

## Identity and playing style

Frontale's identity is possession with purpose: Kazama's "ball-oriented" football, which asked every player to receive on the half-turn and play forward, and Oniki's refinement of it into a 4-3-3 with inverted wingers (Mitoma, Ienaga), a creative number eight (Oshima, Tanaka) and quick combinations around the box. It was Japan's closest equivalent to [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) and [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), and the club's community programme, which has made Todoroki a sell-out despite the athletics track, is the other half of the model. The 2025 Asian run under Hasebe was built on the same ideas with more defensive pragmatism.

## Rivalries

The Kanagawa derby with [Yokohama F. Marinos](/clubs/yokohama-f-marinos) is the sharpest, the 2019 and 2022 champions against the 2017-21 dynasty, and the Tamagawa Clásico with FC Tokyo, across the river, is the oldest. [Kashima Antlers](/clubs/kashima-antlers) are the historic rival for the title of Japan's best club, with Oniki's move adding an edge, and the 2016 and 2020s title races against [Urawa Red Diamonds](/clubs/urawa-red-diamonds) completed the set.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Kawasaki are eighth in the 2026-27 J1 League with 22 points from 14 games, in mid-table, with no head coach confirmed in the current squad data. Yasuto Wakizaka, Marcinho, Erison, Yuto Tokuda and the veteran Oshima are the core. The 2025 Asian final confirmed the club's standing as Japan's most consistent continental performer of the 2020s; the aim under the new calendar is a fifth J1 title and a new football-specific stadium to replace Todoroki's athletics track.
