## History

Venezia were founded in 1907 and have always played at the Stadio Pier Luigi Penzo on the island of Sant'Elena, a ground reachable by water bus or on foot across the city and the second-oldest in Italian professional football. The great years were short: a 1940-41 Coppa Italia won against Roma, with Valentino Mazzola and Ezio Loik in the side before Torino bought them both, and a third-place finish in Serie A in 1941-42, the club's best. Venezia then spent decades in the lower leagues, with a brief Serie A return in 1961-62 and 1966-67, before the shoe magnate Maurizio Zamparini's ownership produced promotion in 1998 under Walter Novellino and three seasons in the top flight with [Álvaro Recoba](/players/alvaro-recoba), on loan from Inter, scoring 11 in 1998-99, and Filippo Maniero the goalscorer. Zamparini took the Venezia squad to Palermo in 2002 and the club collapsed behind him.

## Trajectory

Venezia have been declared bankrupt three times this century, in 2005, 2009 and 2015, each time refounded under a new name and sent to Serie D or Serie C2. The modern revival began in 2015 under the American lawyer Joe Tacopina, who hired Filippo Inzaghi as coach: Serie D and Lega Pro were won in consecutive seasons, and the club reached the Serie B play-offs in 2018. Duncan Niederauer, the former chief executive of the New York Stock Exchange, took over in 2020, hired Paolo Zanetti, and Venezia won the 2021 play-off final against Cittadella to return to Serie A after 19 years; they went down at once, bottom in 2021-22, despite the attention their kits and their lagoon ground attracted worldwide. Paolo Vanoli's 2023-24 side won the play-off final against Cremonese; Eusebio Di Francesco's 2024-25 side finished 19th. Giovanni Stroppa, appointed in summer 2025, won Serie B outright with 82 points, the club's first second-tier title since 1966, but the Serie A start was so poor that he was sacked on 13 October 2026 with two wins in eight, and Davide Nicola, the survival specialist who had kept up Crotone, Torino, Salernitana, Empoli and Cagliari, was appointed the next day.

## Players the club produced

Mazzola and Loik are the historic names, two of the Grande Torino who began in Venice. The modern club has sold well: Gianluca Busio, the American midfielder bought from Sporting Kansas City in 2021, is still the heart of the team at 24; Joel Pohjanpalo, the Finnish striker who scored 22 in Serie B in 2023-24, was sold to Palermo; Jay Idzes went to Sassuolo, Issa Doumbia to Sporting CP for €20m in summer 2026, Michael Svoboda to Brighton, and Tanner Tessmann to Lyon. Christian Maggio and Stefano Bettarini came through in the 1990s, and the captain of the 2021 promotion, Pietro Ceccaroni, and the playmaker Mattia Aramu are the recent folk heroes.

## Identity and playing style

Venezia's brand, the orange, black and green kits designed with the fashion house Bureau Borsche and sold in Tokyo and Los Angeles, has outrun its football. Stroppa's 3-5-2 won Serie B on organisation and the goals of Andrea Adorante and Akor Adams, the Nigerian striker bought from Sevilla for €16m plus add-ons, the club's record. Nicola, as always, has gone for a compact 3-5-2 that defends deep and counters, built on Lorenzo Montipò in goal, Armel Bella-Kotchap, Marin Šverko and Juan Jesus, 35, at the back, Busio, Simon Sohm and Toma Bašić in midfield, and Adams, Albion Rrahmani and John Yeboah up front. The 1-0 win over Napoli in October was the template.

## Rivalries

The Derby del Veneto against Padova and Vicenza are the historic fixtures, and the matches with Verona and [Udinese](/clubs/udinese), the Derby del Triveneto, are the north-eastern rivalries that come alive in Serie A. The supporters in the Curva Sud have a deep dislike of Vicenza and Treviso and a friendship with Lecce.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Venezia are 19th in Serie A with six points from 11 matches, two wins and nine defeats, a point above Udinese and two from safety. The wins, 1-0 over Napoli and a 2-1 against Lecce, and the Coppa Italia first-round win over Modena are what there is to celebrate; the 2-1 exit to Udinese in the cup's second round and nine defeats are the rest. Nicola's record of keeping clubs up from worse positions is the reason for hope, and Niederauer's board sanctioned the Adams fee and the Bella-Kotchap signing to give him a squad capable of it. The new stadium at Tessera, near the airport, remains a plan rather than a building.
