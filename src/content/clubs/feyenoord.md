## History

Feyenoord were founded in 1908 in the Feijenoord district on the south bank of the Maas, the docklands side of Rotterdam, and the club has never lost that working-class identity. De Kuip, opened in 1937, is still the most atmospheric ground in the Netherlands and the only one where you can feel the stands shake.

The club's finest hour came on 6 May 1970 in Milan, when Ernst Happel's team beat [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) 2-1 after extra time to become the first Dutch winners of the European Cup, a year before [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) began their run of three. Ove Kindvall scored the winner; Willem van Hanegem ran the midfield; Coen Moulijn, the club's most beloved player, provided the wing play. The Intercontinental Cup against Estudiantes followed. Four years later Wiel Coerver's side added the 1974 UEFA Cup against Tottenham.

The 1980s were lean until Johan Cruyff, then 36 and in dispute with Ajax, joined for the 1983-84 season and won the double alongside a young [Ruud Gullit](/players/ruud-gullit). A 1993 title under Willem van Hanegem and the 1999 championship under Leo Beenhakker were followed by Bert van Marwijk's 2002 UEFA Cup, won 3-2 against Borussia Dortmund in their own stadium with two goals from Pierre van Hooijdonk and one from a 20-year-old Jon Dahl Tomasson.

Then came the fall. Financial mismanagement in the late 2000s brought the club close to bankruptcy, a 10-0 defeat at PSV in October 2010 and a tenth-place finish. Ronald Koeman rebuilt with academy players, and [Giovanni van Bronckhorst](/managers/giovanni-van-bronckhorst) completed the recovery with the 2017 title, clinched on the final day with a Dirk Kuyt hat-trick, the club's first championship in 18 years. [Arne Slot](/managers/arne-slot) arrived in 2021, reached the inaugural Conference League final in 2022 and won the 2023 Eredivisie with a thrilling pressing side built around Orkun Kökçü, Santiago Giménez and Lutsharel Geertruida, before leaving for Liverpool in 2024.

## Trajectory

Feyenoord's record since 2000 is more volatile than Ajax's or PSV's: two titles in 2017 and 2023, four cup wins, but also a tenth-place finish in 2010-11 and a string of mid-table seasons between 2006 and 2009 when the club's finances were collapsing. Since 2012 they have been a consistent top-three side, with a worst finish of fifth in 2020-21.

Europe has been a mixed story. The 2002 UEFA Cup remains the last continental trophy, but Slot's run to the 2022 Conference League final in Tirana, lost 1-0 to Roma, and Champions League league-phase campaigns in 2023-24 and 2024-25 restored the club's standing. The 2025-26 season was a steep decline: Brian Priske lasted months, [Robin van Persie](/players/robin-van-persie) could not reverse the slide and was sacked in June 2026, and the team finished 19 points behind PSV.

## Players the club produced

Varkenoord, the academy across the road from De Kuip, has supplied the Dutch national team for generations. Its products include Wim Jansen and Wim van Hanegem from the 1970 side, [Giovanni van Bronckhorst](/managers/giovanni-van-bronckhorst), [Robin van Persie](/players/robin-van-persie), Royston Drenthe, Jonathan de Guzmán, Georginio Wijnaldum, Stefan de Vrij, Daryl Janmaat, Bruno Martins Indi, Tonny Vilhena, Lutsharel Geertruida and Quinten Timber. [Ruud Gullit](/players/ruud-gullit) spent three seasons at the club after leaving Haarlem; Dirk Kuyt and Salomon Kalou made their names at De Kuip; and Johan Cruyff's single season in 1983-84 is one of the great footnotes of Dutch football.

## Identity and playing style

Feyenoord's identity is effort and noise: a club that expects its players to run, tackle and respect the shirt before anything else, and a crowd that punishes anything less. [Arne Slot](/managers/arne-slot) gave that identity a modern tactical shape, with a 4-2-3-1 that pressed high and played vertically, but the underlying expectation is older than any coach.

[Giovanni van Bronckhorst](/managers/giovanni-van-bronckhorst), who returned on 17 June 2026 on a two-year contract with an option, is more pragmatic. His teams defend in a compact mid-block, attack quickly through the wide players and are strong at [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces); his Rangers side reached the 2022 Europa League final that way. He has said publicly that the first months of 2026-27 are about structure before style.

The stadium question hangs over the club. Plans for a new 63,000-seat Feyenoord City stadium on the Maas were abandoned in 2022 after years of cost overruns and supporter opposition, and De Kuip, nearly 90 years old, will be renovated instead. The club's finances have improved markedly since the near-bankruptcy of 2010, helped by the sales of Kökçü to Benfica, Giménez to AC Milan and Geertruida to RB Leipzig for a combined fee above €80 million, and the Champions League campaigns of 2023-24 and 2024-25, when Feyenoord beat Bayern Munich 3-0 at De Kuip, brought the revenues to compete with PSV for the first time in a generation. Van Bronckhorst's job is to translate that money into a side that can last.

## Rivalries

De Klassieker against [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) is Dutch football's great rivalry, and one with real edge: Rotterdam against Amsterdam, the port against the capital, with away fans banned from the fixture since 2009. The rivalry with [PSV](/clubs/psv-eindhoven) matters for the title but lacks the same heat. The Rotterdam derby against Sparta is older than the club's professional history, and Excelsior, the city's third club, has long served as a feeder.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Feyenoord are, surprisingly, top of the Eredivisie after eleven rounds, a point ahead of Ajax and two clear of PSV. They are also in the Champions League league phase, where three points from four games places them in the lower half of the table. The squad was turned over heavily in the summer and Van Bronckhorst has asked for caution; both of his previous successes, the 2017 title and the 2022 European run, came in his second season at a club. The early-season table nonetheless suggests the rebuild is going faster than anyone in Rotterdam dared hope.
