## History

Sport Lisboa e Benfica were founded on 28 February 1904 by a group of young men meeting in a Lisbon pharmacy, and the club's founding principle, written into its statutes, was that it should be a club of the people and should field Portuguese players. That second rule was relaxed in the 1970s, but the first has never changed: Benfica remain the most popular club in Portugal by a distance, with more than 300,000 members and support stretching across the Portuguese-speaking world.

The club's greatest era began when Béla Guttmann, the itinerant Hungarian coach, arrived in 1959. Guttmann promoted a generation of African-born players from Portugal's colonies, including Mário Coluna from Mozambique, and in 1961 Benfica beat Barcelona 3-2 in Bern to end Real Madrid's five-year hold on the European Cup. The following year a 19-year-old from Lourenço Marques called [Eusébio](/players/eusebio) scored twice in a 5-3 win over Real Madrid in Amsterdam, a final in which Ferenc Puskás scored a hat-trick and still lost. When the board refused Guttmann a pay rise he left, reportedly saying that Benfica would not win another European Cup for a hundred years. They have since lost five finals, in 1963, 1965, 1968, 1988 and 1990, and the "Guttmann curse" is part of the club's folklore.

Eusébio stayed for 15 years and scored 473 goals in 440 games. The team around him, with Coluna, José Augusto, Simões and José Torres, won eight titles in the 1960s and reached three more European Cup finals, losing to AC Milan, Inter and Manchester United. The 1980s brought another strong era under Sven-Göran Eriksson and Toni, with Nené, Chalana and Diamantino, and the 1988 and 1990 finals, lost to PSV on penalties and to Milan 1-0.

The 1990s and early 2000s were the club's lowest period. Financial chaos, a revolving door of presidents and coaches and the sale of the best players to Italy and Spain left Benfica trophyless from 1996 to 2004 and sixth in the league in 2000-01, the worst finish in the club's history. Giovanni Trapattoni's 2005 title ended the drought, and Jorge Jesus, appointed in 2009, built the most consistent Benfica side of the modern era: three titles, two Europa League finals in 2013 and 2014 (both lost, to Chelsea and on penalties to Sevilla) and a domestic Treble in 2014. Rui Vitória extended the run to four consecutive championships, a club record, and Roger Schmidt's 2022-23 title came with a Champions League quarter-final. [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) returned to the club in 2025 and was then lured back to Real Madrid in July 2026, leaving [Marco Silva](/managers/marco-silva) to pick up a squad that had finished second to Sporting.

The modern club is also a story of institutional scale. Benfica are a multi-sport organisation with professional teams in basketball, futsal, handball and volleyball, and the football club's finances rest on three pillars: the largest membership in Portugal, broadcasting revenue from a league in which Benfica games are the most watched, and player trading. The Estádio da Luz, rebuilt for Euro 2004 on the site of the old 120,000-capacity ground, hosted the 2014 Champions League final and the 2020 final between PSG and Bayern. The eagle that flies from the stands before every home match, named Vitória, is one of the great rituals of European football.

## Trajectory

Benfica's 38 league titles are a Portuguese record, eight more than [Porto](/clubs/porto) and sixteen more than [Sporting](/clubs/sporting-cp). The twenty-first century has been divided neatly in two. From 2000 to 2009 the club won one title and finished as low as sixth and fourth while Porto, under Mourinho and his successors, dominated. From 2010 to 2019 Benfica won seven of ten championships, including the four-in-a-row from 2014 to 2017, and became a Champions League regular. Since 2019 the balance has tilted again: one title in 2023, two third-place finishes in 2021 and 2022, and three consecutive runners-up spots from 2024 to 2026, the last two behind Sporting.

In Europe the record since 2000 includes the two Europa League finals of 2013 and 2014, Champions League quarter-finals in 2006, 2012, 2016 and 2023, and the 2024-25 Club World Cup, where Benfica beat Bayern Munich in the group stage. Benfica are not in the 2026-27 Champions League league phase, a significant financial and sporting setback.

## Players the club produced

Benfica's Seixal academy, opened in 2006, is one of the most productive in Europe and the engine of the club's finances. Its graduates include [Bernardo Silva](/players/bernardo-silva), [Rúben Dias](/players/ruben-dias), [João Félix](/players/joao-felix), [João Neves](/players/joao-neves), [Gonçalo Ramos](/players/goncalo-ramos), António Silva, Nélson Semedo, Renato Sanches, Ederson, Gonçalo Guedes and Florentino Luís. Earlier generations produced [Rui Costa](/players/rui-costa), who returned as president, João Pinto, Nuno Gomes, Vítor Paneira and, in the 1980s, Diamantino and Chalana.

The club's record in developing signings is equally strong: [Ángel Di María](/players/angel-di-maria), [David Luiz](/players/david-luiz), Fábio Coentrão, Axel Witsel, Nemanja Matić, Jan Oblak, Nicolás Otamendi, Enzo Fernández and Darwin Núñez all passed through the Estádio da Luz on the way to the biggest clubs in Europe, usually leaving for record fees. And above all of them stands [Eusébio](/players/eusebio), whose statue outside the stadium is the first thing visitors see.

The economics are striking. Between 2014 and 2024 Benfica generated more than a billion euros in player sales, including [João Félix](/players/joao-felix) to Atlético Madrid for €126 million in 2019, then the third-highest fee in history, Enzo Fernández to Chelsea for €121 million in January 2023, Darwin Núñez to Liverpool for up to €100 million and [João Neves](/players/joao-neves) to Paris Saint-Germain in 2024. Jan Oblak, Ederson, Nemanja Matić, [Rúben Dias](/players/ruben-dias) and [Bernardo Silva](/players/bernardo-silva) all left for sums that would have been unthinkable in the years when the club was nearly bankrupt. The Seixal model, in which a 12-year-old is scouted, educated and promoted through a single coherent playing philosophy, is studied by clubs across Europe, and the Benfica Campus has become a selling point in itself when recruiting young players from Portugal, Brazil and Africa.

## Identity and playing style

Benfica's identity is attacking football and a demand for victory in every match, a product of the club's size and of a crowd that fills 60,000 seats for routine league games. The Jorge Jesus era set the modern template: [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with a [high line](/tactics/high-line), full-backs as auxiliary wingers and a focus on rapid, vertical attacks through the half-spaces. [Jorge Jesus](/managers/jorge-jesus) is now Portugal's national coach, but his influence on the club's expectations remains.

[Marco Silva](/managers/marco-silva), appointed in June 2026, is a pragmatist by comparison. His Fulham sides defended in a compact 4-2-3-1 and attacked at pace through wide players, and his early months at Benfica have been about process and calm after Mourinho's confrontational year. The risk, familiar from his time at Everton, is that his teams can lose shape when they chase games, and Benfica are expected to chase every game they are not winning.

The tactical debate in Lisbon in late 2026 is about intensity. Mourinho's Benfica were compact and transition-based, winning the big matches but drawing too many small ones; Silva has asked for a higher press and quicker circulation, with the full-backs pushed on and Fredrik Aursnes used as the connecting midfielder. The supporters' benchmark is the Jesus era, when Benfica scored freely and the Luz expected three goals a game.

## Rivalries

O Clássico against [Porto](/clubs/porto) is the fiercest fixture in Portugal, north against south, the industrial second city against the capital, with decades of mutual accusations of refereeing bias and boardroom skulduggery. The Lisbon derby against [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) is older and, for most of the city, more personal: families are divided between the two clubs and the stadiums are a few kilometres apart. The 1990s and 2000s belonged to Porto; the last three seasons have made Sporting the more pressing rival. The Lisbon derby has also become a transfer battleground, with the two clubs competing for the same Portuguese and Brazilian teenagers and the same coaches, and the 2026 summer, in which both changed manager, was followed obsessively by a city that treats the fixture as a referendum on who runs the capital.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Benfica are second in the Primeira Liga after ten rounds, five points behind Porto and four ahead of Sporting, and are not in the Champions League. Silva's squad was largely kept together in the summer, with the Greek striker Vangelis Pavlidis and goalkeeper Anatoliy Trubin the spine of the side. The club's priorities are a 39th title, a return to the Champions League and a successful transition from the Mourinho year; the Portuguese press has given Silva respect, but supporters at the Luz measure coaches in trophies and the pressure will mount quickly if Porto pull further clear.
