## History

Parma were founded in December 1913 as Verdi Foot Ball Club, named for the composer born nearby, and for 77 years were a lower-league club in the richest small city in Italy. The modern Parma was created by Nevio Scala, the coach who took them to Serie A for the first time in 1990, and by Calisto Tanzi, the owner of the Parmalat dairy group, who paid for what followed. Between 1992 and 2002 Parma won the Coppa Italia three times, the Cup Winners' Cup in 1993 against Royal Antwerp at Wembley, the UEFA Super Cup against Milan, two UEFA Cups, in 1995 against Juventus and in 1999 against Marseille in Moscow, and the 1999 Supercoppa, and twice finished second in Serie A. The 1998-99 side under Alberto Malesani, with [Gianluigi Buffon](/players/gianluigi-buffon) in goal, [Fabio Cannavaro](/players/fabio-cannavaro) and Lilian Thuram in defence, Juan Sebastián Veron and Dino Baggio in midfield and [Hernán Crespo](/players/hernan-crespo) and Enrico Chiesa up front, is remembered as the best team never to win a Scudetto; Tanzi vetoed the sale of the stars until he could not.

The names from that decade are extraordinary for a club in a city of 200,000: [Gianfranco Zola](/players/gianfranco-zola), Hristo Stoichkov, Faustino Asprilla, Tomas Brolin, Tino Asprilla, Nestor Sensini, Roberto Mussi, Antonio Benarrivo, Luigi Apolloni, Alberto Di Chiara, Lorenzo Minotti, Diego Fuser, Ariel Ortega and Adriano. Parmalat collapsed in December 2003 in Europe's biggest corporate fraud, with a €14bn hole in its accounts, and the club went into administration.

## Trajectory

Parma survived the first bankruptcy in a reduced form, avoiding relegation in a 2005 play-out against Bologna, were relegated in 2008 and came back at once, and under Tommaso Ghirardi's ownership finished sixth in 2013-14 under Roberto Donadoni, only to be refused a UEFA licence for unpaid taxes. The following season the club could not pay its players, had points deducted, finished bottom with 19 points, and in March 2015 was declared bankrupt for a second time and dissolved. The city's business community, led by the pasta firm Barilla and seven other local companies, refounded it as Parma Calcio 1913 in Serie D, with the captain Alessandro Lucarelli the only player to stay. Three straight promotions, Serie D in 2016, Lega Pro via the play-offs in 2017 and second place in Serie B in 2018 under Roberto D'Aversa, took Parma back to Serie A in three years, a record for Italian football. The club was then docked five points for a text-message scandal, stayed up for three seasons, and was relegated in 2021 under the American Krause Group, which had bought it the previous year.

Fabio Pecchia's side won Serie B in 2023-24 and finished 16th in 2024-25, Pecchia being replaced in February 2025 by Cristian Chivu, who saved the club and left for Inter. Carlos Cuesta, a 29-year-old Spaniard who had been Mikel Arteta's assistant at Arsenal, was appointed in June 2025 and finished 13th with 45 points; he left by mutual consent on 27 September 2026 after a poor start, and Alberto Gilardino, the former Parma striker and Genoa coach, was appointed two days later.

## Players the club produced

Buffon, who debuted at 17 against Milan in 1995, and Cannavaro, bought from Napoli at 21, both became world champions in 2006; Thuram, Crespo, Veron and Adriano were sold for enormous fees. The modern academy has been productive again: Giovanni Fabbian's contemporary Adrián Bernabé came from Manchester City; Simon Sohm and Ange-Yoan Bonny, sold to Inter for €23m in 2025, Giovanni Leoni, sold to Liverpool for €31m the same summer at 18, Enrico Del Prato, the captain, and the Serbian goalkeeper Zion Suzuki, the Japanese international sold in 2026, have all been developed and moved on. Alberto Gilardino himself scored 23 goals in 2002-03 before Milan bought him.

## Identity and playing style

Scala's Parma played a 5-3-2 of extraordinary attacking full-backs; Malesani's a 3-5-2 built on Veron. Cuesta brought [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and a 4-3-3 from the Arteta school, which produced good football and few goals. Gilardino plays a more direct 3-5-2 with Diego Carlos, signed from Aston Villa, in the centre of defence, Vincent Sierro, Mandela Keita and Benjamin Cremaschi, the American from Inter Miami, in midfield, Bernabé as the playmaker and El Bilal Touré, on loan from Atalanta, Pontus Almqvist and the Croatian Matija Frigan up front. The Stadio Ennio Tardini, the compact ground in the city centre, is due to be rebuilt.

## Rivalries

The derby with Reggiana, 30 kilometres along the Via Emilia, is the most heartfelt; Piacenza and Modena are the other neighbours. In Serie A the fixture against [Bologna](/clubs/bologna) is the Emilian derby, and the 1990s produced a rivalry with [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), whom Parma beat in the 1995 UEFA Cup final and the 1999 Coppa Italia final.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Parma are 15th in Serie A with eight points from ten matches, two wins, two draws and six defeats, and have scored only eight goals, the joint-fewest in the league. The 3-0 win at Atalanta in October, Gilardino's second game, was the one bright afternoon of the autumn; the Coppa Italia ended in the second round. Krause Group, which has invested more than €200m in the club and the Tardini project, wants stability rather than ambition this season, and the hope is that Gilardino, who kept Genoa up twice, can do the same in the city where he was once the striker.
