## History

AJ Auxerre were founded in 1905 by Abbé Deschamps, a Burgundian priest whose name is on the stadium, and for most of their history were an amateur club. One man changed that: Guy Roux, appointed player-coach in 1961 at 22, who stayed 44 years. He took Auxerre into the second division in 1974 and the first in 1980, and built, in a town of 35,000 on the Yonne, an academy that produced a national team's worth of players and a club that finished in the European places for two decades.

The peak was 1995-96: the double, the league title and the Coupe de France, with Lionel Charbonnier, Laurent Blanc (for one season), Corentin Martins, Sabri Lamouchi and the young Stéphane Guivarc'h. The club reached the 1993 UEFA Cup semi-final and the 1997 Champions League quarter-final, lost to Dortmund. Cup wins followed in 1994, 2003 and 2005 (Djibril Cissé, Philippe Mexès and Olivier Kapo having come through), and Roux retired in 2005 after a final cup success. Jean Fernandez took the club to third in 2010 and into the Champions League; two years later, in 2012, Auxerre were relegated after 32 consecutive seasons in the top flight.

Ten years in Ligue 2 followed, including a 2015 cup final lost to PSG and a brush with the third tier. Jean-Marc Furlan won promotion through the 2022 play-off against Saint-Étienne, Christophe Pélissier won Ligue 2 in 2024 after an immediate relegation, and the club has been in Ligue 1 since, with the Belgian-English coach Will Still appointed for 2026-27 after spells at Reims, Lens and Southampton.

## Trajectory

The Roux era (1980-2005) was an unbroken quarter-century in the top division with four cups and a title; the post-Roux decade declined slowly to relegation in 2012; the Ligue 2 decade was spent rebuilding under the ownership of the Chinese group ORG Packaging from 2016. Since 2022 the club has been promoted twice and relegated once, and the 2024-25 and 2025-26 seasons were the first consecutive top-flight campaigns since 2012.

## Players the club produced

The Auxerre academy is one of the great production lines of French football: Éric Cantona (debut 1983), Basile Boli, Jean-Marc Ferreri, Alain Goma, Lilian Laslandes, Guivarc'h, Cissé (the 2002 and 2004 top scorer), Mexès, Kapo, Jean-Alain Boumsong, Bacary Sagna, Benoît Pedretti, Younès Kaboul, Yaya Sanogo and Paul-Georges Ntep. Roux's policy was to sell one star a year and reinvest. Among signings, Blanc, Martins and Lamouchi were the pick of the 1990s. Gaëtan Perrin and Lassine Sinayoko have been the leaders of the promotion sides, Donovan Léon the goalkeeper.

## Identity and playing style

Roux's Auxerre played a disciplined 4-4-2 built on a strong defence and the pace of academy forwards, and the club's identity is still that of the frugal, self-sufficient outsider. Pélissier's promotion side used a 3-4-3 with high pressing; Will Still, who made his name with Reims's long unbeaten run in 2022-23, plays a 4-2-3-1 with a compact mid-block, [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through fast wingers and attention to [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces). The early 2026-27 results have shown a side that scores but concedes heavily.

## Rivalries

The Derby de Bourgogne against Dijon, and the longer-standing rivalry with [Troyes](/clubs/troyes), 90 kilometres away, are the regional fixtures; Troyes's promotion for 2026-27 has restored that one to Ligue 1. [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) were the cup-final opponents of 2015, and the 1990s matches with Marseille and PSG were the big days of the Roux era.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Auxerre are 14th in Ligue 1 on ten points from nine games (three wins, one draw, five defeats), with 13 scored and 23 conceded, the worst defence in the division. They won 3-1 at Le Havre on 18 October, drew 1-1 at home to Le Mans on 25 October and lost 5-1 at Strasbourg on 1 November. Paris FC visit the Abbé-Deschamps on 7 November.

Still's first months have exposed a leaky defence, and the squad, built around Perrin, Sinayoko, Jubal and Léon, needs to tighten up. The aim is a third consecutive season of survival, and the gap to the relegation places is two points.
