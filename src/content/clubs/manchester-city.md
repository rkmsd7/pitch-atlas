## History

Manchester City were founded in 1880 as St Mark's (West Gorton), a church team set up to keep young men out of trouble in the east of the city, becoming Ardwick in 1887 and Manchester City in 1894. They won the FA Cup in 1904 with Billy Meredith, the Welsh winger who was the game's first superstar, and moved to Maine Road in Moss Side in 1923, a ground that held 84,569 for an FA Cup tie against Stoke in 1934, still the record attendance for an English club ground.

The first great side won the title in 1937 and were relegated the following season while scoring more goals than any team in the division, a City story if ever there was one. The 1956 FA Cup final is remembered for goalkeeper Bert Trautmann, a former German prisoner of war, playing the last 17 minutes with a broken neck. Joe Mercer and Malcolm Allison then built the finest City side of the pre-Abu Dhabi era: champions in 1968 with Colin Bell, Francis Lee and Mike Summerbee, FA Cup in 1969, League Cup and Cup Winners' Cup in 1970.

What followed was three decades of decline that gave the club its self-mocking identity. Relegation in 1983 and 1987, Peter Swales's chairmanship, Francis Lee's return as owner, and the drop to the third tier in 1998, when City played Macclesfield and York while [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) were winning the treble. Paul Dickov's equaliser in the 1999 play-off final against Gillingham, and the 2002 First Division title under Kevin Keegan, brought them back. The move to the City of Manchester Stadium in 2003 gave them a home fit for what came next.

Sheikh Mansour's Abu Dhabi United Group bought the club in September 2008 and signed Robinho on the first day. Roberto Mancini won the FA Cup in 2011 and then, on 13 May 2012, the title: [Sergio Agüero](/players/sergio-aguero)'s goal in the 94th minute against QPR, 93:20 on the clock, winning City's first championship in 44 years on goal difference from United. Manuel Pellegrini won the league in 2014. [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) arrived in 2016 and, over ten seasons, won six Premier Leagues, including 100 points in 2018 and four in a row from 2021 to 2024, the Champions League in 2023 as part of a treble, and 20 trophies in total. He left in summer 2026 and [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca), his former assistant, was appointed.

## Trajectory

No club in England has travelled further in 25 years. In 2000-01 City were relegated from the Premier League; a year later they were back as champions of the First Division with 99 points under Keegan; from 2002 to 2009 they were a mid-table side, finishing between eighth and 16th. The 2008 takeover changed the slope: fifth in 2010, third in 2011, champions in 2012.

Under Guardiola the pattern was near-monotony. From 2017-18 to 2023-24 City won six of seven titles, missing only 2019-20 to Liverpool, and set records for points (100), goals (106) and consecutive wins (18). The 2022-23 treble, with [Erling Haaland](/players/erling-haaland) scoring 52 goals and [Rodri](/players/rodri) the winner in the Champions League final against [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan) in Istanbul, was the peak.

The 2024-25 season, with Rodri injured and the squad ageing, brought third place and no trophy, the first blank since 2017. Guardiola's response was a January rebuild: Omar Marmoush, Nico González, Abdukodir Khusanov and Vitor Reis. In 2025-26 City finished second, seven points behind [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), but won both domestic cups: the League Cup 2-0 against Arsenal in March, with two goals from academy product Nico O'Reilly, and the FA Cup 1-0 against Chelsea on 16 May, Antoine Semenyo flicking in Haaland's cross. Guardiola announced in May that he would leave after ten years.

The 115 charges of alleged financial breaches, brought by the Premier League in February 2023, hung over all of this. The independent commission's verdict and any sanction should be checked against current reporting before being stated.

## Players the club produced

City's academy, housed alongside the first team at the City Football Academy since 2014, has finally begun to deliver. [Phil Foden](/players/phil-foden), from Stockport, is the standard-bearer: a Premier League Player of the Season in 2024 and the most gifted English player of his generation. Rico Lewis, Nico O'Reilly, James McAtee and Cole Palmer, who left for [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in 2023 and became England's best attacking player, all came through the same pipeline. Earlier generations produced Colin Bell's era partners, Paul Lake, Shaun Wright-Phillips, Micah Richards and Kieran Trippier.

The modern legends were signed. [Sergio Agüero](/players/sergio-aguero), 260 goals and the 93:20 moment; [Vincent Kompany](/players/vincent-kompany-player), captain of the first four titles; [Kevin De Bruyne](/players/kevin-de-bruyne), the Premier League's greatest creator; David Silva, Yaya Touré, Joe Hart and Fernandinho. [Rodri](/players/rodri), signed from [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) in 2019, won the 2024 Ballon d'Or and the 2026 World Cup Golden Ball with [Spain](/nations/spain). [Erling Haaland](/players/erling-haaland), signed from [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) for £51m in 2022, has broken most of the club's scoring records.

## Identity and playing style

[Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca) learned under Guardiola as his assistant in 2020-21 and his football is a recognisable branch of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play): a 4-3-3 that becomes a 3-2-5 or 3-1-6 in possession, a goalkeeper who starts the build-up, an [inverted full-back](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) stepping into midfield, and wingers held wide and high to stretch the pitch. His Chelsea were criticised for slow build-up and praised for control; his City are expected to look similar but with far more talent.

The 2026 rebuild gave him the pieces. Elliot Anderson, signed from [Nottingham Forest](/clubs/nottingham-forest) for a club-record £116m in June, has become the midfield engine. [Enzo Fernández](/players/enzo-fernandez), signed from Chelsea for £125m on deadline day, equalling the British record, plays as the deeper playmaker. Ayyoub Bouaddi, 19, arrived from [Lille](/clubs/lille) for around £86m as the long-term number six. Gianluigi Donnarumma, who arrived in 2025, is the goalkeeper, with Gerónimo Rulli as the new deputy. In attack, Haaland remains the reference point, with Semenyo (a January 2026 signing from Bournemouth), Iliman Ndiaye (from Everton), [Jérémy Doku](/players/jeremy-doku) and Rayan Cherki around him. Marc Guéhi and Rayan Aït-Nouri, both January 2026 arrivals, anchor a defence with [Rúben Dias](/players/ruben-dias) and [Joško Gvardiol](/players/josko-gvardiol).

Maresca's teams press in a structured, zonal way rather than man-to-man, and they are patient: City's possession figures in 2026-27 are the highest in the league. The early concern, as it was at Chelsea, is what happens when a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block) refuses to come out.

## Rivalries

The Manchester derby with [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) was for decades a story of the noisy neighbours losing. Since 2008 it has turned: City's 6-1 at Old Trafford in October 2011, the 2012 title won on goal difference, the 2023 FA Cup final (City 2-1) and the 2024 final (United 2-1). [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) became the fiercest sporting rivalry of the Guardiola years through the 2019 and 2022 title races, and City won 3-0 at Anfield on 11 October 2026 in Maresca's first visit. [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), managed by Guardiola's former assistant, are the rivalry of the present: three title races, the 2026 League Cup final, and the question of which of the two Guardiola schools now owns English football.

## Current situation

City are second in early November 2026, three points behind Arsenal: seven wins and two defeats from nine, 20 goals scored, 10 conceded. The defeats were a 2-1 loss at Aston Villa on 24 October and a 1-0 home defeat to Brighton on 31 October, the first time Maresca's side had failed to score. Before that they beat Liverpool 3-0 at Anfield and Ipswich 3-2 at home.

The summer spend of roughly £458m, led by Anderson, Fernández and Bouaddi, was the largest in the club's history and came alongside the departures of the last of the Guardiola veterans. Maresca said in September that he was "not worried or concerned" about the squad's shape and that the club believed it was strong enough to win the title. The Champions League, where City have not gone beyond the quarter-finals since 2023, is the other measure of the new era.

Haaland, 26, remains the goalscorer; Foden, 26, is the local talisman; and Anderson, 24, is already the player the team is built around. The ambition is the title back from Arsenal and a second European Cup, and the Guardiola standard, six titles in ten years, is the one Maresca will be measured against.
