## History

1. FSV Mainz 05 were founded in 1905 in the Rhineland-Palatinate capital and spent most of the twentieth century as a regional club, never reaching the Bundesliga and only occasionally the second division. The modern story begins with Wolfgang Frank, coach from 1995, whose zonal back four and pressing (learned from Arrigo Sacchi) influenced a generation of German coaches, including one of his players, a tall, awkward forward-turned-defender named [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp).

Klopp became coach in February 2001 with the club in the 2. Bundesliga relegation zone, kept them up, and then suffered two agonising near-misses: fourth in 2002, and fourth in 2003 on goal difference after conceding a late goal on the final day. At the third attempt, in 2004, Mainz went up, the smallest club ever to reach the Bundesliga at that point, and stayed three seasons, including a UEFA Cup campaign in 2005-06 earned through the Fair Play ranking. Relegated in 2007, Klopp left for Dortmund in 2008, and Jørn Andersen took the club straight back up.

Thomas Tuchel, promoted from the under-19s in 2009, began 2010-11 with seven straight wins and finished fifth, the club's best, with André Schürrle, Lewis Holtby and Ádám Szalai. Mainz moved into the Coface Arena (now MEWA Arena) in 2011, finished seventh under Tuchel in 2014 and sixth under Martin Schmidt in 2016. Since then they have been a lower-mid-table side with flashes: Bo Henriksen's 2024-25 team finished sixth and qualified for the Conference League, playing a bold, high-pressing game, before a harder 2025-26.

## Trajectory

Mainz have been in the Bundesliga for all but one of the last 22 seasons, a feat for a city of 220,000 with no history in the top flight before 2004. The pattern is one of mid-table consolidation punctuated by three European qualifications (2011, 2014, 2016, plus the 2005 Fair Play entry and 2025). The club's real export is coaches: Klopp, Tuchel, Schmidt, Sandro Schwarz and Henriksen all made their names there, and sporting director Christian Heidel's two spells shaped the model of buying unknowns and selling them on.

## Players the club produced

Mainz are better known for coaches than players, but the academy produced André Schürrle, who scored 20 goals in two seasons before Leverkusen and the 2014 World Cup, Jonathan Burkardt, the Germany striker sold to Frankfurt in 2025, Ridle Baku, Jean-Paul Boëtius and Paul Nebel. The club's shrewdest signings were Shinji Okazaki, Yunus Mallı, Jean-Philippe Gbamin, Abdou Diallo, Jean-Paul Boëtius and Moussa Niakhaté, all sold for multiples of their fees. Robin Zentner has been the goalkeeper for a decade; Nadiem Amiri the creator of the Henriksen side.

## Identity and playing style

Mainz's identity is the pressing school of Wolfgang Frank, carried on by Klopp and Tuchel: a zonal back four, [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) before the term existed, and a willingness to play young players. Tuchel's Mainz introduced the idea of changing shape every week to suit the opponent. Henriksen's 2024-25 side was a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with a ferocious press and [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) high up the pitch. The club is also the self-styled carnival club (Karnevalsverein), and the MEWA Arena has a reputation for a relaxed atmosphere compared with the Ruhr.

## Rivalries

The Rhine-Main derby against [Eintracht Frankfurt](/clubs/eintracht-frankfurt), 40 kilometres away, is the main rivalry, played in the Bundesliga since 2005. Kaiserslautern were the regional giants Mainz looked up to and have since passed; the Südwest derby now takes place only in the cup.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Mainz are tenth in the Bundesliga on ten points from eight games (three wins, one draw, four defeats, 17 scored, 17 conceded). They won 1-0 at Schalke on 17 October, lost 3-2 at home to Werder on 24 October and 4-1 at Elversberg on 30 October. Bayern visit the MEWA Arena on 7 November.

The club's head coach for 2026-27 could not be verified at the time of writing. Zentner, Amiri and Dominik Kohr are the experienced players, Nebel and Armindo Sieb the attacking threats, and the season's goal is a comfortable mid-table finish after the sale of Burkardt and the loss of European football.
