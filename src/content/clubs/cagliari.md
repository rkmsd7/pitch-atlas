## History

Cagliari were founded in 1920 and spent their first four decades in the regional leagues and Serie C, the football of an island that Italy's mainland mostly ignored. The club reached Serie A for the first time in 1964, and within six years had done something no side from the south or the islands had ever done. Manlio Scopigno's 1969-70 Cagliari won the Scudetto, losing only two games and conceding 11 goals all season, with Gigi Riva, "Rombo di Tuono", the left-footed striker from Lombardy who refused every offer from Juventus and Inter and scored 164 goals for the club, as the symbol. Riva's 21 goals that season were his third Capocannoniere, and six Cagliari players, Riva, Pierluigi Cera, Angelo Domenghini, Enrico Albertosi, Comunardo Niccolai and Sergio Gori, went to the 1970 World Cup final with [Italy](/nations/italy). Riva died in January 2024 and the city stopped for his funeral.

That title was never repeated. Cagliari were relegated in 1976, fell as far as Serie C1 in 1987 and came back under Claudio Ranieri with two straight promotions in 1989 and 1990. The early 1990s brought a 1994 UEFA Cup semi-final, lost to Inter, with [Enzo Francescoli](/players/enzo-francescoli) and the young Gianfranco Zola's predecessor Daniel Fonseca, and then another cycle of relegations. Massimo Cellino, the eccentric agricultural magnate, owned the club from 1992 to 2014 and ran through more than 30 coaches; the current owner, Tommaso Giulini, bought it in 2014.

## Trajectory

Cagliari have been relegated from Serie A five times since 1976 and have always come back, most recently in 2023, when the 71-year-old Ranieri returned and won the Serie B play-off final at Bari with a stoppage-time goal in June, then kept the club up in 2023-24 before retiring. The 2015-16 Serie B title under Massimo Rastelli and the 2004 promotion with Zola, who returned home at 37 to finish his career in Sardinia, are the other modern highs. The club spent 2016 to 2022 in the top flight, reaching ninth in 2019-20, before the relegation that brought Ranieri back. Davide Nicola kept Cagliari up in 2024-25 and left for Cremonese; Fabio Pisacane, the former club defender promoted from the Primavera in June 2025, finished 14th in 2025-26 and was kept on.

## Players the club produced

Riva is the club's past, present and future; the Stadio Sant'Elia's replacement, when it is finally built, will carry his name. The modern academy has been remarkable for a club of this size. [Nicolò Barella](/players/nicolo-barella), born in Cagliari, debuted at 18 and was sold to Inter in 2019 for a fee that reached €45m; Gianluca Lapadula's partner Alessandro Deiola is still in the squad; and the forward Zito Luvumbo, the full-back Riccardo Ciervo and the midfielder Alessandro Romano are the latest graduates. Cagliari have also been a launching pad: Zola was born in Oliena but came through at Torres and Nuorese before Napoli bought him; Radja Nainggolan arrived from Piacenza and left for Roma; Marco Sau, the Sardinian striker, scored 75 goals; Andrea Cossu and Daniele Conti, son of Bruno, spent most of their careers at the club.

## Identity and playing style

Scopigno's champions played a tight, counter-attacking 4-3-3 built on an impregnable defence and Riva's left foot. Ranieri's two great escapes were masterpieces of a [low block](/tactics/low-block), set pieces and belief. Pisacane, 40, plays a 3-5-2 that switches to a 4-4-2 out of possession, with Elia Caprile in goal, Yerry Mina and Adam Obert at the back, Harry Winks, the former Tottenham midfielder, as the deep playmaker and Jacopo Fazzini, signed from Empoli, as the creator. Sebastiano Esposito, bought from Inter in summer 2026, M'Bala Nzola and Daniel Maldini, on loan from Atalanta, carry the attack. The style is pragmatic and the home crowd at the Unipol Domus, a temporary ground with 16,000 seats, makes it a hard place to visit.

## Rivalries

The island derby with Torres of Sassari is historic but rarely played. In Serie A the games against [Torino](/clubs/torino) have an edge from the 1970s title races, and there is a curious friendship with [Genoa](/clubs/genoa) and a mutual loathing with Verona and Hellas's ultras. The visits of Juventus, Inter and Milan are the big days of the Sardinian year, and the 2026 defeat of Atalanta 1-0 in October was celebrated as if it mattered more than three points.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Cagliari are 12th in Serie A with 13 points from ten matches, four wins, one draw and five defeats, having scored only nine goals, the fewest outside the bottom three. The wins over Atalanta and at Bologna have offset a 4-0 defeat at Monza, and the Coppa Italia has produced a second-round exit. Pisacane's contract runs to 2027. The club's plan for a 25,000-seat stadium on the site of the old Sant'Elia has finally been approved and construction is due to begin in 2027, more than a decade after it was first promised.
