## History

Grêmio Foot-Ball Porto Alegrense was founded in 1903 by Cândido Dias, a businessman who had brought a football to a kickabout in Porto Alegre, and it has shared the southern capital with Internacional, founded six years later, in the most evenly matched city rivalry in Brazil. The tricolor's identity is gaúcho, the cattle-country culture of Rio Grande do Sul, and its fans see themselves as tougher and more loyal than the clubs of Rio and São Paulo. The nickname "Imortal", immortal, came from a 1917 chant.

The first national title arrived in 1981 under Ênio Andrade, and the global one in 1983: Grêmio beat Peñarol to win the Copa Libertadores and then [Hamburg](/clubs/hamburger-sv) 2-1 after extra time in Tokyo, with Renato Portaluppi, "Renato Gaúcho", scoring both goals. Renato has been the face of the club ever since, as player, coach and legend. A second Libertadores followed in 1995 under Luiz Felipe Scolari, with Mário Jardel's goals and the goalkeeper Danrlei, along with the 1994 Copa do Brasil and the 1996 Brasileirão.

The late 1990s produced [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho), who made his debut in 1998 and left for PSG in 2001 after a bitter contract dispute. Grêmio were relegated in 2004 and promoted in 2005 in the "Batalha dos Aflitos", a match in Recife in which they finished with seven men and still won 1-0 with Anderson's goal. A third relegation came in 2021.

## Trajectory

The 2010s were the club's richest period since the 1990s. The Arena do Grêmio opened in 2012 in the Humaitá district, and Renato returned as coach in 2016 to win the Copa do Brasil that year and the 2017 Copa Libertadores, beating Lanús 3-1 on aggregate with a team of Luan, Arthur, Éverton "Cebolinha", Pedro Geromel and Walter Kannemann. The 2017 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) ended with a 1-0 extra-time defeat to Real Madrid. Renato's team also reached the 2018 Libertadores semi-finals and won the Recopa.

The fall was sudden: a poor 2021 under four coaches ended in relegation, and the club came straight back in 2022. [Luis Suárez](/players/luis-suarez), signed on a free in 2023, scored 29 goals in a season that ended with second place behind Palmeiras before he left for Inter Miami. The 2024 season was ruined by the Rio Grande do Sul floods, which left the Arena underwater and forced the club to play home games in Curitiba for months. Renato returned for a fourth spell in 2025, after a stint at Fluminense, and is listed as head coach in the club's current squad data as of November 2026; Grêmio are 18th in the 2026 Brasileirão with 37 points from 34 games, in the relegation zone on goal difference with four rounds left.

## Players the club produced

The Grêmio academy is among the best in Brazil. [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho), Anderson (to Porto and Manchester United), Lucas Leiva (to Liverpool), Arthur (to Barcelona), Éverton Cebolinha (to Benfica and Flamengo), Emerson Royal, Douglas Costa, Rodrigo Caio's contemporaries and the striker Ferreira all came through. The older generation gave Brazil Tarciso, the 1983 side's winger, and Danrlei. The great signings were Renato, Jardel, Geromel, Kannemann, Luan and Suárez.

## Identity and playing style

Renato Gaúcho's Grêmio is the club's modern template: a 4-2-3-1 with fast wingers, a creative number ten (Luan, now Franco Cristaldo), a tight back four and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) football that is patient in its own half and fast in the opponent's. His 2017 side was praised across South America for its combination play and was the first Brazilian team in years to win the Libertadores with style. The 2026 team has lacked goals and has relied on the Danish striker Martin Braithwaite, the Paraguayan midfielder Mathías Villasanti and the goalkeeper Tiago Volpi.

## Rivalries

The Gre-Nal with Internacional is the only rivalry that matters: two clubs within a few kilometres, both with Libertadores and world titles, both with more than 440 derbies behind them and a city that is divided house by house. Nationally, games with [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) and [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) have decided Libertadores and Copa do Brasil ties in the last decade.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Grêmio are in the relegation zone, 18th with four games left, and Renato, listed as head coach, faces the possibility of a fourth relegation in the club's history. Braithwaite, Villasanti, Cristaldo, Volpi and Kannemann are the senior players. The Arena has been fully restored after the 2024 floods, but the squad has not, and the next four rounds will decide whether the Immortal Tricolor plays the 2027 Série B.
