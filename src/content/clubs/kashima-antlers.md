## History

Kashima Antlers began in 1947 as the works team of Sumitomo Metal Industries in Osaka, moved to the steel town of Kashima in Ibaraki prefecture in 1975 and, when the professional J.League was being assembled in 1991, was told it had "a 99.9999 per cent chance" of being rejected because of its small-town location. The club's answer was to build a 15,000-seat stadium and to sign [Zico](/players/zico), the Brazilian who was then 38 and winding down. Zico scored a hat-trick in the first J.League match in 1993, stayed as a player, then adviser, then technical director, and built a Brazilian pipeline (Leonardo, Jorginho, Bismarck, Bebeto) and a culture of professionalism that no other Japanese club matched. The deer-antler badge comes from the Kashima Shrine's sacred deer.

The club won the first stage of the inaugural 1993 season and its first full title in 1996, and under the Brazilian coaches Toninho Cerezo and Oswaldo de Oliveira it became the dominant force of the 2000s: the 2000 treble of league, Emperor's Cup and League Cup, the 2001 title, and three in a row from 2007 to 2009, with Mitsuo Ogasawara as the midfield general, Atsuto Uchida at right-back, Yasuto Honda and the goalkeeper Hitoshi Sogahata. Kashima's eight titles by 2016 were the J.League record, and the club has never been relegated.

## Trajectory

The 2016 title was won through the two-stage play-off system, and the reward was the [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) on home soil in December 2016, where Kashima beat Auckland City, Mamelodi Sundowns and Atlético Nacional to reach the final against Real Madrid in Yokohama. Gaku Shibasaki scored twice to put the Japanese side 2-1 up before Cristiano Ronaldo's hat-trick won it 4-2 in extra time; it remains the closest an Asian club has come to the world title. In 2018 Kashima won the AFC Champions League for the first time, beating Persepolis 2-0 on aggregate, and finished fourth at that year's Club World Cup.

The years since were the longest title drought in the club's history, with the Brazilian ownership-adjacent model giving way to the e-commerce company Mercari as majority owner in 2019. Toru Oniki, who had won four J1 titles with Kawasaki Frontale, was appointed for 2025 and, as of the end of that season, had led the club to a ninth title; that result and the standings of the short transitional 2026 season are not verifiable here and are stated as of the 2025 season. Oniki is listed as head coach in the club's current squad data as of November 2026, and Kashima are sixth in the first autumn-spring J1 season, 2026-27, with 23 points from 14 games.

## Players the club produced

Kashima's academy and recruitment have supplied Japan's national team for three decades: Ogasawara, Uchida (sold to Schalke in 2010), Shibasaki (to Tenerife and Getafe), Koji Nakata, Takayuki Suzuki, Yuma Suzuki (to Sint-Truiden and back), Daiki Suga, Shoma Doi and the goalkeeper Kwoun Sun-tae's successors. The Brazilian connection gave the club Zico, Leonardo (who went on to Milan and PSG), Jorginho (the 1994 World Cup winner), Marquinhos, Léo Silva and the current striker Léo Ceará.

## Identity and playing style

Kashima's identity is winning ugly when necessary, "Kashima-ness" in the Japanese press: game management, set-piece ruthlessness and defensive organisation inherited from the Brazilian coaches and prized above the possession aesthetics of other J.League clubs. Oniki's Kawasaki played the most attractive possession football in Japan, and at Kashima he has combined that 4-2-3-1 build-up model with the club's traditional pragmatism, using Yuma Suzuki as a complete forward and Kento Misao as the midfield anchor. ## Rivalries

The games with [Urawa Red Diamonds](/clubs/urawa-red-diamonds), the two most-decorated clubs in J.League history, are the league's biggest fixture, and the "Kashima-Urawa" matches have decided several titles. The Ibaraki derby with Mito HollyHock is local, the games with JEF United were the original 1990s rivalry, and [Kawasaki Frontale](/clubs/kawasaki-frontale), now Oniki's former club, has been the main title rival of the 2020s.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Kashima are sixth in the 2026-27 J1 League, the first season played on the new August-to-May calendar, with 23 points from 14 games under Oniki. Yuma Suzuki, Léo Ceará, Misao and Koki Anzai are the core. The club's ninth title, as of the end of the 2025 season, restored its record-champion status and its place in the 2026-27 AFC Champions League Elite; the stadium, expanded for the 2002 World Cup, remains one of the best-attended in the league despite the town's 65,000 population.
