## History

Club Deportivo Guadalajara was founded in 1906 as Union Football Club by a Belgian shopkeeper, Edgar Everaert, and renamed two years later. Its defining policy dates from 1943, when the club decided to field only Mexican-born players, a rule it has kept through every era of globalisation and which makes it unique among major clubs in the world. The nickname Chivas, "goats", was an insult from a 1948 newspaper that the fans adopted.

The great years were the late 1950s and 1960s, when the "Campeonísimo" side won seven of nine championships between 1957 and 1965, with Salvador "Chava" Reyes, the club's all-time scorer, the goalkeeper Jaime "Tubo" Gómez, and the defender José "Jamaicón" Villegas, whose homesickness on a tour of Europe gave Mexican Spanish the phrase "síndrome del Jamaicón". Chivas also won the first CONCACAF Champions' Cup in 1962. The 1970s and 1980s were leaner, with a single title in 1987, and the club's status as the national team's pipeline, [Mexico](/nations/mexico) squads sometimes half-filled by Chivas players, mattered more than trophies.

Jorge Vergara, a nutritional-supplements entrepreneur, bought the club in 2002, built the Estadio Akron (opened 2010, the first in Mexico designed as a modern bowl) and oversaw the Apertura 2006 title and the Copa Libertadores final of 2010, lost to Internacional. He also presided over years of chaos: constant coaching changes, a failed Chivas USA franchise in MLS, and a broken relationship with the fans before his death in 2019. His son Amaury Vergara now runs the club.

## Trajectory

The last title was the Clausura 2017 double under Matías Almeyda, a team of Alan Pulido, Rodolfo Pizarro, Orbelín Pineda and Rodolfo Cota that beat Tigres in the final and then won the 2018 CONCACAF Champions League against Toronto on penalties, earning a trip to the [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) in the UAE. Almeyda was pushed out in 2018 and the club spent the next years oscillating between mid-table and the Liguilla, with Javier "Chicharito" Hernández's emotional return in 2024 the most-discussed signing.

The Argentine Gabriel Milito, listed as head coach in the club's current squad data as of November 2026, arrived in 2025 and has steadied the side. The Apertura 2026 has been the best regular season in years: Chivas sit second on 29 points from 15 games, level with América and Cruz Azul.

## Players the club produced

Every Chivas team is, by definition, a producing team. The academy has turned out [Carlos Vela](/players/carlos-vela) (sold to Arsenal at 16), Javier Hernández (sold to [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in 2010), Omar Bravo, Ramón Morales, Oswaldo Sánchez, Marco Fabián, Carlos Salcido, Jesús "Canelo" Angulo and, more recently, Uriel Antuna and Fernando Beltrán. The policy means every great Mexican player who is not from the capital has probably been linked with the club, and the national team's World Cup squads have always had a Chivas core.

## Identity and playing style

The Mexican-only rule gives Chivas a cultural identity no tactic can match, and it has always played with a national-team style: quick, technical, built around wide players and a number ten rather than a pressing machine. Almeyda's 2017 team was the modern template, a 4-2-3-1 that pressed as a unit and broke through Pizarro and Pineda. Milito has introduced a more possession-based, Argentine-schooled approach, with Roberto Alvarado as the creator and a higher defensive line. The constraint is the market: with no foreign signings, a bad transfer window cannot be fixed with a Brazilian in January.

## Rivalries

The Clásico Nacional against [América](/clubs/club-america) is the match that divides Mexico, the provincial, Mexican-only club against the capital's television-backed giant. The Clásico Tapatío with Atlas, the other Guadalajara club, is older and more local, and Atlas' back-to-back titles in 2021-22 added bite. Games against [Pumas UNAM](/clubs/pumas-unam), another club that prides itself on producing players, carry a similar philosophical charge.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Chivas are second in the Apertura 2026 table with 29 points from 15 games under Milito, with Alvarado, Pulido and the veteran Hernández as the main attacking names. The club has not won a league title since 2017 and the Liguilla in December is the obvious target. The 2026 World Cup, with matches at the Estadio Akron, brought the stadium global exposure, and the club's new academy complex is the long-term bet.
