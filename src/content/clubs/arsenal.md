## History

Arsenal began in 1886 as Dial Square, a works team formed by munitions workers at the Royal Arsenal in Woolwich, south-east London. They turned professional in 1891, joined the Football League in 1893 as the first southern club to do so, and in 1913 made the move that defined them, crossing the river to Highbury in north London. That move, and the controversial election to the First Division in 1919 ahead of neighbours [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur), gave the club its home and its rivalry in one go.

The first great Arsenal was built by Herbert Chapman, appointed in 1925. Chapman invented the WM formation to exploit the new offside law, pushed for floodlights and numbered shirts, and had the local Underground station renamed Arsenal. His side won the FA Cup in 1930 and the league in 1931 and 1933. He died suddenly in January 1934, but the machine he built, with Alex James pulling strings and Cliff Bastin and Ted Drake scoring, won five titles in the 1930s. Arsenal were the club of the decade.

The post-war years were quieter until Bertie Mee's team won the Fairs Cup in 1970 and then the Double in 1971, Charlie George lying on the Wembley turf after scoring the winner against Liverpool. George Graham, a player in that side, returned as manager in 1986 and built a defensively brilliant team around Tony Adams, Lee Dixon, Nigel Winterburn, Steve Bould and David Seaman. Michael Thomas's last-minute goal at Anfield in May 1989 won the title in the most dramatic finish English football has seen, and a second title followed in 1991 with only one defeat. Graham added the FA Cup and League Cup double in 1993 and the Cup Winners' Cup in 1994 before leaving over the bungs affair.

Then came Arsène Wenger. Appointed in October 1996 as an unknown from Japan, he changed how English players ate, trained and thought, and he married Graham's back four to a new attacking elegance: [Dennis Bergkamp](/players/dennis-bergkamp), [Patrick Vieira](/players/patrick-vieira), Emmanuel Petit, Marc Overmars and [Ian Wright](/players/ian-wright), later [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry) and Robert Pires. Arsenal won the Double in 1998 and 2002 and then, in 2003-04, went through a 38-game league season unbeaten. The Invincibles stretched the run to 49 matches. The club left Highbury for the 60,000-seat Emirates Stadium in 2006 and reached the Champions League final the same year, losing 2-1 to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in Paris after Jens Lehmann's early red card.

The stadium debt brought a long drought. Wenger kept Arsenal in the top four every year until 2016 but sold [Cesc Fàbregas](/players/cesc-fabregas-player), Samir Nasri and [Robin van Persie](/players/robin-van-persie) and did not win the league again. Three FA Cups between 2014 and 2017 softened the end, but he left in 2018 after 22 years. Unai Emery lasted 18 months. In December 2019 the club appointed its former captain [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta), then Pep Guardiola's assistant, and the modern Arsenal began.

## Trajectory

Arsenal's defining statistic used to be continuity: from 1919 to the present they have never been relegated, the longest unbroken top-flight run in England. Within that, the shape of the last quarter-century is a long plateau, a dip and a climb.

From 2000-01 to 2004-05 they finished first or second every year, winning the league in 2002 and 2004. The stadium years brought third and fourth places with Champions League football every season but no title; the closest call was 2007-08, when a February draw at Birmingham and Eduardo's broken leg started a slide from the top. The team of 2015-16 finished second but ten points behind Leicester, which felt like a missed opening.

Then the floor fell out. Fifth in 2017 ended two decades of Champions League qualification. Sixth, fifth, eighth, eighth and fifth followed. Arteta's first full seasons were spent clearing the squad, promoting academy players and taking a lot of criticism. The turn came in 2022-23: a 20-year-old [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka), [Martin Ødegaard](/players/martin-odegaard) as captain, the signing of [Gabriel Magalhães](/players/gabriel-magalhaes) and [William Saliba](/players/william-saliba) as a pairing, and a title race that lasted until May before [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) overtook them. Second place followed in 2024 (89 points) and 2025.

The 2025-26 season was the pay-off. With [Declan Rice](/players/declan-rice) and Martin Zubimendi in midfield, [Viktor Gyökeres](/players/viktor-gyokeres) and [Kai Havertz](/players/kai-havertz) sharing the centre-forward role and [Eberechi Eze](/players/eberechi-eze) added in attack, Arsenal won the league on 85 points, seven clear of City, with the meanest defence in the division. They lost the League Cup final 2-0 to City in March but reached the Champions League final in Budapest on 30 May, where Havertz scored in the sixth minute and Arsenal lost 4-3 on penalties to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) after a 1-1 draw. It was the club's first league title since the Invincibles and its second European Cup final.

## Players the club produced

Arsenal's academy at Hale End has become one of the most productive in the country. [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka) is the model: a left-footed right winger who joined at seven, debuted at 17 and became the face of the title-winning team and a Sócrates Award winner in 2026. Myles Lewis-Skelly, Ethan Nwaneri and the 16-year-old Max Dowman, who featured in the first team in 2026-27, are the next wave. Earlier generations produced Tony Adams, who captained the club for 14 years, Liam Brady, David Rocastle, Ray Parlour, Ashley Cole, Jack Wilshere and [Cesc Fàbregas](/players/cesc-fabregas-player), who arrived from Barcelona at 16 and left as captain.

Signings who became legends are a longer list. [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry), converted from a winger into the club's record scorer with 228 goals; [Dennis Bergkamp](/players/dennis-bergkamp), whose 1997 hat-trick at Leicester and 2002 pirouette against Newcastle remain the club's most replayed moments; [Ian Wright](/players/ian-wright), who broke Cliff Bastin's scoring record in 1997; [Patrick Vieira](/players/patrick-vieira), the midfield enforcer who captained the Invincibles; Robert Pires, Sol Campbell, [Robin van Persie](/players/robin-van-persie), Santi Cazorla and Alexis Sánchez. The current core of [Martin Ødegaard](/players/martin-odegaard), [William Saliba](/players/william-saliba), [Gabriel Magalhães](/players/gabriel-magalhaes), [Declan Rice](/players/declan-rice) and [David Raya](/players/david-raya) will join that list; Rice's £105m move from West Ham in 2023 is already regarded as one of the best signings of the decade.

## Identity and playing style

[Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s Arsenal is a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) team built on structure, set pieces and a defence that concedes very little. The base shape is a 4-3-3 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession: one full-back, usually Jurriën Timber or Riccardo Calafiori, steps into midfield as an [inverted full-back](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) alongside Zubimendi or Rice, the other tucks in beside the centre-backs, and Ødegaard drifts into the right half-space to combine with Saka.

Two things separate this Arsenal from Guardiola's City, where Arteta learned the trade. The first is set pieces: under coach Nicolas Jover, Arsenal have been the most dangerous dead-ball team in Europe for three seasons, with Gabriel's near-post runs and Rice's deliveries worth a dozen goals a year. The second is pressing. Arsenal go man-to-man high up the pitch far more aggressively than most possession sides, with Havertz and Rice leading the trigger, and they are comfortable defending a [high line](/tactics/high-line) because Saliba and Gabriel are quick enough to recover.

In 2025-26 the attack became more flexible: Havertz as a false nine who drops and links, Gyökeres as a battering ram when the game needs a target, Eze and Noni Madueke on the left as alternatives to Gabriel Martinelli. The early weeks of 2026-27 have seen Christos Tzolis, signed from Club Brugge after Leandro Trossard's departure, added to that rotation, and Bruno Guimarães arriving from [Newcastle United](/clubs/newcastle-united) to deepen the midfield.

## Rivalries

The North London derby with [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) is the one that matters most. It dates to Arsenal's move to Highbury in 1913 and the 1919 election to the First Division, and it has produced Sol Campbell's defection, Arsenal clinching the 2004 title at White Hart Lane, and "St Totteringham's Day", the date each year when Spurs could no longer finish above Arsenal, which has been celebrated in every season since 2017 except 2016-17.

[Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) were the great rivals of the Wenger-Ferguson years, from the 1999 FA Cup semi-final replay and Ryan Giggs's goal to the Battle of Old Trafford in 2003 and the pizza-throwing of 2004. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) became a London rival once Roman Abramovich's money arrived in 2003, and the 2017 FA Cup final and 2019 Europa League final kept that edge. [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) are the rivalry of the Arteta era: three title races in four years, the 2026 League Cup final, and the question, now that Pep Guardiola has gone, of whether Arsenal have taken over.

## Current situation

Arsenal are the champions and, in early November 2026, the leaders again. After nine matches they had eight wins and one defeat, 24 points, 14 goals scored and five conceded, and they lead [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) by three points and [Brighton & Hove Albion](/clubs/brighton) by five. The run includes a 1-0 win at the City Ground on 18 October, a 2-0 home win over Everton on 24 October and a 1-0 win at Anfield on 1 November.

The summer was about depth rather than headline spending. Piero Hincapié's move from [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) was made permanent, Christos Tzolis arrived to replace Trossard, Illan Meslier came in as goalkeeping cover and, late in the window, Bruno Guimarães joined from Newcastle. Gyökeres said in August that he felt in better shape than when he arrived, but Havertz has held the central striking role in the opening months and the Swede has had to wait for starts.

The ambitions are explicit: retain the title, which Arsenal have not done since 1935, and go one step further in the Champions League after Budapest. [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) is in his seventh year, [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka) is the Ballon d'Or-nominated face of the team, and the defence of Raya, Saliba and Gabriel has been the league's best for three seasons running. The clearest threat is Maresca's expensively rebuilt City; the surprise is Brighton, who beat City 1-0 on 31 October and sit third.
