## History

Celtic were founded in November 1887 by Brother Walfrid, a Marist monk, to raise money for the poor of the Irish immigrant community in Glasgow's East End, and that origin still shapes the club's self-image as a charitable, outward-looking institution with a strong Irish identity. The first match was played in May 1888 against a newly formed club called Rangers.

The club dominated the early decades of Scottish football under Willie Maley, who managed for 43 years and won 16 titles, with Jimmy McGrory scoring 468 league goals, still a British record. The greatest era, though, belongs to [Jock Stein](/managers/jock-stein). Appointed in 1965, Stein won nine consecutive titles from 1966 to 1974 and, on 25 May 1967 in Lisbon, led a team of eleven players born within 30 miles of Celtic Park to a 2-1 win over Helenio Herrera's Inter in the European Cup final. The Lisbon Lions, with Billy McNeill as captain, Jimmy Johnstone on the wing and goals from Tommy Gemmell and Stevie Chalmers, overwhelmed Inter's [catenaccio](/tactics/catenaccio) with relentless attacking and became the first British club to win the trophy. They reached the final again in 1970, losing to [Feyenoord](/clubs/feyenoord) in Milan.

[Kenny Dalglish](/players/kenny-dalglish) emerged from Stein's second great side and was sold to Liverpool in 1977. The 1980s brought the centenary double of 1988 under McNeill as manager, but the early 1990s were the club's darkest hour: near-bankruptcy in 1994, rescued at the last moment by Fergus McCann, who rebuilt Celtic Park and the club's finances. Wim Jansen's 1998 title stopped Rangers winning ten in a row, and [Martin O'Neill](/managers/martin-oneill), appointed in 2000, restored Celtic to the top with a Treble in his first season, Henrik Larsson's 53 goals, two more titles and the 2003 UEFA Cup final in Seville, lost 3-2 after extra time to [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s Porto.

Gordon Strachan won three titles and reached two Champions League last-16 ties; Neil Lennon's side beat Barcelona in 2012. Then came the most dominant period in the club's history: nine consecutive championships from 2012 to 2020, including four successive Trebles under Brendan Rodgers and Lennon, the "quadruple Treble", with Rodgers' 2016-17 side going through the domestic season unbeaten. [Ange Postecoglou](/managers/ange-postecoglou) won two titles and a Treble in 2023 after the 2021 blip, Rodgers returned for two more championships, and in 2025-26 O'Neill, 73, came back as interim manager in October 2025 and won the league and Scottish Cup. After a disastrous 33-day spell for Wilfried Nancy, O'Neill stepped in again and was given the job permanently in June 2026.

## Trajectory

Celtic's 55 championships are level with [Rangers](/clubs/rangers)' claim to a world record, and the modern distribution is lopsided: Celtic have won 21 of the 26 completed Scottish titles since 2000-01, finishing second in the other five. Rangers' financial collapse and relegation in 2012 removed the only serious domestic challenger for four seasons, and Celtic's nine-in-a-row from 2012 to 2020 coincided with it. The only interruption since was 2020-21, when a tired squad chasing a tenth straight title finished 25 points behind Steven Gerrard's Rangers.

Europe has been harder. The 2003 UEFA Cup final remains the modern high point, with Champions League last-16 appearances in 2007, 2008, 2013 and 2025, the last under Rodgers after a league-phase campaign that included a 3-1 win over RB Leipzig. The gap between dominating Scotland and competing in the Champions League is the club's defining frustration.

## Players the club produced

Celtic's academy produced the entire Lisbon Lions side, and later [Kenny Dalglish](/players/kenny-dalglish), Danny McGrain, Paul McStay, Charlie Nicholas and, in the modern era, Kieran Tierney, James Forrest and Callum McGregor. The club has also been a launch pad for signings: Henrik Larsson arrived from Feyenoord for £650,000 and left as the greatest player in modern Celtic history; [Virgil van Dijk](/players/virgil-van-dijk) spent two seasons at Celtic Park before Southampton and Liverpool; Victor Wanyama, Moussa Dembélé, Odsonne Édouard, Kristoffer Ajer, Jota and Matt O'Riley were all sold for large fees. Lubomír Moravčík, Chris Sutton, Stiliyan Petrov, John Hartson and Scott Brown define different eras for supporters who watched them.

## Identity and playing style

Celtic's identity, set by Stein and insisted on by the support ever since, is attacking football with width and pace. Postecoglou's "we never stop" 4-3-3, with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) and relentless [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), was its purest modern expression, and Rodgers refined it with more control.

[Martin O'Neill](/managers/martin-oneill) is more direct. His 2000-05 Celtic played a 3-5-2 with Larsson and Sutton or Hartson up front, long diagonals to the wingers and a ferocious work rate, and his second coming has leaned on the same principles: a [back three](/tactics/back-three) when needed, [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) in Europe, [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) as a weapon and a dressing room managed by force of personality.

Off the pitch, Celtic are the best-run club in Scotland by some distance. Majority shareholder Dermot Desmond and a board that has prioritised cash reserves over transfer spending have produced annual profits unusual for a club of Celtic's size, with player sales (Tierney to Arsenal, Édouard to Crystal Palace, Jota to Al-Ittihad, O'Riley to Brighton) funding both the squad and a healthy bank balance. The criticism from supporters is that the club has been too cautious to build a Champions League side; the defence is that the model has kept Celtic solvent while Rangers collapsed and has delivered thirteen titles in fifteen seasons.

## Rivalries

The Old Firm against [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) is the most intense derby in British football, a rivalry layered with religion, Irish and British identity, class and 140 years of history. Celtic have had the better of it for most of the last two decades. Matches against [Aberdeen](/clubs/aberdeen) and [Hearts](/clubs/hearts) are the next in importance, Aberdeen having been the club's most frequent cup-final opponents.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Celtic lead the Scottish Premiership after twelve rounds, four points clear of Rangers, and are in European competition. The club's accounts for the year to June 2026 were published with chief executive Michael Nicholson confirming O'Neill would remain in charge, and the 74-year-old has joked about retreating to a "darkened room" after difficult nights while dealing with an autumn injury list. Rangers under [Derek McInnes](/managers/derek-mcinnes) have made the Old Firm race more open than for several seasons, but Celtic are expected to retain the title. The longer question is succession: O'Neill has said he will manage as long as he feels he is helping, and the board has not yet named what comes next.
