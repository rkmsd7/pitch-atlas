## History

Racing Club de Strasbourg were founded in 1906, when Alsace was part of Germany, and joined the French league after the region returned to France in 1919. They were the first club to field a professional team in Alsace, lost the 1937 Coupe de France final and finished second in Division 1 in 1935 with Oskar Rohr. The Coupe de France was won in 1951 and 1966, and in 1979 Gilbert Gress, a former Strasbourg player, coached a side of Jacky Novi, Albert Gemmrich and Francis Piasecki to the club's only league title, finishing two points clear of Nantes and Saint-Étienne.

The 1990s were the next good spell: Frank Lebœuf, Olivier Dacourt, Marc Keller and Aleksandr Mostovoi played at the Meinau, Ivica Osim coached a UEFA Cup run in 1997-98 that beat Liverpool 3-0, the Coupe de la Ligue was won in 1997, and the Coupe de France in 2001, days before relegation was confirmed. The 2005 Coupe de la Ligue was the last trophy for 14 years. Relegations in 2006 and 2008, a Ligue 2 relegation in 2010 and, in 2011, liquidation sent the club to the CFA 2, the fifth tier.

The rebuild under president Marc Keller took six seasons: four promotions in six years, with the National title in 2016 and the Ligue 2 title in 2017 under Jacky Duguépéroux and Thierry Laurey. Laurey's side won the 2019 Coupe de la Ligue on penalties against Guingamp and played in the Europa League. BlueCo, the Chelsea ownership consortium, bought the club in June 2023, and Liam Rosenior's young side finished seventh in 2024-25 to reach the Conference League and sixth in 2025-26. Hugo Oliveira, promoted from the coaching staff, was appointed head coach for 2026-27 after Rosenior left for Paris FC.

## Trajectory

Fifth tier in 2011, Ligue 1 in 2017, a trophy in 2019, Europe in 2025 and 2026: Strasbourg's trajectory is the great rebirth story of the French game, driven by a supporters' base that filled the Meinau in the CFA. The BlueCo era has brought money, a stream of young loan players from Chelsea and a debate among fans about the club's independence, with regular protests at the Meinau, but also the best results since the 1990s.

## Players the club produced

Strasbourg's academy produced Gress, Keller, Gemmrich, Olivier Dacourt and more recently Habib Diarra, sold to Sunderland in 2025, and Guéla Doué. The BlueCo model is about developing Chelsea's and the group's young players: Andrey Santos spent 2024-25 on loan before returning to Chelsea and then moving to Manchester United, Mike Penders was the goalkeeper of 2025-26, and Emanuel Emegha, the Dutch striker signed in 2023, was the goalscorer of the European seasons before his own move within the group. Dimitri Liénard, who stayed from the CFA to Ligue 1, and Kevin Gameiro, who returned to his first club in 2021, are the terraces' favourites.

## Identity and playing style

Laurey's Strasbourg were a counter-attacking 3-5-2 built on Ludovic Ajorque's hold-up play and Liénard's set pieces. Rosenior brought the BlueCo template: [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with the youngest squad in Ligue 1, [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs), a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and counter-pressing. Hugo Oliveira has kept the structure and the youth policy, with a 4-2-3-1 that attacks quickly through Dilane Bakwa and Emegha; the early 2026-27 results have been erratic, with heavy wins and heavy defeats.

## Rivalries

The Derby de l'Est against Metz, 160 kilometres away, is the main rivalry, with Metz now in Ligue 2 after relegation in 2026; Nancy are the other Lorraine opponent from the Ligue 2 years. The BlueCo connection has made [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), the other foreign-owned superpower, a point of comparison rather than a rival, and the games between the two since 2023 have been among the more open in the league.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Strasbourg are 13th in Ligue 1 on ten points from ten games (three wins, one draw, six defeats, 18 scored, 22 conceded). They lost 3-0 at Rennes on 25 October, beat Auxerre 5-1 at the Meinau on 1 November and lost 3-1 at Toulouse on 6 November. The next match is at home to Brest on 21 November.

Oliveira's team has the attacking quality of the Rosenior side without, so far, the defensive control, and the squad is again the youngest in the division. The club is in the Conference League for a second straight season and the aim is a top-half finish; the question at the Meinau, as ever since 2023, is how long the best young players stay.
