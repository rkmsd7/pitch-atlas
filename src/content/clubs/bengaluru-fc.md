## History

Bengaluru FC were founded on 20 July 2013 by the JSW Group, the steel and energy conglomerate, as a corporate club entering the I-League directly. They were built to be different: professional training facilities, punctual wages, sports science and marketing that treated supporters as customers. The first head coach was the Englishman Ashley Westwood, and the first captain was [Sunil Chhetri](/players/sunil-chhetri), already India's best player.

The impact was immediate. Bengaluru won the 2013-14 I-League in their debut season, added the Federation Cup in 2015, took a second I-League title in 2015-16 and another Federation Cup in 2017. Under Albert Roca they became, in 2016, the first Indian club to reach the final of the AFC Cup, beating Malaysia's Johor Darul Ta'zim in the semi-final before losing 1-0 to Iraq's Air Force Club in Doha.

## Trajectory

Bengaluru moved to the [Indian Super League](/leagues/indian-super-league) in 2017-18 and topped the table at the first attempt, losing the final 3-2 to Chennaiyin, then won the 2018 Super Cup. In 2018-19, under Carles Cuadrat, they finished top again and won the ISL Cup, Rahul Bheke heading the winner in extra time against [FC Goa](/clubs/fc-goa) in Mumbai.

The early 2020s were a slump: seventh, sixth and a 2022 Durand Cup win under Simon Grayson, then a revival under Grayson in 2022-23 that took the club from the bottom half to the ISL final, lost on penalties to ATK Mohun Bagan. Gerard Zaragoza's side reached another final in 2024-25, losing 2-1 after extra time to [Mohun Bagan](/clubs/mohun-bagan). After a tenth-place finish in 2023-24 and a poor start to the delayed 2025-26 season, Renedy Singh had a spell in charge before the Spaniard Pep Muñoz was appointed on 25 March 2026. Bengaluru finished fourth in the points-only season, three points behind the champions.

## Players the club produced

Chhetri is the club. He is the club's all-time top scorer by a distance, retired from international football in June 2024 and came back to the national team in March 2025, and at 42 remains in the squad. Goalkeeper Gurpreet Singh Sandhu, India's number one, has been here since 2017. Bengaluru turned Udanta Singh, Rahul Bheke, Nishu Kumar and Ashique Kuruniyan into internationals and gave the league foreign stars such as Miku, Erik Paartalu, Dimas Delgado and Cleiton Silva, while its youth system has produced players including Suresh Singh Wangjam, who arrived as a teenager.

## Identity and playing style

Bengaluru's teams have usually been organised, physical and strong at [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), a habit that runs from Westwood through Roca and Cuadrat. Muñoz has leaned towards a more possession-minded approach, with patient build-up from the back and the veteran Chhetri used mainly as a penalty-box finisher. See [positional play](/tactics/positional-play).

## Rivalries

The south Indian derby with [Kerala Blasters](/clubs/kerala-blasters) is the most hostile fixture in the league, and its low point came in the 2022-23 knockout match, when Chhetri's quickly taken free-kick in extra time led Kerala to walk off the pitch in protest. The All India Football Federation fined Kerala and banned coach Ivan Vukomanović. Bengaluru's supporters group, West Block Blues, also count [Mumbai City](/clubs/mumbai-city) and [Mohun Bagan](/clubs/mohun-bagan), their opponents in recent finals and semi-finals, as rivals.

## Current situation

As of 8 November 2026, Bengaluru are third in the 2026-27 ISL and unbeaten, with 10 points from four games: a 2-0 win over Chennaiyin, a 1-1 draw with NorthEast United and a 2-1 win at FC Goa on 6 November. They sit two points behind leaders Mohun Bagan with a game in hand. Muñoz's squad blends Chhetri, Sandhu, Ryan Williams and Ashique Kuruniyan with the Spanish forward Borja Martínez and Dutch defender Menno Koch, and the aim is a first league title since 2018-19.
