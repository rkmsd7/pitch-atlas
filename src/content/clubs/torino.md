## History

Torino were founded on 3 December 1906 in a Turin beer hall by dissidents from Juventus and the remnants of the old Football Club Torinese, and the Derby della Mole has been an argument between the two halves of the city ever since. The first title came in 1928 with the "trio of wonders", Julio Libonatti, Adolfo Baloncieri and Gino Rossetti, and then came the side that defines the club and, for many, Italian football itself. The Grande Torino of Ferruccio Novo and the coach Egri Erbstein won five straight championships from 1943 to 1949, missing only the war years, with Valentino Mazzola as captain and playmaker, Ezio Loik, Franco Ossola, Guglielmo Gabetto and Romeo Menti; in May 1947 ten of the eleven players in the Italy side were from Torino. On 4 May 1949, returning from a friendly in Lisbon, the team's plane hit the wall of the Basilica of Superga in fog, and all 31 people on board died. Half a million people lined the streets of Turin for the funeral. The club's youth team completed the season, opponents fielded their own youth sides, and Torino were champions for a fifth time. Every 4 May the club climbs to Superga to read the names.

Torino were relegated in 1959 and won a sixth Coppa Italia in 1968 under the shadow of another death, Gigi Meroni, the winger and bohemian who was killed by a car outside the stadium in 1967, driven by a 19-year-old Torino fan. The seventh Scudetto came in 1975-76 under Gigi Radice, with the strike pair of Paolo Pulici and Francesco Graziani, "the twin goals", Claudio Sala on the wing and Patrizio Sala in midfield; Torino missed a second in 1977 by a point to Juventus with 50 points from 30 games, a record for a runner-up.

## Trajectory

Emiliano Mondonico's 1991-92 side reached the UEFA Cup final, losing to Ajax on away goals after two draws, with Mondonico raising a chair to the sky in protest in Amsterdam, and won the 1993 Coppa Italia, the club's last trophy. Then came the decades of mismanagement: relegation in 1989, 1996, 2000, 2003 and 2009, two returns via play-offs, and in 2005 a bankruptcy that annulled a promotion won on the pitch and forced a refounding as Torino FC under Urbano Cairo, the publisher who has owned the club since. Cairo's Torino under Gian Piero Ventura finished seventh in 2014 and reached the Europa League round of 16, beating Athletic Bilbao in Bilbao; Walter Mazzarri's 2018-19 side finished seventh with 63 points, the club's best since 1992; and [Ivan Jurić](/managers/ivan-juric) kept them in the top half for three seasons. Paolo Vanoli finished 11th in 2024-25 and left; Marco Baroni's 2025-26 was a struggle, Roberto D'Aversa finishing 12th, and on 12 June 2026 Ignazio Abate, the former Milan full-back who had coached the Milan Primavera and Ternana, was appointed.

## Players the club produced

The Filadelfia, the training ground where the Grande Torino played and whose ruins became a shrine, has produced Claudio Sala's successors Giuseppe Dossena, Gianluigi Lentini, sold to Milan for a world-record £13m in 1992, Christian Vieri, who began there, Diego Fuser, Marco Ferrante's teammate Simone Barone, and more recently Alessandro Buongiorno, born in Turin and sold to Napoli for €35m in 2024, Samuele Ricci and Wilfried Singo. The signings who became symbols are Pulici and Graziani, Andrea Belotti, the "Gallo" who scored 113 goals and captained the club for seven years, Alessio Cerci, Ciro Immobile, who scored 22 in 2013-14 before Dortmund, Bremer, sold to Juventus for €41m in 2022 to the fans' fury, and Duván Zapata, the Colombian striker still in the squad at 35.

## Identity and playing style

Radice's champions played a pressing, zonal game two decades before Sacchi; Jurić's Torino was the Gasperini 3-4-2-1 of man-marking and wing-back runs. Abate plays a 4-3-3 with Lucas Perri, signed from Lyon, in goal; Saúl Coco, Adrian Ismajli and the 21-year-old Pietro Comuzzo, bought from Fiorentina, at the back; Nikola Vlašić, Rolando Mandragora and Cesare Casadei in midfield; and Zapata, Giovanni Simeone, signed from Napoli, Ché Adams and Zakaria Aboukhlal rotating in attack. The identity Cairo wants is a competitive, self-sustaining mid-table club; the identity the Curva Maratona wants is the Grande Torino, and the gap between the two has made the stadium a hostile place for the owner.

## Rivalries

The Derby della Mole against [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) is one of the oldest in Italy and the most one-sided of the great derbies: Torino's win in April 2015, 2-1 with a Fabio Quagliarella goal, was their first in 20 years, and the 2024-25 draw at the Allianz was celebrated like a victory. The supporters' friendships are with Fiorentina, the last side the Grande Torino faced in the league, and Genoa; their enemies are Juventus, [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) and Atalanta. [Cagliari](/clubs/cagliari) are a respected opponent from the 1970s title races.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Torino are tenth in Serie A with 13 points from ten matches, four wins, one draw and five defeats, in the mid-table position that has been the club's address for a decade. The Coppa Italia is at the round of 16. Abate, 39, is the youngest coach in Cairo's tenure and has been given a two-year contract; the question for the season is whether Simeone and Zapata can score enough, and whether the president, 20 years into his ownership and the target of protest banners and empty-seat demonstrations, will sell the club as supporters have demanded.
