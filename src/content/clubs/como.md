## History

Como were founded in 1907 in the lakeside town 50 kilometres north of Milan, and for most of a century were a small club with a beautiful setting. The Stadio Giuseppe Sinigaglia, opened in 1927 on the shore of Lake Como next to the rowing club, is the most photographed ground in Italy and one of the smallest in Serie A. The club's best years came in the 1980s, when a side coached by Rino Marchesi and then Emiliano Mondonico spent five straight seasons in the top flight, finishing ninth in 1985-86, with Stefano Borgonovo and the young Pietro Vierchowod and Gianluca Zambrotta, born in Como and sold to Bari in 1997, as its products. Como also won Serie B in 1949 and 1980.

The 2000s were a catalogue of disaster. Como won Serie B in 2001-02, lasted one season in Serie A, finishing 17th in 2002-03, were relegated again from Serie B in 2004, and in the same year went bankrupt and were sent to Serie C2 and then, after failing to register, to Serie D in 2005. A slow climb back through Serie C was undone by a second bankruptcy in 2016, after a season in Serie B had ended in relegation, and another restart in Serie D in 2017.

## Trajectory

The turning point came in April 2019, when SENT Entertainment, a vehicle of the Hartono brothers, the Indonesian owners of the Djarum tobacco group and the richest family in their country, bought the club from the English businessman Michael Essien's former partner for a reported €1m. The investment that followed was patient rather than reckless: Serie D was won in 2019, Serie C in 2020-21, and two mid-table Serie B seasons were spent building a squad and a recruitment operation while Thierry Henry and Dennis Wise joined as minority investors and advisers. [Cesc Fàbregas](/managers/cesc-fabregas), who had signed as a player in 2022, retired and took over as coach in November 2023, and Como finished second in Serie B behind Parma to reach Serie A for the first time in 21 years.

The 2024-25 season, a tenth-place finish with 48 points and the most expensive squad outside the big clubs, was the proof of concept. The 2025-26 season was the breakthrough: Como finished fourth with 71 points, scored 65 goals, conceded 29 and qualified for the Champions League, the first European competition of any kind in the club's history. They also reached the Coppa Italia semi-finals.

## Players the club produced

Historically the list is short: Zambrotta, a World Cup winner in 2006; Marco Tardelli's early career; Borgonovo, who died of motor neurone disease in 2013; Dan Petrescu, who played a season there before Genoa and Chelsea. The modern club is a different kind of producer. Nico Paz, bought from Real Madrid for €6m in 2024 with a buy-back clause, became the best young player in Serie A and remains the creative heart at 22. Assane Diao, signed from Betis at 19, and Jesús Rodríguez, 20, from the same club, are the next generation; Alberto Moreno, Pepe Reina and Sergi Roberto were the experienced imports who have now moved on. Patrick Cutrone, the 2024 promotion striker, was sold to Monza.

## Identity and playing style

Fàbregas has built the most clearly defined possession side in Italy outside Inter: a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, with [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) principles learned under Guardiola and Wenger, full-backs who [invert](/tactics/inverted-full-backs), a goalkeeper, Robert Sánchez, bought from Chelsea, who starts every attack, and Paz as the free number ten. Maxence Caqueret and Samuele Ricci, on loan from Milan with an obligation to buy, run the midfield; Trevoh Chalobah, a €30m signing from Chelsea, and Marc-Oliver Kempf anchor the defence; and Tasos Douvikas, the Greek striker, has 11 goals in all competitions, five of them in the league, with Moise Kean, on loan from Fiorentina with an obligation to buy, as the alternative. The 9-1 win at Lens on 4 November 2026 set a Champions League record for goals scored by an away team.

## Rivalries

Como's traditional derbies are with Lecco, across the lake, and Varese, neither of whom have been in the same division for years. In Serie A the fixture that has taken on meaning is against [Monza](/clubs/monza), the other Lombard town club, and the local supporters have nursed a grievance against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) and [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), whose fans fill the Sinigaglia on visiting days. The 0-0 draw with Roma in October 2026 drew the ground's record crowd of the season, 12,415.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Como are fourth in Serie A with 23 points from ten matches, seven wins, two draws and one defeat, the loss a 2-0 at Frosinone in September, and the 1-4 win at Genoa and 2-1 at Napoli the standout results. In the Champions League, after a nervous 1-0 defeat to RB Leipzig in the first ever European match at the Sinigaglia, the Lens result has put the club on course for the knockout play-offs. The Coppa Italia round of 16 is in January.

The constraints are the stadium, whose 13,602 capacity gives Como the smallest gate receipts in the division and has required UEFA dispensation for European games, and the inevitable interest in Fàbregas, who has been linked with every big job in Europe. The Hartonos have promised a new 20,000-seat stadium within the decade and signed the coach to a contract until 2028; the test of the project is whether Como can hold on to him and to Paz, whom Real Madrid can buy back.
