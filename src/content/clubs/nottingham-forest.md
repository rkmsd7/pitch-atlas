## History

Nottingham Forest were founded in 1865 by a group of shinty players at the Clinton Arms, which makes them one of the oldest professional clubs in the world. They gave Arsenal their first red shirts, helped introduce the referee's whistle and shin pads, and won the FA Cup in 1898 and 1959, the latter with ten men after Roy Dwight broke his leg. For most of a century they were a respectable provincial club overshadowed by Nottingham's older neighbours, Notts County.

Then, in January 1975, with Forest 13th in the Second Division, the club appointed [Brian Clough](/managers/brian-clough). With Peter Taylor alongside him, Clough won promotion in 1977, the First Division title in 1978 as a newly promoted side, the League Cup in 1978 and 1979, and the European Cup in 1979 against Malmö in Munich (Trevor Francis, the first £1m footballer, heading the winner) and in 1980 against Hamburg in Madrid (John Robertson's goal, Peter Shilton's saves). Forest went 42 league games unbeaten between 1977 and 1978, a record until Arsenal's Invincibles. The team of John McGovern, Kenny Burns, Larry Lloyd, Viv Anderson, Archie Gemmill, Garry Birtles and Tony Woodcock was built from cast-offs and is the greatest story in English club football.

Clough stayed 18 years. His later Forest, with Stuart Pearce, Des Walker, Nigel Clough and the young [Roy Keane](/players/roy-keane), won the League Cup in 1989 and 1990, reached the 1991 FA Cup final, and were relegated in 1993 in his final, alcohol-blighted season. Frank Clark took them straight back up and to third in 1995; relegation in 1997, promotion in 1998 and relegation again in 1999 began two decades outside the top flight, including three seasons in League One from 2005.

Evangelos Marinakis, the owner of Olympiacos, bought the club in 2017. Steve Cooper, appointed in September 2021 with Forest bottom of the Championship, won promotion through the play-offs the following May, beating Huddersfield at Wembley. Nuno Espírito Santo took them to seventh and Europe in 2024-25; his falling-out with Marinakis led to his sacking in September 2025, and after short spells for Ange Postecoglou and Vítor Pereira, [Oliver Glasner](/managers/oliver-glasner) was appointed in July 2026.

## Trajectory

Forest's quarter-century began in the second tier and went down before it went up: relegation to League One in 2005, three seasons there, promotion as runners-up in 2008, and then 14 Championship seasons ranging from third (2010, losing the play-offs) to 21st (2017, surviving on goal difference). The Marinakis years brought spending, managerial churn and, finally, Cooper's promotion.

In the Premier League the club has been 16th, 17th (after a four-point deduction for breaching spending rules), seventh and 16th. The 2024-25 season, when Forest sat third in March and qualified for the Conference League, was the best since 1995. The 2025-26 season had three head coaches, a Europa League campaign that reached the knockout rounds, and a 16th-place finish on 44 points that was closer to the relegation zone than anyone at the club wanted.

## Players the club produced

Forest's academy produced Stuart Pearce's partner Des Walker, Nigel Clough, Steve Chettle, Steve Stone, Jermaine Jenas, Michael Dawson, Andy Reid, Wes Morgan, Ben Brereton Díaz, Oliver Burke, Brennan Johnson, sold to Tottenham for £47.5m in 2023, and Ryan Yates, the captain and one-club man of the current squad. Viv Anderson, the first black player to be capped by England, came through at the City Ground and was a European champion at 23.

The signings who became legends include John Robertson, Peter Shilton, Trevor Francis, Kenny Burns, Stuart Pearce, bought from Coventry for £200,000 in 1985 and the club's greatest captain, [Roy Keane](/players/roy-keane), bought from Cobh Ramblers for £47,000 in 1990, Stan Collymore, and, in the modern era, Morgan Gibbs-White, signed from Wolves for £25m in 2022 as the club's record purchase and now its captain and creative centre; Chris Wood, who scored 20 league goals in 2024-25; Murillo and Nikola Milenković at centre-back; and Elliot Anderson, bought from Newcastle for £35m in 2024 and sold to Manchester City for £116m in June 2026, the largest sale in the club's history.

## Identity and playing style

[Oliver Glasner](/managers/oliver-glasner) won the Europa League with Eintracht Frankfurt in 2022 and the FA Cup, Community Shield and Conference League with Crystal Palace between 2025 and 2026, all with the same system: a 3-4-2-1, a [back three](/tactics/back-three) that defends in a compact mid-block, wing-backs who provide the width, and two attacking midfielders who play off a physical centre-forward. It is a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) structure that becomes very direct once the ball is won, and at Palace it made Jean-Philippe Mateta, Eberechi Eze and Adam Wharton into stars.

At Forest the pieces are familiar: Murillo, Milenković and the summer signing Ousmane Diomande (from Sporting CP for £34m) in the back three; Ola Aina and Neco Williams or Daniel Muñoz as wing-backs; Xaver Schlager, signed on a free from RB Leipzig, Douglas Luiz (from Juventus for £21m) and Ibrahim Sangaré in the pivot; Gibbs-White and Dan Ndoye behind Wood, Igor Jesus or Liam Delap. The early weeks have shown the shape without the fluency; Forest have scored six goals in nine league matches.

## Rivalries

[Derby County](/clubs/derby-county), 15 miles down the A52, is the rivalry that matters, given extra bite by Clough managing both clubs and by the Brian Clough Trophy that the fixture is played for. [Leicester City](/clubs/leicester-city) are the East Midlands rival of the Premier League era and [Notts County](/clubs/notts-county), across the Trent, the oldest neighbour, though the two have rarely met in recent decades.

## Current situation

Forest are 19th in early November 2026, with one win, three draws and five defeats from nine, six goals scored and 12 conceded. The autumn produced a 3-1 defeat at Crystal Palace on 11 October against Glasner's former club, a 1-0 home loss to Arsenal on 18 October, a 1-1 draw at Ipswich on 23 October and a 2-0 defeat at Brentford on 31 October.

The summer was net profitable: Anderson's £116m sale, Taiwo Awoniyi to Coventry for £9m, and six arrivals including Diomande, Luiz and Schlager. Glasner said in July that the market was "crazy" but the club was ambitious. The immediate ambition, after a season that began with Europe in mind, is simply to climb out of the bottom three; Marinakis's record with head coaches makes the next two months significant.
