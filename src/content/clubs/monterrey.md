## History

Monterrey was founded in 1945 by a group of businessmen in Mexico's industrial capital and spent its first decades as a yo-yo club, relegated twice and playing in the shadow of the capital's giants. The nickname Rayados comes from the striped shirt, and the first title took 41 years: the 1986 championship under the Argentine Cayetano Rodríguez, with the Uruguayan striker Francisco "Pancho" Peralta. Since then the club has been owned by FEMSA, the Coca-Cola bottler and convenience-store conglomerate based in the city, and its budget has grown with the group's ambitions.

The second title came in Clausura 2003 with Daniel Passarella as coach, Guillermo Franco and Walter Erviti, and the golden era under the Uruguayan Víctor Manuel Vucetich: Apertura 2009 and Apertura 2010 championships with the Chilean striker Humberto "Chupete" Suazo, Aldo de Nigris and the playmaker Luis Pérez, and three consecutive CONCACAF Champions Leagues (2011, 2012, 2013) that sent the club to three [Club World Cups](/tournaments/club-world-cup), finishing third in 2012 after a semi-final against Chelsea.

In 2015 FEMSA opened the Estadio BBVA, a 53,500-seat ground with a view of the Cerro de la Silla mountain that is widely considered the best stadium in Latin America. Antonio Mohamed's Apertura 2019 side beat [América](/clubs/club-america) on penalties for the fifth title, having won the Champions League again that year; a fifth CONCACAF crown followed in 2021 under Javier Aguirre.

## Trajectory

Rayados have been a top-four regular-season side almost every tournament since 2016 and have won only once in that time, a pattern that has frustrated the owners through the Fernando Ortiz and Domènec Torrent years. The 2025 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) was a highlight: with Sergio Ramos, signed that February, the team drew 1-1 with [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), drew with River Plate, beat Urawa 4-0 and lost 2-1 to [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) in the round of 16. The club's current squad data lists Matías Almeyda, the Argentine who had won the Clausura 2017 with Chivas and then coached Sevilla, as head coach as of November 2026. Rayados are ninth in the Apertura 2026 table with 21 points from 14 games.

## Players the club produced

Monterrey's academy has produced César Montes (sold to Espanyol and now at Lokomotiv Moscow), Jesús Gallardo, Johan Vásquez, Jonathan Orozco, the brothers Antonio and Aldo de Nigris, and in the 2020s the forward Alfonso González and the midfielder Jesús Corona's successors. The club's reputation rests on its imports from South America and Spain: Suazo, Rogelio Funes Mori (the all-time top scorer), César "Chelito" Delgado, Erviti, Sergio Canales from Betis in 2023, Lucas Ocampos, Esteban Andrada and Germán Berterame.

## Identity and playing style

Rayados are a club built on money and the expectation that goes with it, and their best teams have been organised, counter-punching sides rather than possession machines: Vucetich's 4-4-2 with two strikers and quick wide play, Mohamed's 2019 team built around Funes Mori's finishing and a deep block, Aguirre's pragmatic 2021 champions. Torrent tried a Guardiola-school possession game in 2025 with Canales as the brain; Almeyda has returned to a pressing, vertical 4-3-3 with Ocampos and Berterame running the channels. See [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) for the family of styles the club has favoured.

## Rivalries

The Clásico Regiomontano against [Tigres UANL](/clubs/tigres-uanl) is the most intense derby in Mexico, two clubs from the same metropolitan area divided by class and campus, FEMSA's Rayados against the university-rooted Tigres, and it has decided a championship final (Apertura 2017, won by Tigres) and multiple CONCACAF ties. The fixtures with [América](/clubs/club-america) and Santos Laguna, a northern rival in two Liguilla finals, come next.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Monterrey are ninth in the Apertura 2026 table under Almeyda, with Canales, Berterame, Ocampos and Andrada as the core and a game in hand on most of the clubs above them. The title drought since 2019 is the longest since the 1990s for a club with the league's biggest budget, and the Estadio BBVA, which hosted four World Cup matches in June 2026, is the stage on which the owners expect it to end.
