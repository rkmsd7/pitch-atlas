## History

Manchester City's women's section dates to 1988 but the modern club was relaunched in 2014, when City Football Group brought the team into the WSL with professional contracts, a purpose-built stadium at the Etihad Campus and a recruitment drive that signed Steph Houghton, [Lucy Bronze](/players/lucy-bronze), Jill Scott and Karen Bardsley. Within two seasons, under Nick Cushing, City were champions: the 2016 title was won unbeaten.

What followed was a decade of near misses. City finished second five times between 2017 and 2024, most painfully in 2023-24 when Gareth Taylor's side lost the title to [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women) on goal difference on the final day after a late collapse. Cups kept coming, with FA Cups in 2017, 2019 and 2020 and League Cups in 2016, 2019 and 2022, and the club reached the [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) semi-finals in 2018, but the league stayed out of reach.

Taylor was sacked in March 2025 after a fourth-place slide. Andrée Jeglertz, the Swede who had led Denmark and won the Champions League with Umeå in 2004, took over for 2025-26 and delivered immediately: 18 wins from 22, 62 goals, a four-point margin over [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal-women), a 5-1 thrashing of Chelsea in February 2026 and a 3-0 derby win over United in March. A 4-0 FA Cup final win over Brighton in May 2026 completed the double.

## Trajectory

Champions in 2016, runners-up seven times, never below fourth, and champions again in 2025-26. The decade-long wait was the longest any club has gone between titles while being consistently in contention, and the 2026 success has reset expectations: City are now a Champions League league-phase side with the money and the manager to go deep.

## Players the club produced

City's academy has produced Lauren Hemp (signed young from Bristol), Jess Park, Esme Morgan and Ruby Mace. The bigger impact has been signings: Houghton, Bronze (twice), Scott, Ellen White, Keira Walsh, [Chloe Kelly](/players/chloe-kelly), Alex Greenwood, [Khadija Shaw](/players/khadija-shaw) and [Vivianne Miedema](/players/vivianne-miedema) all played for City, and the club was the production line for the England team that won Euro 2022.

## Identity and playing style

Jeglertz plays a 4-3-3 that is more direct than Taylor's possession-heavy version. Yui Hasegawa, the Japan captain, controls from the base of midfield; Hemp attacks from the left; Shaw, the most prolific striker in the league, finishes; and Miedema, moved to the number 10 shirt in 2025, drops in as a second creator. Greenwood, the captain, organises a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and the team presses in coordinated waves. [Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) from Hemp's running were the difference in the title run-in.

## Rivalries

The Manchester derby against [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united-women), a fixture only since 2019, has quickly become heated. Chelsea were the title rival of the 2020s, Arsenal the obstacle in 2025-26.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 City are defending the title with Jeglertz, Shaw, Hemp, Hasegawa and Mary Fowler, and with Miedema back from a June 2026 knee operation. The Champions League, in which City have never passed the semi-finals, is the stated goal. Club captain Greenwood and goalkeeper Khiara Keating lead a defence that conceded just 19 league goals in the title season.
