## History

Futbol Club Barcelona were founded on 29 November 1899 by Joan Gamper, a Swiss accountant who placed an advertisement in Los Deportes looking for players. From the beginning the club was tied to Catalan identity: it won the first Copa del Rey in 1910, the first [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) title in 1929, and under the Franco dictatorship Camp Nou, opened in 1957, became the one public place where Catalan could be spoken freely. The phrase "més que un club" (more than a club), coined by president Narcís de Carreras in 1968, describes that political weight.

The 1950s side of László Kubala, Luis Suárez and Evaristo won two league titles and the first two Fairs Cups, but the signing of [Johan Cruyff](/players/johan-cruyff) in 1973 shaped everything that followed. Cruyff's single title as a player (1973-74, with a 5-0 at the Bernabéu) was less important than his return as coach in 1988. The Dream Team of [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), Ronald Koeman, Michael Laudrup, Hristo Stoichkov and Romário won four consecutive leagues (1991-94) and the club's first European Cup at Wembley in 1992, and Cruyff's insistence on the 3-4-3, positional play and the La Masia academy created a philosophy the club still measures itself against.

The decade after Cruyff brought [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario), [Rivaldo](/players/rivaldo), [Luís Figo](/players/luis-figo) and two titles under Louis van Gaal, then a slump: sixth in 2002-03 and no trophy between 1999 and 2005. Frank Rijkaard, [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho), [Samuel Eto'o](/players/samuel-etoo) and a teenage [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) restored the club with two leagues and the 2006 Champions League, and Guardiola's appointment in 2008 produced the greatest club side of the century: the 2009 sextuple, 14 trophies in four years, the 2011 Wembley final against Manchester United, with [Xavi](/players/xavi), [Andrés Iniesta](/players/andres-iniesta), Sergio Busquets, [Carles Puyol](/players/carles-puyol) and Gerard Piqué. [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique)'s MSN of Messi, [Luis Suárez](/players/luis-suarez) and Neymar added a second treble in 2015.

The late 2010s were an era of decline masked by Messi: the 2017 Paris collapse, the 2018 Rome and 2019 Anfield comebacks against them, the 8-2 against Bayern in 2020, Messi's forced exit in 2021 when the club could not register his contract, debts above €1bn. [Xavi](/managers/xavi-manager) won the 2022-23 title before [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick), arriving in summer 2024, rebuilt the team around teenagers and won the domestic treble in 2024-25 and the league again in 2025-26.

Guardiola's four seasons deserve the detail. In 2008-09 Barcelona won the league, the Copa del Rey (4-1 against Athletic) and the Champions League (2-0 against Manchester United in Rome, Eto'o and Messi), then the UEFA Super Cup, the Supercopa and the Club World Cup for an unprecedented six trophies in a calendar year. The 2009-10 league was won with 99 points; the 2010-11 side produced the 5-0 Clásico in November and the 3-1 Wembley final against United that Alex Ferguson called the best team he had faced. Messi scored 73 goals in 2011-12, the final season, and Guardiola left exhausted. Luis Enrique's 2014-15 treble side, with Messi, Suárez and Neymar scoring 122 goals between them, beat Juventus 3-1 in Berlin.

## Trajectory

Barcelona have never been relegated and have finished in the top three in every season since 2003-04. The Messi era produced ten league titles in thirteen seasons (2009-2019) and four Champions Leagues (2006, 2009, 2011, 2015). The barren spell between 2019 and 2023 was more about money than football: the club's wage bill forced sales and a reliance on the academy that, ironically, produced the next great generation.

The last two seasons under Flick have been the most dominant since Guardiola. In 2024-25 Barcelona won La Liga, the Copa del Rey (a 3-2 final win over Real Madrid in Seville) and the Supercopa, and reached the Champions League semi-final, losing 7-6 on aggregate to Inter. In 2025-26 they retained the title with 94 points and 95 goals, won the Supercopa 3-2 against Madrid in Jeddah on 11 January 2026 with two goals from [Raphinha](/players/raphinha), and went out of the Champions League in the quarter-finals to [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid), 3-2 on aggregate. The women's side, [Barcelona Women](/clubs/barcelona-women), won a fourth Champions League in Oslo in May 2026, and Alexia Putellas's third Ballon d'Or in October completed a year in which the club's two senior teams were both the best in their leagues. The Champions League, though, is the gap in the Flick era: a semi-final in 2025, a quarter-final in 2026, and the sixth title the club has not won since 2015 is the obvious next target.

The financial story runs underneath all of it. The Bartomeu board's spending after Neymar's €222m sale in 2017 (Coutinho, Dembélé, Griezmann) left the club with debts above €1.3bn by 2021, forced Messi's departure and led Joan Laporta's second presidency to sell future television and merchandising income ("palancas") to register players. The squad rebuilt from the academy out of necessity, and the result, a team with an average age under 25 winning consecutive titles, has become the model other clubs cite.

## Players the club produced

La Masia is the most celebrated academy in the game. The 2010 Ballon d'Or podium of Messi, Iniesta and Xavi was entirely home-grown, as were Puyol, Piqué, Busquets, Víctor Valdés, Cesc Fàbregas, Pedro and Guardiola himself. The current generation is just as striking: [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal) debuted at 15 in 2023, won Euro 2024 and the 2026 World Cup with [Spain](/nations/spain), finished third in the 2026 Ballon d'Or and won a third consecutive Kopa Trophy; [Pau Cubarsí](/players/pau-cubarsi), 19, was the World Cup's Best Young Player; [Gavi](/players/gavi) and Fermín López came through the same academy; [Pedri](/players/pedri), signed from Las Palmas at 17, is the midfield's conductor and was integral to Spain's World Cup win alongside Golden Ball winner [Rodri](/players/rodri), who now plays for Barcelona after his summer 2026 move from Manchester City.

Signed greats fill out the roll: Kubala, Cruyff, Diego Maradona briefly, Koeman, Stoichkov, Romário, Ronaldo, Rivaldo, Ronaldinho, Eto'o, Suárez, Neymar, [Robert Lewandowski](/players/robert-lewandowski), and the 2026 arrivals Gabriel Jesus and Anthony Gordon. [Antoine Griezmann](/players/antoine-griezmann) and [Luís Figo](/players/luis-figo) are the two whose stays ended more awkwardly than they began.

## Identity and playing style

No club is more defined by a single idea. Cruyff's [total football](/tactics/total-football) became Guardiola's [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and the [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) of the 2008-2012 era: a 4-3-3, a passing midfield, pressing within six seconds of losing the ball, the [false nine](/tactics/false-nine) Messi. The academy still teaches rondos from the age of seven.

[Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick) has kept the 4-2-3-1 base but added German aggression. His defining tool is a [high line](/tactics/high-line) held 35 metres from goal, which caught opponents offside at record rates in 2024-25 and 2025-26 and compresses the pitch so that [Gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) can be sustained. Pedri and De Jong (or Rodri) build from a double pivot, Raphinha and Yamal attack from the wings with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) Jules Koundé and Alejandro Balde overlapping, and Dani Olmo plays between the lines. The cost is exposure in transition, as the Clásico defeat on 25 October 2026 and the 46-12 goal record after 11 games (both extremes) show.

Flick's methods are worth describing because they are so unusual. He trains the offside line as a collective action, with the whole defence stepping on a trigger (the pass being played, not the runner moving), and accepts that the occasional goal conceded on the break is the price of pinning opponents in their own half. In 2024-25 Barcelona caught opponents offside more than 200 times in the league, far more than any other team in Europe. The pressing is coordinated by Pedri, who reads the trigger and leads the midfield forward, and Yamal is excused some defensive work so that his dribbling is fresh for the moments that decide matches.

## Rivalries

The Clásico against [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) is the defining fixture in Spain and arguably the world, a contest of two cities, two cultures and two models. The Derbi Barceloní against [Espanyol](/clubs/espanyol), the city's other club, has decided a title (2025) and a relegation. The rivalry with Atlético grew in the Simeone years and intensified when Atlético knocked Barcelona out of the 2025-26 Champions League. In Europe, [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) (the 6-1 of 2017, the Neymar saga, Dembélé) and [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) (the 8-2) carry the sharpest memories.

## Current situation

Barcelona lead La Liga on 7 November 2026 with 30 points from 11 matches, ten wins and one defeat, 46 goals scored. The only loss was the Clásico at Camp Nou on matchday 10, and Flick's side responded by hammering Rayo Vallecano 5-2. The squad was reshaped in summer 2026: Rodri arrived from Manchester City, Gabriel Jesus from Arsenal, Anthony Gordon from Newcastle and Karim Adeyemi from Dortmund, while Lewandowski and Ter Stegen moved on and Joan García, signed from Espanyol in 2025, competes with Wojciech Szczęsny in goal.

The season's complications are familiar: Lamine Yamal's groin problem has flared up again, with Flick publicly criticising the Spanish federation for playing him through pain and ruling him out of the Champions League tie against Brest on 26 November; Flick's own future beyond 2027 is unresolved; and the rebuilt Camp Nou, reopened in stages through 2025-26, is still being completed. Three league titles in a row and a sixth Champions League are the targets. Barcelona are, as of November 2026, the best team in Spain, and they know it.
