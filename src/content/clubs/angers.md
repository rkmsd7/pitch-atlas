## History

Angers SCO were founded in 1919 and turned professional in 1945. Their best years were the 1950s and 1960s: second in Division 1 in 1957 and third in 1959, with a Coupe de France final in 1957 lost to Toulouse, and the presence from 1960 of Jean-Pierre Dogliani, the club's great playmaker. The stadium, Jean-Bouin, was renamed in 2017 for [Raymond Kopa](/players/raymond-kopa), the Ballon d'Or winner who began his career at Angers in 1949 before Reims and Real Madrid.

Relegation in 1981 began a long decline: a brief Division 1 return in 1993-94, then the second division, and in 2002 relegation to the National (third tier), where the club spent five seasons. Promoted in 2007, Angers hovered in the Ligue 2 play-off places for years under Jean-Louis Garcia and then Stéphane Moulin, a former player who coached the club from 2011 to 2021. Fourth four seasons running, they finally went up as third in 2015, and Moulin's side finished ninth in its first Ligue 1 campaign since 1994 and reached the 2017 Coupe de France final, lost 1-0 to PSG to a stoppage-time own goal.

Six more seasons of lower-mid-table followed before a disastrous 2022-23 under three coaches that ended in relegation with 18 points, one of the worst records in Ligue 1 history. Alexandre Dujeux brought them straight back up as runners-up in 2024, and the club has survived two seasons since on a small budget, finishing 14th and 15th. Stéphane Gilli, Dujeux's former assistant, is head coach for 2026-27.

## Trajectory

From the third tier in 2007 to the top flight in 2015 and a cup final in 2017, then a decline to relegation in 2023 and an immediate return. Angers have spent ten of the last eleven seasons in Ligue 1 with a budget among the smallest, a stadium of under 19,000 and a policy of selling anyone who attracts an offer. The owner since 2011, Saïd Chabane, has kept the club solvent through sales.

## Players the club produced

Kopa is the name, though he left at 20. The modern academy has produced Angelo Fulgini (sold to Mainz), Mohamed-Ali Cho (Real Sociedad), Azzedine Ounahi (Marseille), Ibrahima Niane and Himad Abdelli (Marseille, 2025), and the club has been a stepping stone for Nicolas Pépé (Lille, then Arsenal), Jeff Reine-Adélaïde, Rayan Aït-Nouri and Sofiane Boufal. Cheikh N'Doye, Ismaël Traoré, Pierrick Capelle and goalkeeper Ludovic Butelle were the leaders of the Moulin years. Esteban Lepaul has been the recent goalscorer.

## Identity and playing style

Moulin's Angers were the archetypal small-club survivors: a 4-4-2 or 4-2-3-1, a [low block](/tactics/low-block), [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking), with a famous team spirit and a coach who stayed ten years. Dujeux's promotion side played more on the front foot, and Gilli has continued with a 4-3-3 that presses in midfield and relies on the pace of its wide forwards, though the goals have been hard to find.

## Rivalries

Nantes, 90 kilometres down the Loire, are the regional rival, though Nantes's 2026 relegation has suspended the fixture; [Le Mans](/clubs/le-mans), 95 kilometres north-east, returned to Ligue 1 in 2026 and revived the derby of the Pays de la Loire. [Stade Rennais](/clubs/rennes) are the western neighbour Angers have rarely matched.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Angers are 15th in Ligue 1 on eight points from nine games (two wins, two draws, five defeats, nine scored, 17 conceded), level on points with Troyes in the play-off place and one above the automatic relegation spots. They lost 5-2 at home to Marseille on 18 October, drew 0-0 with Lorient at home on 25 October and lost 2-0 at Lyon on 1 November. Nice visit the Raymond-Kopa on 8 November.

Gilli's side has the usual Angers profile, organised but short of goals, and the squad built around Lepaul, Yassine Belkhdim, Carlens Arcus and goalkeeper Hervé Koffi is in a familiar fight. The objective, as every year, is survival, and the winter window will decide how much help Gilli gets.
