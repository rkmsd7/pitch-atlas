## History

Bodø/Glimt were founded in 1916 in Bodø, a fishing and military town of 50,000 people on the Norwegian coast 90 kilometres north of the Arctic Circle, and for most of their history they were an outsider in every sense. Clubs from northern Norway were barred from the national league until 1972, and Glimt's early fame came from the cup: in 1975 they became the first northern club to win the Norwegian Cup, beating Vard Haugesund in Oslo, and they won it again in 1993 under Trond Sollied, the year they were promoted. Harald "Dutte" Berg, the club's first great player, became a Norwegian international in the 1960s and his son Runar and grandson Patrick followed him into the first team.

The club's history until 2017 was a cycle of promotion and relegation. A second-place finish in 2003 and a cup final the same year were followed by relegation in 2005 and 2009, promotion in 2007 and 2013, relegation again in 2016. Nothing suggested what was coming.

Kjetil Knutsen was promoted from assistant to head coach in 2018 and, with the sporting director Aasmund Bjørkan and a set of ideas borrowed from the mental coach Bjørn Mannsverk, a former fighter pilot, he built a team that won the Norwegian First Division in 2017 and then, remarkably, finished second in the top flight in 2019 and won it in 2020 with 81 points, 19 clear of Molde, scoring 103 goals. It was the first title for any club from northern Norway. The team was retained in 2021 and, in the Conference League group stage, beat [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s Roma 6-1 at Aspmyra on an artificial pitch in the Arctic cold, a result that made the club famous across Europe. Titles followed in 2023 and 2024, and in 2024-25 Glimt reached the Europa League semi-final, beating Twente, Olympiacos and Lazio before losing to Tottenham, the first Norwegian club to reach a European semi-final. The 2025-26 Champions League league phase was the club's first and included a win over Tottenham at Aspmyra.

## Trajectory

The trajectory is the story: a second-division club in 2017, Norwegian champions in 2020, Europa League semi-finalists in 2025 and Champions League regulars by 2026. Four titles in five seasons from 2020 to 2024, two second-place finishes, a Conference League quarter-final in 2022 (after knocking out Celtic), a Europa League quarter-final in 2023-24 and the semi-final the following year. Glimt are in the 2026-27 Champions League league phase alongside fellow Norwegians Viking, with four points from four matches as of November 2026.

## Players the club produced

Glimt's model is to develop players in their system and sell them at the top of the market: Jens Petter Hauge to AC Milan in 2020, Kasper Junker to Urawa, Philip Zinckernagel to Watford, Hugo Vetlesen to Club Brugge, Albert Grønbæk to Rennes, Victor Boniface to Union Saint-Gilloise and then Bayer Leverkusen, Amahl Pellegrino and Ola Solbakken to Roma. Patrick Berg, the captain and the third generation of his family at the club, returned from Lens; Ulrik Saltnes has played more than 400 games as the midfield's organiser. The club has also developed a knack for turning overlooked Norwegian and Danish players into European performers.

## Identity and playing style

Bodø/Glimt play a fast, high-pressing 4-3-3 that Knutsen has barely changed in eight years: a [high line](/tactics/high-line), [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) after loss, quick combinations through the half-spaces and relentless running, all drilled on the artificial surface at Aspmyra, which the club argues is a legitimate home advantage. The club's identity is cultural as much as tactical, built on the psychological work of Mannsverk, a flat hierarchy, and an insistence that a town of 50,000 can compete with capitals. The yellow kit, the Arctic setting and the Roma result have made Glimt one of the best-known small clubs in the world.

## Rivalries

The northern derby against Tromsø, the only other club from the far north with a top-flight history, is the local rivalry. Rosenborg, the dominant Norwegian club of the 1990s and 2000s, and Molde, the main title rival of the 2020s, are the national ones.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Bodø/Glimt are in the Champions League league phase with four points from four matches, in the lower-middle of the table and fighting for a play-off place. The Norwegian season runs on a calendar year, and the club's domestic campaign should be checked against the final Eliteserien table. Knutsen remains in charge, Berg and Saltnes still anchor the side, and the ambition is the one the club has stated for years: to prove that the model works in the Champions League as well as it has in the Europa and Conference Leagues.
