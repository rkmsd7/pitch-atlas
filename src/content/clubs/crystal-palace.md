## History

Crystal Palace were founded in 1905 by the company that owned the Crystal Palace exhibition building and its grounds, where the FA Cup final was played from 1895 to 1914. Evicted during the First World War, the club settled at Selhurst Park in 1924 and spent four decades in the lower divisions, winning the Third Division in 1921. Bert Head took them to the First Division for the first time in 1969; Malcolm Allison's rebranding in the 1970s gave them the eagle, the red and blue stripes and the "Team of the Eighties" tag that Terry Venables's young side briefly justified by topping the table in 1979.

Steve Coppell's team was the best Palace side of the 20th century: promotion in 1989, the 1990 FA Cup final (3-3 with Manchester United, lost 1-0 in the replay) with [Ian Wright](/players/ian-wright) scoring twice off the bench, third in the First Division in 1991 with Wright and Mark Bright, and the Full Members Cup at Wembley the same year. Wright left for Arsenal and the club became a yo-yo side: four relegations from the Premier League between 1993 and 2005. Administration in 1999 and again in 2010, when a 10-point deduction nearly relegated them to League One, were the lows; the CPFC 2010 consortium of supporters led by Steve Parish bought the club out of the second administration.

Ian Holloway won promotion through the play-offs in 2013, Tony Pulis kept them up, and Alan Pardew took them to the 2016 FA Cup final. Roy Hodgson's four seasons and Patrick Vieira's two consolidated a club that was no longer going down. [Oliver Glasner](/managers/oliver-glasner), appointed in February 2024, then won the first major trophy in the club's history, the FA Cup, 1-0 against Manchester City on 17 May 2025 with Eberechi Eze's goal, followed by the Community Shield against Liverpool and, in May 2026, the UEFA Conference League. Glasner left for Nottingham Forest and [Pierre Sage](/managers/pierre-sage), the former Lyon and Lens coach, was appointed on a three-year deal in June 2026.

## Trajectory

Palace's 2000s were a Championship club's: finishes between fifth and 21st, one promotion in 2004 and an immediate relegation in 2005 on the last day at Charlton. Since 2013 they have been in the Premier League for 14 consecutive seasons, the longest run in their history, always between tenth and 15th. The 2024-25 season (12th, FA Cup) and 2025-26 (15th, Conference League) were the two most successful in the club's history despite the modest league positions. The Conference League win brought a Europa League place for 2026-27.

## Players the club produced

Palace's academy is one of the most productive in London: Wilfried Zaha, the club's modern icon with 458 appearances across two spells; Victor Moses, Nathaniel Clyne, John Bostock, Aaron Wan-Bissaka, Tyrick Mitchell and, in the current squad, Zavier Gozo and Caleb Kporha. [Ian Wright](/players/ian-wright) was signed from non-league Greenwich Borough at 21, which Palace count as their own development. The signings who became legends include Mark Bright, Geoff Thomas, Nigel Martyn, Julián Speroni, Jean-Philippe Mateta, [Eberechi Eze](/players/eberechi-eze), bought from QPR for £17m and sold to Arsenal in 2025 after scoring the FA Cup final winner, Marc Guéhi, sold to Manchester City in January 2026, Maxence Lacroix, sold to Chelsea for £52m in summer 2026, and Adam Wharton, the midfielder who stayed.

## Identity and playing style

[Pierre Sage](/managers/pierre-sage) built his reputation at Lyon, where he took over a side bottom of Ligue 1 in November 2023 and finished sixth, and at Lens, where his 2025-26 side finished in the top half playing attractive, vertical football. He prefers a 4-3-3 with a high press and quick combinations, which is a departure from Glasner's 3-4-2-1 and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) structure, and the transition has been uneven. Óscar Mingueza, his first signing on a free from Celta Vigo, and Takehiro Tomiyasu have been used at full-back and centre-back; Dean Henderson remains the goalkeeper; Wharton and Daichi Kamada run the midfield; and Mateta, Ismaïla Sarr, Yéremy Pino and Jørgen Strand Larsen, the striker signed from Wolves, share the attack. Dwight McNeil arrived from Everton and Brennan Johnson went the other way.

Palace completed 11 incoming transfers over the summer, a figure reported by a single outlet, with Lacroix's sale the headline departure.

## Rivalries

The M23 derby with [Brighton & Hove Albion](/clubs/brighton) is the club's defining rivalry, dating from the Venables-Mullery feud of the 1970s; Brighton's 5-0 win on 18 October 2026 was the heaviest result in the fixture's recent history. [Millwall](/clubs/millwall) and [Charlton Athletic](/clubs/charlton-athletic) are the south London neighbours from the lower-division years.

## Current situation

Palace are 18th in early November 2026 with two wins, one draw and six defeats from nine: nine goals scored and 21 conceded, seven points. The results include a 3-1 home win over Nottingham Forest on 11 October, then the 5-0 at Brighton, a 2-0 home defeat to Newcastle on 25 October and a 2-0 loss at Tottenham on 31 October. The Europa League campaign, the club's first in that competition, has added fixtures to a squad that lost Guéhi, Lacroix, Eze and Johnson within a year.

Sage was hired for a three-year project and the chairman Steve Parish has historically been patient, but a club that won two trophies in two seasons now finds itself in a relegation fight. The ambition is a mid-table recovery; the immediate need is defensive solidity.
