## History

San Lorenzo de Almagro was founded in 1908 by a group of boys playing in the streets of Almagro under the protection of the Salesian priest Lorenzo Massa, who let them use the churchyard on condition they attended mass, and the club's name honours him and the saint. The club moved to the Boedo neighbourhood in 1916 and built the Gasómetro, the stadium that hosted the national team for decades and was sold under financial duress in 1979, during the dictatorship, to a supermarket chain. The loss of Boedo is the club's wound; the "Vuelta a Boedo" campaign to return there has been the fans' cause for forty years, and in 2012 the city legislature voted to allow the land to be bought back.

The nickname El Ciclón came from the 1930s team of Bernabé Ferreyra's rival forwards, and the club's golden teams were the 1946 champions of the "Terceto de Oro" (Rinaldo Martino, René Pontoni, Armando Farro), who toured Spain and Portugal to acclaim, and the "Matadores" of 1968, the first undefeated champions of the professional era, with the Uruguayan Pedro González and the striker Héctor "Bambi" Veira. José Sanfilippo, the club's all-time top scorer, and Héctor Scotta, who scored 60 goals in the calendar year 1975, were the strikers of the 1950s and 1970s.

In 1981 San Lorenzo was relegated, the first of the big five to go down, and the 1982 Nacional B season, with 60,000 crowds following a second-division team, became a point of pride. The 1990s brought the Nuevo Gasómetro in the Bajo Flores district in 1993 and the 1995 Clausura title under Héctor Veira, and the early 2000s Ramón Díaz's Clausura 2001 champions, the Copa Mercosur of 2001 and the first Copa Sudamericana in 2002 with Leandro Romagnoli as the playmaker.

## Trajectory

The modern peak came in August 2014, when Edgardo Bauza's team won the Copa Libertadores for the first time, 2-1 on aggregate against Nacional of Paraguay, with Néstor Ortigoza's penalty settling the second leg at the Nuevo Gasómetro. Ignacio Piatti, Romagnoli, Ortigoza and the young [Ángel Correa](/players/angel-correa), sold to Atlético Madrid that year, were the core. The club lost the [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) final 2-0 to Real Madrid in Morocco that December, with Pope Francis, a club member since childhood and the most famous San Lorenzo fan in the world, following from Rome. The Pope's death in April 2025 was mourned at the club with a mass at the stadium.

The years since 2014 have been difficult. Second place in 2015 and a 2017-18 top-three finish gave way to debt, unpaid wages, a 2021 season near the bottom, and in 2025 a governance crisis that saw the president Marcelo Moretti suspended over an alleged bribery video and the club placed under provisional administration. Miguel Ángel Russo coached the team in early 2025 before leaving for Boca; the club's current squad data lists Rubén Darío Insúa, who had coached the club to third place in 2023, as head coach as of November 2026. San Lorenzo are 11th in Group A of the Torneo Clausura 2026 with 19 points from 16 games.

## Players the club produced

The Ciudad Deportiva academy has produced Correa, Pablo Zabaleta (via Espanyol to Manchester City), Ezequiel Lavezzi, Gonzalo Bergessio, Ortigoza's contemporaries, Adolfo Gaich, Agustín Giay (sold to Palmeiras in 2024) and the current goalkeeper Orlando Gill. Romagnoli, "Pipi", played more than 400 games over four spells; Néstor Gorosito, Alberto "Beto" Acosta, Piatti and the Uruguayan striker Alexis Cuello were the great signings.

## Identity and playing style

San Lorenzo's identity is Boedo, Catholic social origins and a sense of grievance, and its football has been playmaker-centred for a century: Pontoni, Veira, Gorosito, Romagnoli. Bauza's 2014 champions were the exception, a pragmatic 4-4-2 that defended in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and attacked through Piatti's dribbling and Ortigoza's set pieces. Insúa's teams play a compact 5-3-2 or 4-4-2 and counter, with the goalkeeper Gill and the defender Gastón Hernández the foundations and Cuello and Matías Reali the outlets; the squad is thin and the budget is the thinnest of the big five.

## Rivalries

The Clásico Porteño against Huracán, the neighbours from Parque Patricios, is the fixture that matters most to the barrio, and its derbies have decided title races and relegation battles for both clubs. The big-five games against [Boca Juniors](/clubs/boca-juniors), [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate), [Independiente](/clubs/independiente) and [Racing](/clubs/racing-club) complete the calendar.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 San Lorenzo are 11th in Group A of the Torneo Clausura, outside the knockout places with the Argentine season in its final weeks, under Insúa, with Gill, Hernández, Elián Iraola, Cuello and Reali as the core. The club remains in administration-led restructuring, the return to Boedo is still a project rather than a stadium, and the ambition is simple: stability, a return to the continental competitions and, one day, a ground in the neighbourhood where it was born.
