## History

SV Werder Bremen were founded on 4 February 1899 by sixteen-year-old students who had won a football in a tug-of-war competition. "Werder" refers to the river island on the Weser where the club played, and the Weserstadion still sits on that bank. Werder won their first championship in 1965, two years into the Bundesliga, under Willi Multhaup, but were relegated in 1980, which turned out to be the making of them: Otto Rehhagel, appointed in 1981, stayed 14 years and built the club's greatest era.

Rehhagel's Werder, with Rudi Völler, Karl-Heinz Riedle, Marco Bode, Dieter Eilts, Andreas Herzog and Wynton Rufer, won the title in 1988 and 1993, the DFB-Pokal in 1991 and 1994 and the 1992 Cup Winners' Cup, beating Monaco 2-0 in Lisbon. They lost the 1986 title on the final day when Michael Kutzop's penalty hit the post against Bayern. Rehhagel's "controlled offensive" became a phrase in German football, and his departure for Bayern in 1995 was followed by a decade of modest results.

Thomas Schaaf, a one-club man as a player, took over in 1999 and stayed 14 years too. His 2003-04 side, with Johan Micoud, Ailton (28 goals), Ivan Klasnić, Torsten Frings and Frank Baumann, won the double, sealing the title at Bayern in May 2004. Werder then finished in the top three five times in seven seasons with Miroslav Klose, Diego, Mesut Özil and Claudio Pizarro, reached the 2009 UEFA Cup final (lost to Shakhtar in Istanbul after beating Hamburg in the semi on away goals) and won the 2009 DFB-Pokal against Leverkusen. The Weserstadion was rebuilt around them.

The money ran out. Schaaf left in 2013, and a succession of coaches finished between 8th and 16th before Florian Kohfeldt's side survived the 2020 play-off against Heidenheim and then went down in 2021. [Ole Werner](/managers/ole-werner) got them straight back up in 2022, finished 13th, 9th and 8th and left for Leipzig in 2025. Horst Steffen, the coach who had taken Elversberg from the fourth tier to the brink of the Bundesliga, succeeded him for 2025-26, and Daniel Thioune, formerly of Osnabrück, Hamburg and Fortuna Düsseldorf, is head coach for 2026-27.

## Trajectory

Werder's trajectory is a long plateau at the top (1983-2010, with four titles and consistent European football) followed by a decline that bottomed out with relegation in 2021 and has since stabilised in the top half. The club lost its competitive edge when its model of developing and selling (Özil to Real Madrid, Diego to Juventus) stopped producing successors, and the Covid years pushed it over the edge financially. Under Werner and his successors, with an owner-less, member-run structure and a modest budget, the club has been upper-mid table for four seasons and in 2026-27 is pushing for the European places.

Two moments stand for the Schaaf era. On 8 May 2004 Werder went to the Olympiastadion needing a win to clinch the title with a game to spare and beat Bayern 3-1, with Ailton scoring and Micoud running the game; a week later they beat Alemannia Aachen in the cup final for the double. Five years on, on 30 April 2009, they drew 2-2 at the Volksparkstadion in the UEFA Cup semi-final to go through on away goals against Hamburg, a week after losing to them in the cup semi-final and days before beating them again in the league: three northern derbies in a fortnight that defined the rivalry for a generation.

## Players the club produced

Werder's academy has produced Marco Bode, Dieter Eilts, Frank Baumann, Per Mertesacker, Tim Borowski, Aaron Hunt, Maximilian Eggestein, Nick Woltemade (sold to Stuttgart) and Eren Dinkçi. The club's best work was in buying: Völler from 1860, Riedle from Blau-Weiß Berlin, Micoud from Parma, Ailton from Guarani, Klose from Kaiserslautern, Diego from Porto (26 league goals and assists in his first season), Özil from Schalke and Pizarro, who had three spells and scored a Bundesliga goal at 40. Marvin Ducksch has been the goalscorer of the post-promotion years.

## Identity and playing style

Werder's identity is attacking football and loyalty to coaches: Rehhagel and Schaaf gave the club 28 years of continuity, and both played a 4-4-2 or 4-3-3 that pushed full-backs high and trusted creative number tens. Schaaf's 2004-10 sides were among the most entertaining in Europe, scoring 79 league goals in the title season. Werner's post-promotion Werder played a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with wing-backs and two strikers; Daniel Thioune has kept the back three and added a more structured press with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) in midfield and quick [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Ducksch and Romano Schmid.

## Rivalries

The **Nordderby** against [Hamburg](/clubs/hamburg) is the northern classic, with Werder's 2009 UEFA Cup semi-final win on away goals (a Frings goal in a 3-2 defeat at the Weserstadion) and their joint spell in the 2. Bundesliga in 2021-22 the recent chapters; HSV's promotion in 2025 restored it to the top flight. [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) were the title rivals of the 1980s and 2000s, with the Kutzop penalty and the 2004 title at the Olympiastadion the two extremes. St Pauli are the other northern neighbour.

## Current situation

Werder have made a good start to 2026-27. As of 7 November 2026 they are fifth in the Bundesliga on 15 points from eight games (four wins, three draws, one defeat, 17 scored, 14 conceded). They drew 2-2 with Paderborn on 17 October, won 3-2 at Mainz on 24 October and beat Hoffenheim 2-0 at home on 31 October. The next fixture is at Stuttgart on 7 November, followed by the Nordderby at home to Hamburg on 22 November.

Thioune works with a squad built around Ducksch, Mitchell Weiser, Schmid, Jens Stage and captain Marco Friedl. The club's aim, stated cautiously, is a return to European football for the first time since 2010, and the early table has them in the Europa League places.
