## History

Vitória Sport Clube were founded in 1922 in Guimarães, the medieval city that calls itself the birthplace of Portugal because the country's first king, Afonso Henriques, was born there in the twelfth century. The stadium is named after him and the club's nickname, the Conquerors, borrows his legend. Vitória are the most popular club in Portugal outside the big three and Braga, with a passionate support that fills the D. Afonso Henriques for every big match.

The club's history has been one of near-misses rather than trophies. Vitória reached the Taça de Portugal final in 1942, 1963, 1976 and 1988, losing each time, and finished third in the league in 1969, 1987 and 2008. The 1988 Supertaça, won against Porto, was the first silverware, and the 1986-87 side under Marinho Peres that qualified for the UEFA Cup and beat Atlético Madrid remains fondly remembered. A relegation in 2006, the first since the 1960s, was followed by an immediate return as second-division champions and a third-place finish in 2008 under Manuel Cajuda.

The breakthrough came on 26 May 2013 at the Jamor, when Rui Vitória's team came from behind to beat [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) 2-1 in the Taça de Portugal final, with two goals from the Brazilian striker Tomané, to win the club's first major trophy. Vitória has since reached the 2017 final, lost to Benfica, and been a consistent top-six side, finishing fifth in three consecutive seasons from 2023 to 2025 and qualifying for the Conference League.

## Trajectory

Vitória's league record since 2000 is one of mid-table respectability with occasional peaks and one trough: sixth in 2001, the shock relegation of 2006, third in 2008, sixth with the cup in 2013, fourth in 2017 and fifth from 2023 to 2025. Europe has brought group stages in the Europa League in 2019-20 and the Conference League in 2023-24 and 2024-25, when the team reached the knockout rounds. The gap to Braga, who have finished above Vitória in most recent seasons, is the measure the club's supporters use.

## Players the club produced

Vitória's academy and scouting have supplied a long list of Portugal internationals. Nuno Gomes, who went on to Benfica and Fiorentina, began his career at Guimarães; Paulo Bento, later Portugal's coach, played there in the 1990s; and Pedro Mendes, Moreno and João Alves were club stalwarts. More recently the club developed or launched Rafael Martins, Tiago Silva, the winger Jota (sold to Celtic), André Almeida (to Valencia) and Jota Silva (to Nottingham Forest). The club's connection to agent Jorge Mendes, whose first business was in the city, is part of Portuguese football folklore.

## Identity and playing style

Vitória's identity is built on the crowd and on a tradition of hard-working, well-organised teams that compete physically with the big three and attack with pace in transition, typically in a 4-3-3 or 4-2-3-1. As of November 2026 the team is led by Tiago Margarido, a young Portuguese coach appointed during the season, with an emphasis on [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) and strong [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces).

## Rivalries

The Minho derby against [Braga](/clubs/braga) is one of the most intense in Portugal, with the two cities thirteen miles apart and the rivalry rooted in centuries of civic competition. Matches against [Porto](/clubs/porto), the nearest of the big three, draw the biggest crowds.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Vitória are thirteenth in the Primeira Liga after ten rounds with nine points, a worrying start that has already cost one coach his job and left the club closer to the relegation places than to the European ones. The immediate task for Margarido is survival-proofing the season and restoring the top-six consistency of the last three years; the squad is thinner than it was after summer departures, and the club's supporters have made their frustration clear.
