## History

Columbus was the first team announced for MLS, in June 1994, and in 1999 it opened the first soccer-specific stadium built for the league, Columbus Crew Stadium, paid for by the owner Lamar Hunt. The early teams revolved around Brian McBride, the United States striker, and the Ohio crowd took to the game faster than the sceptics expected. The club won the U.S. Open Cup in 2002 and the Supporters' Shield in 2004.

The golden era arrived with Guillermo Barros Schelotto, the Boca Juniors legend who joined in 2007 at 34 and was MLS MVP in 2008 as Sigi Schmid's side won the Shield and MLS Cup double, beating the New York Red Bulls 3-1 in the final. Frankie Hejduk's leadership, Chad Marshall's defending and the "Nordecke" supporters' corner all date from that era. A second Shield followed in 2009.

The club's most important moment came off the pitch. In October 2017 the owner Anthony Precourt announced plans to move the team to Austin, Texas. The "Save The Crew" campaign, backed by the city, state legislators and supporters across the league, kept the team in Columbus; the Haslam family and Pete Edwards bought it in 2018, and a new downtown stadium opened in 2021.

## Trajectory

Caleb Porter won the club's second MLS Cup in December 2020, a 3-0 victory over Seattle at the old stadium with Lucas Zelarayán scoring twice, and Wilfried Nancy, appointed in 2023, delivered the third: a 2-1 win over [LAFC](/clubs/lafc) in December 2023 with Cucho Hernández and Yaw Yeboah scoring. Nancy's 2024 team then won the Leagues Cup, beating LAFC again in the final, and the Campeones Cup against Club América, and reached the CONCACAF Champions Cup final, losing to Pachuca. The 2025 season was quieter, with Hernández sold to [Real Betis](/clubs/real-betis) and a seventh-place finish.

Nancy left after 2025 and the club's current squad data lists Laurent Courtois, formerly of Montréal, as head coach. The 2026 regular season ended eighth in the East with 44 points, a wild-card play-off place as of early November 2026.

## Players the club produced

The Crew academy produced Wil Trapp, the long-serving captain, and Aidan Morris, sold to Middlesbrough in 2024. The club's great recruits were McBride, Schelotto, Federico Higuaín (Gonzalo's elder brother, a decade in Columbus), Zelarayán, Hernández and Darlington Nagbe, the most decorated MLS player of his generation. Goalkeeper Patrick Schulte and the forward Diego Rossi, signed from Fenerbahçe in 2023, are the current pillars.

## Identity and playing style

Nancy's Crew were the most tactically distinctive side in MLS: a 3-4-2-1 built on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), with a goalkeeper involved in build-up, centre-backs stepping into midfield and patient circulation designed to pull the opposition apart. It was the closest thing MLS had to a European model club and it won two trophies in a season with it. Courtois has kept the three-at-the-back shape and the emphasis on possession while asking for more directness in the final third. The identity beneath all of it is the Nordecke's self-image: a small, working-class Midwestern city that refused to lose its team.

## Rivalries

The Trillium Cup with Toronto FC and the older rivalry with Chicago Fire were the traditional fixtures; the Hell Is Real derby with FC Cincinnati, named after a notorious Ohio motorway billboard, has become the most intense fixture in the league since 2019 and now regularly draws 60,000 to Ohio Stadium when it is staged there.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 the Crew are eighth in the East and in the wild-card round of the play-offs under Courtois. Rossi and Nagbe remain the senior players, Schulte is one of the best goalkeepers in the league, and the squad has been refreshed with younger signings from South America and France. Three MLS Cups since 2008 and the Leagues Cup win keep Columbus among the most consistently successful MLS clubs of the past two decades.
