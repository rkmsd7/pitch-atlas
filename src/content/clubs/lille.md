## History

Lille Olympique Sporting Club were formed in 1944 by the merger of Olympique Lillois, the first professional champions of France in 1933, and SC Fives. The new club was immediately the best in the country: champions in 1946 and 1954, and five Coupe de France wins between 1946 and 1955, with the "Dogues" (mastiffs) nickname inherited from Olympique Lillois. A long decline followed, with relegations in 1969 and the 1970s and a second-tier spell in 1997-2000.

Vahid Halilhodžić took the promoted side to third in 2001 and into the Champions League, and Claude Puel's team of Mathieu Bodmer, Kader Keïta and Peter Odemwingie finished second in 2005 and third in 2006, beating Manchester United in the Champions League in 2006. Then Rudi Garcia built the best Lille side since the 1950s: [Eden Hazard](/players/eden-hazard), Gervinho, Yohan Cabaye, Moussa Sow, Mathieu Debuchy and Rio Mavuba won the 2011 double, the league by eight points and the Coupe de France against PSG, and Hazard was Ligue 1 player of the year at 20. The club moved from the Stadium Nord to the new 50,000-seat Grand Stade (now Pierre-Mauroy) in 2012.

Gérard Lopez bought the club in 2017 and the Marcelo Bielsa experiment nearly relegated them (17th in 2018). Christophe Galtier, with Luís Campos recruiting, rebuilt: second in 2019 with Nicolas Pépé, fourth in 2020 with Victor Osimhen, and in 2020-21 the title, finishing one point ahead of PSG with Burak Yılmaz, Jonathan David, José Fonte and Mike Maignan, a championship won on the final day at Angers that remains Ligue 1's great modern upset. Lopez was forced out by the club's debts in December 2020 and Olivier Létang took over.

[Paulo Fonseca](/managers/paulo-fonseca) (2022-24) restored the club to the Champions League; [Bruno Génésio](/managers/bruno-genesio) (2024-26) beat Real Madrid 1-0 in the 2024-25 league phase and finished third in 2025-26 to qualify again, then left for Marseille when his contract expired. Lille appointed Davide Ancelotti, son of Carlo, on 1 June 2026 on a two-year deal.

## Trajectory

Lille have been in the top five in eleven of the last sixteen seasons, with two titles (2011, 2021) and a model that sells its best players every summer (Hazard to Chelsea, Pépé to Arsenal, Osimhen to Napoli, Maignan to Milan, Leny Yoro to Manchester United, Lucas Chevalier to PSG in 2025) and keeps finishing in Europe. The exceptions are the Bielsa season (17th in 2018) and 2021-22, the hangover from the title. The club returned to the Champions League league phase in 2024-25 and again in 2026-27.

## Players the club produced

Lille's academy at Luchin produced Hazard (signed at 14 from Tubize, debut at 16), Lucas Digne, Divock Origi, Benjamin Pavard, Boubakary Soumaré, Leny Yoro and Lucas Chevalier, the goalkeeper sold to PSG in 2025. The recruitment department's record is better still: Gervinho and Cabaye in the double side, Pépé, Osimhen, Maignan, Sven Botman, Amadou Onana, Renato Sanches, David (sold to Juventus in 2025 after 109 goals) and Edon Zhegrova. Olivier Giroud, who signed in 2024 at 37, has been the veteran finisher of the last two seasons.

## Identity and playing style

Garcia's Lille played a fluid 4-3-3 with Hazard and Gervinho cutting in from the wings; Galtier's champions were a compact 4-4-2 that sat in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and attacked on the counter with devastating efficiency, the best defence in the league. Fonseca added [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), Génésio a balanced mix of controlled build-up and rapid transitions through David and Zhegrova.

Davide Ancelotti's approach echoes his father's: adaptable, player-first, a solid 4-3-3 that becomes a 4-4-2 without the ball, [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through fast wingers and a compact mid-block with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) rather than an all-out press. He ran Real Madrid's [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) programme and has made Lille a threat from dead balls. His early work has focused on keeping the solidity that won third place while adding combination play in the final third.

The 2021 title deserves a closer look, because it was won against the richest squad in the world with a wage bill a sixth of PSG's. Galtier's side conceded 23 goals in 38 games, won at the Parc des Princes in April, and went to Angers on the final day one point ahead; Yılmaz, 35, scored 16 league goals, David 13, and Maignan kept 21 clean sheets before leaving for Milan. The title came three months after Lopez had been forced out by the club's lenders, which made it a triumph of the coaching staff and the dressing room over the boardroom.

## Rivalries

The **Derby du Nord** against [Lens](/clubs/lens) is the fixture: the regional capital against the mining town, 40 kilometres apart, and a rivalry sharpened by the two clubs' alternating fortunes. Lille won the most recent meeting 1-0 at the Pierre-Mauroy on 30 October 2026. Valenciennes are the other northern rival. [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) became an enemy in 2011 (the cup final) and 2021 (the title), the two occasions Lille denied them.

## Current situation

Lille are the best-placed of the chasers. As of 7 November 2026 they are second in Ligue 1 on 18 points from nine games (five wins, three draws, one defeat), two behind PSG, with the best defence in the league (six conceded). They drew 1-1 with Brest on 17 October and 1-1 at Monaco on 25 October before the 1-0 derby win over Lens on 30 October. The next match is at Rennes on 8 November, and Bayern Munich visit the Pierre-Mauroy in the Champions League on 25 November.

Davide Ancelotti, 37, is in his first season as a head coach in Europe, with Benjamin André as captain, Hákon Haraldsson and Zhegrova as the creators, Giroud and younger forwards sharing the goals and Alexsandro and Bafodé Diakité in defence. The club's model is young coaches, young players and smart sales, and the ambition is a second-place finish and a Champions League knockout place.
