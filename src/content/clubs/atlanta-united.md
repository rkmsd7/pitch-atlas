## History

Arthur Blank, the Home Depot co-founder who owns the NFL's Falcons, was awarded an MLS franchise in April 2014 on the strength of a new downtown stadium and a city that had never had a top-flight team. The club was built on an idea rarely tried in American sport: sign young South Americans, play attacking football and trust the fans to come. They came. Atlanta averaged 48,200 in 2017, broke the single-game MLS record repeatedly, and drew 73,019 for the 2018 MLS Cup final.

The first coach was Gerardo "Tata" Martino, who had managed [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) and [Argentina](/nations/argentina), and the first signings were Miguel Almirón from Lanús and Josef Martínez from Torino. Both were revelations. Martínez scored 31 goals in 2018, then a league record, and Almirón's running from midfield made the team the most watchable in the league. Atlanta beat Portland 2-0 in the 2018 final at home, the fastest expansion team to win MLS Cup since Chicago Fire in 1998.

## Trajectory

Martino left for the [Mexico](/nations/mexico) job, Almirón was sold to [Newcastle](/clubs/newcastle-united) for a league-record fee, and Frank de Boer's 2019 team still won the U.S. Open Cup and the Campeones Cup against Club América. The decline came quickly after that: Martínez's ACL injury in 2020, a bottom-of-the-table pandemic season, and a sequence of coaches (Gabriel Heinze, Gonzalo Pineda) who never recreated the first team's energy. Thiago Almada, signed from Vélez Sarsfield, won the 2022 World Cup with Argentina while an Atlanta player and was sold to Botafogo in 2024 for a club-record fee.

2024 produced the club's happiest recent memory, a first-round play-off upset of Supporters' Shield winners [Inter Miami](/clubs/inter-miami), and 2025 its unhappiest, a 14th-place finish despite the return of Almirón and the signing of Emmanuel Latte Lath for a league-record fee. The club's current squad data lists Martino as head coach again as of November 2026, a return that supporters greeted as the restoration of the original project; the 2026 season still ended 13th in the East, outside the play-offs.

## Players the club produced

The academy has produced George Bello (sold to Arminia Bielefeld), Caleb Wiley (sold to [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea)) and the goalkeeper Justin Garces, and the club's recruitment of South American talent has been its signature: Almirón, Martínez, Ezequiel Barco, Pity Martínez, Almada and Franco Ibarra all arrived young from Argentina or Venezuela. Brad Guzan, the United States goalkeeper, has been the constant since 2017.

## Identity and playing style

Martino's original Atlanta were a vertical 4-2-3-1 with [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) speed, a high defensive line and a willingness to trade blows, with Almirón running the channels and Martínez finishing. De Boer tried to impose a slower possession game and was resisted by the squad. The club has spent the years since trying to reconcile the two, and the return of Martino is an explicit vote for the first approach. The stadium, with its 360-degree video board and retractable roof, is as much a part of the identity as any tactic: it is the loudest regular crowd in American soccer.

## Rivalries

The rivalry with Orlando City, the first MLS club in the Southeast, is sharp and geographical. [Inter Miami](/clubs/inter-miami) became a rival through the 2024 play-off upset and the Messi effect on ticket demand. Charlotte FC, 250 miles up Interstate 85, is the newest fixture and has been promoted by both clubs as a derby since 2022.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Atlanta have finished 13th in the Eastern Conference with 33 points and missed the play-offs for a second straight year. Martino is listed as head coach, Almirón and Latte Lath lead the attack, and Guzan, now in his forties, is still the goalkeeper. The club's strength remains its crowd and its owner's willingness to spend; the task for 2027 is to turn the second Martino era into something closer to the first.
