## History

Kerala Blasters were founded in 2014 as one of the eight original [Indian Super League](/leagues/indian-super-league) franchises, co-owned at the start by the cricketer Sachin Tendulkar. Kerala had always been football country, the home of I. M. Vijayan and of a passionate Santosh Trophy tradition, and the Blasters became its first top-flight professional club. The response was extraordinary: crowds of 50,000 and more filled the Jawaharlal Nehru Stadium in Kochi in the early seasons, and the club quickly became the best-supported team in Indian football, with the largest social media following of any Indian club.

On the pitch, the story has been one of near-misses. David James, player-manager in 2014, took the team to the first ISL final, lost 1-0 to Atlético de Kolkata through Mohammed Rafique's injury-time header. Steve Coppell's side reached the 2016 final, played in Kochi, and lost to ATK on penalties after a 1-1 draw.

## Trajectory

After 2016 the club drifted, with finishes of sixth, ninth, seventh and tenth and a turnover of coaches that included Rene Meulensteen, David James again and Eelco Schattorie. Ivan Vukomanović, appointed in 2021, transformed the mood. His first season produced a fourth-place finish and a third final, lost on penalties to Hyderabad after a 1-1 draw in Goa in March 2022. Kerala reached the knockout round in each of the next two seasons, but the 2023 tie in Bengaluru ended with the team walking off in protest at Sunil Chhetri's quickly taken free-kick, and Vukomanović was banned and the club fined.

Mikael Stahre replaced him in 2024 and was sacked that December after a poor run; Kerala finished eighth in 2024-25. David Català took over for the delayed 2025-26 season, then left by mutual consent in March 2026, and Ashley Westwood, the Englishman who founded [Bengaluru FC](/clubs/bengaluru-fc)'s winning culture, took over on 27 March 2026. Kerala finished eighth again, nine points off the title, and Westwood stayed on for 2026-27.

## Players the club produced

The club's greatest home-grown success is Sandesh Jhingan, the Chandigarh-born centre-back who became an India captain after joining in 2014, and Kerala's own Sahal Abdul Samad, C. K. Vineeth, Rahul K. P. and Vibin Mohanan have been crowd favourites. The foreign heroes include Iain Hume, the Canadian forward fans called "Hume-ettan", the Uruguayan playmaker Adrian Luna, who arrived in 2021 and became captain, and Greek striker Dimitrios Diamantakos, Golden Boot winner in 2023-24.

## Identity and playing style

The identity is the support: the yellow-clad Manjappada fan group, the noise at Kochi and the sense that the club belongs to a whole state. Tactically the team has swung between Vukomanović's energetic 4-4-2 with Luna roaming and the more direct, set-piece-driven ideas associated with British coaches. Westwood, whose Bengaluru teams were built on organisation and [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), has made Kerala harder to beat, with three clean sheets in his first four 2026-27 games.

## Rivalries

The south Indian rivalry with [Bengaluru FC](/clubs/bengaluru-fc) is the fiercest, poisoned for good by the 2023 walk-off. Kerala also have a long-running grudge against the ATK and [Mohun Bagan](/clubs/mohun-bagan) lineage, who beat them in two finals, and an emerging regional rivalry with Chennaiyin.

## Current situation

As of 8 November 2026, Kerala Blasters are eighth in the 2026-27 ISL with five points from four games: a 2-0 win at Inter Kashi, a 4-1 defeat at NorthEast United and goalless draws against Mumbai City and Chennaiyin. Luna, now 34, still runs the team, with Korou Singh, Vibin Mohanan and new forwards Andre Green and Momo Yansane around him. The ambition never changes: a first major trophy for the most loyal support in India.
