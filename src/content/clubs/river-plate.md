## History

Club Atlético River Plate was founded on 25 May 1901 in La Boca, the same dockside barrio that produced Boca Juniors four years later, and the two clubs' shared origin is the root of the Superclásico. River moved north in 1923 and in 1938 opened the Monumental in the wealthy Núñez district, the move that earned the nickname "Los Millonarios" after a series of record transfer fees, including the purchase of Bernabé Ferreyra in 1932. The club's self-image, elegance, money and style, was set by then.

La Máquina, "the Machine", of the 1940s is the first great team of Argentine football: José Manuel Moreno, Adolfo Pedernera, Ángel Labruna, Félix Loustau and Juan Carlos Muñoz, an attacking quintet whose rotations prefigured Total Football, with a young [Alfredo Di Stéfano](/players/alfredo-di-stefano) on the fringe before his move to Colombia and then [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid). Labruna, the club's all-time top scorer, later coached River to the 1975 titles that ended an 18-year drought, with Norberto Alonso, Daniel Passarella and Ubaldo Fillol.

The 1986 Copa Libertadores, won against América de Cali under Héctor Veira with Enzo Francescoli's elegance and Antonio Alzamendi's goals, was the first, and the Intercontinental Cup against Steaua Bucharest followed in Tokyo. The 1990s added a second Libertadores under Ramón Díaz in 1996, with Francescoli back from Marseille, Ariel Ortega, Hernán Crespo and the young Marcelo Gallardo and Pablo Aimar, and a run of titles that took River past Boca in the all-time count. Javier Mascherano, Javier Saviola and Andrés D'Alessandro were the next generation.

## Trajectory

Then came the unthinkable. Years of mismanagement under the president Daniel Passarella culminated in June 2011 with River losing a relegation play-off to Belgrano de Córdoba, the first time the club had left the first division in its history. The Monumental was torn apart by rioting that night. River won promotion at the first attempt in 2012 under Matías Almeyda, won the Torneo Final in 2014 under Ramón Díaz, and then, in June 2014, appointed Marcelo Gallardo.

Gallardo's eight and a half years are the greatest in the club's history. His River won the 2014 Copa Sudamericana, the 2015 Copa Libertadores (3-0 against Tigres UANL in the second leg at the Monumental), three Recopas, four Copa Argentina titles, the 2021 league title with [Julián Álvarez](/players/julian-alvarez) and [Enzo Fernández](/players/enzo-fernandez) in the team, and, above all, the 2018 Copa Libertadores: a 3-1 extra-time win over Boca Juniors in the final second leg at the Santiago Bernabéu in Madrid on 9 December 2018, after the match had been moved from the Monumental following the attack on Boca's bus. Juan Fernando Quintero's extra-time strike and Gonzalo "Pity" Martínez's run into an empty net in the last seconds are the club's defining modern images. River lost the 2019 final to Flamengo in the last two minutes in Lima.

Gallardo left in late 2022; Martín Demichelis won the 2023 title in his first season; and Gallardo returned in August 2024. The expanded Monumental, now the largest stadium in South America at 84,567, hosted the 2024 Libertadores final (won by Botafogo). The 2025 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) in the United States brought a 3-1 win over Urawa, a goalless draw with Monterrey and a 2-0 defeat to Inter that sent the club home at the group stage; Franco Mastantuono, the 17-year-old playmaker, was sold to Real Madrid that summer. The club's current squad data lists Leonardo Ponzio, the long-serving midfielder of the Gallardo years who moved into coaching, as head coach as of November 2026.

## Players the club produced

River's academy is the most productive in Argentina. [Di Stéfano](/players/alfredo-di-stefano), Passarella, Alonso, Fillol, Gallardo, Aimar, Saviola, Mascherano, D'Alessandro, Radamel Falcao, Erik Lamela, Gonzalo Higuaín, Exequiel Palacios, [Julián Álvarez](/players/julian-alvarez) (sold to Manchester City in 2022, now at [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) and a World Cup finalist in 2026), [Enzo Fernández](/players/enzo-fernandez) (sold via Benfica to Chelsea, sent off in the 2026 World Cup final), Claudio Echeverri (to Manchester City) and Mastantuono (to Real Madrid) all came through. The great signings were Labruna's partners, Francescoli, Ortega, Crespo, Quintero, Pity Martínez and the goalkeeper Franco Armani.

## Identity and playing style

River has always been the club of the Argentine left-wing footballing tradition in the Menotti sense: possession, technique, attacking from the back. [César Luis Menotti](/managers/cesar-luis-menotti) never coached the club for long, but his school, and Gallardo's version of it, defines its football. Gallardo's River pressed high, dominated possession, played a 4-3-1-2 or 4-1-3-2 with a creative number ten (Quintero, Nacho Fernández) and overlapping full-backs, and was the most sophisticated team in South America for most of a decade; see [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and [high line](/tactics/high-line). Ponzio, his former midfield anchor, has inherited that framework with an emphasis on midfield control through Kevin Castaño and the veteran Quintero, with Miguel Borja and Sebastián Driussi as the strikers.

## Rivalries

The Superclásico against [Boca Juniors](/clubs/boca-juniors) is the fixture that organises Argentine life twice a year, the Millonarios of the north against the Xeneizes of the port, and since Madrid 2018 River has held the upper hand in the arguments. The other big-five clubs, [Independiente](/clubs/independiente), [Racing](/clubs/racing-club) and [San Lorenzo](/clubs/san-lorenzo), are the next tier, and the 2019 Lima final made [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) a continental rival.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 River are fourth in Group B of the Torneo Clausura with 26 points from 16 games, inside the qualification places for the knockout phase that closes the Argentine season, with Ponzio listed as head coach. Armani, now 40, Quintero, Borja, Driussi, Castaño and the 2022 World Cup winner Marcos Acuña are the senior players, and the academy continues to sell at record prices. The ambition is a fifth Copa Libertadores and a 39th league title; the Monumental, the biggest crowd in the Americas every fortnight, expects both.
