## History

Rayo Vallecano were founded on 29 May 1924 in Vallecas, a working-class district in the south-east of Madrid, and the red sash on the white shirt was borrowed from River Plate in 1949. The club did not reach [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) until 1977 and has never finished higher than ninth, but its identity is as strong as any in Spain: the neighbourhood, the left-wing Bukaneros stand, the stadium with one open end, and a history of fighting above its weight.

The modern era began under the ownership of José María Ruiz-Mateos's family in the 1990s, when Rayo, coached briefly by Michael Robinson's generation of players and then by Juande Ramos, reached the top flight for a long spell (1995-97, 1999-2003). Hugo Sánchez played a final season there in 1994-95. The best moment came in 2000-01, when a fair-play place in the UEFA Cup took Rayo to the quarter-finals, where they lost 4-3 on aggregate to Alavés, and finished ninth in the league. Relegation in 2003 and a second drop in 2004 took the club to Segunda B for four seasons, and the Ruiz-Mateos empire's collapse in 2011 left players unpaid during the 2010-11 promotion season under José Ramón Sandoval.

Paco Jémez's Rayo of 2012-16 were the romantic ideal: a wildly attacking 4-3-3 that finished eighth in 2012-13 and was relegated in 2016. Promotion in 2018 as Segunda champions under Míchel was followed by another relegation, another promotion through the 2021 play-offs under Andoni Iraola, and a 2021-22 season of Copa semi-finals and a win at the Bernabéu. [Iñigo Pérez](/managers/inigo-perez), Iraola's former assistant, took over in 2024 and led Rayo to eighth in 2024-25, a first European qualification in 24 years, and then to the 2026 Conference League final in Leipzig, beating Strasbourg 2-0 on aggregate in the semi-final before losing 1-0 to Crystal Palace on 27 May 2026 to a Jean-Philippe Mateta goal.

## Trajectory

Rayo have been relegated from La Liga four times since 2000 (2003, 2016, 2019 and the double drop of 2003-04) and promoted four times (2011, 2018, 2021 and the 2008 return to Segunda). Since 2021 they have been more stable than at any point in their history: 12th, 11th, 17th, eighth and eighth. The 2025-26 season produced 50 points and a European final with a budget among the three smallest in the division. Iñigo Pérez left for [Villarreal](/clubs/villarreal) in June 2026, and after a pursuit of Jagoba Arrasate fell through the club paid Eibar's release clause for Beñat San José, who was confirmed on 18 June 2026.

## Players the club produced

The Vallecas academy produced Míchel Sánchez, the midfielder who played 300 games and coached the club to promotion; Luis Cembranos; and the forward Raúl de Tomás, who left for Real Madrid's academy and returned in 2024. The signings are the folklore: Hugo Sánchez's swansong, Wilfred Agbonavbare in goal in the 1990s, Piti and Leo Baptistão under Jémez, Bebé, Roberto Trashorras, Óscar Trejo (ten seasons as captain), Isi Palazón and Álvaro García, the wide pair who have defined the Iraola and Pérez eras, and Andrei Rațiu at right-back.

## Identity and playing style

Rayo's style under Iraola and Pérez was the bravest in the league below the top three: a 4-2-3-1 that pressed man-to-man high up the pitch, used Isi and Álvaro García to attack from the wings and asked the full-backs to overlap constantly, with Óscar Valentín and Pathé Ciss covering. It was a model of [Gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) on a small budget. San José, whose Eibar were organised and direct, has kept the pressing base but is more pragmatic in the block; the Conference League run's core (Batalla in goal, Lejeune and Luiz Felipe at centre-back, Isi, García, De Frutos and De Tomás) remains in place.

## Rivalries

Rayo's rivals are the city's giants, with [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid), the other club of Madrid's south, the derby that carries the most feeling, and [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) the enemy the Bukaneros most enjoy beating (as in 2022). [Getafe](/clubs/getafe) and Leganés are the small-club derbies of the southern suburbs.

## Current situation

Rayo are 12th on 7 November 2026 with 12 points from ten games (three wins, three draws, four defeats) and a game in hand on most of the division, after a 5-2 defeat at Barcelona and a match against Celta on matchday 11. San José's first autumn has been uneven; he asked for signings after an early defeat and has had to manage the loss of the Conference League momentum. There is no European football this season, which the club's structure can absorb.

The squad is the familiar Vallecas mix: Augusto Batalla and Dani Cárdenas in goal; Rațiu, Florian Lejeune, Luiz Felipe, Marash Kumbulla and Adrià Pedrosa in defence; Unai López, Pedro Díaz, Valentín and Ciss in midfield; Isi, García, De Frutos, Sergio Camello and De Tomás in attack. Survival is assured in most seasons now; the aim is to stay in the top half and keep the neighbourhood dreaming of another European night.
