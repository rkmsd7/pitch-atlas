## History

Club Atlético Independiente was founded on 1 January 1905 by employees of a Buenos Aires department store who wanted a team independent of their employer, and it settled in Avellaneda, where it has shared the industrial suburb with Racing ever since. The red shirts, adopted in 1908 after a visit from Nottingham Forest, gave the club its nickname. In the amateur era its forwards were the best in Argentina: Raimundo Orsi, who won the 1934 World Cup with Italy, and the Paraguayan Arsenio Erico, whose 295 goals remain the Argentine league record and who inspired Di Stéfano.

The club's identity was fixed in the 1960s and 1970s. Independiente won the Copa Libertadores in 1964 and 1965, the first Argentine club to do so, and then four in a row from 1972 to 1975, a record that still stands, with Ricardo Bochini, the small, pale playmaker whose "pase bochinesco" through-ball Maradona idolised, Daniel Bertoni and Ricardo Pavoni. The 1973 Intercontinental Cup was won against Juventus in Rome; a seventh Libertadores in 1984 and a second Intercontinental, 1-0 against Liverpool in Tokyo with José Percudani's goal, confirmed the title "Rey de Copas", King of Cups, and Jorge Burruchaga, who scored the 1986 World Cup final winner, was the star.

The last league title came in Apertura 2002 under Américo Gallego, with the 17-year-old [Sergio Agüero](/players/sergio-aguero) making his debut the following year at 15, the youngest in Argentine league history, and leaving for Atlético Madrid in 2006. Then came the decline.

## Trajectory

A decade of chaotic administration, unpaid debts and the bulldozing and slow rebuilding of the stadium, renamed Libertadores de América in 2009, ended with the club's first relegation in June 2013. Independiente returned a year later and, under Ariel Holan, won the 2017 Copa Sudamericana against Flamengo at the Maracanã, its second after 2010, a victory that briefly restored the self-image.

The years since have been among the worst in the club's history: financial penalties from FIFA over unpaid transfer fees, a 2023 season in the bottom half, and in August 2025 the abandoned Copa Sudamericana tie against Universidad de Chile, when violence in the stands at the Libertadores de América led to the club's expulsion from the competition and the resignation of coach Julio Vaccari. Gustavo Quinteros followed; the club's current squad data lists the Uruguayan Jadson Viera as head coach as of November 2026, and Independiente are sixth in Group A of the Torneo Clausura 2026 with 23 points from 16 games, inside the knockout places.

## Players the club produced

Independiente's academy produced Bochini, Bertoni, Burruchaga, Gabriel Milito, [Agüero](/players/sergio-aguero), Oscar Ustari, Nicolás Tagliafico (sold to Ajax), Alan Velasco (to FC Dallas), Fabricio Bustos and, most recently, the forward Santiago Montiel and the defender Kevin Lomónaco. [Diego Forlán](/players/diego-forlan) played there from 1998 to 2002 before Manchester United, and Erico, Orsi, Pavoni and the Paraguayan striker Gabriel Ávalos were the great imports.

## Identity and playing style

The club's tradition is attacking, possession football built around a number ten, the Bochini model, and even its worst teams have tried to play that way. Holan's 2017 side pressed high and played out from the back in a 4-2-3-1; Vaccari's 2024-25 team was more pragmatic, defending in a mid-block and counter-attacking through Montiel and Ávalos, and Viera has kept that structure. See [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking). The goalkeeper Rodrigo Rey has been the most consistent player of the period.

## Rivalries

The Clásico de Avellaneda against [Racing Club](/clubs/racing-club) is the fixture, two stadiums a few hundred metres apart and a rivalry that has produced some of the most violent scenes in Argentine football. Independiente's seven Libertadores against Racing's one is the trump card; Racing's 2024 Sudamericana was a blow. Games with [Boca Juniors](/clubs/boca-juniors), [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate) and [San Lorenzo](/clubs/san-lorenzo) complete the big-five calendar.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Independiente are sixth in Group A of the Torneo Clausura, inside the knockout places with the Argentine season in its final weeks, under Viera. Rey, Lomónaco, Montiel, Felipe Loyola and Ávalos are the core, and the club remains under financial supervision while it recovers from the 2025 expulsion and sanctions. The Rey de Copas has not won the league since 2002 or the Libertadores since 1984; the ambition is modest by its own history, a return to the continental competitions with a stable budget.
