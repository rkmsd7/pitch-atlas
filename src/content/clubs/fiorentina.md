## History

Fiorentina were formed in August 1926 by the fascist aristocrat Luigi Ridolfi from the merger of two Florentine clubs, and the purple came about by accident in 1928 when the original red-and-white kit ran in the wash. The Stadio Artemio Franchi, Pier Luigi Nervi's concrete masterpiece with its helicoidal staircases and the Marathon Tower, opened in 1931 and is still, in the middle of a drawn-out renovation, the club's home. The first Scudetto came in 1955-56 under Fulvio Bernardini, with the Brazilian Julinho on the right wing and Miguel Montuori as playmaker, and that side lost the 1957 European Cup final to Real Madrid in Madrid. Fiorentina won the first Cup Winners' Cup in 1961 against Rangers and, with Kurt Hamrin, the Swedish winger who scored 151 goals, and the 18-year-old Giancarlo De Sisti's successor Giancarlo Antognoni arriving later, a second title in 1968-69 under Bruno Pesaola.

Antognoni, the elegant midfielder from Marsciano, played 412 games between 1972 and 1987 and nearly died after a collision with a goalkeeper in 1981, which is why the city regards him as something more than a footballer. The Pontello family's spending in the 1980s brought [Roberto Baggio](/players/roberto-baggio), whose sale to Juventus in 1990, days after Fiorentina lost the UEFA Cup final to the same club, provoked riots in Florence and the enmity that still defines the fixture. Vittorio Cecchi Gori, the film producer who bought the club in 1990, delivered [Gabriel Batistuta](/players/gabriel-batistuta), 168 goals in nine seasons and a statue outside the ground, Manuel Rui Costa, Francesco Toldo, two Coppa Italias in 1996 and 2001 and a Supercoppa in 1996, and then, through fraud and mismanagement, the collapse of the whole thing.

## Trajectory

In the summer of 2002 Fiorentina, relegated and €50m in debt, were refused registration for Serie B and declared bankrupt. The shoe magnate Diego Della Valle refounded the club as Florentia Viola in Serie C2, the fourth tier, where it played in front of 30,000 people and won the division with Angelo Di Livio, the only player to stay, as captain. The Italian federation then bumped the club straight to Serie B in 2003 through the controversial "Caso Catania" ruling that expanded the division; Fiorentina won promotion via the play-off in 2004 and have been in Serie A since 2004-05. The Calciopoli penalties of 2006 cost 15 points; the club still finished sixth and under Cesare Prandelli reached a Champions League knockout tie in 2010, going out to Bayern on away goals.

The Della Valles sold to the Italian-American billionaire Rocco Commisso in June 2019. Commisso built the Viola Park training centre, the largest in Italy, and under Vincenzo Italiano the club reached three straight Conference League finals, losing to West Ham in 2023, Olympiacos in 2024 and, under Raffaele Palladino, to Chelsea in 2025, a hat-trick of defeats that was somehow both the club's best European run in 60 years and a source of lasting frustration. Paolo Vanoli replaced Palladino for 2025-26 and finished 15th; his contract was not renewed, Fabio Grosso was appointed on 8 June 2026 and sacked after three months on 6 September, and Vanoli was reappointed the next day.

## Players the club produced

The Florentine academy produced Antognoni's contemporaries, the full-back Claudio Gentile, Federico Chiesa, who was sold to Juventus for €60m in 2020 to the city's disgust, and the 2006 World Cup winner Riccardo Montolivo's partner Marco Donadel. The club's great buys are its real legacy: Hamrin, Baggio, bought from Vicenza, Batistuta, from River Plate at 22, Rui Costa, Toldo, Luca Toni, who scored 31 league goals in 2005-06, Adrian Mutu, Stevan Jovetić, Dušan Vlahović, sold to Juventus for €70m in January 2022, and [Nicolás González](/players/nicolas-gonzalez). Davide Astori, the captain who died in his sleep before a match in March 2018, is commemorated at every home game. Moise Kean scored 25 goals in 2024-25 after joining from Juventus and was sold to Como in summer 2026.

## Identity and playing style

Italiano's Fiorentina was a high-pressing, high-line 4-2-3-1 that scored freely and conceded in the same measure; Palladino used a [back three](/tactics/back-three) and the counter; Vanoli's first spell was a more cautious 3-5-2 built around Kean. His second has been forced to improvise with a squad assembled by Grosso and the sporting director Daniele Pradè in a frenetic summer: Franco Mastantuono, the 19-year-old Argentine on loan from Real Madrid, has been the one bright spot with four league goals; Beto, bought from Everton, leads the line; Radu Drăgușin, on loan from Tottenham, and Marin Pongračić anchor the defence in front of David de Gea, 36 and still the club's best player; Nicolò Fagioli, Marco Brescianini and the €26m Ivorian Christ Inao Oulaï compete in midfield, and Wilfried Gnonto and Mateo Pellegrino, the €25m striker from Parma, round out an attack that has not yet clicked.

## Rivalries

[Juventus](/clubs/juventus) are the enemy, for Baggio, for 1990, for Chiesa and Vlahović, and the fixture is the one the Curva Fiesole cares about most. The Derby dell'Appennino with [Bologna](/clubs/bologna) is the regional rivalry, revived by Bologna's rise. There is hostility with [Roma](/clubs/roma), [Atalanta](/clubs/atalanta), Verona and Pisa, and a historic friendship with Torino born of the Superga tragedy, since Fiorentina were the opponents of the Grande Torino's last league match.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Fiorentina are 16th in Serie A with eight points from ten matches, two wins, two draws and six defeats, only goal difference above the relegation zone, with 23 goals conceded. The 4-0 defeat at Roma in August cost Grosso his job within weeks, and Vanoli's return has stabilised little: a 1-1 draw with Napoli and a 2-2 at Atalanta are the best results, and the club has not won two games in a row all season. The Coppa Italia has been kinder, with 4-1 and 3-0 wins over Benevento and Pisa taking the club to the round of 16.

Commisso's patience and the club's finances are the backdrop. More than €150m was committed to transfers in the summer, Kean and Albert Guðmundsson were sold or loaned out, and the Franchi renovation, which has cut capacity to around 22,000 and will not be complete before 2027, is draining goodwill. A first relegation since 2002 would be a catastrophe for a club that spent the previous three springs in European finals.
