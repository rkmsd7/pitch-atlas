## History

Sunderland were founded in 1879 by a schoolteacher, James Allan, as Sunderland and District Teachers' Association FC, and joined the Football League in 1890. The Team of All the Talents won the title in 1892, 1893 and 1895, with Johnny Campbell's goals and the Scottish professionals who filled the side, and again in 1902 and 1913, when Charlie Buchan led them to within one match of the Double. Raich Carter captained the sixth championship in 1936 and the first FA Cup in 1937. For 68 years Sunderland were never outside the top division, until relegation in 1958 amid an illegal-payments scandal.

The 1973 FA Cup final is the club's greatest day: a Second Division side under Bob Stokoe beat Don Revie's Leeds 1-0 at Wembley, Ian Porterfield scoring and Jim Montgomery's double save from Trevor Cherry and Peter Lorimer the most famous in the competition's history. Stokoe's run across the pitch at the final whistle is now a statue outside the stadium. The 1980s and 1990s were a long yo-yo: relegation to the Third Division in 1987, the 1992 FA Cup final, the move from Roker Park to the Stadium of Light in 1997, and Peter Reid's two seventh-place Premier League finishes in 2000 and 2001 with Kevin Phillips, the Golden Boot winner in 2000, and Niall Quinn.

The 2000s produced two record-low relegations, 19 points in 2003 and 15 in 2006, and two Championship titles under Mick McCarthy and Roy Keane. Ten consecutive Premier League seasons from 2007 to 2017 were spent mostly escaping relegation, with the 2014 League Cup final against Manchester City the one trip to Wembley; a sixth consecutive derby win over Newcastle in 2015 the one sustained pleasure. Relegation in 2017 under David Moyes and again in 2018 under Chris Coleman took Sunderland into League One for four seasons, a decline documented in the Netflix series Sunderland 'Til I Die. Kyril Louis-Dreyfus, who became the youngest chairman in the League at 23 in 2021, rebuilt the club with young players: promotion through the play-offs in 2022, and, under [Régis Le Bris](/managers/regis-le-bris), appointed in 2024, promotion through the play-offs again in 2025, Tommy Watson's 95th-minute winner beating Sheffield United at Wembley.

## Trajectory

The last 25 years run from seventh in 2001 to 20th with 19 points two years later, from two Championship titles to two relegations in a row, from League One to the Premier League and, in 2025-26, to seventh place and Europe. That season was the surprise of the division: Le Bris's young side, with Granit Xhaka signed from Bayer Leverkusen as captain, Enzo Le Fée moved into central midfield, Robin Roefs in goal and Chris Rigg, the teenage local midfielder, emerging, finished on 54 points and qualified for the Europa League through the cascade of places created by Crystal Palace's Conference League win and Manchester City's domestic cup double.

## Players the club produced

Sunderland's academy has produced two of England's most important players of the last 15 years: [Jordan Henderson](/players/jordan-henderson), sold to Liverpool in 2011 and the captain who lifted the 2019 Champions League, and [Jordan Pickford](/players/jordan-pickford), England's goalkeeper since 2018. Jobe Bellingham, Jude's brother, was signed from Birmingham at 17, developed at the Academy of Light and sold to Borussia Dortmund for around £30m in 2025; Chris Rigg, from Hebburn, debuted at 16 in 2023 and is the current side's most valuable asset; Dan Neil and Trai Hume, from Northern Ireland, are other recent graduates. Earlier generations produced Gary Rowell, the scorer of a derby hat-trick in 1979, Colin Todd and Dennis Tueart.

The signings include Charlie Buchan, Raich Carter, Len Shackleton, the Clown Prince of Soccer, Charlie Hurley, voted the club's player of the century, Kevin Phillips, Niall Quinn, Jermain Defoe, Darren Bent and, in the current team, Xhaka, Le Fée, Nordi Mukiele from PSG and Brian Brobbey from Ajax.

## Identity and playing style

[Régis Le Bris](/managers/regis-le-bris) built his reputation at Lorient's academy and with a 4-2-3-1 that defends compactly, presses in triggers rather than constantly, and attacks through quick combinations between the number ten and the wide players. In 2025-26 the move of Le Fée into the pivot alongside Xhaka gave the side a passing platform, with Habib Diarra and Noah Sadiki as the runners, Wilson Isidor and Brobbey as the strikers, and Romaine Mundle, Chemsdine Talbi and Malick Fofana as the wide threats. The defence of Dan Ballard, Omar Alderete, Kevin Danso and Mukiele, with Hume and Reinildo at full-back, is physical and good at [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces).

The summer brought six signings for an estimated £60m, including Mukiele, Brobbey, Thomas Meunier on a free, Jules Ahoka from Antwerp and Dayann Methalie from Toulouse, and Le Bris signed a new long-term contract.

## Rivalries

The Tyne-Wear derby with [Newcastle United](/clubs/newcastle-united) is the fiercest in England, returned to the Premier League in 2025-26 after a nine-year gap, and remains the fixture by which every Sunderland season is partly judged. [Middlesbrough](/clubs/middlesbrough) are the other North East rivalry.

## Current situation

Sunderland are bottom of the table in early November 2026, with one win, two draws and six defeats from nine: seven goals scored, 17 conceded, five points. The autumn brought a 2-0 defeat at Bournemouth on 18 October, a 0-0 draw with Leeds at home on 25 October and a 3-1 loss at Coventry on 31 October. A 5-3 defeat at Manchester City in September, in which Brobbey scored a hat-trick, summed up the attacking promise and defensive frailty. The Europa League league phase, the club's first European campaign since 1973-74, has added Thursday fixtures to a young squad's schedule.

Le Bris's second Premier League season is the first in which he has had to manage expectation rather than relief. The ambition after seventh place was to consolidate in the top half and enjoy Europe; the immediate task is to climb out of the relegation zone before the derby and the winter.
