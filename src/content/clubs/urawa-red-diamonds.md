## History

Urawa Red Diamonds are the football club of Mitsubishi Motors, founded in 1950 as Mitsubishi Heavy Industries' team and four-time champions of the amateur Japan Soccer League in the 1960s, 1970s and 1980s, with the great Japanese winger Kunishige Kamamoto's rival Yasuyuki Kuwahara and the defender Hiroshi Ochiai... the 1978 treble side was the best in the amateur era. When the J.League began in 1993 the club moved to Urawa, a football-mad district of Saitama north of Tokyo, and took the diamond from the Mitsubishi logo. The early years were poor, the club finishing bottom in 1993 and 1994 and being relegated in 1999, but the support was already the largest and most vocal in Japan.

Promotion in 2000 coincided with the opening of Saitama Stadium 2002, built for the World Cup and the largest football-specific ground in the country, and with the emergence of Shinji Ono, the most gifted Japanese midfielder of his generation, who had left for Feyenoord in 2001 and returned in 2006. Under the German coach Guido Buchwald, with the Brazilians Robson Ponte and Washington, the captain Keita Suzuki and Yuki Abe, Urawa won the Emperor's Cup in 2005, the J.League in 2006, and in 2007, under Holger Osieck, the AFC Champions League, beating Sepahan in the final and finishing third at the [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) after a 1-0 semi-final defeat to AC Milan.

## Trajectory

The 2006 title remains the only one. Mihailo Petrović's 2012-17 teams, playing an intricate 3-4-2-1 possession game, finished second in 2014 and topped the aggregate table in 2016 only to lose the play-off final to Kashima, a result that still rankles. The consolation was a second AFC Champions League in 2017, won against Al Hilal, and a third in 2022 under Ricardo Rodríguez, beating Al Hilal again in front of 53,000 at Saitama in May 2023 (the final was played across two years). The 2019 final was lost to Al Hilal.

The 2022 title qualified Urawa for the expanded 2025 Club World Cup in the United States, where they lost all three group games, 3-1 to River Plate, 2-1 to Inter and 4-0 to Monterrey, after a 2024 season in which Per-Mathias Høgmo was sacked and Maciej Skorża returned. The club's current squad data lists the Korean coach Cho Kwi-jae as head coach as of November 2026, and Urawa are ninth in the 2026-27 J1 League with 21 points from 13 games.

## Players the club produced

The Urawa academy produced Yuki Abe's successors, the midfielder Tomoya Ugajin, the forward Shinzo Koroki's partners, Yoshiaki Takagi, the goalkeeper Zion Suzuki (sold to Sint-Truiden and then Parma in 2024) and the winger Takahiro Sekine. Ono was a Shimizu-born academy product who became the club's symbol; Keita Suzuki played his whole career there; Makino, Abe, Nishikawa, Ponte, Washington, Emerson, Márcio Richardes and the striker Tadanari Lee were the great signings.

## Identity and playing style

Urawa's identity is the crowd: the north stand of Saitama Stadium is the loudest in Asian football and the club's self-image is of a European-style mass club in a league of corporate teams. Petrović's "Mischa football" gave the club its most distinctive style, a 3-4-2-1 that became 4-1-5 in possession with the wing-backs pushed high, an early J.League version of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play). Rodríguez and Skorża were more pragmatic, winning Asia with a compact 4-2-3-1 and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) transitions. Cho Kwi-jae has introduced a higher press and a more vertical game built around the Swedish midfielder Samuel Gustafson and the Brazilian striker Thiago Santana, with the 40-year-old Shusaku Nishikawa still in goal.

## Rivalries

The games with [Kashima Antlers](/clubs/kashima-antlers) are the J.League's biggest, the two most-decorated clubs with the 2016 play-off final as a scar. The Saitama derby with Omiya Ardija (now RB Omiya Ardija under Red Bull ownership) is the local fixture, and games with Gamba Osaka and [Kawasaki Frontale](/clubs/kawasaki-frontale) have been the league's marquee matches in different eras.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Urawa are ninth in the 2026-27 J1 League, the first autumn-spring season, under Cho Kwi-jae, with Nishikawa, Gustafson, Thiago Santana, Takuro Kaneko and Yoichi Naganuma as the core. The 2025 Club World Cup exposed the gap between the J.League and the world's elite, and the club's ambition for the new calendar is a second domestic title; the crowd, averaging more than 35,000, has waited since 2006.
