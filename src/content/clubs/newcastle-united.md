## History

Newcastle United were formed in 1892 by the merger of Newcastle East End and Newcastle West End and took over the latter's ground at St James' Park. The Edwardian side, with Colin Veitch and Bill McCracken, were the best in England: champions in 1905, 1907 and 1909, FA Cup winners in 1910, and finalists five times in seven years. Hughie Gallacher captained the fourth title in 1927. The 1950s brought three FA Cups in five years with Jackie Milburn, Wor Jackie, the Ashington miner who scored twice in the 1951 final and whose statue stands outside the ground.

The Fairs Cup of 1969, won against Újpest Dózsa, is the club's only European trophy. Malcolm Macdonald scored the goals of the 1970s, [Kevin Keegan](/players/kevin-keegan) played the last two years of his career on Tyneside and won promotion in 1984 alongside the teenage Peter Beardsley and Chris Waddle, and Paul Gascoigne emerged in the mid-1980s before leaving for Tottenham. Then came Keegan the manager, appointed in February 1992 with the club near the bottom of the Second Division, backed by Sir John Hall's money: promotion as champions in 1993, third in the Premier League in 1994, and in 1995-96 the Entertainers, with Les Ferdinand, David Ginola, Beardsley and Rob Lee, led the league by 12 points in January and lost it to Manchester United. The 4-3 at Anfield and Keegan's "I would love it" speech are the folklore. [Alan Shearer](/players/alan-shearer) arrived for a world-record £15m that summer and stayed ten years, scoring 206 goals, the club record.

Sir Bobby Robson's side finished third in 2003 and fourth in 2002, and the Champions League nights against Juventus and Barcelona were the high point of the 2000s. Mike Ashley bought the club in 2007 and ran it for 14 years as a shop window for his sportswear business: relegation in 2009 and 2016, two Championship titles under Chris Hughton and Rafael Benítez, and a stadium that for years was called the Sports Direct Arena. The Saudi Public Investment Fund's takeover in October 2021 ended that. Eddie Howe, appointed a month later, took Newcastle from 19th to 11th, then fourth and the Champions League in 2023, and on 16 March 2025 won the Carabao Cup against Liverpool, the first domestic trophy since 1955, with goals from Dan Burn and [Alexander Isak](/players/alexander-isak). Howe stepped down on 31 July 2026, and [Matthias Jaissle](/managers/matthias-jaissle), who had won two AFC Champions Leagues with Al-Ahli, was appointed on 5 August.

## Trajectory

The shape of the last 25 years is a Robson peak, an Ashley trough and a PIF revival. Fourth, third and fifth between 2002 and 2004 were followed by a slow decline to relegation in 2009. Hughton's Championship title in 2010 brought the club straight back; Alan Pardew's fifth place in 2012 was an outlier in a decade otherwise spent between tenth and 16th, with a second relegation in 2016 and Benítez's promotion in 2017.

Howe's four full seasons were the best since Robson: 11th, fourth, seventh and fifth, with two League Cup finals and the 2025 trophy. The 2025-26 season was a comedown. Isak's refusal to play and his sale to Liverpool for £125m in September 2025, injuries to Tonali and Bruno Guimarães, and a Champions League campaign that ended in the league phase left Newcastle 12th on 49 points, the lowest finish of the PIF era. Howe left the following summer; Tonali was sold to Tottenham for around £100m and Bruno Guimarães to Arsenal, and the club's net transfer receipts for the window were about £130m.

## Players the club produced

Newcastle's academy has produced a steady line of first-team players. Paul Gascoigne, Peter Beardsley (who left before returning as a signing), Steve Watson, Andy Carroll, sold to Liverpool for £35m in 2011, Sean and Matty Longstaff, Elliot Anderson, sold to Nottingham Forest in 2024 and then to Manchester City for £116m in 2026, and Lewis Miley, who debuted at 17 in 2023 and is now a first-team regular. Jackie Milburn, from Ashington, is the greatest of them all.

The signings include Hughie Gallacher, Malcolm Macdonald, [Kevin Keegan](/players/kevin-keegan), Beardsley, [Alan Shearer](/players/alan-shearer), Shay Given, Nolberto Solano, Yohan Cabaye, Bruno Guimarães, signed from Lyon in January 2022 as the first statement of the new ownership, Sandro Tonali, Isak, and Kieran Trippier, the first signing of the Howe era and its captain.

## Identity and playing style

[Matthias Jaissle](/managers/matthias-jaissle) is a product of the Red Bull system, having coached Liefering and Red Bull Salzburg, where he won two Austrian doubles before moving to Al-Ahli in 2023. His football is [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) in the Salzburg tradition: a 4-2-2-2 or 4-4-2 diamond with narrow attacking midfielders, vertical passing, and a press triggered the moment the ball goes backwards. It is not far from Howe's Newcastle in intensity but more structured and less reliant on individual duels.

The 2026-27 squad lost Tonali and Guimarães, and the search for an Isak replacement has lingered: Yoane Wissa, signed from Brentford in 2025, and William Osula share the centre-forward role, with Anthony Elanga, Harvey Barnes and Jacob Murphy wide and Bazoumana Touré, signed from Hoffenheim for £43m, the big summer attacking signing. Joelinton, Joe Willock, Jacob Ramsey, Nico González and Miley make up the midfield; Sven Botman, Malick Thiaw, Dan Burn and Fabian Schär the defence, with Tino Livramento and Lewis Hall at full-back and Nick Pope in goal.

## Rivalries

The Tyne-Wear derby with [Sunderland](/clubs/sunderland), 12 miles away, is the fiercest regional rivalry in England and returned to the Premier League in 2025-26 after nine years. [Middlesbrough](/clubs/middlesbrough) are the Tees-Tyne rivalry. Among the big clubs, [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) through the 1996 title race and 1999 FA Cup final, and [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) through the 4-3s of 1996 and 1997 and the 2025 League Cup final, are the ones with history.

## Current situation

Newcastle are 13th in early November 2026 with three wins, two draws and four defeats from nine: 12 goals scored, 15 conceded, 11 points. The autumn has been poor: a 4-1 defeat at Coventry on 12 October, a 1-0 home loss to Aston Villa on 17 October, a 2-0 win at Crystal Palace on 25 October, then a 1-0 home defeat to Everton on 2 November. Jaissle's pressing is visible; the end product, without Isak, Tonali or Guimarães, is not.

The ambition is a return to Europe and a settled identity after a summer of upheaval. The squad's spine of Pope, Botman, Joelinton and Livramento is strong, the owners' resources are enormous but constrained by profit and sustainability rules, and the January window is expected to bring a striker. The sale of Guimarães in particular was felt as the end of the Howe era.
