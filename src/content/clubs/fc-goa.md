## History

FC Goa were founded in 2014 as one of the eight original [Indian Super League](/leagues/indian-super-league) franchises, with the Indian cricket captain Virat Kohli among the early co-owners. Goa, with its Portuguese heritage, Catholic parish leagues and clubs such as Dempo, Salgaocar and Churchill Brothers, was the natural home of Indian football, and the new club tapped into that culture immediately at Fatorda.

The first head coach was the Brazilian legend [Zico](/players/zico), whose sides played open, technical football. Goa reached the semi-finals in 2014 and in 2015 topped the table and hosted the final at Fatorda, losing 3-2 to Chennaiyin after leading late on, a defeat followed by ugly scenes and a dispute between the two clubs' owners. A last-place finish in 2016 ended Zico's time.

## Trajectory

The Spaniard Sergio Lobera, appointed in 2017, built the best attacking team the ISL has seen. Ferran Corominas, "Coro", won the Golden Boot in 2017-18 and 2018-19; Hugo Boumous and Ahmed Jahouh ran midfield; Goa won the 2019 Super Cup and reached the 2019 ISL final, lost 1-0 to [Bengaluru FC](/clubs/bengaluru-fc) in extra time. In 2019-20 they won the League Winners' Shield, which made them the first Indian club to qualify for the group stage of the AFC Champions League, although Lobera was sacked before the play-offs and later joined [Mumbai City](/clubs/mumbai-city).

Juan Ferrando took the team to the 2021 ACL group stage and won the 2021 Durand Cup, Goa's first national cup. After two lean seasons, Manolo Márquez, who combined the job with the India national team for a time, made the club a contender again: third in 2023-24, second in 2024-25 and Super Cup winners in April 2025 and again in the 2025-26 edition. In the shortened 2025-26 league Goa finished seventh, six points behind the champions. Márquez left for Mumbai City in July 2026 and on 24 July the club promoted his assistant, the former India defender Gouramangi Singh, who had been on the staff since 2022.

## Players the club produced

Goa's youth system and the state's football tradition have produced Brandon Fernandes, Mandar Rao Desai, Lenny Rodrigues, Seriton Fernandes, and Brison Fernandes among others, and the club has given a stage to foreign creators like Coro, Boumous, Edu Bedia, Jorge Ortiz and Iker Guarrotxena. India captain Sandesh Jhingan and winger Udanta Singh are the senior Indian figures in the current squad.

## Identity and playing style

Goa's identity, from Zico to Lobera to Márquez, has been possession football: short passing, patient build-up and a high line, in the spirit of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka). Gouramangi has promised continuity, saying he wants his team to control games more than their opponents, while adding a harder defensive edge around Jhingan.

## Rivalries

The rivalry with [Mumbai City](/clubs/mumbai-city) is the fiercest in Goa's calendar, and Lobera's move from Fatorda to Mumbai in 2020 made it personal; Márquez's identical move in 2026 added another chapter. Goa's two finals and several semi-finals against [Bengaluru FC](/clubs/bengaluru-fc) have produced a second rivalry, while the old Goan clubs such as Churchill Brothers, back in the ISL in 2026-27, provide a local derby flavour.

## Current situation

As of 8 November 2026, FC Goa are seventh in the 2026-27 ISL with five points from four games, after a 1-1 draw with Sporting Club Delhi, a 2-1 win at Inter Kashi and a 2-1 home defeat to Bengaluru on 6 November. The season began early for Goa, with an AFC Champions League Two preliminary round tie against Arkadag of Turkmenistan in August. The challenge for Gouramangi Singh, the club's first full-time Indian head coach, is to get Goa back into the title race and prove a home-grown coach can win in the ISL.
