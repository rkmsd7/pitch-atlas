## History

Al Ittihad, "the Union", was founded in Jeddah in 1927, which makes it the oldest surviving club in Saudi Arabia and earns it the nickname "Al-Ameed", the dean. The club's identity is Jeddah's: the Red Sea port, more cosmopolitan and less formal than Riyadh, with a fan base that regards the capital's clubs as the establishment. The first league title came in 1982, and the golden era arrived in the late 1990s and 2000s: championships in 1997, 1999, 2000, 2001, 2003, 2007 and 2009, the 1999 Asian Cup Winners' Cup, and back-to-back AFC Champions League titles in 2004 and 2005, the first beating Seongnam of South Korea 6-3 on aggregate after a 3-1 first-leg defeat, the second beating Al Ain. Mohammed Noor, the playmaker, Hamzah Idris, Osama Al-Muwallad and the Moroccan Hicham Aboucherouane were the core.

The club finished fourth at the 2005 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) in Japan, losing to São Paulo in the semi-finals, and lost the 2009 AFC Champions League final to Pohang. Then came a long decline: no title between 2009 and 2023, flirtations with relegation in 2018-19, and financial penalties from FIFA.

## Trajectory

Nuno Espírito Santo's 2022-23 side ended the 14-year wait, winning the league with the Brazilian striker Romarinho and Abderrazak Hamdallah, and the Public Investment Fund's 2023 takeover brought [Karim Benzema](/players/karim-benzema), the reigning Ballon d'Or winner, from Real Madrid, along with N'Golo Kanté from Chelsea and Fabinho from Liverpool. The first PIF season was chaotic: Nuno was sacked, the club hosted the 2023 Club World Cup in Jeddah and lost its opening match to Al Ahly, and it finished fifth.

Laurent Blanc's 2024-25 team got it right: a league and King Cup double, with Benzema scoring 21 league goals, Moussa Diaby arriving from Aston Villa and Houssem Aouar from Roma. Blanc was sacked in September 2025 after a poor start to the following season and replaced by Sérgio Conceição; the club's current squad data lists Jens Wissing, the German coach, as head coach as of November 2026. Al Ittihad are fourth in the 2026-27 Saudi Pro League with 24 points from 11 games.

## Players the club produced

Al Ittihad's academy produced Noor, Idris, Al-Muwallad, Mohammed Al-Khilaiwi, Ahmed Jamil Madani and Manaf Abushgeer, the heart of the Saudi national team in the 2000s, and more recently the midfielder Abdulrahman Al-Aboud and the goalkeeper Marcelo Grohe's Saudi understudies. The imports are the modern story: Benzema, Kanté, Fabinho, Diaby, Aouar, Steven Bergwijn from Ajax and the Brazilian goalkeeper Grohe.

## Identity and playing style

Blanc's champions played a controlled 4-3-3 with Fabinho and Kanté screening, Benzema dropping into the number ten space he perfected at Real Madrid, and Diaby and Bergwijn attacking the channels; it was the most balanced team in the league that season. Conceição and then Wissing have made the side more vertical and more aggressive in the press, using Aouar as a third midfielder and asking the full-backs to push high. See [positional play](/tactics/positional-play). The King Abdullah Sports City, "the Shining Jewel", holds 62,000 and its atmosphere is the best in the kingdom.

## Rivalries

The Jeddah derby against [Al Ahli](/clubs/al-ahli), the other half of the city, is Saudi Arabia's most passionate local rivalry and has been intensified by both clubs' PIF-funded squads. [Al Hilal](/clubs/al-hilal), the Riyadh establishment, is the national rival, and the Benzema-versus-Ronaldo fixtures with [Al Nassr](/clubs/al-nassr) have become the league's biggest television draw.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Al Ittihad are fourth in the Saudi Pro League with 24 points from 11 games under Wissing, with Benzema, now 38 and still scoring, Kanté, Fabinho, Diaby, Bergwijn and Aouar as the core. The 2024-25 double put the club back among the country's elite and the ambition is a third AFC Champions League, where the club has not won since 2005; the squad is ageing, and the next window will decide whether the PIF project in Jeddah is renewed or wound down.
