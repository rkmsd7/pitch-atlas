## History

FC Augsburg were founded in 1907 and, for most of their history, were a regional club in Swabian Bavaria, 60 kilometres from Munich, whose claim to fame was producing Helmut Haller, the 1966 World Cup finalist, and Bernd Schuster, the great midfielder of Barcelona and Real Madrid. The club spent the 1970s in the 2. Bundesliga, drawing big crowds to the Rosenaustadion when Haller returned from Italy, and then declined into the amateur leagues, playing in the Bayernliga (fourth tier) as recently as 2002.

The modern club was built by Walther Seinsch, a textile businessman who took over in 2000 and financed a decade of promotion: Bayernliga champions in 2002, Regionalliga Süd champions in 2006, and 2. Bundesliga football from 2006. Jos Luhukay's side lost the 2010 promotion play-off to Nürnberg, then went up as runners-up in 2011. The WWK Arena, opened in 2009, gave the club a home fit for the top flight.

In the Bundesliga Augsburg have never been relegated. Markus Weinzierl's team finished fifth in 2014-15, qualified for the Europa League and drew with Liverpool at Anfield in the round of 32, the club's European high point. Since then they have finished between 11th and 15th every season, with Manuel Baum, Martin Schmidt, Heiko Herrlich, Markus Weinzierl again, Enrico Maaßen, Jess Thorup and Sandro Wagner in charge, surviving through the consistency of their structure rather than the quality of any one squad.

## Trajectory

Three promotions in nine years took Augsburg from the fourth tier to the Bundesliga, and fifteen consecutive seasons in the top flight have made them one of only a handful of clubs never to go down since arriving. The ceiling has been the 2015 fifth place; the floor, several 15th-place finishes without ever falling into the bottom three. The 2025-26 season continued the pattern, and Manuel Baum, who had coached the club from 2016 to 2019 and later Schalke and Germany's youth teams, returned for 2026-27.

## Players the club produced

Haller and Schuster are the two world-class players the Augsburg youth system has produced, decades apart. The modern club is known for finding and selling: Kevin Vogt, Dominik Kohr, Philipp Max, Felix Uduokhai, Ruben Vargas and Ermedin Demirović were all sold on for profit. Daniel Baier played more than 300 games in midfield, Paul Verhaegh captained the European season, Tobias Werner was the promotion-era winger, Alfred Finnbogason the goalscorer of the 2015-18 side and Jeffrey Gouweleeuw the long-serving centre-back. André Hahn had two spells on the wing.

## Identity and playing style

Augsburg's identity is survival through organisation. Weinzierl's best side played a compact 4-2-3-1 and attacked on the counter; most coaches since have used a [back three](/tactics/back-three) and a [low block](/tactics/low-block), with [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) as the main sources of goals. Manuel Baum, in his second spell, has tried to add more possession and a higher press, and the 2026-27 team has scored freely (18 goals in eight games) while conceding too easily.

## Rivalries

[Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) are the regional superpower, and Augsburg's rare wins against them (2015, 2019, and a 1-0 in 2024) are celebrated as cup finals. 1860 Munich were the traditional Bavarian rival before their decline, Nürnberg and Ingolstadt the other in-state matches, and the games against Stuttgart carry the Swabia-Bavaria undertone.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Augsburg are 11th in the Bundesliga on nine points from eight games (two wins, three draws, three defeats), with a plus-three goal difference. They lost a 4-3 thriller at Elversberg on 17 October, drew 1-1 at home to Union Berlin on 24 October and lost 2-1 at home to Freiburg on 31 October. The next match is at RB Leipzig on 7 November.

Baum's squad is built around Gouweleeuw, Finn Dahmen in goal, Phillip Tietz and Alexis Claude-Maurice in attack and Elvis Rexhbecaj in midfield. The aim, as every year, is a safe mid-table finish, and the early signs are of a more open team than Augsburg usually field.
