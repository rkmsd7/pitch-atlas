## History

Olympique de Marseille were founded on 31 August 1899 by René Dufaure de Montmirail, a sports patron who gave the club its motto, "Droit au but" (straight to goal), and its Olympic-inspired name. They won the Coupe de France in 1924, 1926 and 1927 and a first league title in 1937, and moved into the Stade Vélodrome, built for the 1938 World Cup, the following year. Gunnar Andersson's 169 goals in the 1950s and Josip Skoblar's 44 in 1970-71 (still the Ligue 1 single-season record) gave the club its early heroes, and titles followed in 1971 and 1972.

The modern club was made by Bernard Tapie. The businessman took over in 1986, signed Jean-Pierre Papin, Chris Waddle, Enzo Francescoli, Abedi Pelé, Basile Boli, Marcel Desailly, Didier Deschamps and Fabien Barthez, and won four straight titles from 1989 to 1992. The 1991 European Cup final was lost on penalties to Red Star Belgrade in Bari; two years later, on 26 May 1993 in Munich, Boli's header beat Milan 1-0 and Marseille became, and remain, the only French winners of the competition. Within weeks the VA-OM match-fixing affair broke: Tapie's club had bribed Valenciennes players before a league match. OM were stripped of the 1993 league title, barred from defending the European Cup and relegated to Division 2 in 1994.

Robert Louis-Dreyfus bought the club in 1996 and funded the return: a UEFA Cup final in 1999 (lost to Parma), [Didier Drogba](/players/didier-drogba)'s single, sensational season in 2003-04 that ended with another UEFA Cup final defeat to Valencia, and the Deschamps era (2009-12) that brought the 2010 title, three straight League Cups and the 2010 Trophée des Champions. Marcelo Bielsa's 2014-15 side led the league at Christmas and lost its nerve; Rudi Garcia's reached the 2018 Europa League final, lost 3-0 to Atlético in Lyon. Frank McCourt bought the club in 2016.

[Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi) arrived in 2024 and his elaborate possession football, with Mason Greenwood and Adrien Rabiot, took OM to second in 2024-25 and back into the Champions League. He left for Tottenham, and the 2025-26 season went through three coaches, Habib Beye lasting four months, before the club turned to [Bruno Génésio](/managers/bruno-genesio) on a contract to June 2028.

## Trajectory

Marseille have been a top-five club in France for most of this century without winning more than one title. Second-place finishes in 2007, 2009, 2011, 2013, 2020, 2022, 2025 and 2026 show the ceiling: since Qatar bought [PSG](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) in 2011 the title has been out of reach, and OM have been the best of the rest more often than anyone. The lows were 15th in 2000-01, when relegation was a real possibility, 13th in 2015-16 after Bielsa's walkout, and 8th in 2023-24, a season of three coaches.

In Europe OM have reached three finals since 1993 (1999, 2004, 2018) and lost all three. The Champions League record is poorer: they went through the 2020-21 group stage with one point and have not reached a knockout tie since the 2012 quarter-final.

The Vélodrome itself is a character in the story. Built for the 1938 World Cup, rebuilt for 1998 with its open ends and famous wind, and roofed and expanded to 67,000 for Euro 2016, it is the largest club stadium in France and the home of the two great ultra groups, the South Winners and the Commando Ultra 84, whose tifos and the "Aux Armes" anthem give OM matches an intensity no other French ground matches. The club's attendance has averaged above 60,000 for most of the 2020s, second-tier crowds included, and the pressure that generates has cost a long line of coaches their jobs: 15 permanent appointments since 2010, including Marcelo Bielsa, Rudi Garcia, André Villas-Boas, Jorge Sampaoli, Igor Tudor, Gennaro Gattuso and De Zerbi.

Ownership has been equally restless. After Louis-Dreyfus's death in 2009 his widow Margarita ran the club through the Deschamps era and the Bielsa season, sold to Frank McCourt in 2016, and McCourt's "OM Champions Project" promised a title within five years that never came. Pablo Longoria, president since 2021, has been the constant through the De Zerbi and Génésio appointments, and persistent rumours of a Saudi takeover have hung over the club for most of the decade.

## Players the club produced

Marseille's academy at La Commanderie has produced Samir Nasri, Mathieu Flamini, Boubacar Kamara, Maxime López and, most recently, a stream of players sold to the Premier League. The club's better record is in making stars: Papin won the 1991 Ballon d'Or in Marseille; [Didier Drogba](/players/didier-drogba) arrived from Guingamp in 2003 and left for Chelsea a year later as one of the best strikers in Europe; [Franck Ribéry](/players/franck-ribery) spent two seasons (2005-07) at the Vélodrome before Bayern; Dimitri Payet's two spells made him a club icon; Steve Mandanda played 613 games in goal.

The 1990s side produced three of France's 1998 World Cup winners in Barthez, Desailly and Deschamps. The current generation includes Leonardo Balerdi as captain, Mason Greenwood as the main goalscorer since 2024, Amine Gouiri, signed from Rennes in 2025, and Pierre-Emile Højbjerg in midfield.

## Identity and playing style

OM's identity is noise. The Vélodrome, rebuilt for Euro 2016 to hold 67,000, is the most intense stadium in France, and its demand is for commitment first and football second. De Zerbi gave the club two seasons of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), with elaborate goalkeeper-led build-up, and the crowd admired it without quite loving it.

[Bruno Génésio](/managers/bruno-genesio), appointed in summer 2026, has moved the team toward his own instincts: a 4-3-3 or 4-2-3-1, a mid-block with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the touchline rather than a full press, quick [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through fast wide forwards, and careful work on [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces). It is more direct and more suited to a crowd that wants to see the ball go forward. His record of never having a bad season in France was the reason the board chose him.

The best OM sides of the last fifteen years each had a clear signature. Deschamps's champions of 2010 were a counter-attacking 4-3-3 with Mamadou Niang and Brandão up front and Lucho González pulling the strings, the tightest defence in the league with Mandanda behind it. Bielsa's 2014-15 team pressed man-to-man all over the pitch, led the table for most of the autumn and collapsed in spring when the squad ran out of legs; André Ayew and Dimitri Payet were its stars. Garcia's 2018 Europa League finalists were a Payet-led counter-attacking side; Tudor's 2022-23 team, with Alexis Sánchez, played a back three and finished third; De Zerbi's reached second with Greenwood's goals and Rabiot's running. None won the title, and the club's European trophy drought, now past three decades, remains the fact that every coach is reminded of.

## Rivalries

**Le Classique** against [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) is the great French rivalry, sharpened in the Tapie-Canal+ years and sustained since by the gulf in resources. The **Olympico** with [Lyon](/clubs/lyon) was the title rivalry of the 2000s and still produces the most volatile atmospheres in the league. The Derby de la Méditerranée against [Nice](/clubs/nice) and the Côte d'Azur rivalry with [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) complete the regional set, and OM supporters have a long-standing grievance against the Paris-based media and federation that gives every big match an edge.

## Current situation

Génésio began work on 6 July 2026 and spent the pre-season simplifying the squad he inherited from De Zerbi. As of 7 November 2026 Marseille are sixth in Ligue 1 on 15 points from nine games, with five wins and four defeats and no draws, the most volatile record in the league: a 5-2 win at Angers on 18 October, a 4-3 home win over Le Havre on 25 October and a 2-0 win over Toulouse on 1 November. They visit [Lens](/clubs/lens) on 8 November. The gap to leaders PSG is five points, and second to fourth, held by Lille, Lyon and Paris FC, are within reach.

The stated aim is second place and a credible Champions League campaign, with Lyon under Paulo Fonseca (reported in early November to be in talks with Sporting), Lille under Davide Ancelotti and Monaco under Filipe Luís as the rivals for the podium. Greenwood, Gouiri and Abdelli carry the attack, Balerdi the defence. The early reports are of a more settled, less spectacular Marseille, which is exactly what the club asked for; the test, as always at the Vélodrome, is whether steadiness satisfies a crowd that wants more.
