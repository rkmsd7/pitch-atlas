## History

Monza were founded in 1912 in the Brianza, the industrial belt north of Milan, and spent most of a century as the great nearly club of Italian football: 40 seasons in Serie B, more than any club never to reach Serie A, four Coppa Italia Serie C wins, two Anglo-Italian Cups in 1976 and 1977, and a 1979 Serie B promotion play-off lost to Pescara. The Stadio Brianteo, opened in 1988, was built for a top flight that did not arrive for 34 years. The club produced [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti)'s teammate Daniele Massaro, the Italy striker Pietro Anastasi finished his career there, and Monza were the first club of the midfielder Gianfranco Matteoli and the Milan goalkeeper Francesco Antonioli.

The decline began in 2001, with relegation from Serie B in 2002, bankruptcy in 2004 and a second bankruptcy in 2015 that sent the club to Serie D. The rescue came from an unexpected direction. In September 2018 Silvio Berlusconi, the former Milan owner and prime minister, bought the club with Adriano Galliani, his long-time lieutenant at Milan and a Monza native who had once been the club's president. Their plan, stated openly, was to take Monza to Serie A and then to Europe.

## Trajectory

The rise was fast. Serie C was won in 2019-20 under Cristian Brocchi, the pandemic-shortened season ending with Monza promoted; the 2020-21 Serie B season ended in a play-off semi-final defeat to Cittadella despite Mario Balotelli and Kevin-Prince Boateng in the squad; and in 2021-22 Giovanni Stroppa's side finished fourth and beat Pisa in the play-off final, 6-4 on aggregate after extra time in Pisa on 29 May 2022, to reach Serie A for the first time in the club's 110 years. [Raffaele Palladino](/managers/raffaele-palladino), promoted from the Primavera in September 2022 after Stroppa's sacking, took a side that had lost its first five games to 11th place, beating Juventus twice, and finished 12th the next year. Berlusconi died in June 2023.

The 2024-25 season was the collapse: Alessandro Nesta was sacked, rehired and sacked again, Salvatore Bocchetti could not save them, and Monza finished bottom with 18 points. The Berlusconi family sold the club in 2025 to Beckett Layne Ventures, an American investment group, with Lauren Crampsie as president and Galliani departing after seven years. Paolo Bianco won promotion back at the first attempt via the play-offs, Monza finishing third and beating Catanzaro on the regular-season tiebreak after a 2-2 aggregate final in June 2026, and then left; [Ivan Jurić](/managers/ivan-juric), the Croatian who had coached Torino and briefly Roma and Southampton, was appointed on 29 June.

## Players the club produced

The Berlusconi-era Monza was built on Milan connections and shrewd buys: Matteo Pessina, born in Monza, who came back from Atalanta to captain the club and is still there; Michele Di Gregorio, the goalkeeper sold to Juventus for €18m in 2024; Andrea Colpani, the "Flaco", who went to Fiorentina and returned; Dany Mota and Patrick Ciurria, the forwards of the promotion team; Christian Gytkjær, the Danish striker whose goals won the 2022 play-offs; Andrea Carboni and Warren Bondo. The academy produced Davide Frattesi's predecessor at Monza, the midfielder Samuele Vignato, and Lorenzo Colombo spent a season on loan.

## Identity and playing style

Palladino's Monza was a surprisingly elegant possession side built on a [back three](/tactics/back-three) and Pessina's passing. Jurić plays the 3-4-2-1 man-marking, high-pressing game he learned under Gasperini at Genoa and took to Verona and Torino, a demanding style for a promoted squad. The 2026-27 side mixes experienced Serie A names with loans: Pessina, Colpani and Michael Folorunsho, on loan from Napoli, in midfield; Patrick Cutrone, signed from Como, Dany Mota, Cyril Ngonge, Keita Baldé and the 21-year-old Uruguayan Gustavo Varela, bought from Benfica B for €2.5m and already the league's rising star of October with eight goals, in attack. Noel Törnqvist is the goalkeeper. The defence, with Andrea Carboni, Jan Ziółkowski and Saba Goglichidze, has conceded 24 goals in ten games.

## Rivalries

The derby with [Como](/clubs/como), the two provincial Lombard clubs that reached Serie A within two years of each other with foreign money, is the modern rivalry, and Como's rise to the Champions League has sharpened it. Pro Sesto and Seregno are the historic local enemies from the lower leagues. The games against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) have the emotional charge of the Berlusconi-Galliani connection, and the 2023 win over Milan was the moment the old owner enjoyed most.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Monza are 18th in Serie A with seven points from ten matches, two wins, a draw and seven defeats, in the relegation zone and a point behind Sassuolo. The wins, 2-1 over Sassuolo and 4-0 over Cagliari, and Varela's goals are the positives; 4-1 defeats at Inter and at home to Napoli the pattern. The Coppa Italia has been better, two wins without conceding including 3-0 against Avellino to reach the round of 16. Jurić has a contract to 2028 and the American owners have said they will spend in January; the problem is a defence that cannot cope with his high line, and a return to Serie B after one season would repeat the Pisa and Cremonese pattern of recent promoted clubs.
