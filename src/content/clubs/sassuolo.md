## History

Sassuolo is a town of 40,000 in the ceramic-tile district of Emilia, and its football club, founded in 1920, had never been above Serie C2 when Giorgio Squinzi, the owner of the Mapei chemicals group, took it over in 2003. What followed was the most efficient rise in modern Italian football: Serie C2 champions in 2006, Serie C1 champions in 2008, and five seasons in Serie B, the last of them a 2012-13 title under Eusebio Di Francesco with Domenico Berardi, 18, scoring 11 goals, that made Sassuolo the smallest town ever to host a Serie A club. The club plays in Reggio Emilia, 25 kilometres away, at the Mapei Stadium, the former Giglio that Squinzi bought in 2013.

Di Francesco's first Serie A season ended in 17th and survival, and the second, 2014-15, produced the result that defined the club: a 4-3 win over Milan in January 2014 with four goals from Berardi, the first player to score four against Milan in Serie A. The 2015-16 side finished sixth and qualified for the Europa League, beating Luzern and Red Star Belgrade in the qualifiers and playing a group containing Athletic Bilbao and Genk, the only European campaign in the club's history.

## Trajectory

Sassuolo were a fixture in mid-table for 11 straight seasons, between sixth and 17th, under Di Francesco, Cristian Bucchi, [Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi), whose 2020-21 side finished eighth playing the most ambitious possession football outside the big clubs, and Alessio Dionisi. Squinzi died in October 2019 and his family kept the club. The 2023-24 season, under Dionisi and then Davide Ballardini, ended in a shock relegation, 19th with 30 points, after a decade in which the sporting director Giovanni Carnevali had sold players for more than €300m. Fabio Grosso took the club straight back as Serie B champions in 2024-25, with Berardi and Armand Laurienté restored, and finished 11th in 2025-26 with 49 points. Grosso left for Fiorentina on 4 June 2026 and Alberto Aquilani, the former Roma midfielder who had coached Pisa and Catanzaro, was appointed on 12 June.

## Players the club produced

Sassuolo's model has been to buy from Serie B and the Primavera leagues and sell to the big clubs: Simone Zaza and Nicola Sansone to Juventus and Villarreal; Francesco Acerbi to Lazio; Manuel Locatelli, bought from Milan for €12m and sold to Juventus for €37m; Jérémie Boga; Giacomo Raspadori to Napoli for €35m; Gianluca Scamacca to West Ham for €36m; Davide Frattesi to Inter for €33m; Domenico Berardi, who could have gone anywhere and never left, 150 goals and counting for the club at 32. Francesco Magnanelli, the captain who played 450 games across the climb from Serie C2, is the club's conscience, and Andrea Consigli kept goal for 11 seasons.

## Identity and playing style

De Zerbi's Sassuolo was the reference for [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in Italy, with the goalkeeper and centre-backs inviting pressure and playing through it, and Dionisi continued the approach. Grosso was more pragmatic. Aquilani, 42, has returned to a 4-2-3-1 built on possession and Berardi's left foot: Arijanet Murić in goal, Jay Idzes, the Indonesia captain, and Duje Ćaleta-Car at centre-back, Daniel Boloca and the 38-year-old Nemanja Matić in midfield, Kristian Thorstvedt and Cristian Volpato behind a striker from Laurienté, Kieron Bowie or Benjamín Domínguez. The side scores, 16 goals in ten games, and concedes more, 26, the worst defensive record in the league.

## Rivalries

Sassuolo have no derby of note in Serie A; the local enemies are Modena, Reggiana, whose stadium they occupy, and Carpi. The games against [Bologna](/clubs/bologna) and Parma are regional but friendly. The fixture against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) has its own history after Berardi's four goals and a run of wins at San Siro between 2014 and 2023.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Sassuolo are 17th in Serie A with eight points from ten matches, two wins, two draws and six defeats, outside the relegation zone only on goal difference from Monza and having conceded 26, including six at home to Lazio on 27 October and a 2-2 draw with Bologna. The Coppa Italia ended at the second round. Aquilani's first Serie A job has been harder than Carnevali expected, and the sporting director, who has never sacked a coach before Christmas, is under pressure to do so; a second relegation in three seasons would end the long Mapei experiment as it has been known.
