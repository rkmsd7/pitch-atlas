## History

Clube de Regatas do Flamengo was founded in 1895 as a rowing club in the Rio neighbourhood that gave it its name, and the football section was born in 1911 when a group of dissident players left Fluminense. The red-and-black hoops, the rowing origins and the mass popular following that grew during the radio era in the 1930s and 1940s gave Flamengo an identity unlike any other Brazilian club: it belongs to the people in a way that is partly myth and partly census. Polls consistently make it the most-supported club in the country, with more than 40 million fans, and the Maracanã, built for the 1950 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), has been its home ever since.

The early decades brought Carioca state titles with Leônidas da Silva, Zizinho and Domingos da Guia, but the club's national and world status was created by one generation. [Zico](/players/zico), the "White Pelé", came through the youth system in the 1970s and led a team with Júnior, Leandro, Adílio and Nunes that won the first national title in 1980, the Copa Libertadores in 1981 (3-0 on aggregate against Cobreloa after a bruising series) and the 1981 Intercontinental Cup, a 3-0 dismantling of Liverpool in Tokyo in which Zico was untouchable. Further Brasileirão titles followed in 1982, 1983 and 1987, the last under disputed circumstances with a parallel "Copa União".

[Romário](/players/romario) returned from Barcelona to score the goals in the 1992 title, Bebeto played for the club before the 1994 World Cup, and the Serbian playmaker Dejan Petković gave the 2000s their signature with a last-minute free-kick to win the 2001 Carioca final against Vasco. Those decades were otherwise a story of debt and disorder: Flamengo was one of the most badly run clubs in Brazil, flirted with relegation in 2002, 2004 and 2005, and won the 2009 title under Andrade almost in spite of its administration. [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho)'s 2011-12 spell produced a Carioca title and a 5-4 hat-trick against Santos before ending in a dispute over unpaid wages.

## Trajectory

The modern club dates from the 2013 election of Eduardo Bandeira de Mello, who restructured the finances, and from the 2019 appointment of [Jorge Jesus](/managers/jorge-jesus), the Portuguese coach who turned a strong squad into one of the greatest teams in Brazilian history. The 2019 side of Gabigol, Bruno Henrique, Arrascaeta, Everton Ribeiro, Gerson and Rafinha won the Brasileirão by 16 points and the Copa Libertadores, the second in the club's history, with Gabigol scoring twice in the last two minutes to beat River Plate 2-1 in Lima. It then took Liverpool to extra time in the [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) final in Qatar.

Jesus left for Benfica in 2020, and Flamengo retained the league under Rogério Ceni, lost the 2021 Libertadores final to Palmeiras, and won a third Libertadores in 2022 under Dorival Júnior, beating Athletico Paranaense 1-0 in Guayaquil with a Gabigol goal, alongside the Copa do Brasil. The club then burned through coaches: Vítor Pereira, Jorge Sampaoli, Tite, before Filipe Luís, the former left-back, took the job in late 2024 and won the Copa do Brasil within weeks.

The 2025 season was the best since 2019. Flamengo went to the [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) in the United States and beat [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) 3-1 in Philadelphia, topped a group containing Espérance and Los Angeles FC, and lost 4-2 to [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) in the round of 16. In November 2025 the club won its fourth Copa Libertadores, beating [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) 1-0 in Lima through a Danilo header, and in December it clinched a ninth Brasileirão ahead of the same opponent. Filipe Luís was lured to [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) in the European summer of 2026; the club's current squad data lists Leonardo Jardim, formerly of Cruzeiro and Monaco, as head coach as of November 2026, and Flamengo lead the 2026 Brasileirão with 76 points from 34 games, three clear of Palmeiras with four rounds left.

## Players the club produced

The Ninho do Urubu academy is one of the most productive in the world. [Zico](/players/zico), Júnior, Leandro and Adílio were all home-grown; so were [Vinícius Júnior](/players/vinicius-junior), sold to [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) at 16 for €45m in 2017, Lucas Paquetá (to [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) and West Ham), Reinier (to Real Madrid), Matheus França (to Crystal Palace), Lorran, and Adriano, Júlio César and Leonardo Moura of earlier generations. [Romário](/players/romario), Bebeto and [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho) came as stars; Gabriel "Gabigol" Barbosa, Giorgian de Arrascaeta, Pedro and Bruno Henrique were signed from elsewhere and became legends in the Jesus era. In 2025 the club added Jorginho from Arsenal and the left-back Alex Sandro, Danilo and Samuel Lino from European clubs.

## Identity and playing style

Jorge Jesus gave Flamengo a European idea that stuck: a 4-2-3-1 built on a very high defensive line, aggressive pressing in the opposition half and quick combinations between the number ten (Arrascaeta) and the forwards. It was the first Brazilian team in a generation to dominate with a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and sustained pressure rather than counter-attacks, and every successor has been measured against it. Filipe Luís kept the possession framework and added a back three in build-up and more control; Jardim, in the club's 2026 campaign, has used a 4-3-3 with Jorginho as the deep pivot, Arrascaeta as the creator and Pedro and Samuel Lino as finishers, with [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) principles in the build-up. The crowd expects "jogo bonito" with trophies: a defensive Flamengo is never tolerated for long.

## Rivalries

Fla-Flu against [Fluminense](/clubs/fluminense) is the oldest and most romantic derby in Brazil, born of the 1911 schism and played at the Maracanã since the 1950 era with crowds of up to 194,000 in the old configuration. The Clássico dos Milhões with Vasco da Gama, the club of Rio's Portuguese community and the Petković final of 2001, is often more hostile, and the Clássico da Rivalidade with [Botafogo](/clubs/botafogo) has been sharpened by Botafogo's 2024 double. Nationally, the fixture against [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) has been the biggest in Brazil since 2019, with Libertadores finals in 2021 and 2025, the 2025 league race and the 2026 title race all decided between the two.

## Current situation

As of November 2026 Flamengo are top of the Brasileirão with 76 points and four rounds to play, chasing a tenth title and a first back-to-back league double since 2019-20, under Jardim. Arrascaeta, Pedro, Jorginho, Léo Pereira and Danilo lead a squad that remains the deepest in South America, and the 2025 Libertadores win qualified the club for the 2029 [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup) cycle and the 2025 Intercontinental Cup. The club's long-running plan for its own stadium in the Gasômetro area of Rio is the other big story. For the rubro-negro nation, the question is not whether Flamengo will win, but by how much.
