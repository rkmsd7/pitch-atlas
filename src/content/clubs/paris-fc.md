## History

Paris FC were founded in 1969 as part of the campaign to give the capital a top-flight club, and in 1970 merged with Stade Saint-Germain to create Paris Saint-Germain. The marriage lasted two years. In 1972 the Paris city council, which wanted a club bearing the city's name alone, forced a split: Paris FC kept the professional licence, the first-team squad and the place in Division 1, while PSG were demoted to the third tier. Paris FC's advantage lasted two seasons; they were relegated in 1974, passed PSG on the way down, and spent the next forty years in the second, third and fourth divisions, with a single Division 1 season in 1978-79.

The club was a National (third tier) side for most of the 2000s and 2010s, with a brief Ligue 2 spell in 2015-16 that ended in relegation. Promotion in 2017 (as runners-up, after Bastia's demotion) began a long campaign for Ligue 1 under president Pierre Ferracci: play-off defeats in 2019, 2021, 2022 and 2024, with Jean-Philippe Mateta and Gaëtan Laborde among the forwards who passed through, and the Bahraini state as a minority shareholder from 2020.

In October 2024 Agache, the Arnault family's holding company, bought a majority stake with Red Bull as a minority partner, and the money transformed the project. Stéphane Gilli's side finished second in Ligue 2 in 2024-25 and went up, the first Paris FC season in the top flight since 1979, and the club moved from the Stade Charléty to the 20,000-seat Stade Jean-Bouin, next door to the Parc des Princes. A tenth-place finish in 2025-26 was followed by the appointment of Liam Rosenior, the former Strasbourg and Chelsea coach, for 2026-27.

## Trajectory

From the National in 2014 to Ligue 1 in 2025, with one false start in 2016 and four play-off defeats along the way; then, with the Arnault-Red Bull backing, from promotion to the Champions League places within eighteen months. Paris FC's trajectory is the sharpest in French football since the Qatari takeover of PSG, and the comparison is made constantly.

## Players the club produced

Paris FC's academy was marginal for decades; Jean-Marc Guillou, the France midfielder, played for the 1970s side. The club's modern reputation is for developing lower-league talent: Mateta (sold to Mainz, now Crystal Palace), Laborde, Morgan Guilavogui, Julien Lopez and Vincent Demarconnay, the goalkeeper of the long Ligue 2 campaign. Since 2024 the recruitment has been of a different order, with Ilan Kebbal, Jean-Philippe Krasso and Maxime López among the Ligue 1-ready signings, and the Red Bull network supplying young players.

## Identity and playing style

Paris FC under Gilli were a well-organised 4-2-3-1 that pressed in midfield and attacked through Kebbal's creativity. Liam Rosenior, who made his name with Strasbourg's young, possession-based side in 2024-25, plays [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs), a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and aggressive counter-pressing, the Red Bull school adapted to a technical squad. The 2026-27 team is the most surprising in France, scoring freely and sitting in the Champions League qualifying place in November.

## Rivalries

The rivalry is with [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), the club Paris FC once were half of and now share a neighbourhood with; the first Ligue 1 derby in 2025-26 was the fixture of the promoted season, and the women's team finished above PSG's in 2025-26. Red Star, the historic club of Saint-Ouen, are the old Paris rival from the lower leagues, and [Le Havre](/clubs/le-havre) a frequent Ligue 2 opponent.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Paris FC are fourth in Ligue 1 on 17 points from nine games (five wins, two draws, two defeats, 16 scored, ten conceded), level on points with Lyon and in the Champions League qualifying place. They beat Rennes 4-3 at Jean-Bouin on 18 October, lost 2-1 at Lens on 24 October and beat Monaco 3-1 at home on 1 November. The next match is at Auxerre on 7 November.

Rosenior's side has exceeded every expectation, and the question in Paris is whether the Arnault-Red Bull project can sustain a European challenge in only its second top-flight season. Kebbal, Krasso and López are the key players; the stadium question (Jean-Bouin is small, and a move or expansion has been discussed) is the next one.
