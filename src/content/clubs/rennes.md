## History

Stade Rennais were founded in 1901 and have played in red and black since a 1904 merger. They are Brittany's oldest major club and were founder members of the French professional league in 1932, winning the Coupe de France in 1965 and 1971 under Jean Prouff with Daniel Rodighiero and Raymond Keruzoré. For most of the twentieth century they moved between the first and second divisions, with relegations in 1975, 1977, 1987 and 1991, and the club nearly went bankrupt in the mid-1990s.

The Pinault family's arrival changed the scale. François Pinault, the luxury-goods billionaire and a Breton, bought the club in 1998 through Artémis, and his investment went into the academy at La Piverdière and a rebuilt Stade de la Route de Lorient (renamed Roazhon Park in 2015). Shabani Nonda and Sylvain Wiltord were the early stars; Petr Čech spent two seasons in goal (2002-04) before Chelsea. László Bölöni's side finished fourth in 2005, the club's first UEFA Cup qualification, and Guy Lacombe's fourth in 2007 and a 2009 cup final lost to Guingamp followed. Yoann Gourcuff, a Rennes academy product who had left for Bordeaux, returned in 2015.

Julien Stéphan, promoted from the academy in December 2018, delivered the club's first trophy in 48 years: the 2019 Coupe de France, beating PSG on penalties at the Stade de France after trailing 2-0, with a side that included Eduardo Camavinga, then 16, Ismaïla Sarr and Benjamin Bourigeaud. A third-place finish in 2019-20 brought a first Champions League campaign. [Bruno Génésio](/managers/bruno-genesio) (2021-23) produced the most entertaining football in the club's history, fourth in 2022 with 82 goals and fourth again in 2023, with Martin Terrier, Gaëtan Laborde and Lovro Majer. The seasons since were harder, with Stéphan's return, then Jorge Sampaoli and Habib Beye all failing to restore the top-four form, before Franck Haise, the former Lens and Nice coach, was appointed for 2026-27.

## Trajectory

Rennes rose from a mid-table, occasionally relegated club in the 1990s to a consistent top-half side in the 2000s and a European regular in the 2020s: Champions League in 2020-21, Europa League in 2021-22 and 2022-23, Conference League quarter-finals in 2022. The 2023-24 and 2024-25 seasons (10th and 12th, with four coaches in two years) were a regression, and the 2025-26 campaign a modest recovery. The Pinault ownership, now led by François-Henri Pinault, remains one of the wealthiest and most patient in France.

The 2019 cup final is the day the club is measured against. Rennes had lost finals in 2009 and 2014 to Guingamp and trailed Neymar's and Mbappé's PSG 2-0 inside 21 minutes at the Stade de France; Presnel Kimpembe's own goal and Mexer's header levelled it before half-time, the match went to penalties, and Christopher Nkunku's miss gave Rennes a 6-5 shoot-out win. Camavinga did not play but was on the pitch for the celebrations; the Roazhon Park crowd of 30,000 that welcomed the team home the next day was the largest the city had seen for a sporting event. The Champions League that followed in 2020-21, with a group of Chelsea, Sevilla and Krasnodar, produced one point and a lesson in the gap to the elite.

## Players the club produced

Rennes's academy is the great export house of French football. [Ousmane Dembélé](/players/ousmane-dembele) played one season (2015-16) before Dortmund; Eduardo Camavinga debuted at 16 and was sold to Real Madrid at 18 in 2021; Désiré Doué came through and was sold to PSG in 2024 for around 50 million euros; Yann M'Vila, Yacine Brahimi, Jérémy Doku (signed young from Anderlecht), Mathys Tel (Bayern), Warmed Omari and Lorenz Assignon are others. Sylvain Wiltord came through in the 1990s, Yoann Gourcuff in the 2000s. Among signings, Nonda, Čech, Steve Mandanda (2022-24), John Arne Riise, M'Baye Niang, Bourigeaud (over 300 games) and Terrier are the names fans cite.

## Identity and playing style

Rennes play attacking, youth-driven football by policy. Génésio's side was a 4-3-3 [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) machine with Terrier and Laborde; Stéphan's cup winners pressed in a 4-4-2 and relied on the pace of Sarr. Franck Haise brings the 3-4-3 he used at Lens, a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with wing-backs, [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) and vertical transitions, a style the Roazhon Park crowd has taken to quickly. The results so far have been high-scoring at both ends.

The coaching churn of 2023-25 is the counter-example to the Pinault patience. Génésio's departure in November 2023 was followed by Julien Stéphan's return, which lasted a year, then Jorge Sampaoli, who lasted three months, and Habib Beye, who kept the club up in 2025 before leaving for Marseille. Four coaches in eighteen months produced two mid-table finishes and a squad turnover that undid much of the Génésio era, and Haise's appointment was framed by sporting director Frederic Massara as the start of a longer cycle.

## Rivalries

The Derby Breton against Nantes is the great western rivalry, played since the 1960s, with Nantes now in Ligue 2 after their 2026 relegation. [Brest](/clubs/brest) and [Lorient](/clubs/lorient) are the Breton rivals in Ligue 1, and Guingamp, who beat Rennes in the 2009 and 2014 cup finals, the small-town enemy. [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) are the club Rennes beat in the 2019 final and have since sold Doué to.

## Current situation

As of 7 November 2026 Rennes are fifth in Ligue 1 on 16 points from nine games (five wins, one draw, three defeats), with 20 goals scored and 19 conceded, the most open record in the league. They lost 4-3 at Paris FC on 18 October, beat Strasbourg 3-0 at home on 25 October and lost 5-2 at Nice on 30 October. Lille visit Roazhon Park on 8 November.

Haise's team scores and concedes freely, with Arnaud Kalimuendo and Ludovic Blas the attacking leaders and Brice Samba, signed from Lens in 2025, in goal. The aim is a return to Europe after three seasons without it, and the current position has the club in the Europa League places.
