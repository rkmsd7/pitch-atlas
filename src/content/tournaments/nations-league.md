## History

The Nations League was UEFA's answer to a problem of its own making: too many meaningless friendlies clogging the calendar and too little competitive football for smaller nations. Approved in 2014 and launched in September 2018, it placed all 55 member associations into four tiered leagues with promotion and relegation, a four-team finals tournament for the top league and a back-door route into Euro and World Cup qualifying for the rest.

The first finals, in Porto and Guimarães in June 2019, were won by hosts [Portugal](/nations/portugal), with [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) scoring a hat-trick against Switzerland in the semi-final and Gonçalo Guedes the winner against the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands). [France](/nations/france) won the second edition in Milan in October 2021, coming from behind to beat Belgium 3-2 in the semi-final and [Spain](/nations/spain) 2-1 in the final through [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe)'s contested winner. Spain beat Croatia on penalties in Rotterdam in 2023, Luis de la Fuente's first trophy and a springboard to Euro 2024 and the 2026 World Cup.

The 2024-25 edition added quarter-finals in March 2025, which produced some memorable ties (Spain beat the Netherlands on penalties, Portugal beat Denmark 5-3 on aggregate, Germany beat Italy 5-4, France beat Croatia on penalties) before Portugal beat hosts [Germany](/nations/germany) 2-1 and then Spain on penalties in Munich for a second title. The competition remains divisive, loved by nations such as Georgia and Kosovo who have used it to reach major tournaments and tolerated by the biggest sides, but it has produced consistently better matches than the friendlies it replaced.

## Format

The 55 UEFA members are split into League A, B and C (16 teams each, in four groups of four) and League D (seven teams in two groups). Each team plays its group opponents home and away between September and November. In League A, the group winners and runners-up go to two-legged quarter-finals in March, and the four winners contest a finals tournament in June (semi-finals, third-place match and final) hosted by one of the finalists. Group winners in Leagues B, C and D are promoted and bottom teams relegated, with promotion/relegation play-offs between the second-bottom of one league and runners-up of the one below.

The Nations League also feeds qualifying: the best-ranked teams not qualified through the regular groups enter play-offs for the European Championship, and from 2026 World Cup qualifying also reserves play-off places for Nations League group winners. The competition runs in even-numbered autumns with finals the following June, so it never clashes with the Euros or World Cup.

## Greatest editions and moments

- **2018, Spain 6-0 Croatia.** The first league phase announced the competition with Luis Enrique's Spain thrashing the World Cup runners-up in Elche; Croatia won the return 3-2.
- **2019, Ronaldo's hat-trick.** Against Switzerland in Porto, two goals in the last three minutes of a 3-1 semi-final win.
- **2019, the first final.** Guedes' 60th-minute strike beat the Netherlands 1-0 in Porto, Portugal's second trophy in three years after Euro 2016.
- **2020, Germany 0-6 Spain.** Ferran Torres' hat-trick in Seville was Germany's heaviest defeat since 1931 and effectively ended Joachim Löw's long reign.
- **2021, France's comebacks.** From 2-0 down against Belgium in Turin (Benzema, Mbappé, Theo Hernández 90') and 1-0 down against Spain in Milan, where Mbappé's winner was allowed despite a marginal offside.
- **2022, Netherlands and Italy.** The Dutch topped a group containing Belgium; Italy, who had missed the World Cup, reached the finals by beating Hungary in Budapest.
- **2023, Unai Simón in Rotterdam.** Two shootout saves against Croatia, and Dani Carvajal's Panenka to win it after a goalless 120 minutes; Luka Modrić's last chance at a trophy with Croatia.
- **2024, Spain 5-1 Switzerland and Kosovo's rise.** Spain's post-Euro team swept their group; Kosovo and Georgia used Leagues C and B to continue their climb.
- **2025, Germany 3-3 Italy.** The second leg of a quarter-final in Dortmund, Germany leading 3-0 at half-time (5-1 on aggregate) before Italy pulled three back.
- **2025, Nuno Mendes and Ronaldo.** In the Munich final, Mendes equalised Zubimendi's opener, Oyarzabal restored Spain's lead, Ronaldo scored his 138th international goal in the 61st minute, and Diogo Costa saved Morata's kick in the shootout.

## Records

- **Most titles:** Portugal 2 (2019, 2025); France and Spain 1.
- **Most finals:** Spain 3 (2021, 2023, 2025), winning one.
- **Top scorer, all editions:** Cristiano Ronaldo, 14 goals including the 2025 final.
- **Most goals in one league phase:** Erling Haaland, 9 for Norway (2024-25 group stage, League B); Ronaldo 7 in League A (2024-25).
- **Biggest win in League A:** Spain 6-0 Germany (2020) and Spain 6-0 Croatia (2018).
- **Most finals tournaments hosted:** one each (Portugal, Italy, Netherlands, Germany).
- **Promotions:** Kosovo and Georgia have each climbed two tiers since 2018; Georgia qualified for Euro 2024 via the Nations League play-off path.
- **Oldest scorer in a final:** Ronaldo, 40, in 2025.

## Philosophies that won

The Nations League rewards squads that can play at club-level intensity in short international windows. Portugal's 2019 team under Fernando Santos was a cautious 4-4-2 that defended in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and relied on Ronaldo and Bernardo Silva in transition; the 2025 version under Roberto Martínez had far more of the ball, a 4-2-3-1 with Vitinha and Bruno Fernandes controlling the middle in a possession style Martínez called "positional", with Nuno Mendes as an attacking left-back and Ronaldo as a pure penalty-box striker.

France in 2021 under Didier Deschamps were the archetype of modern [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking): a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with wing-backs, Pogba and Rabiot shielding, and Benzema and Mbappé released on the break. Spain under Luis de la Fuente in 2023 were a work in progress, a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) side built on [Rodri](/players/rodri) and Gavi that won the final with defensive discipline rather than domination; by 2025 the same side, with [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal) and Nico Williams wide, had a far more vertical identity and were unfortunate to lose on penalties. The Nations League has also been a laboratory for mid-ranked nations: Georgia's Willy Sagnol built a pressing 3-4-3, Scotland under Steve Clarke a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with overlapping wing-backs, and the Netherlands under Ronald Koeman an orthodox Dutch 4-3-3.

## Latest edition

The 2024-25 Nations League introduced the quarter-final round and ended with a finals tournament in Germany on 4-8 June 2025. The league phase saw Spain, Portugal, Germany, Italy, France, Croatia, Denmark and the Netherlands reach the last eight. The quarter-finals in March 2025 were outstanding: Spain drew 2-2 and 3-3 with the Netherlands before winning 5-4 on penalties in Valencia; Portugal lost 1-0 in Copenhagen and beat Denmark 5-2 after extra time in Lisbon; Germany beat Italy 2-1 at San Siro and drew 3-3 in Dortmund; France lost 2-0 in Split and won 2-0 in Paris before a shootout.

The semi-finals in Munich and Stuttgart went to Portugal, who came from behind to beat the hosts 2-1 (Francisco Conceição and Ronaldo cancelling out Florian Wirtz's opener), and Spain, who beat France 5-4 in a nine-goal thriller in which Lamine Yamal scored twice and Rayan Cherki and Mbappé scored for France. France beat Germany 2-0 for third place.

The final at the Allianz Arena on 8 June 2025 finished 2-2. Martín Zubimendi gave Spain the lead on 21 minutes, Nuno Mendes equalised five minutes later with a thunderous finish, Mikel Oyarzabal restored Spain's lead on the stroke of half-time, and Ronaldo poked in Mendes' cross in the 61st minute before going off injured. In the shootout Diogo Costa saved from Álvaro Morata and Rúben Neves converted the fifth Portuguese kick for a 5-3 win. Portugal became the first team to win the Nations League twice; Nuno Mendes was player of the tournament. It was only the second defeat of Luis de la Fuente's Spain reign, and both teams went on to the 2026 World Cup, where Spain won the title and knocked Portugal out in the round of 16 with a late winner.

## Next edition

The 2026-27 Nations League league phase began on 24 September 2026 and runs to 17 November 2026, with the final two matchdays on 12-14 and 15-17 November. League A again has 16 teams in four groups, with group winners and runners-up going to two-legged quarter-finals in March 2027 and a finals tournament in June 2027 in a host to be chosen from the four qualifiers. As of 7 November 2026 the group tables were not settled and Pitch Atlas has not verified standings; the competition also decides seeding and play-off places for Euro 2028 qualifying, which begins in March 2027. Portugal, now coached by Jorge Jesus, defend the trophy; Spain, world champions, and France under [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager) and Germany under [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) are using the competition to begin new cycles.
