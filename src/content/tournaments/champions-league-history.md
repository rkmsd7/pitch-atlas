## History

The European Cup was dreamed up by Gabriel Hanot of *L'Équipe* in 1954, partly in irritation at English newspapers calling Wolves "champions of the world" after beating Honvéd in a friendly. Sixteen invited clubs played the first edition in 1955-56, and [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), with Alfredo Di Stéfano, won it and the next four. The 7-3 win over Eintracht Frankfurt at Hampden Park in 1960, with Puskás scoring four and Di Stéfano three in front of 127,000, remains the benchmark final. [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) with Eusébio, [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) and [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan) under Helenio Herrera took over in the 1960s, [Celtic](/clubs/celtic)'s Lisbon Lions of 1967 were the first British winners, and [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) won at Wembley in 1968 a decade after the Munich air disaster.

The 1970s belonged to the Dutch and the Germans: [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) and [Johan Cruyff](/players/johan-cruyff)'s [Total Football](/tactics/total-football) won three in a row (1971-73), then [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) under Beckenbauer and Gerd Müller did the same (1974-76). English clubs then won seven of eight editions between 1977 and 1984, [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) four times and Nottingham Forest twice under Brian Clough, before the Heysel disaster in 1985 led to a five-year ban. Milan under Arrigo Sacchi (1989, 1990) reinvented pressing and the offside line; Red Star Belgrade and [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) under Cruyff bookended the old straight-knockout format.

The Champions League, with group stages and a television-driven rebrand, began in 1992-93. Marseille won the first, Milan's 4-0 over Barcelona in 1994 is the best final performance of the modern era, and Ajax's youngsters in 1995, [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) and Real Madrid in 1998 (their first for 32 years) filled the decade before United's stoppage-time comeback against Bayern in 1999. Real's *galácticos* won in 2000 and 2002 (Zidane's volley at Hampden), Milan and Liverpool traded Istanbul 2005 and Athens 2007, Barcelona under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) won in 2009 and 2011 with [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) and [Xavi](/players/xavi), and Real Madrid under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) and [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager) won five in six years between 2014 and 2018 with [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo). Since then the trophy has gone to Liverpool (2019), Bayern (2020), [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) (2021), Real (2022, 2024), [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) (2023) and [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), who finally won it in 2025 and retained it in 2026.

## Format

Since 2024-25 the Champions League has 36 teams in a single league phase ("Swiss model") instead of eight groups. Each club plays eight different opponents, four at home and four away, drawn from four seeding pots. The top eight go straight to the round of 16; teams finishing 9th to 24th play a two-legged knockout play-off for the remaining eight places, and 25th to 36th are eliminated with no drop into the Europa League. From the round of 16 onwards the competition is a seeded, two-legged knockout to a single-match final at a neutral venue.

Places are allocated by UEFA's association coefficients, with the top leagues ([Premier League](/leagues/premier-league), [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga), [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a), [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga)) receiving four direct places and two further "European Performance Spot" places going to the two associations with the best collective results the previous season. The away-goals rule was abolished in 2021; extra time and penalties decide level ties.

## Greatest editions and moments

- **1960, Hampden.** Real Madrid 7-3 Eintracht Frankfurt, Puskás four and Di Stéfano three, the fifth straight title and the match that taught Britain what European football was.
- **1967, the Lisbon Lions.** Jock Stein's Celtic, all born within 30 miles of Celtic Park, beat Herrera's Inter 2-1 with Tommy Gemmell and Steve Chalmers scoring.
- **1968, Wembley.** Bobby Charlton's two goals and George Best's extra-time dribble around the keeper, 10 years after Munich.
- **1972, Ajax 2-0 Inter.** Cruyff's two goals in Rotterdam, the second of three straight Ajax titles and the high-water mark of Total Football.
- **1981, Alan Kennedy.** Liverpool's left-back scored the only goal against Real Madrid in Paris; Bob Paisley's third European Cup in five years.
- **1994, Milan 4-0 Barcelona.** Fabio Capello's side, without Baresi and Costacurta, destroyed Cruyff's Dream Team in Athens; Savićević's lob the goal of the night.
- **1999, Camp Nou.** Sheringham 90+1', Solskjær 90+3', and [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson)'s treble completed against Bayern. "Football, bloody hell."
- **2002, Zidane's volley.** The left-footed strike from Roberto Carlos's looping cross at Hampden, Real's ninth title in their centenary year.
- **2005, Istanbul.** Milan 3-0 up at half-time, Liverpool level by the hour through Gerrard, Šmicer and Alonso, Dudek's save from Shevchenko and the shootout.
- **2009 and 2011, Guardiola's Barcelona.** Two finals against Manchester United, 2-0 in Rome and 3-1 at Wembley, the closest club football has come to perfection.
- **2014, Ramos 92:48.** Sergio Ramos's header in the 93rd minute of the Lisbon final against [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) took it to extra time and Real won 4-1 for *La Décima*.
- **2019, Anfield 4-0 Barcelona.** Origi and Wijnaldum's doubles overturned a 3-0 first-leg deficit, Trent Alexander-Arnold's quick corner the moment of the tie.
- **2022, Real's resurrections.** Rodrygo's two goals in 90 seconds against Manchester City, after comebacks against PSG and Chelsea, before Courtois beat Liverpool in Paris.
- **2025, PSG 5-0 Inter.** Désiré Doué's two goals in Munich, the largest winning margin in a final, and [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique)'s young team completing a treble.

## Philosophies that won

The European Cup is the competition where tactical eras are crowned. Real Madrid's five in a row were a product of star quality and a fluid attacking shape around Di Stéfano as a deep-lying centre-forward, an early [false nine](/tactics/false-nine). Herrera's Inter in 1964 and 1965 won with [catenaccio](/tactics/catenaccio), a sweeper behind man-markers and Giacinto Facchetti as the first great attacking full-back. Ajax and Bayern in the 1970s offered two answers: [Total Football](/tactics/total-football) with positional interchange and a high line, and Bayern's Beckenbauer-led libero system with Müller as a pure finisher.

English dominance from 1977 to 1984 came from pass-and-move 4-4-2s and the patience of Paisley's Liverpool; Sacchi's Milan in 1989-90 replaced the sweeper with a zonal [back four](/tactics/high-line), a compact 4-4-2 and the pressing that became [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing). Cruyff's Barcelona in 1992 and Guardiola's in 2009 and 2011 won with [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and Messi as a false nine; [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s Porto in 2004 and Inter in 2010, and Di Matteo's Chelsea in 2012, proved a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and brilliant [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) could beat it. [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s Liverpool in 2019 won with gegenpressing; Guardiola's Manchester City in 2023 with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) and a [box midfield](/tactics/box-midfield); Ancelotti's Real Madrid in 2022 and 2024 with pragmatic game management and the ability to switch from possession to transition. Luis Enrique's PSG in 2025 and 2026 combined positional play with ferocious counter-pressing and three fluid forwards, the current state of the art.

## Latest edition

The 2025-26 Champions League was the second under the league-phase format and ended with PSG becoming the first club to retain the trophy since Real Madrid in 2018. The season's defining tie was the semi-final between PSG and Bayern Munich: PSG won the first leg 5-4 in Paris, an extraordinary match in which [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane), on his way to 73 goals in the season and the 2026 Ballon d'Or, kept dragging Bayern back, and the return at the Allianz Arena finished 1-1, Ousmane Dembélé scoring early and Kane's late leveller not enough; 6-5 on aggregate. In the other semi-final [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), who had already secured a first [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) title in 22 years under [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta), drew 1-1 at Atlético Madrid and won the second leg 1-0 at the Emirates with [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka)'s finish on the stroke of half-time.

The final at the Puskás Aréna in Budapest on 30 May 2026 was Arsenal's first since 2006 and PSG's third in a row. Kai Havertz headed Arsenal in front in the sixth minute, and for an hour Arteta's side defended their lead with the organisation that had won them the league. Dembélé equalised from the penalty spot in the 65th minute, extra time produced no further goals, and PSG won the shootout 4-3. Luis Enrique's third Champions League as a coach (after Barcelona 2015 and PSG 2025) put him level with Guardiola and Zidane and one behind Ancelotti. PSG's victory celebrations in Paris were marred by rioting for the second year running.

Elsewhere in the season, Real Madrid's campaign was disrupted when Xabi Alonso left the club mid-season, while domestic champions Inter (Cristian Chivu), Barcelona and Bayern all fell short of the final. The 2026 win took PSG to two titles and confirmed Dembélé, Vitinha, Doué and Nuno Mendes as the core of the sport's best club side.

## Next edition

The 2026-27 Champions League league phase began in September 2026 and the final will be played at Atlético Madrid's Estadio Metropolitano on 5 June 2027, the first final in Madrid since 2019. PSG defend the trophy under Luis Enrique; Arsenal, Barcelona, Inter, Bayern and Liverpool (now under Andoni Iraola) are the obvious challengers, and Real Madrid begin a new era under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho), who returned on 13 July 2026 and is attempting to become the first coach to win the competition with three different clubs. UEFA has confirmed the 2028 final for Munich's Allianz Arena and the 2029 final for the rebuilt Camp Nou in Barcelona.
