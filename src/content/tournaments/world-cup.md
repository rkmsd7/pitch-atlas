## History

The World Cup was Jules Rimet's idea and Uruguay's party. Thirteen teams travelled to Montevideo in 1930, only four of them from Europe, and the hosts beat [Argentina](/nations/argentina) 4-2 in the Estadio Centenario. Italy won the next two under Vittorio Pozzo, the war took 1942 and 1946, and when the tournament returned in 1950 it produced the most traumatic result in football history: Uruguay 2, [Brazil](/nations/brazil) 1, in front of around 200,000 at the Maracanã.

The modern World Cup really begins in 1954 with the first televised edition and West Germany's "Miracle of Bern" over the Hungary of Puskás, and in 1958 with a 17-year-old [Pelé](/players/pele) scoring twice in the final in Stockholm. Brazil's 1958-70 side set the template for everything the tournament would become: a global television event built around individual genius. England's 1966 win at Wembley, the Dutch "[Total Football](/tactics/total-football)" that lost two finals in 1974 and 1978, and [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona)'s one-man tournament in 1986 each belong to that era of 16 and then 24 teams.

The field grew to 32 in 1998 and the geography grew with it: Asia hosted in 2002, Africa in 2010, the Gulf in 2022. European sides won every edition from 2006 to 2018 before Argentina's 2022 triumph in Qatar, the greatest final ever played, gave [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) the one medal he lacked. In 2026 the tournament expanded again to 48 teams and three host nations, and [Spain](/nations/spain) emerged from it as the first European winner on American soil.

## Format

Since 2026 the World Cup is a 48-team, 104-match tournament. Teams are drawn into 12 groups of four; the top two in each group plus the eight best third-placed sides go to a new round of 32, followed by a round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals, a third-place play-off and the final. Qualification runs over the preceding two and a half years through the six confederations, with the final two places decided by an inter-confederation play-off tournament in the host country in March of the World Cup year.

Knockout matches that are level after 90 minutes go to 30 minutes of extra time and then penalties. The old rule that a champion qualifies automatically was scrapped in 2006; only hosts are exempt from qualifying. Squads were 26 players in 2022 and 2026.

## Greatest editions and moments

- **1950, Maracanazo.** Uruguay needed to win and Brazil only needed a draw. Alcides Ghiggia's 79th-minute goal silenced the largest crowd in football history and shaped Brazilian football's psyche for decades.
- **1954, Miracle of Bern.** Hungary had beaten West Germany 8-3 in the group. In the final the Germans came from 2-0 down to win 3-2 in the rain, Helmut Rahn scoring the winner with six minutes left.
- **1958, Pelé arrives.** Aged 17, he scored a hat-trick in the semi-final against France and two in the final against Sweden, including the chest-and-lob that defines the tournament's archive footage.
- **1966, Wembley.** Geoff Hurst's hat-trick, the ghost goal off the crossbar, and Kenneth Wolstenholme's "they think it's all over". [England](/nations/england)'s only title.
- **1970, the perfect team.** Brazil's fourth goal in the final, Carlos Alberto finishing a move involving nine players, is still the most replayed team goal ever. Pelé, Jairzinho, Tostão, Gérson and Rivellino in one side.
- **1974, Total Football falls short.** Cruyff's Netherlands led the final after 80 seconds without a German touching the ball, then lost 2-1 to Beckenbauer's West Germany. See [Johan Cruyff](/players/johan-cruyff).
- **1982, Italy v Brazil.** Paolo Rossi's hat-trick knocked out the most loved losing team in the tournament's history, Telê Santana's Brazil of Zico, Sócrates and Falcão.
- **1986, Maradona's tournament.** The Hand of God and the Goal of the Century against England within four minutes of each other, then a semi-final double against Belgium and the assist for Burruchaga's winner in the final.
- **1998, Zidane's two headers.** [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane) scored twice from corners as France beat Brazil 3-0 in Saint-Denis, the night a multi-ethnic team became a national symbol.
- **2002, Ronaldo's redemption.** [Ronaldo Nazário](/players/ronaldo-nazario) scored eight goals four years after his breakdown before the 1998 final, including both in the 2-0 win over Germany.
- **2014, 7-1.** Germany's semi-final demolition of hosts Brazil in Belo Horizonte, four goals in six first-half minutes, is the most shocking scoreline in World Cup history.
- **2022, the greatest final.** Messi 2, [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe) 3, 3-3 after extra time, Emiliano Martínez's save from Kolo Muani with the last kick, Argentina on penalties.

## Records

- **Most titles:** Brazil 5 (1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, 2002); Germany and Italy 4; Argentina 3; Spain, France and Uruguay 2.
- **Most tournament goals:** Miroslav Klose 16 (2002-2014); Ronaldo 15; Gerd Müller 14; Messi 13; Just Fontaine 13 in a single tournament (1958); Mbappé 10 in 2026, the most in one edition since Fontaine.
- **Most appearances:** Messi 31 matches across 2006-2026, the only man to play in six World Cups. Mbappé and Messi share the record for most knockout-stage goals.
- **Only player with three World Cup winners' medals:** Pelé (1958, 1962, 1970).
- **Biggest win:** Hungary 10-1 El Salvador (1982). **Biggest final margin:** 3 goals (1958, 1970, 1998).
- **Youngest scorer:** Pelé, 17 years 239 days (1958). **Oldest:** Roger Milla, 42 (1994).
- **Hosts who won:** Uruguay 1930, Italy 1934, England 1966, West Germany 1974, Argentina 1978, France 1998.
- **Fastest goal:** Hakan Şükür, 11 seconds, Turkey v South Korea (2002).

## Philosophies that won

The World Cup is a short tournament with tired players, so it has rewarded organisation more often than beauty. Pozzo's Italy of the 1930s won with the *metodo* and physical discipline; Brazil's 1958-70 sides married a 4-2-4 and then 4-3-3 to improvisational genius, the ancestor of what is now called [relationism](/tactics/relationism). West Germany in 1974 and 1990 won with a libero and a straightforward pressing structure; Italy in 1982 and 2006 with the defensive inheritance of [catenaccio](/tactics/catenaccio), a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and devastating transitions.

Spain's 2010 win was the high point of [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka): 77 per cent possession in the final, 1-0 wins through the knockouts, Iniesta in the 116th minute. Germany's 2014 and France's 2018 titles were hybrid, possession-based teams that could switch to [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) at will, with Didier Deschamps' France the purest example of a team happy without the ball. Argentina in 2022 and 2026 were built on a compact 4-4-2 out of possession and freedom for Messi inside it. Spain in 2026 under Luis de la Fuente won with a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) system that was less about long possession spells and more about speed through the thirds, [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal) and Nico Williams isolated one-on-one, and [Rodri](/players/rodri) controlling the centre. The lesson of a century: the winners are the teams that defend the box well and have one or two players who can decide a tight match.

## Latest edition

The 2026 World Cup, 11 June to 19 July, was the first 48-team edition and the first spread across three countries: 11 stadiums in the United States, three in Mexico and two in Canada. The opening match was in Mexico City's Estadio Azteca, hosting its third World Cup, and the final at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey.

The expanded format produced some lopsided group matches but a strong knockout bracket. In the quarter-finals [France](/nations/france) beat [Morocco](/nations/morocco) 2-0 in Boston with goals from Mbappé and Ousmane Dembélé; Spain beat [Belgium](/nations/belgium) 2-1 in Los Angeles, Mikel Merino's 88th-minute winner after Fabián Ruiz and Charles De Ketelaere had traded first-half goals; England came from behind to beat Norway 2-1 through two [Jude Bellingham](/players/jude-bellingham) goals after Andreas Schjelderup had scored for Norway; and Argentina beat Switzerland 3-1 after extra time, Breel Embolo's red card on 72 minutes tipping a tight match before Julián Álvarez and Lautaro Martínez scored in the extra period. Germany, under Julian Nagelsmann, had already gone out early, a result that led directly to [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s appointment as national coach in July. [Portugal](/nations/portugal) and Brazil both fell before the last eight.

Spain beat France 2-0 in the first semi-final in Dallas on 14 July: Mikel Oyarzabal converted a 22nd-minute penalty won by Lamine Yamal and Pedro Porro scored from a one-two with Dani Olmo on 58 minutes. In Atlanta the next night Anthony Gordon put England ahead on 55 minutes from a Morgan Rogers cross, but Argentina equalised and then, deep into stoppage time, Messi's pass sent Lautaro Martínez through for a 2-1 winner. England beat France 6-4 in a chaotic third-place match in Miami.

The final on 19 July was cagey, tactical and ended in extra time. Enzo Fernández was sent off for Argentina, and in the 106th minute Ferran Torres scored the only goal. Spain's second title, 16 years after the first, came with a clean sweep of the awards: Rodri took the Golden Ball, Mbappé the Golden Boot with 10 goals, Unai Simón the Golden Glove with seven clean sheets and Pau Cubarsí the Best Young Player award. It was Messi's sixth and almost certainly last World Cup, ending in a second consecutive final. Luis de la Fuente was given a contract extension within days; Deschamps left France after 14 years, replaced by [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager).

## Next edition

The 2030 World Cup marks the centenary and will be shared by Spain, Portugal and Morocco, with three opening matches in Uruguay, Argentina and Paraguay to honour the first tournament. Spain will attempt to defend the trophy at home, something only Italy (1934-38) and Brazil (1958-62) have managed. The 2034 tournament has been awarded to [Saudi Arabia](/nations/saudi-arabia). As of November 2026 qualifying for 2030 has not begun.
