## History

The UEFA Cup replaced the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup in 1971 as a competition for the best non-champions of each league, and its early decades were a story of two-legged finals and English, German and Dutch strength. [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) beat Wolves in the first final in 1972, [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) won in 1973 and 1976, Borussia Mönchengladbach twice, and Ipswich Town under Bobby Robson in 1981. IFK Göteborg won twice from Sweden, [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid)'s *Quinta del Buitre* won back-to-back in 1985 and 1986, and Maradona's [Napoli](/clubs/napoli) took it in 1989.

Serie A then owned it: between 1989 and 1999 Italian clubs won eight of 11 editions, [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan) three times, [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) twice and Parma twice, with [Ronaldo Nazário](/players/ronaldo-nazario)'s goal for Inter in the first single-match final in Paris in 1998. The 2000s belonged to surprise and to Spain: [Galatasaray](/clubs/galatasaray) in 2000, Liverpool's 5-4 golden-goal final against Alavés in 2001, [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s [Porto](/clubs/porto) in 2003, CSKA Moscow, Zenit and Shakhtar, and the start of [Sevilla](/clubs/sevilla)'s extraordinary relationship with the trophy under Juande Ramos in 2006 and 2007.

Rebranded the Europa League in 2009, the competition has been defined since by Sevilla's seven titles, four of them under [Unai Emery](/managers/unai-emery) (2014-16) and Julen Lopetegui (2020) and one more in 2023, by [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid)'s three under Quique Sánchez Flores and Diego Simeone, and by English clubs rediscovering it: [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in 2013 and 2019, [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in 2017, Tottenham in 2025 and [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa) in 2026, when Emery's fifth title ended Villa's 30-year wait for a trophy.

## Format

Since 2024-25 the Europa League mirrors the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history): 36 teams in a single league phase, each playing eight matches against eight different opponents. The top eight go to the round of 16; teams finishing 9th to 24th contest two-legged play-offs; the bottom 12 are eliminated. Clubs no longer drop in from the Champions League group stage. Knockout rounds are two-legged until a single-match final at a neutral venue. Qualification comes through league position (typically fifth or sixth in the top leagues), domestic cup winners, and qualifying rounds for smaller associations; the third tier, the Conference League, sits below. The winner earns a Champions League place for the following season.

## Greatest editions and moments

- **1972, Spurs over Wolves.** Martin Chivers scored twice at Molineux and Alan Mullery at White Hart Lane, the only all-English final until 2019.
- **1981, Ipswich.** Robson's team of Muhren, Thijssen and John Wark (14 goals, a record for the competition then) beat AZ 5-4 on aggregate.
- **1989, Maradona's Napoli.** Two-legged win over Stuttgart, Maradona and Careca scoring in a 5-4 aggregate, Napoli's only European trophy.
- **1998, Ronaldo in Paris.** Inter 3-0 Lazio, the Brazilian's goal after a stepover around Marchegiani the image of the first one-off final.
- **2001, Dortmund.** Liverpool 5-4 Alavés, Delfí Geli's own-goal golden goal in the 117th minute, the wildest final ever played.
- **2003, Mourinho's Porto.** Derlei's extra-time winner against Celtic in Seville, Henrik Larsson scoring twice in defeat, the start of the Mourinho legend.
- **2010, Forlán.** Diego Forlán's two goals, the second in the 116th minute, beat Fulham in Hamburg.
- **2014-16, Sevilla's three.** Gameiro's penalty against Benfica in Turin, 3-2 over Dnipro with Bacca's two goals, and a 3-1 comeback against Liverpool in Basel, all under Emery.
- **2021, 22 penalties.** Villarreal's Gerónimo Rulli scored the 21st kick and saved David de Gea's 22nd for an 11-10 shootout win over Manchester United in Gdańsk.
- **2022, Frankfurt in Seville.** Eintracht, 30,000 of whose fans had taken Camp Nou in the quarter-final, beat Rangers on penalties after Rafael Borré's equaliser.
- **2024, Lookman's hat-trick.** Ademola Lookman ended Leverkusen's 51-match unbeaten run in Dublin, Atalanta's first major trophy in 61 years.
- **2026, Villa in Istanbul.** Youri Tielemans and Emiliano Buendía scored in the first half and Morgan Rogers added a third before the hour as Villa beat Freiburg 3-0.

## Records

- **Most titles:** Sevilla 7 (2006, 2007, 2014, 2015, 2016, 2020, 2023); Inter, Juventus, Liverpool and Atlético Madrid 3.
- **Most titles as a coach:** Unai Emery, 5 (Sevilla 2014, 2015, 2016; Villarreal 2021; Aston Villa 2026).
- **Most goals, all-time:** Radamel Falcao and Aritz Aduriz among the leading scorers; Henrik Larsson holds the record for most goals across UEFA Cup/Europa League matches by a single player in the pre-2009 era.
- **Most goals in a final:** three each for Jupp Heynckes (1975) and Ademola Lookman (2024).
- **Biggest final win:** Juventus 6-1 Dortmund (1993, two legs); single match Sevilla 4-0 Middlesbrough (2006).
- **Longest shootout in a final:** Villarreal 11-10 Manchester United (2021).
- **Consecutive titles:** Sevilla 3 (2014-16); Real Madrid 2 (1985-86); Sevilla 2 (2006-07).
- **Sevilla's record in finals:** 7 played, 7 won.

## Philosophies that won

The UEFA Cup of the 1970s and 1980s was won by well-drilled 4-4-2 and 4-3-3 sides built on fitness and set pieces, from Liverpool's pass-and-move to Gladbach's quick wingers and Göteborg's Swedish zonal pressing under Sven-Göran Eriksson. Italian dominance in the 1990s came from [catenaccio](/tactics/catenaccio)'s descendants, the compact 4-4-2 and [low block](/tactics/low-block) of Trapattoni and Lippi with transitions through a world-class striker.

The Europa League era has rewarded coaches who treat it as a project. Emery's Sevilla and Villarreal and Simeone's Atlético played organised [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) 4-4-2s with disciplined [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps); Gian Piero Gasperini's Atalanta in 2024 won with a man-marking [back three](/tactics/back-three) and relentless verticality; Ange Postecoglou's Tottenham in 2025 abandoned his [high line](/tactics/high-line) for a pragmatic deep block to beat United in Bilbao. Emery's 2026 Villa were a mature version of his method: a 4-2-3-1 that defended in a mid block, pressed on triggers and attacked with pace through Rogers, Leon Bailey and the running of Tielemans and Buendía from midfield. The lesson of the modern Europa League is that organisation beats talent over a 15-match season.

## Latest edition

The 2025-26 Europa League was the second under the league-phase format and ended with [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa) winning the trophy for the first time since the 1982 European Cup and the club's first trophy since the 1996 League Cup. Villa, under Unai Emery, had qualified by finishing sixth in the 2024-25 [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) and built a run through the league phase and knockouts around Emiliano Martínez in goal, Boubacar Kamara and Youri Tielemans in midfield and Morgan Rogers as the attacking focal point. SC Freiburg, under Julian Schuster, were the surprise finalists, reaching their first European final through a disciplined season that overachieved relative to their [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) budget.

The final at Beşiktaş Park in Istanbul on 20 May 2026 was one-sided. Tielemans and Buendía scored in the first half, Buendía then crossed for Rogers to finish from close range just before the hour, and Villa won 3-0, controlling the match from the first minute. The result made Emery the most successful coach in the competition's history with five titles across three clubs, and his first final as a coach of a non-Spanish club. Rogers, whose performances had also made him a regular for [England](/nations/england), left for Chelsea in the summer of 2026 in a British record £117m transfer; Villa entered 2026-27 as holders with a Champions League place secured by the win. Tottenham, the 2025 winners, had moved up to the Champions League, and Manchester United, the previous year's beaten finalists, were absent from Europe.

## Next edition

The 2026-27 Europa League league phase began in September 2026 and the final will be played in Frankfurt's Deutsche Bank Park in May 2027, the third German host after Hamburg (2010) and Cologne (2020). Villa, now in the Champions League, do not defend the trophy. UEFA has already named Bucharest's National Arena for the 2028 final and Belgrade's National Stadium for 2029. The winner again earns a Champions League place for the following season.
