## History

Football was at the Olympics before there was a World Cup, and until 1930 the Games were the closest thing to a world championship. The first two editions, 1900 and 1904, were played by club sides (Upton Park of London and Galt FC of Ontario are the unlikely first gold medallists). Great Britain's amateur selections won properly in 1908 and 1912, and then in 1924 and 1928 [Uruguay](/nations/uruguay) arrived in Europe and changed everyone's idea of how football could be played, winning both tournaments with a short-passing, improvisational game that was the direct reason Uruguay hosted and won the first World Cup. The Uruguayans still count those two golds as world titles, and their shirt carries four stars.

Once the World Cup existed, the Olympics became an amateur event, which in practice meant Eastern Europe. Hungary (1952, 1964, 1968), the Soviet Union, Yugoslavia, Poland, East Germany and Czechoslovakia won every gold from 1952 to 1980 with state-sponsored "amateurs". Western Europe and South America sent students and part-timers; the 1952 Hungarian team was Puskás, Kocsis and Hidegkuti, the Mighty Magyars in all but name.

FIFA and the IOC rewrote the rules in 1984 to allow professionals who had not played in a World Cup, then in 1992 settled on the current under-23 format, with three over-age players allowed from 1996. That turned the tournament into a showcase for the next generation: [Spain](/nations/spain) with Guardiola in 1992, Nigeria's Kanu and Okocha in 1996, [Argentina](/nations/argentina) with Tevez in 2004 and [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) in 2008, [Brazil](/nations/brazil) with Neymar in 2016, and Spain again in 2024 with Fermín López and a 17-year-old [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal).

## Format

The men's tournament is for under-23 players (born within 23 years of the tournament year) with up to three over-age players per squad. Qualification runs through continental under-23 championships: the UEFA Under-21 Championship, CONMEBOL's Pre-Olympic tournament, the AFC U-23 Asian Cup, the CAF U-23 Cup of Nations, the CONCACAF Olympic qualifiers and the OFC qualifiers. The host qualifies automatically.

From 1992 to 2024 the tournament had 16 teams in four groups of four, the top two advancing to quarter-finals. For Los Angeles 2028 the IOC cut the men's field to 12 teams (three groups of four, with the top two in each group and the two best third-placed teams reaching the quarter-finals) while expanding the women's tournament to 16. Matches start before the opening ceremony because of the fixture density, and ties are settled by extra time and penalties.

## Greatest editions and moments

- **1924, Uruguay's arrival.** The Uruguayans beat Yugoslavia 7-0, the USA 3-0, France 5-1 and Switzerland 3-0 in the final, and invented the lap of honour. Europe had never seen football like it.
- **1936, Berlin.** Italy's students, coached by Vittorio Pozzo, won gold between the two World Cup titles; Peru's withdrawal after a replayed quarter-final against Austria remains a diplomatic sore.
- **1952, the Magyars.** Hungary beat Yugoslavia 2-0 in Helsinki with Puskás and Czibor scoring, the first major title of the team that would beat England 6-3 at Wembley a year later.
- **1984, Los Angeles.** France's Platini-less team won gold with Daniel Xuereb and François Brisson scoring against Brazil in front of 101,799 at the Rose Bowl, still the record Olympic football crowd.
- **1992, Barcelona.** Spain won at Camp Nou with a team containing [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), Luis Enrique, Abelardo and Kiko, whose 90th-minute winner beat Poland 3-2.
- **1996, Nigeria's Dream Team.** Jay-Jay Okocha, Kanu and Amunike beat Brazil 4-3 (from 3-1 down) in the semi-final and Argentina 3-2 in the final. Africa's first Olympic football gold.
- **2000, Cameroon on penalties.** Samuel Eto'o and Lauren came from 2-0 down against Spain in Sydney and won the shootout, the first of a run of five titles for Cameroon and Nigeria combined across continental competition.
- **2008, Messi's gold.** Argentina's Messi, Agüero, Di María, Riquelme and Mascherano beat Nigeria 1-0 in Beijing, Di María lobbing the winner from Messi's pass. Messi had to go against Barcelona's wishes to play.
- **2012, Mexico over Brazil.** Oribe Peralta scored after 29 seconds at Wembley and Mexico beat Neymar's Brazil 2-1.
- **2016, Neymar's penalty.** At the Maracanã, a year after the 7-1, Neymar scored a free-kick and then the winning penalty against Germany for Brazil's first Olympic gold.
- **2024, 5-3 in Paris.** Spain led France 3-1 at the Parc des Princes, Jean-Philippe Mateta's 90th-minute penalty forced extra time, and Sergio Camello scored twice (100' and 120'+1) for Spain's second gold.

## Records

- **Most golds:** Hungary and Great Britain 3; Uruguay, Soviet Union, Argentina, Brazil and Spain 2.
- **Most medals:** Brazil 7 (2 gold, 3 silver, 2 bronze).
- **Top scorer, single tournament:** Ferenc Bene, 12 goals (1964). Sophus Nielsen scored 10 in one match for Denmark v France in 1908.
- **Record attendance:** 101,799, Rose Bowl, 1984 final.
- **First African gold:** Nigeria 1996. **First Asian medal:** Japan bronze 1968.
- **Youngest gold medallist of the modern era:** Lamine Yamal, 17, in 2024.
- **Over-age legends:** Neymar (2016), Dani Alves (2020 as captain at 38), Jesús Navas (2020), Alexandre Lacazette (2024).

## Philosophies that won

The Olympic tournament has always rewarded teams with a coherent national style, because squads have little preparation time. Uruguay in the 1920s won with the Rioplatense short game, Hungary's 1952 side with the deep-lying centre-forward that became the [false nine](/tactics/false-nine), and the Eastern Bloc teams of the 1960s and 1970s with fitness and rigid team structure. Spain's 1992 team was the first flowering of the Cruyff-era Barcelona idea at international level; its 2024 team under Santi Denia was pure [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), with Fermín López arriving from midfield and the wingers kept wide.

Argentina in 2004 and 2008 under Marcelo Bielsa and Sergio Batista were built on intense pressing, early precursors to modern [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing), and Nigeria and Cameroon won with [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) verve and physical dominance. Brazil's two golds came from well-drilled 4-2-3-1s with Neymar or Richarlison as the focal point and a [low block](/tactics/low-block) when needed. For under-23 football the general rule is simple: the team that can keep the ball under pressure wins.

## Latest edition

Paris 2024 ran from 24 July to 9 August across seven cities. France, coached by Thierry Henry, played in front of enormous home crowds and used Alexandre Lacazette and Jean-Philippe Mateta as over-age forwards. [Morocco](/nations/morocco) were the story of the group stage, beating Argentina 2-1 in Saint-Étienne in a match halted for two hours by a pitch invasion and a VAR review that disallowed Argentina's late equaliser; Morocco went on to take bronze, beating Egypt 6-0.

Spain beat Japan 3-0 in the quarter-finals and Morocco 2-1 in the semis, Fermín López scoring in both. France beat Argentina 1-0 in a fractious quarter-final in Bordeaux and Egypt 3-1 after extra time. The final at the Parc des Princes on 9 August was the best Olympic final in living memory: Enzo Millot gave France the lead, Fermín López scored twice and Alex Baena added a free-kick before half-time, Maghnes Akliouche pulled one back and Mateta's stoppage-time penalty made it 3-3. Sergio Camello, on as a substitute, scored in the 100th minute and again with the final kick to win it 5-3. Spain became the first European men's team to win Olympic gold since their own in 1992. The United States reached the quarter-finals for the first time since 2000, losing to Morocco.

## Next edition

The Los Angeles 2028 men's tournament will have 12 teams for the first time since 1976, with matches from 12 July across seven venues in the United States (including the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, Levi's Stadium, Gillette Stadium and venues in Nashville, Columbus and Dallas) and the gold-medal match at the Rose Bowl on 28 July 2028. The [United States](/nations/united-states) qualify as hosts; the remaining 11 places come through the continental under-23 championships during 2027 and early 2028, with the UEFA Under-21 Championship in Albania and Serbia in June 2027 deciding Europe's representatives. Spain, as holders, must qualify through that route.
