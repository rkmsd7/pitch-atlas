## History

The Asian Cup is the second-oldest continental championship after the Copa América, first played in Hong Kong in 1956 with four teams and won by [South Korea](/nations/south-korea), who retained it at home in 1960 and have not won it since, the longest drought of any major football nation relative to its strength. Israel, then an AFC member, won in 1964 with [India](/nations/india) as runners-up, India's finest hour, before being expelled from the confederation in 1974. [Iran](/nations/iran) then won three in a row (1968, 1972, 1976) with Ali Parvin and Hassan Rowshan, a run nobody has matched.

The Gulf took over in the 1980s: Kuwait at home in 1980, then [Saudi Arabia](/nations/saudi-arabia) three times in four editions between 1984 and 1996, winning two finals on penalties. [Japan](/nations/japan) had never qualified before 1988; from 1992, when they won at home in Hiroshima under Hans Ooft, they became the dominant force, adding titles in 2000 (Hidetoshi Nakata, Shunsuke Nakamura), 2004 (in hostile Beijing, against China) and 2011 (Tadanari Lee's volley against Australia in Doha). Iraq's 2007 win, achieved during the civil war with a team that could not play at home, is the competition's emotional high point.

[Australia](/nations/australia) joined the AFC in 2006 and won as hosts in 2015 with James Troisi's extra-time goal against South Korea. Qatar, with a generation produced by the Aspire Academy, won in 2019 without conceding until the final and retained the trophy at home in early 2024, Akram Afif scoring three penalties against Jordan, who had reached a first final under Hussein Ammouta. The 2027 edition in Saudi Arabia will be the first of three consecutive Gulf-hosted tournaments if Saudi Arabia's World Cup preparations proceed as planned.

## Format

Since 2019 the Asian Cup has 24 teams in six groups of four, with the top two in each group and the four best third-placed teams advancing to a round of 16, then quarter-finals, semi-finals and a final; there is no third-place match. Qualifying is linked to World Cup qualifying: the teams that reach the third round of World Cup qualifying qualify directly for the Asian Cup, with a further qualifying round deciding the rest. The host qualifies automatically. The tournament runs every four years, usually in January, which has caused friction with European clubs over players such as [Son Heung-min](/players/son-heung-min) and Takehiro Tomiyasu.

## Greatest editions and moments

- **1956, Hong Kong.** South Korea beat the hosts, Israel and South Vietnam in a four-team round-robin to become the first Asian champions.
- **1964, India's silver.** Chuni Goswami's India, the Asian Games champions of 1962, finished second to hosts Israel, still India's best result.
- **1976, Tehran.** Iran's third straight title, beating Kuwait 1-0 in front of 100,000 at the Azadi; Ali Parvin scored the winner.
- **1988, Doha.** Saudi Arabia beat South Korea on penalties, Mohammed Al-Deayea and Majed Abdullah establishing the Saudi golden age.
- **1992, Japan at home.** Takuya Takagi's goal beat Saudi Arabia in Hiroshima and launched the J.League era.
- **2004, Beijing.** Japan beat China 3-1 in a final played amid anti-Japanese protests, Koji Nakata's handball goal and Keiji Tamada's late clincher.
- **2007, Iraq.** Younis Mahmoud's header beat Saudi Arabia 1-0 in Jakarta; the team had trained in Jordan and celebrations in Baghdad were marked by bombings.
- **2011, Tadanari Lee.** A left-footed volley from Yuto Nagatomo's cross in the 109th minute beat Australia; Eiji Kawashima was outstanding.
- **2015, Troisi.** Australia's James Troisi scored in extra time after Son Heung-min had equalised in stoppage time, Massimo Luongo's long-range opener the goal of the tournament.
- **2019, Qatar's clean sweep.** Seven wins from seven, 19 goals scored and one conceded, Almoez Ali's nine goals and a 3-1 win over Japan in Abu Dhabi.
- **2024, Jordan's run and Afif's three.** Jordan beat Iraq, Tajikistan and South Korea to reach a first final; Akram Afif scored three penalties (22', 73', 90+5') in a 3-1 win for Qatar at Lusail.

## Records

- **Most titles:** Japan 4; Saudi Arabia and Iran 3; South Korea and Qatar 2.
- **Most goals, all tournaments:** Ali Daei, 14 (1996-2004). **Single tournament:** Almoez Ali, 9 (2019).
- **Most appearances:** Mehdi Mahdavikia and Hassan Al-Haidos, 20-plus matches each; Al-Haidos captained Qatar to both titles.
- **Most consecutive titles:** Iran 3 (1968-1976).
- **Biggest win:** Japan 8-1 Uzbekistan (2000).
- **Host winners:** South Korea 1960, Iran 1968 and 1976, Kuwait 1980, Japan 1992, Australia 2015, Qatar 2023.
- **Record attendance:** 100,000, Azadi Stadium, 1976.
- **Only champion to win every match without conceding before the final:** Qatar 2019.

## Philosophies that won

Asian football's champions have mirrored the coaching imports of their eras. Iran's 1970s sides played a muscular, direct 4-3-3 built on [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and the finishing of Parvin and Rowshan. Saudi Arabia's 1980s and 1990s teams, under Brazilian coaches, were technical 4-4-2s with quick wide players and deep-lying playmakers. Japan's four titles came from the Dutch and Japanese coaching lineage, a short-passing, high-tempo game that was [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) before Spain made it famous, through Ooft, Philippe Troussier's flat [back three](/tactics/back-three) in 2000 and Alberto Zaccheroni's 2011 side with Keisuke Honda as a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine).

Iraq's 2007 team under Jorvan Vieira were pure [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) resilience. Qatar's two titles under Félix Sánchez and Tintín Márquez came from the Aspire Academy's Barcelona-influenced curriculum: a 3-5-2 in 2019 that pressed in a block and released Afif and Almoez Ali, and a more cautious 5-3-2 in 2024 that controlled matches through Hassan Al-Haidos and won on moments. Australia in 2015 under Ange Postecoglou were the most aggressive champions, a full-throttle 4-3-3 with inverted wingers and a [high line](/tactics/high-line) that Postecoglou took to Celtic and Tottenham.

## Latest edition

The 2023 Asian Cup, delayed to 12 January to 10 February 2024 after China withdrew as host, was played in the same Qatari stadiums used for the 2022 World Cup. The holders beat Lebanon 3-0 in the opening match at Lusail and won all seven games, though three of their knockout ties were tight: Palestine were beaten 2-1 in the round of 16, Uzbekistan on penalties after a 1-1 draw in the quarter-final, and Iran 3-2 in a semi-final settled by Almoez Ali's goal in the 82nd minute.

The tournament's story was Jordan. Under Moroccan coach Hussein Ammouta they drew with South Korea in the group, beat Iraq 3-2 with two stoppage-time goals in the round of 16, beat Tajikistan 1-0 and then shocked a South Korean side containing Son Heung-min, Kim Min-jae and Lee Kang-in 2-0 in the semi-final, Yazan Al-Naimat and Mousa Al-Tamari scoring. Jürgen Klinsmann was sacked by South Korea within days, with reports of a training-ground altercation between Son and Lee. Japan, pre-tournament favourites, lost to Iraq in the group and to Iran in the quarter-finals; Australia went out to South Korea in extra time.

The final at Lusail on 10 February was decided from the penalty spot. Afif converted in the 22nd minute, Yazan Al-Naimat equalised with a lob in the 67th, and Afif scored two more penalties in the 73rd and 95th minutes to complete the first hat-trick in an Asian Cup final. He was named player of the tournament and shared the Golden Boot with eight goals; Meshaal Barsham won the goalkeeper award. Qatar became the first team to retain the title since Japan in 2004.

## Next edition

The 2027 AFC Asian Cup will be held in Saudi Arabia in January and February 2027, the kingdom's first time hosting and a dress rehearsal for the 2034 World Cup. Matches are expected in Riyadh, Jeddah and other cities. Qualification was completed through World Cup qualifying and a third round of Asian Cup qualifying during 2025 and 2026; the hosts, Japan, South Korea, Iran, Australia and Qatar are among the teams in the 24-nation field. Japan, who reached the 2026 World Cup knockouts, will be favourites to win a fifth title, while Saudi Arabia under Hervé Renard aim to win at home for the first time since 1996.
