## History

For four decades the world's club champion was decided by the Intercontinental Cup, a straight match (two legs until 1979, then a single game in Tokyo) between the winners of the European Cup and the [Copa Libertadores](/tournaments/copa-libertadores). FIFA's first attempt at a broader tournament was the 2000 Club World Championship in Brazil, won by Corinthians on penalties against Vasco da Gama at the Maracanã; the collapse of FIFA's marketing partner ISL killed the planned 2001 edition, and the competition returned only in 2005 in Japan, absorbing the Intercontinental Cup.

From 2005 to 2023 the Club World Cup was a December event for six or seven continental champions plus the host nation's champion, usually in Japan, the UAE, Morocco or Qatar. South American clubs won the first two (São Paulo over [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool), Internacional over [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona)), and Corinthians beat [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in 2012, but European champions won every other edition and the tournament was widely treated in Europe as a mid-season inconvenience. [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid)'s five titles between 2014 and 2022, [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo)'s hat-trick against Kashima in 2016 and [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola)'s [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) completing a quintuple in Jeddah in December 2023 were the highlights.

FIFA president Gianni Infantino's expanded tournament, 32 teams every four years in the summer, was first announced for China in 2021, cancelled by the pandemic and finally staged in the United States in June and July 2025 as a dress rehearsal for the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup). It was contentious (player unions sued over the calendar, some stadiums were half-empty in the heat, and the $1bn prize fund was criticised as distorting), but it produced a genuine tournament, with Brazilian clubs [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo), [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras), [Botafogo](/clubs/botafogo) and Fluminense all reaching the knockouts, [Al Hilal](/clubs/al-hilal) beating Manchester City, and Chelsea beating European champions [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) 3-0 in the final. A separate annual FIFA Intercontinental Cup, pitting the Champions League winner against the other continental champions, now fills the December slot.

## Format

The expanded Club World Cup has 32 clubs: 12 from UEFA, six from CONMEBOL, four each from AFC, CAF and CONCACAF, one from OFC and one from the host country. Places are earned by winning a continental championship in the preceding four-year cycle, with the remaining slots filled by a confederation ranking; a two-clubs-per-country cap applies except where a country has more than two continental champions. The tournament runs over four weeks in June and July with eight groups of four, the top two advancing to a round of 16, then quarter-finals, semi-finals and a final; there is no third-place match. Matches level after 90 minutes in the knockouts go to extra time and penalties. The 2025 edition awarded $1bn in prize money, with Chelsea earning well over $100m.

## Greatest editions and moments

- **2000, Corinthians at the Maracanã.** Edilson's dribbling and a 4-3 shootout over city rivals Vasco in front of 73,000, the only edition before 2025 held in South America.
- **2005, São Paulo over Liverpool.** Mineiro's goal and Rogério Ceni's goalkeeping beat the Champions League winners 1-0 in Yokohama.
- **2006, Internacional.** Adriano Gabiru's 82nd-minute goal beat Ronaldinho's Barcelona 1-0 in Yokohama.
- **2009, Messi's chest.** [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) chested in the 110th-minute winner against Estudiantes in Abu Dhabi, Barcelona's sixth trophy of 2009.
- **2010, TP Mazembe.** The Congolese side beat Internacional 2-0 in the semi-final, the first non-European, non-South American finalist.
- **2011, Barcelona 4-0 Santos.** Messi twice and Xavi against Neymar's Santos, one of the finest 90 minutes of the Guardiola era.
- **2012, Corinthians again.** Paolo Guerrero's header beat Chelsea 1-0 in Yokohama, with 30,000 Corinthians fans having travelled to Japan.
- **2016, Ronaldo's hat-trick.** Kashima Antlers led Real Madrid 2-1 in Yokohama before Ronaldo scored three, two in extra time.
- **2019, Liverpool in Doha.** Roberto Firmino's extra-time winner against Flamengo gave Liverpool a first world title after 2005 and 1981 defeats.
- **2022, Real 5-3 Al Hilal.** Vinícius Júnior and Valverde scored twice each in Rabat in the highest-scoring final.
- **2025, Al Hilal 4-3 Manchester City.** Marcos Leonardo's extra-time winner in Orlando knocked out City in the round of 16, the shock of the first expanded tournament.
- **2025, Palmer's final.** Cole Palmer scored twice and set up João Pedro inside 43 minutes as Chelsea beat PSG 3-0 at MetLife; João Neves was sent off for pulling Cucurella's hair.

## Records

- **Most titles:** Real Madrid 5 (2014, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2022); Barcelona 3; Corinthians, Bayern Munich and Chelsea 2.
- **Including the Intercontinental Cup:** Real Madrid 8; AC Milan and Boca Juniors 4; Nacional, Peñarol, Bayern, Inter and Juventus among those with multiple titles.
- **Most goals, all editions:** Cristiano Ronaldo 7; Messi, Luis Suárez and Gareth Bale 5.
- **Most appearances in finals:** Ronaldo and Toni Kroos among those with four winners' medals; Kroos, Modrić and Carvajal have five.
- **Biggest final win:** Barcelona 4-0 Santos (2011), Manchester City 4-0 Fluminense (2023).
- **Record attendance:** 81,118 at MetLife Stadium for the 2025 final; 73,000 at the Maracanã in 2000.
- **Most European titles in a row:** Real Madrid 3 (2016-18).
- **First non-European or South American finalist:** TP Mazembe (2010); Raja Casablanca (2013), Kashima (2016) and Al Ain (2018) also reached finals.

## Philosophies that won

Before 2025 the Club World Cup rewarded whoever had won the Champions League six months earlier, and so its tactical history is a compressed version of Europe's: Guardiola's Barcelona with [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine), Ancelotti's and Zidane's Real Madrid with pragmatic control and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Ronaldo and Bale, [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) Liverpool, Guardiola's City with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs). The South American wins of 2005, 2006 and 2012 came from a [low block](/tactics/low-block), a great goalkeeper and a single clinical chance, the classic Libertadores recipe.

The 2025 tournament, played in summer heat with thunderstorm delays, favoured depth and energy over pure possession. Enzo Maresca's Chelsea were a hybrid: possession-based build-up with a [box midfield](/tactics/box-midfield) of Caicedo, Enzo Fernández, Palmer and Reece James inverting, aggressive counter-pressing and the ability to sit deep and break, as they did against PSG. [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique)'s PSG, who had thrashed Real Madrid 4-0 in the semi-final and Inter Miami 4-0, played the most complete football in the tournament before being out-thought in the final. Simone Inzaghi's Al Hilal and Filipe Luís's Flamengo showed that well-organised sides with transition threat could trouble Europe's best. For 2029 the question is whether European clubs treat it as a full priority.

## Latest edition

The first 32-team Club World Cup ran from 14 June to 13 July 2025 across 12 stadiums in 11 US cities. The group stage saw Botafogo beat PSG 1-0, Flamengo beat Chelsea 3-1 and Inter Miami, with Messi's free-kick, beat Porto, the first non-European side to beat a European team in the competition's history on US soil; Atlético Madrid and Porto went out at the group stage. In the round of 16 Al Hilal beat Manchester City 4-3 after extra time in Orlando, PSG beat Inter Miami 4-0 and Real Madrid, under new coach [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso), beat [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) 1-0. Fluminense, with 40-year-old Thiago Silva, beat Inter 2-0 and then Al Hilal 2-1 to reach the semi-finals, where they lost 2-0 to Chelsea (João Pedro scoring twice against his former club). PSG beat Bayern 2-0 in a quarter-final that saw Jamal Musiala suffer a serious leg injury, then Real Madrid 4-0 in the semi-final with two goals from Fabián Ruiz.

The final at MetLife Stadium on 13 July, with Donald Trump and Gianni Infantino presenting the trophy, was expected to be a PSG coronation. Instead Chelsea led 3-0 by half-time: Cole Palmer finished low into the corner in the 22nd minute, repeated the finish from almost the same spot in the 30th, and then slipped in João Pedro, signed three weeks earlier from Brighton, to lift a finish over Donnarumma in the 43rd. PSG's frustration ended with João Neves sent off in the 85th minute for pulling Marc Cucurella's hair, and a post-match scuffle involving Luis Enrique and João Pedro. Palmer was named player of the tournament, Chelsea collected a second Club World Cup after 2022 and more than $100m in prize money, and the attendance of 81,118 was the tournament's largest. The tournament drew around 2.5 million spectators in total, though several group matches were played in front of sparse crowds.

## Next edition

The next Club World Cup is scheduled for June and July 2029 with 32 teams. FIFA had not confirmed the host as of November 2026; bids and expressions of interest were reported from Brazil, Spain, Morocco, Qatar and the United States, and FIFA's broader calendar discussions, including a possible expansion to 48 clubs, were ongoing. Qualification runs across the 2025-2028 continental cycles: PSG (2025 and 2026 Champions League), Flamengo (2025 Libertadores), [Al Ahli](/clubs/al-ahli) (2025 AFC Champions League Elite) and Pyramids (2025 CAF Champions League) are among the clubs who have already earned places. Chelsea will defend the title.
