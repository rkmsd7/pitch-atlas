## History

Henri Delaunay, the French federation secretary, proposed a European championship in 1927; it took until 1958 for UEFA to organise it and 1960 for the first four-team finals in France, which the Soviet Union won with Lev Yashin in goal. The trophy still bears Delaunay's name. The early editions were short, four-team affairs tacked on to a two-year qualifying knockout; [Spain](/nations/spain) won at home in 1964, [Italy](/nations/italy) needed a replay in 1968 and West Germany's 1972 side of Beckenbauer, Netzer and Gerd Müller is still rated among the best national teams ever.

The finals grew to eight teams in 1980, when Italy hosted and West Germany won a second title, and the 1984 tournament in France produced Michel Platini's nine goals in five matches. The [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) of Gullit, Rijkaard and Van Basten won in 1988, [Denmark](/nations/denmark) came off the beach to win in 1992 after Yugoslavia's exclusion, and the 1996 expansion to 16 teams in [England](/nations/england) gave the competition its modern shape, "Football's Coming Home" and Germany's golden-goal win over the Czechs.

The 2000s belonged to surprise and then to Spain. Greece's 2004 win in Portugal under Otto Rehhagel remains the greatest shock in international football; Spain's 2008 and 2012 titles, bookending the 2010 World Cup, are the only back-to-back Euros. [Portugal](/nations/portugal) beat hosts [France](/nations/france) in 2016 with Éder's extra-time goal, the field having grown to 24 teams. The delayed pan-European Euro 2020 ended at Wembley with Italy beating England on penalties, and Euro 2024 in [Germany](/nations/germany) ended with Spain beating England 2-1 in Berlin for a record fourth title.

## Format

Since 2016 the finals have 24 teams in six groups of four. The top two in each group and the four best third-placed teams go to a round of 16, followed by quarter-finals, semi-finals and a final; there is no third-place match. Qualifying involves all 55 UEFA members in groups over the preceding 18 months, with three further places decided through play-offs seeded by the [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league). Hosts qualify automatically. Knockout ties go to extra time and penalties, the golden goal having been used only in 1996 and 2000 and the silver goal in 2004.

## Greatest editions and moments

- **1960, Yashin's tournament.** The Soviet Union beat Yugoslavia 2-1 in extra time at the Parc des Princes, Viktor Ponedelnik scoring in the 113th minute, in front of fewer than 18,000.
- **1972, the perfect Germany.** Netzer and Beckenbauer's side beat England 3-1 at Wembley in the quarter-final and the USSR 3-0 in the final with Gerd Müller scoring twice.
- **1976, Panenka.** Antonín Panenka's chipped penalty beat Sepp Maier and West Germany in Belgrade, the first major final decided by a shootout and the origin of a word.
- **1984, Platini.** Nine goals in five games, including hat-tricks against Belgium and Yugoslavia and the free-kick that squirmed through Luis Arconada in the final.
- **1988, Van Basten.** The volley from an impossible angle against the USSR in Munich, after a winner against West Germany in the semi-final, in the Netherlands' only major title.
- **1992, Denmark.** Called up 10 days before the tournament after Yugoslavia's expulsion, they beat the Netherlands on penalties and Germany 2-0 in the final with Peter Schmeichel immense.
- **1996, Gascoigne and golden goal.** Gazza's goal against Scotland, England's 4-1 over the Netherlands, Southgate's missed penalty and Oliver Bierhoff's golden goal against the Czech Republic.
- **2000, Trezeguet.** Sylvain Wiltord equalised in the 94th minute and David Trezeguet's golden goal beat Italy in Rotterdam for France's double with the 1998 World Cup. The best Euro ever played.
- **2004, Greece.** A 1-0 win over hosts Portugal in Lisbon, Angelos Charisteas' header, after beating France and the Czech Republic by the same score.
- **2012, Spain 4-0 Italy.** The most emphatic final in the tournament's history, with Xavi and Iniesta dominating and Jordi Alba running 60 yards to score.
- **2016, Portugal without Ronaldo.** [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) was carried off in the 25th minute and Éder scored in the 109th; Iceland's thunderclap and their win over England were the tournament's other story.
- **2024, Lamine Yamal.** A 16-year-old's curling goal against France in the semi-final, and Nico Williams and Mikel Oyarzabal finishing England in the final.

## Records

- **Most titles:** Spain 4 (1964, 2008, 2012, 2024); Germany 3; Italy and France 2.
- **Most goals, all tournaments:** Cristiano Ronaldo 14 (2004-2024). **Most in one tournament:** Michel Platini 9 (1984).
- **Most appearances:** Ronaldo, 30 matches at six tournaments, the only player to appear in six Euros.
- **Youngest scorer:** [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal), 16 years 362 days (2024 semi-final v France).
- **Oldest scorer:** Ivica Vastić, 38 (2008). **Oldest player:** Pepe, 41 (2024).
- **Biggest win:** Netherlands 6-1 Yugoslavia (2000). **Biggest final margin:** Spain 4-0 Italy (2012).
- **Only team to retain the title:** Spain (2008, 2012). **Only host winners:** Spain 1964, Italy 1968, France 1984.
- **Highest-scoring tournament:** 2024, 117 goals in 51 matches.

## Philosophies that won

The Euros have often been where a tactical idea announced itself. The Soviet Union's 1960 win was a product of Gavriil Kachalin's fitness-first collectivism; West Germany in 1972 played a fluid, attacking game with Beckenbauer as a sweeper stepping into midfield, a forerunner of the modern [back three](/tactics/back-three) with a libero. Czechoslovakia in 1976 and Greece in 2004 were disciplined [low block](/tactics/low-block) teams who won through [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and transitions; Rehhagel's Greece used man-marking two decades after everyone else had stopped.

The Netherlands in 1988 were the last flowering of [Total Football](/tactics/total-football) under Rinus Michels. France in 2000 combined Zidane's genius with Deschamps and Vieira's power in a 4-2-3-1 that became the dominant formation of the decade. Spain in 2008 and 2012 under Luis Aragonés and Vicente del Bosque played [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka), with Cesc Fàbregas as a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine) in the 2012 final. Portugal in 2016 and Italy in 2021 won with pragmatic [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) 4-3-3s that could defend deep; Roberto Mancini's Italy also pressed high in a modern way. Spain in 2024 under Luis de la Fuente were a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) side with a twist: two genuine wingers (Yamal and Williams) staying wide and attacking one-on-one, [Rodri](/players/rodri) as the single pivot, and more verticality than any previous Spanish team.

## Latest edition

Euro 2024 in Germany, 14 June to 14 July, was played across 10 cities and was the most-attended edition since 2016, with 2.68 million spectators. Germany opened with a 5-1 win over Scotland and Toni Kroos, coming out of international retirement, ran the hosts' midfield until a quarter-final defeat to Spain in Stuttgart, where Mikel Merino's 119th-minute header settled an outstanding match marred by Marc Cucurella's unpunished handball.

Spain won all seven games, the first team to do so at a Euro, beating Croatia 3-0, Italy 1-0 and Albania 1-0 in the group, Georgia 4-1 in the round of 16, Germany 2-1 and then France 2-1 in the Munich semi-final, where Lamine Yamal's curling equaliser from 25 yards at 16 years and 362 days became the goal of the tournament and Dani Olmo scored the winner. England, under Gareth Southgate, scraped through: Jude Bellingham's 95th-minute overhead kick against Slovakia, a shootout win over Switzerland, and Ollie Watkins' 90th-minute winner against the Netherlands in Dortmund.

The final in Berlin's Olympiastadion on 14 July turned after Rodri was injured at half-time. Nico Williams scored from Yamal's cross 47 seconds into the second half, Cole Palmer equalised with a low shot in the 73rd, and Oyarzabal slid in Cucurella's cross with four minutes left. Olmo cleared Marc Guéhi's header off the line in stoppage time. Rodri was named player of the tournament, Yamal young player, and six players shared the Golden Boot on three goals. Southgate resigned two days later; Thomas Tuchel replaced him. Georgia, at their first tournament, beat Portugal and reached the round of 16; Austria topped a group containing France and the Netherlands.

## Next edition

Euro 2028 will be hosted by the United Kingdom and the Republic of Ireland from 9 June to 9 July 2028, across Wembley (the final), the Principality Stadium in Cardiff, Hampden Park, the Aviva Stadium in Dublin, Casement Park in Belfast, Everton's Hill Dickinson Stadium, Etihad Stadium, St James' Park and Villa Park. Qualifying for the 24-team finals begins in March 2027, after the 2026-27 Nations League group stage; the five host associations must qualify, with two reserved places available should they fail. Spain, world and European champions under Luis de la Fuente, will be favourites to become the first team to win three consecutive major tournaments since their own run of 2008-2012. Euro 2032 goes to Italy and Turkey.
