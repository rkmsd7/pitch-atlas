## History

UEFA launched the Women's Cup in 2001-02 with 33 clubs, and the first decade belonged to Germany and Sweden. Frankfurt won the inaugural final at home and took four titles in all (2002, 2006, 2008, 2015), Umeå won twice with a teenage Marta scoring in the 2004 final, Turbine Potsdam twice and Duisburg once. [Arsenal Women](/clubs/arsenal-women), with Kelly Smith, Alex Scott and Vic Akers as manager, became the only British winners in 2007 with a 1-0 aggregate win over Umeå as part of a quadruple.

The rebrand to the Women's Champions League in 2009-10, with a single-match final in the same city as the men's, coincided with the arrival of [Lyon Women](/clubs/lyon-women). Jean-Michel Aulas's investment produced the most dominant club in the history of either game: eight titles between 2011 and 2022, including five in a row from 2016 to 2020 with Wendie Renard, Amandine Henry, Eugénie Le Sommer and Ada Hegerberg, and finals won against PSG on penalties in Cardiff, [Wolfsburg Women](/clubs/wolfsburg-women) 4-1 in Kyiv and [Barcelona Women](/clubs/barcelona-women) 4-1 in Budapest in 2019. Wolfsburg (2013, 2014) and Frankfurt (2015) were the only interruptions.

Barcelona's rise, built on La Masia's [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and the Ballon d'Or-winning midfield of Alexia Putellas and Aitana Bonmatí, began with a 4-0 win over [Chelsea Women](/clubs/chelsea-women) in Gothenburg in 2021 and continued with comebacks against Wolfsburg in 2023 (from 2-0 down) and a 2-0 win over Lyon in Bilbao in 2024. Arsenal's 1-0 upset of Barcelona in Lisbon in 2025, Stina Blackstenius scoring, briefly broke the sequence; Barcelona answered with a 4-0 demolition of the rebranded OL Lyonnes in Oslo in May 2026 for a fourth title.

## Format

Since 2025-26 the Women's Champions League has an 18-team league phase replacing the 16-team group stage: each club plays six matches against six different opponents, three at home and three away. The top four go directly to the quarter-finals; teams finishing fifth to 12th play two-legged knockout play-offs for the other four places. Quarter-finals and semi-finals are two-legged and the final is a single match at a neutral venue. Clubs qualify through their domestic leagues (the top leagues such as [Liga F](/leagues/liga-f), the [WSL](/leagues/wsl), the [Frauen-Bundesliga](/leagues/frauen-bundesliga) and [D1 Féminine](/leagues/d1-feminine) send up to three) and through two rounds of qualifying for champions of smaller associations. A second-tier Women's Europa Cup launched in 2025-26 for clubs eliminated in qualifying.

## Greatest editions and moments

- **2002, Frankfurt at home.** Steffi Jones and Birgit Prinz's club beat Umeå 2-0 in the Waldstadion in front of 12,000.
- **2004, Marta at 18.** Umeå's 8-0 aggregate over Frankfurt included three goals from the Brazilian in the first leg.
- **2007, Arsenal's quadruple.** Alex Scott's goal in Umeå in the first leg and a 0-0 at Boreham Wood, with Kelly Smith and Jayne Ludlow in the side.
- **2010, Getafe.** Turbine Potsdam beat Lyon 7-6 on penalties after a goalless draw in the first final under the Champions League name.
- **2012, Lyon at the Olympiastadion.** Wendie Renard and Camille Abily scored in a 2-0 win over Frankfurt in Munich, in front of 50,212.
- **2014, Wolfsburg 4-3 Tyresö.** Marta's Tyresö led 2-0 and 3-2; Martina Müller and Alex Popp completed the comeback in Lisbon.
- **2018, Hegerberg in Kyiv.** Lyon 4-1 Wolfsburg after extra time, Hegerberg's hat-trick in a year she became the first women's Ballon d'Or winner.
- **2019, Lyon 4-1 Barcelona.** Hegerberg's hat-trick inside 30 minutes in Budapest, Lyon's fourth consecutive title.
- **2021, Barcelona 4-0 Chelsea.** 1-0 up inside a minute through Melanie Leupolz's own goal, 4-0 by the 36th minute, Barcelona's first.
- **2022, Lyon's revenge.** Henry, Hegerberg and Macario scored in the first 33 minutes in Turin to beat Barcelona 3-1.
- **2023, Eindhoven comeback.** Barcelona trailed Wolfsburg 2-0 at half-time; Patri Guijarro scored twice in five minutes and Fridolina Rolfö won it.
- **2025, Blackstenius in Lisbon.** Arsenal's substitute striker scored in the 74th minute to beat the holders; Renée Slegers' first season in charge.
- **2026, Oslo.** Ewa Pajor and Salma Paralluelo scored twice each in the second half as Barcelona beat OL Lyonnes 4-0.

## Records

- **Most titles:** Lyon 8 (2011, 2012, 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2022); Barcelona and Frankfurt 4; Umeå, Turbine Potsdam, Wolfsburg and Arsenal 2.
- **Most consecutive titles:** Lyon 5 (2016-20).
- **Most goals, all-time:** Ada Hegerberg, 60-plus, the only player past 50; Anja Mittag held the record before her.
- **Most goals in a single season:** Ewa Pajor, 15 (2022-23, for Wolfsburg).
- **Most finals as a player:** Wendie Renard, 10 with Lyon.
- **Biggest final win:** Barcelona 4-0 Chelsea (2021), Lyon 4-1 (2018, 2019), Barcelona 4-0 OL Lyonnes (2026).
- **Record attendance:** 91,648 at Camp Nou for Barcelona v Wolfsburg (2022 semi-final), a world record for a women's match.
- **Ballon d'Or winners in finals:** Hegerberg (2018), Putellas (2021, 2022), Bonmatí (2023, 2024, 2025), Putellas again in 2026.

## Philosophies that won

The German and Swedish winners of the 2000s played a physical, organised 4-4-2 with strong wingers and dominance in the air, the [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) model of their national teams. Lyon's dynasty was built on a possession-based 4-3-3 with the best individual players in the world in every position, a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and the ability to win [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) through Renard. Wolfsburg's two titles and later finals came from a compact, transition-based game with Alexandra Popp as a target and a disciplined [low block](/tactics/low-block).

Barcelona changed the competition's grammar. Under Lluís Cortés, Jonatan Giráldez and now Pere Romeu, they play the Guardiola-era [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) model: a 4-3-3 with Guijarro as the pivot, Putellas and Bonmatí as interiors, [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) and relentless counter-pressing. Arsenal under Renée Slegers beat them in 2025 with a more pragmatic version, a mid block and quick transitions through Blackstenius and Alessia Russo, while Chelsea under Sonia Bompastor pressed aggressively from a 4-2-3-1. OL Lyonnes under Jonatan Giráldez, Barcelona's former coach, reached the 2026 final playing a possession game but were overwhelmed in the second half by Barcelona's speed.

## Latest edition

The 2025-26 Women's Champions League was the first with the 18-team league phase, and it ended with Barcelona beating OL Lyonnes 4-0 at the Ullevaal Stadion in Oslo on 23 May 2026 for a fourth title. Barcelona, who had lost the 2025 final to Arsenal, came through the league phase and the two-legged knockouts to reach a sixth final in seven seasons; Arsenal, the holders, did not reach the final. Lyon, rebranded OL Lyonnes under new owner Michele Kang, were coached by Jonatan Giráldez, who had led Barcelona to the 2023 and 2024 titles, and had Lindsey Heaps, Melchie Dumornay and Tabitha Chawinga.

The final was tighter than the scoreline for an hour: Lyonnes had a goal ruled out for offside in the first half and matched Barcelona for possession before the break. After half-time Ewa Pajor, the Polish striker signed from Wolfsburg in 2024, scored twice, and Salma Paralluelo, back from the knee problems that had limited her 2024-25, added two more. Barcelona drew level with Frankfurt on four titles, second only to Lyon's eight, and Alexia Putellas's season ended with her third Ballon d'Or in October 2026. Separately, Arsenal won the inaugural 2026 FIFA Women's Champions Cup, the new intercontinental club competition.

## Next edition

The 2026-27 Women's Champions League league phase began in October 2026 with 18 teams. Barcelona defend the trophy; OL Lyonnes, Arsenal, Chelsea (with [Manchester City Women](/clubs/manchester-city-women) representing the WSL as 2025-26 champions), [Bayern Munich Women](/clubs/bayern-munich-women) and Wolfsburg are the main challengers. The 2027 final venue had not been confirmed by UEFA as of November 2026; UEFA has previously stated an intention to continue pairing the final with a city capable of a 20,000-plus attendance.
