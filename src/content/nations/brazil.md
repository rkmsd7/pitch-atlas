## History

Brazil's first international was a 2-0 win over Exeter City in 1914, and for forty years the national team promised more than it delivered. The 1950 World Cup at home ended with the Maracanazo, Uruguay winning 2-1 in front of around 200,000 people, a wound that still shapes the national psyche.

Everything changed in Sweden in 1958. A 17-year-old [Pelé](/players/pele) scored twice in the final, Garrincha bewitched full-backs and Brazil won the first of five [World Cups](/tournaments/world-cup). They retained it in 1962 and then produced, in Mexico in 1970, the side most often called the greatest ever, Carlos Alberto's fourth goal against Italy the signature moment of jogo bonito.

The 1982 team of Zico, Sócrates and Falcão lost to Italy in one of the most beloved defeats in history. Brazil went twenty-four years without a title before Carlos Alberto Parreira's pragmatic 1994 side beat Italy on penalties, and Luiz Felipe Scolari's 2002 team, with [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) scoring eight, won the fifth. Since then the story has been frustration: quarter-final exits in 2006 and 2010, the 7-1 humiliation by Germany in Belo Horizonte in 2014, quarter-finals again in 2018 and 2022.

The 2026 cycle was chaotic. Dorival Júnior was sacked after a 4-1 defeat in Argentina in March 2025, and in May 2025 the CBF appointed Carlo Ancelotti, the first foreign coach of the Seleção. Brazil drew 1-1 with Morocco in the group stage, beat Japan 2-1 in the round of 32, and then went out before the final four; their precise exit round could not be verified at the time of writing.

## Philosophy and style

The Brazilian way is the dribbler, the improviser and the attacking full-back. Cafu and Roberto Carlos defined the role for a generation and [Vinícius Júnior](/players/vinicius-junior) and Raphinha carry the wing tradition today. Ancelotti has imposed structure: a 4-2-3-1 with Casemiro or Bruno Guimarães protecting the back four, a compact mid-block and quick transitions into the wide players. It is closer to [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) than to the carnival football of 1970, but the final-third freedom remains. Brazil are also pioneers of the [back three](/tactics/back-three) in the 1950s and of the 4-2-4 that became the global standard.

Brazil's club structure and the [Brasileirão](/leagues/brasileirao) have revived in the 2020s, with [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo), [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) and [Botafogo](/clubs/botafogo) winning the Copa Libertadores and selling teenagers to Europe for record fees. Estêvão's move to Chelsea, Endrick's to Real Madrid and Vitor Roque's to Barcelona were all agreed before the players turned 18. The national team benefits from the talent but suffers from a dressing room that is assembled only in international windows, which is why the CBF turned to Ancelotti, who had coached Vinícius, Rodrygo, Militão and Casemiro at Madrid.

The rivalry with Argentina, the Superclásico de las Américas, has swung the visitors' way since 2021, and the humiliation of 2014 still haunts every match against Germany. Ancelotti's mandate is to restore calm: a settled XI, fewer squad changes and a 4-2-3-1 that gives the forwards freedom without exposing the full-backs. The sixth star is the only measure the public accepts.

## Managers who shaped them

- Vicente Feola: the 1958 champions.
- Mário Zagallo: 1970 as coach after winning 1958 and 1962 as a player.
- Telê Santana: the 1982 and 1986 teams that lost beautifully.
- Carlos Alberto Parreira: 1994.
- Luiz Felipe Scolari: 2002, and the 2014 collapse.
- [Tite](/managers/tite): 2016-2022, Copa América 2019.
- [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti): from May 2025, the first foreign coach.

## Current squad and setup

Ancelotti remains in charge in November 2026 with a contract through the 2026 cycle and a fresh brief to prepare for 2030. Alisson keeps goal behind Marquinhos, the captain, and Gabriel Magalhães. Bruno Guimarães runs midfield, Vinícius Júnior, Raphinha and Rodrygo supply the attack and the teenager Estêvão is the most hyped prospect since Neymar. Next up: Copa América 2028 and the 2028 Olympics, where Brazil will defend a proud under-23 record.

## Legends

- [Pelé](/players/pele): three World Cups, over 1,000 career goals.
- [Garrincha](/players/garrincha): the dribbler who won 1962 almost alone.
- [Zico](/players/zico): the white Pelé and the heart of 1982.
- [Romário](/players/romario): the 1994 Golden Ball.
- [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario): 15 World Cup goals, the 2002 redemption.
- [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho): the 2002 free-kick and the 2005 Ballon d'Or.
- [Cafu](/players/cafu): the only man to play in three World Cup finals.
- [Sócrates](/players/socrates): philosopher, doctor and 1982 captain.
