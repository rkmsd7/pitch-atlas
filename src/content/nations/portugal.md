## History

Portugal's first international was a 1921 defeat to Spain, and the national team spent four decades in the shadows before the [Eusébio](/players/eusebio) generation of 1966. Built around the Benfica side that had won two European Cups, Portugal finished third at their first [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), Eusébio scoring nine goals, including four in the comeback from 3-0 down against North Korea.

Then came a long silence: no World Cup until 1986 and a scandal-ridden exit there. The golden generation of Luís Figo, Rui Costa and Fernando Couto, world youth champions in 1989 and 1991, reached the [Euro 2000](/tournaments/euro) semi-final and the 2004 final at home, losing to Greece in one of the great upsets. The 2006 World Cup brought a fourth-place finish under Luiz Felipe Scolari.

[Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) arrived in 2003 and has defined the team ever since. Fernando Santos' pragmatic side won Euro 2016 in Paris despite Ronaldo limping off in the final, Éder scoring the extra-time winner against France, and the first Nations League followed in 2019. Roberto Martínez took over in 2023, reached the Euro 2024 quarter-final and won the 2025 Nations League on penalties against Spain in Munich. At the 2026 World Cup Portugal beat Croatia 2-1 in the round of 32 before losing to eventual champions Spain in the round of 16. Martínez departed and Jorge Jesus, a serial winner at [Benfica](/clubs/benfica), Flamengo and [Al-Hilal](/clubs/al-hilal), was appointed; four straight Nations League wins in the autumn lifted Portugal to fifth in the October 2026 FIFA ranking.

## Philosophy and style

Portugal's football culture prizes the dribbler and the number 10: Futre, Figo, Rui Costa, Quaresma, Ronaldo. The modern team adds the midfield control of [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain)'s Vitinha and João Neves, so possession phases look like [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with [Bruno Fernandes](/players/bruno-fernandes) as the creator between the lines. Jesus prefers a 4-4-2 or 4-2-3-1 that defends in a mid-block and attacks with width from Rafael Leão and Nuno Mendes, letting Ronaldo or Gonçalo Ramos finish. The team is also strong at [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), with Rúben Dias and Ronaldo as aerial threats.

Portugal's success since 2016 reflects a production line as strong as any in Europe. [Benfica](/clubs/benfica), [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) and [Porto](/clubs/porto) sell a generation of players every two years, from Figo and Rui Costa to João Félix, Nuno Mendes, Vitinha, João Neves and Viktor Gyökeres, and the national team is now drawn almost entirely from the Premier League, La Liga, Serie A and Ligue 1. The Portuguese coaching school, which produced Mourinho, Villas-Boas, Rúben Amorim and Jesus himself, adds a tactical sophistication the 1990s teams lacked.

The rivalries are Spain, the Iberian derby, now sharpened by the 2025 Nations League final and the 2026 World Cup round of 16; France, the Euro 2016 final and Euro 2020 and 2024 meetings; and Brazil, the shared language and the 2010 and 2022 World Cup matches. Jesus's brief is to manage the end of the Ronaldo era, find a successor to Pepe at the heart of the defence and arrive at the 2030 World Cup, co-hosted at home, as genuine contenders.

## Managers who shaped them

- Otto Glória: the 1966 campaign and third place.
- Carlos Queiroz: coach of the 1989 and 1991 world youth champions.
- Luiz Felipe Scolari: Euro 2004 final and fourth place at the 2006 World Cup.
- Fernando Santos: Euro 2016 and the 2019 Nations League.
- Roberto Martínez: the 2025 Nations League.
- [Jorge Jesus](/managers/jorge-jesus): appointed after the 2026 World Cup.

## Current squad and setup

Jesus has largely kept the 4-2-3-1. Diogo Costa is in goal, Rúben Dias and Nuno Mendes anchor the defence, Vitinha and João Neves pair in midfield, Bruno Fernandes plays as the 10 with Bernardo Silva and Rafael Leão wide, and Ronaldo, 41 and still captain at [Al-Nassr](/clubs/al-nassr), shares the striker role with Gonçalo Ramos. Reports of tension over Ronaldo's role circulated in the autumn, but he remains in the squad. Portugal will co-host the 2030 World Cup with Spain and Morocco, so Euro 2028 qualifying in 2027 and the Nations League are the stepping stones, with an under-23 side targeting the 2028 Olympics.

## Legends

- [Eusébio](/players/eusebio): the Black Panther, 41 goals in 64 games.
- [Luís Figo](/players/luis-figo): 2000 Ballon d'Or and 127 caps.
- [Rui Costa](/players/rui-costa): the elegant playmaker of the golden generation.
- [Paulo Futre](/players/paulo-futre): the 1987 European Cup winner with Porto.
- [Fernando Peyroteo](/players/fernando-peyroteo): a scoring ratio of more than a goal a game in the 1940s.
- [Mário Coluna](/players/mario-coluna): captain of the 1966 side.
- [Nuno Gomes](/players/nuno-gomes): Euro 2000 and 2004 goalscorer.
- [Pepe](/players/pepe): Euro 2016 winner, still playing at Euro 2024 aged 41.
