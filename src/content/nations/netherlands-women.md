## History

The Dutch women arrived at the top table only in the 2010s, but they arrived in style. [Sarina Wiegman](/managers/sarina-wiegman) took charge in January 2017 and seven months later the Oranje Leeuwinnen won a home [Euro](/tournaments/womens-euro), beating Denmark 4-2 in Enschede with goals from [Vivianne Miedema](/players/vivianne-miedema) (two), [Lieke Martens](/players/lieke-martens) and Sherida Spitse. Martens was named Player of the Tournament and later FIFA's World Player of the Year; the whole country turned orange for a women's team for the first time.

Two years later Wiegman's side went all the way to the 2019 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) final in Lyon, beating Japan, Italy and Sweden on the way before losing 2-0 to the [United States](/nations/united-states-women). A first Olympic appearance in Tokyo ended with a 2-2 quarter-final draw against the USA and a penalty shootout defeat. Wiegman then left for [England](/nations/england-women), taking her assistant Arjan Veurink with her, and the Netherlands went through Mark Parsons (one year) and Andries Jonker, who reached the 2023 World Cup quarter-finals, losing to Spain in extra time, but oversaw a dismal Euro 2025, with defeats to England and France.

Veurink, who had won Euro 2017 and Euro 2022 and 2025 as an assistant, came home after Euro 2025 on a contract to 2029.

## Philosophy and style

Dutch football means a 4-3-3 with real wingers and a creative midfield, and the women's team is no exception. Jill Roord and Daniëlle van de Donk drive from midfield, Esmee Brugts and Lineth Beerensteyn stretch the pitch, and Miedema, the Netherlands' record scorer with over 100 goals and now at [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city-women), drops into pockets as a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine). Veurink has tightened the press and the defensive block, and the 2-1 win over France in April 2026 showed the new structure holding against elite opposition.

## Current squad and setup

The Netherlands finished second in [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) qualifying Group A2 behind France with 11 points, beating France 2-1 in Breda in April 2026 but losing 3-2 in Ireland in June. Hungary were beaten 11-0 on aggregate in the October play-off; the round-two tie against Slovenia is on 1 and 5 December 2026. Janssen captains, Daphne van Domselaar of [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal-women) is the goalkeeper, and Miedema, 30, remains the focal point after a knee operation in June 2026.
