## History

Colombia played their first international in 1938 but did not reach a [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) until 1962, where a 4-4 draw with the Soviet Union, from 4-1 down, was the highlight. The next qualification took 28 years. Francisco Maturana's team of the early 1990s, with Carlos Valderrama's blond mane, René Higuita's scorpion kick and Faustino Asprilla's pace, beat Argentina 5-0 in Buenos Aires in 1993 and went to USA 94 as one of the favourites. They went out at the group stage, and Andrés Escobar, who had scored an own goal against the hosts, was murdered on his return home.

The 2001 [Copa América](/tournaments/copa-america) title at home, won without conceding a goal, remains the only major trophy. José Pékerman's 2014 side, lit up by [James Rodríguez](/players/james-rodriguez)'s Golden Boot and his volley against Uruguay, reached the quarter-finals for the first time before losing to Brazil. The 2018 team lost to England on penalties in the round of 16, and Colombia missed Qatar 2022.

Néstor Lorenzo, Pékerman's former assistant, took over in 2022 and built a 28-match unbeaten run that ended only in the 2024 Copa América final, lost to Argentina after extra time. At the 2026 World Cup Colombia beat Ghana 1-0 in the round of 32 before going out to Switzerland on penalties after a 0-0 draw in the round of 16. The federation confirmed in July 2026 that Lorenzo would continue into the next cycle.

## Philosophy and style

Colombian football is about rhythm and touch, the inheritance of Valderrama and the Atlético Nacional school of Maturana. Lorenzo's 4-2-3-1 keeps the ball through Richard Ríos and Kevin Castaño, gives James the freedom of the number 10 and lets [Luis Díaz](/players/luis-diaz) and Jhon Arias attack from the wings, with Daniel Muñoz overlapping aggressively. Out of possession they are compact and well drilled, a long way from the romantic chaos of the 1990s.

Colombian football's social role is enormous: the 1994 tragedy and the narco era gave way to the 2014 team as a symbol of a changing country, and the 2024 Copa América run filled plazas from Bogotá to Barranquilla. The Barranquilla heat and the Atanasio Girardot in Medellín give Colombia a home advantage in qualifying that Lorenzo exploited to the full. The rivalry with Argentina, their conqueror in the 2024 Copa final, and with Brazil, 2014 quarter-final opponents, defines the continental calendar.

## Managers who shaped them

- Francisco Maturana: the 1990 and 1994 teams and the 5-0 in Buenos Aires.
- Francisco Maturana (second spell): the 2001 Copa América.
- José Pékerman: the 2014 quarter-final and 2018 round of 16.
- Néstor Lorenzo: the 2024 Copa final and 2026 round of 16.

## Current squad and setup

Camilo Vargas keeps goal behind Davinson Sánchez; Muñoz and Johan Mojica are the full-backs; Ríos and Castaño sit in midfield; James, 35, remains captain and creator; Díaz, Arias and Jhon Durán or Luis Suárez lead the attack. The next big target is Copa América 2028, with Lorenzo also tasked with blooding the generation that won the 2024 South American under-20 silver.
