## History

South Korea's women were overshadowed for decades by the men's programme and by North Korea, who were the stronger Korean side through the 2000s. The first [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) came in 2003, where Kim Jin-hee scored the team's first goal in a 7-1 defeat to Norway, and the breakthrough came in Canada in 2015, when a 2-1 win over Spain sent the Taegeuk Ladies to the round of 16 for the first time.

[Ji So-yun](/players/ji-so-yun), who spent eight years at [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women) and is the country's record cap-holder and scorer with 76 goals, carried the team through its best era. Colin Bell's side reached the 2022 [Asian Cup](/tournaments/asian-cup) final, losing 3-2 to China after leading 2-0, and the 2023 World Cup brought a historic moment when Casey Phair, at 16 years and 26 days, became the youngest player in World Cup history, though the team went out in the group stage after a 1-1 draw with Germany.

Shin Sang-woo, a former Under-20 coach, took over the senior side and led them to the 2026 Asian Cup semi-finals in Australia: group wins over Iran and the Philippines, a 3-3 draw with the hosts that topped the group, a 6-0 quarter-final win over Uzbekistan that sealed a World Cup place, and then a 4-1 semi-final defeat to [Japan](/nations/japan-women).

## Philosophy and style

Korea have always been a passing side, comfortable in a 4-3-3 with Ji pulling the strings, and the weakness has always been goals. Shin has leaned into a more aggressive press and faster transitions, with Choe Yu-ri and Jang Sel-gi running wide and Park Soo-jeong a creative force from midfield. The core of the squad plays in the WK League at home, with a growing contingent abroad in Japan, England and the United States; Phair, of Angel City, is the most-watched prospect.

## Current squad and setup

South Korea qualified for the [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) in Brazil through the Asian Cup quarter-final win in March 2026. Ji So-yun, now 35 and back in Korea with Suwon FC, remains in the squad as captain and talisman; Kim Min-ji, Kang Chae-rim and Kim Shin-ji were among the scorers in 2026. A 5-1 defeat to [Brazil](/nations/brazil-women) in April 2026 showed the gap to the top tier that Shin's side must close before June 2027.
