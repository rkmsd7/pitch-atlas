## History

Japan's football was amateur until the J.League launched in 1993, and the national team's first qualification for the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) came in 1998, after the "Agony of Doha" had cost them a place in 1994. The 1992 [Asian Cup](/tournaments/asian-cup), won at home, was the first trophy, and the 2000, 2004 and 2011 titles made Japan the competition's most successful nation.

Co-hosting in 2002, Philippe Troussier's team reached the round of 16; Takeshi Okada's 2010 side did the same in South Africa; Akira Nishino's 2018 team led Belgium 2-0 before losing 3-2 in the last minute. Hajime Moriyasu, appointed in 2018, produced the greatest results yet in Qatar in 2022, beating Germany and Spain to top the group before a penalty defeat to Croatia.

The 2026 cycle saw Japan dominate Asian qualifying and become the first team to book a place, but a 2-2 draw with the Netherlands in the group stage was followed by a 2-1 defeat to Brazil in the round of 32. The JFA kept Moriyasu on a six-month contract to lead the 2027 Asian Cup in Saudi Arabia (7 January to 5 February 2027), after which Go Oiwa, the under-23 coach, takes over from March 2027.

## Philosophy and style

Japan's technical foundation is the J.League youth system and the Brazilian influence of Zico's era. Moriyasu's innovation was the switch to a 3-4-2-1, with [Takefusa Kubo](/players/takefusa-kubo) and [Kaoru Mitoma](/players/kaoru-mitoma) operating as inside forwards behind Ayase Ueda, wing-backs Ritsu Doan and Hiroki Itō providing width, and Wataru Endō screening. The team presses in coordinated [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) and [counter-attacks](/tactics/counter-attacking) at speed; against lesser Asian opposition they dominate possession in the manner of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play).

Japan's success is a planned project. The JFA's 2005 declaration set the goal of winning the World Cup by 2050, and the J.League's academy system, the high-school championship and the mass export of players to the Bundesliga and Premier League have turned Japan into Asia's deepest talent pool. More than twenty of the 2026 squad played in Europe. The rivalry with South Korea is the fiercest in Asia, while Saudi Arabia and Australia are the regular obstacles in qualifying.

## Managers who shaped them

- Hans Ooft: the 1992 Asian Cup.
- Philippe Troussier: the 2000 Asian Cup and 2002 round of 16.
- Zico: the 2004 Asian Cup.
- Takeshi Okada: 2010.
- Alberto Zaccheroni: the 2011 Asian Cup.
- Hajime Moriyasu: 2018-27, the 2022 wins over Germany and Spain.

## Current squad and setup

Moriyasu's final campaign will be the 2027 Asian Cup. Zion Suzuki is in goal, Ko Itakura and Takehiro Tomiyasu lead the back three, Endō captains from midfield beside Daichi Kamada, and Kubo, Mitoma and Doan supply the creativity. Oiwa will also coach the under-21 side targeting the 2028 Olympics, so the two squads are already being integrated.

- [Makoto Hasebe](/players/makoto-hasebe): captain at three World Cups.
