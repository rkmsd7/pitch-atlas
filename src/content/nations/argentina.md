## History

Argentina played the first international outside Britain, against Uruguay in 1902, and that rivalry defined the early decades. They lost the first [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) final in Montevideo in 1930 and spent the next forty years dominating the [Copa América](/tournaments/copa-america) while under-achieving on the world stage.

The 1978 World Cup at home, won under César Luis Menotti with Mario Kempes scoring twice in the final, delivered the first title. Eight years later Carlos Bilardo's team won in Mexico in the most individual triumph the tournament has seen: [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona) scored the Hand of God and the goal of the century against England in the same quarter-final and dragged the team past Belgium and West Germany.

The 1990 side lost a bitter final to West Germany, and a generation of Batistuta, Verón and Ortega never got beyond the quarter-finals. The 2014 team, built around [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi), lost the final to Germany in extra time, and the 2018 campaign under Jorge Sampaoli fell apart in the round of 16.

Lionel Scaloni, appointed as an interim in 2018, rebuilt the team around Messi but with a younger, more combative spine. The 2021 Copa América, Argentina's first senior title in 28 years, broke the drought, and the 2022 World Cup in Qatar followed with an epic final against France, 3-3 after extra time and 4-2 on penalties. Argentina retained the Copa América in 2024 and reached the 2026 World Cup final, beating Cape Verde, Egypt and England along the way before losing 1-0 to Spain after extra time, Enzo Fernández sent off and Ferran Torres scoring in the 106th minute.

## Philosophy and style

Scaloni's Argentina are organised rather than romantic. They defend in a compact 4-4-2 or 4-3-3, press in midfield with the energy of Rodrigo De Paul and Enzo Fernández, and break quickly. The ball goes to Messi as early as possible and the runners, Julián Álvarez and Alexis Mac Allister in particular, attack the spaces he creates. It is [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) football played by technically superb players, with a mean streak in Cristian Romero and Nicolás Otamendi at the back. The national tradition remains the enganche, the creative number 10, and Messi is the latest in a line that runs from Bochini through Maradona to Riquelme.

The relationship between Argentina and its national team is unusually intense. The Boca-River divide, the memory of Maradona and the cult of Messi mean every tournament is a referendum on the national character. Scaloni's achievement was to build a team the public loved for its humility as much as its results: De Paul, Otamendi and Dibu Martínez became folk heroes, and the three-million-strong parade through Buenos Aires in December 2022 was the largest public gathering in the country's history.

The 2026 campaign was the hardest of the cycle. Argentina needed extra time to beat Cape Verde 3-2 in the round of 32, overcame Egypt 3-2 in the round of 16 and ground past England in the semi-final before the final against Spain. The rivalry with Brazil, renewed by the 2021 Copa final at the Maracanã and the 4-1 win in Buenos Aires in 2025, remains the fixture by which every Argentine side is judged, and the Finalissima against the European champions is a modern addition to the calendar.

## Managers who shaped them

- César Luis Menotti: the 1978 title and the romantic, attacking school.
- Carlos Bilardo: pragmatism and the 1986 World Cup.
- [Marcelo Bielsa](/managers/marcelo-bielsa): the 1998-2004 era whose pressing ideas shaped a generation of coaches.
- Alejandro Sabella: the 2014 final.
- Lionel Scaloni: Copa América 2021 and 2024, World Cup 2022, 2026 runners-up.

## Current squad and setup

Scaloni's contract runs to December 2026 and his future was still unresolved in November 2026. Messi, 39, remains captain and a starter when fit, now playing for [Inter Miami](/clubs/inter-miami). The team sets up in a 4-3-3 with Emiliano Martínez in goal, Cristian Romero and Nahuel Molina in defence, De Paul, Enzo Fernández and Mac Allister in midfield, and Julián Álvarez and Lautaro Martínez sharing the forward load. Franco Mastantuono is the most exciting of the next wave.

Next competitions: the 2027 Nations League-style CONMEBOL fixtures, Copa América 2028, and the 2028 Olympics for the under-23s.

## Legends

- [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona): the 1986 World Cup almost single-handedly.
- [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi): record caps, record goals, eight Ballons d'Or, a World Cup.
- [Mario Kempes](/players/mario-kempes): top scorer and final hero in 1978.
- [Gabriel Batistuta](/players/gabriel-batistuta): hat-tricks at two World Cups.
- [Daniel Passarella](/players/daniel-passarella): captain of the 1978 champions.
- [Juan Román Riquelme](/players/juan-roman-riquelme): the last classic enganche.
- [Javier Zanetti](/players/javier-zanetti): 143 caps of understated excellence.
- [Alfredo Di Stéfano](/players/alfredo-di-stefano): six caps before switching to Spain, still an Argentine icon.
- [Ángel Di María](/players/angel-di-maria): scorer in the 2021 Copa, 2022 Finalissima and 2022 World Cup finals.
