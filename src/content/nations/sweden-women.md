## History

Sweden won the very first UEFA women's competition in 1984, beating [England](/nations/england-women) on penalties in a two-legged final, and have remained a top-ten nation ever since without ever winning another major title. The near misses are a genre of their own: World Cup bronze in 1991, a 2003 final in Los Angeles lost to a Nia Künzer golden goal for [Germany](/nations/germany-women), Euro finals in 1987, 1995 and 2001, and Olympic silver in both Rio 2016 and Tokyo 2020, the latter lost on penalties to [Canada](/nations/canada-women) after dominating the match.

Pia Sundhage, Sweden's greatest player of the 1980s, later coached the [United States](/nations/united-states-women) to two Olympic golds before returning to coach her own country from 2012 to 2017. Peter Gerhardsson then presided over the most consistent era: World Cup bronze in 2019 and 2023 (the latter after knocking out the United States on penalties in the round of 16, with Lina Hurtig's spot-kick confirmed by goal-line technology), the Tokyo silver, and a Euro 2022 semi-final. [Euro 2025](/tournaments/womens-euro) ended in a quarter-final heartbreak, Sweden leading England 2-0 before losing on penalties, and Gerhardsson stepped down. Tony Gustavsson, the former [Australia](/nations/australia-women) coach, had already been named his successor in February 2025 and took over on 1 August.

## Philosophy and style

Sweden's identity is [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) executed with discipline. The back line of Magdalena Eriksson and Nathalie Björn defends deep when needed, the full-backs push on, and the attack goes to [Stina Blackstenius](/players/stina-blackstenius) and [Fridolina Rolfö](/players/fridolina-rolfo) in behind as quickly as possible. Corners and long throws are rehearsed to the inch. Gustavsson has tried to add more possession to that base, but the 2026 qualifying results, with two defeats to Denmark, show a side still searching for a settled midfield as Caroline Seger's generation gives way to Filippa Angeldahl and Hanna Lundkvist.

## Current squad and setup

Sweden finished third in [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) qualifying Group A1 behind Denmark and Italy with 8 points, and must go through the play-offs. Lithuania were swept aside 15-0 on aggregate in October 2026 (5-0 away, 10-0 at home); the round-two tie against Scotland is on 1 and 5 December 2026, with the winner needing a high enough ranking to claim one of the seven direct European places. Asllani, 37, still captains; Rolfö moved from Barcelona to [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united-women) in 2026, Blackstenius leads the line for [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal-women), and Eriksson organises the [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich-women) defence.
