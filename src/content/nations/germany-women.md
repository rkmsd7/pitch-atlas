## History

For two decades Germany were the dominant force in European women's football. They won the Euro in 1989 at home and then six of the next seven editions, a run that included 2005 and 2009 teams widely considered the best the continent had produced. At the [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) they lost the 1995 final to Norway and then won back-to-back titles: 2003 in the United States, with Nia Künzer's golden goal against Sweden, and 2007 in China without conceding a single goal in six matches, Nadine Angerer saving a Marta penalty in the final. [Birgit Prinz](/players/birgit-prinz) was the face of both triumphs and remains the World Cup's second-highest scorer with 14 goals.

The 2011 home World Cup ended in a quarter-final shock against [Japan](/nations/japan-women), and the next decade was less tidy: Olympic gold under Silvia Neid in Rio 2016, a quarter-final exit at Euro 2017, then a revival under Martina Voss-Tecklenburg that took Germany to the Euro 2022 final at Wembley, lost 2-1 after extra time to [England](/nations/england-women). The 2023 World Cup brought a first-ever group-stage exit, Voss-Tecklenburg left and Horst Hrubesch steered the side to bronze in Paris 2024, beating [Spain](/nations/spain-women) 1-0.

Christian Wück, who had won the Under-17 World Cup with Germany's boys in 2023, took over after the Olympics. At [Euro 2025](/tournaments/womens-euro) his side beat France on penalties with ten players before losing the semi-final to Spain 1-0 in extra time, then lost the 2025 Nations League final to Spain. [Alexandra Popp](/players/alexandra-popp), captain since 2019, retired from international football in October 2024.

## Philosophy and style

Wück inherited a squad in transition and leaned into youth. Germany now press high from a 4-2-3-1, with Lena Oberdorf and Sjoeke Nüsken as the double pivot, [Giulia Gwinn](/players/giulia-gwinn) overlapping from right-back, and Klara Bühl and Jule Brand providing the pace that makes them dangerous in [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) situations. Ann-Katrin Berger, the hero of the Paris Olympics, has been replaced in goal by a younger group as she plays her club football at [Gotham FC](/clubs/gotham-fc). [Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) remain a strength; Germany are still one of the most physically imposing sides in Europe.

## Current squad and setup

Germany were the first European team to qualify for the [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup), topping Group A4 with 16 points after a 4-0 win in Norway in March 2026 and a 5-1 defeat of Austria in April. Gwinn, of [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich-women), captains; Bühl, Oberdorf and Lea Schüller (now at [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united-women)) are the core. The [Frauen-Bundesliga](/leagues/frauen-bundesliga) remains the main supplier, with Bayern's fourth straight title and [Wolfsburg](/clubs/wolfsburg-women) providing most of the squad, though more players are moving to England and Spain each summer.
