## History

Spain played their first international in 1920 at the Antwerp Olympics and won silver, yet for most of the twentieth century they were football's great under-achievers. A single European Championship in 1964, won at home against the Soviet Union, and a quarter-final habit at the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) summed up decades of talent without reward. The 1950 side finished fourth in Brazil; the 1994 team lost to Italy in a quarter-final remembered for Luis Enrique's bloodied face; Euro 2004 and the 2006 World Cup ended early despite squads that looked complete on paper.

The turning point came between 2008 and 2012. Luis Aragonés dropped Raúl, built around a small, technical midfield and won [Euro 2008](/tournaments/euro). Vicente del Bosque kept the machine running, adding the 2010 World Cup in South Africa, where Andrés Iniesta's volley beat the Netherlands, and a 4-0 demolition of Italy in the Euro 2012 final. No men's side had won three straight major tournaments before and none has since.

The decline was sharp. Group-stage exit in 2014, round-of-16 defeats in 2016, 2018 and 2022, and an awkward sacking of Julen Lopetegui on the eve of the 2018 World Cup. Luis Enrique's youthful side reached the Euro 2020 semi-final but lost on penalties to Morocco in Qatar.

Luis de la Fuente, promoted from the youth set-up in December 2022, rebuilt the identity with a faster, more vertical edge. Spain won the 2023 Nations League, swept Euro 2024 with seven wins from seven, and then beat Austria, Portugal, Belgium, France and finally Argentina to lift the 2026 World Cup, Ferran Torres scoring the only goal in extra time at MetLife Stadium on 19 July 2026. [Rodri](/players/rodri) took the Golden Ball, Unai Simón the Golden Glove, and [Pau Cubarsí](/players/pau-cubarsi) the young player award.

## Philosophy and style

Spain are the national-team home of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play). The roots are in [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka), the Barcelona-influenced passing game of Xavi and Iniesta, but de la Fuente's version is less patient. The pivot, usually Rodri, dictates tempo; Pedri and Dani Olmo rotate in the half-spaces; and the wingers, [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal) and Nico Williams, stay wide and attack full-backs one-on-one.

Out of possession Spain press high with coordinated [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps), forcing play to a touchline before the ball-near midfielders jump. The [high line](/tactics/high-line) is protected by the recovery pace of Cubarsí and the sweeping of Unai Simón. Set pieces, long a weakness, became a weapon under set-piece specialists in the 2024-26 cycle.

The 2026 final itself deserves its own paragraph. Spain had beaten Argentina's press for most of normal time without creating much, Unai Simón making two outstanding saves from Julián Álvarez. Enzo Fernández's red card early in extra time tilted the match, and Ferran Torres, on as a substitute, finished a move that began with a Cubarsí interception and ran through Pedri and Yamal. It was Spain's fourteenth clean sheet in sixteen tournament matches across Euro 2024 and the World Cup, and it confirmed de la Fuente's side as the first to hold the European and world titles together since Spain themselves in 2010-12.

Spain's club pipeline explains the depth. [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona)'s La Masia supplies Yamal, Pedri, Cubarsí, Gavi and Fermín López; [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) supplies Dani Carvajal and Dean Huijsen; [Athletic Bilbao](/clubs/athletic-bilbao) supplies the Williams brothers and Simón; and [Real Sociedad](/clubs/real-sociedad), [Villarreal](/clubs/villarreal) and [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) fill the rest. The rivalry with Italy, decided in finals and quarter-finals since 1994, and the newer one with France, beaten in the Euro 2024 and 2026 World Cup semi-finals, frame the modern story.

## Managers who shaped them

- Luis Aragonés: the architect of the passing revolution and Euro 2008.
- Vicente del Bosque: calm continuity, the 2010 World Cup and Euro 2012.
- [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique): modernised the press and promoted the generation of Pedri and Gavi.
- Luis de la Fuente: Euro 2024 and the 2026 World Cup, the most decorated Spain coach by major trophies.

## Current squad and setup

De la Fuente extended his contract after the World Cup and remains in charge in November 2026. The shape is a 4-3-3 that becomes a 3-2-5 with the ball, Marc Cucurella tucking inside. Rodri captains from the pivot, Pedri and Mikel Merino share the interior roles, and Yamal, now a three-time Kopa Trophy winner and Ballon d'Or runner-up, is the focal point of the attack. Ferran Torres, the World Cup final scorer, and Nico Williams complete the front line, with Unai Simón in goal behind Cubarsí.

Next competitions: Euro 2028 qualifying begins in 2027, the Nations League Finals in 2027, and the 2028 Olympic tournament in Los Angeles for the under-23 side.

## Legends

- [Andrés Iniesta](/players/andres-iniesta): scorer of the 2010 World Cup winner.

- [Raúl](/players/raul): Spain's record scorer until David Villa.
- [Sergio Ramos](/players/sergio-ramos): 180 caps, the most of any Spaniard.
- [David Villa](/players/david-villa): all-time top scorer with 59 goals.
- [Fernando Hierro](/players/fernando-hierro): defender who scored 29 international goals.
