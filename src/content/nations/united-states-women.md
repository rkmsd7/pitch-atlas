## History

No team has shaped women's international football like the [United States](/nations/united-states-women). They won the first [Women's World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) in China in 1991, with Michelle Akers scoring ten goals in six games, and then changed the sport's commercial reality in 1999, when 90,185 people packed the Rose Bowl to watch Brandi Chastain's winning penalty against China. The "99ers" generation of [Mia Hamm](/players/mia-hamm), Julie Foudy, Kristine Lilly and Briana Scurry made the national team a cultural institution long before most federations funded a women's programme at all.

The next decade belonged to the Olympics, with golds in 2004, 2008 and 2012, and to [Abby Wambach](/players/abby-wambach), whose 184 international goals stood as a world record until 2020. The World Cup proved harder: a final lost on penalties to [Japan](/nations/japan-women) in 2011 after Wambach's famous 122nd-minute header against Brazil, then redemption in Canada in 2015 as Carli Lloyd scored a hat-trick inside 16 minutes of the final against Japan. In 2019 the side of [Megan Rapinoe](/players/megan-rapinoe) and [Alex Morgan](/players/alex-morgan) retained the trophy in France while fighting, and eventually winning, an equal-pay case against its own federation.

The programme then stumbled. A bronze at the Tokyo Olympics was followed by a round-of-16 exit to [Sweden](/nations/sweden-women) on penalties at the 2023 World Cup, the worst finish in American history. Vlatko Andonovski left, and in May 2024 US Soccer appointed [Emma Hayes](/managers/emma-hayes) from [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women). Within three months she had won Olympic gold in Paris, beating [Brazil](/nations/brazil-women) 1-0 in the final through [Mallory Swanson](/players/mallory-swanson).

## Philosophy and style

For thirty years the American game was built on fitness, directness and a relentless mentality that opponents struggled to match over 90 minutes. Hayes has kept the athleticism but asked for more control: a 4-3-3 with a proper build-up structure, a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and a front three that attacks early. The "Triple Espresso" trio of [Trinity Rodman](/players/trinity-rodman), [Sophia Wilson](/players/sophia-smith) and Swanson defined Paris 2024, running in behind at every turnover. Hayes has also deliberately rotated the squad through 2025 and 2026, giving debuts to a wave of younger players while [Lindsey Heaps](/players/lindsey-horan) and [Naomi Girma](/players/naomi-girma) anchor the spine.

## Current squad and setup

As of November 2026 Hayes is building towards the [2027 Women's World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) in Brazil. The USA has not yet qualified: the Concacaf W Championship, played in the United States from 27 November to 5 December 2026, sends its four quarter-final winners straight to Brazil. The autumn friendlies against world champions [Spain](/nations/spain-women) in October 2026 (a 0-2 defeat in Washington, then a 2-2 draw in Chester, Pennsylvania) were the clearest test yet of the rebuild. Rodman, now the highest-paid player in the [NWSL](/leagues/nwsl) at the [Washington Spirit](/clubs/washington-spirit), Wilson back from maternity leave with 15 goals for Portland, and Swanson, also back from having a child, form the attack; Heaps, who moved from [Lyon](/clubs/lyon-women) to expansion side Denver Summit in January 2026, remains captain.
