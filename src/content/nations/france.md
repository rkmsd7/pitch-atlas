## History

France helped found FIFA in 1904 and the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) itself, Jules Rimet being a Frenchman, yet Les Bleus took sixty years to win anything. The 1958 side finished third with Just Fontaine scoring a record 13 goals. The 1980s brought the Michel Platini generation: semi-finalists in 1982 and 1986 and [Euro 1984](/tournaments/euro) champions at home with Platini scoring nine goals in five games.

The modern era began in 1998. Aimé Jacquet's multicultural team, with [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane) heading two goals in the final against Brazil, won the World Cup at the Stade de France, and Roger Lemerre added Euro 2000. The collapse of 2002, out at the group stage without scoring, and the mutiny of Knysna in 2010 were the lows. Zidane's headbutt in the 2006 final was the strangest exit of all.

Didier Deschamps took over in 2012 and became the most successful coach in French history: runners-up at Euro 2016, World Cup winners in 2018 with the teenage [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe), Nations League winners in 2021 and runners-up again in Qatar in 2022 after a 3-3 final with Argentina. The 2026 World Cup ended with a semi-final defeat to Spain and a 6-4 loss to England in the third-place match; Mbappé won the Golden Boot with 10 goals. Deschamps stepped down and Zidane was appointed on 28 July 2026.

## Philosophy and style

Under Deschamps, France were the great pragmatists: a 4-2-3-1 or 4-3-3 that sat in a mid-block, trusted the individual quality of its defenders and sprung [counter-attacks](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Mbappé and Dembélé. Zidane inherits that spine but, as at Real Madrid, prefers a midfield diamond or a 4-3-3 with more sustained possession and fluid rotations between the forwards. France's production line, the Clairefontaine academy and the banlieue clubs, keeps supplying the athleticism that makes either approach work.

The pipeline is the real story of French football. Clairefontaine, opened in 1988, and the network of regional academies produce more professionals in Europe's top five leagues than any other country, and the national team has fielded a second XI that could win most tournaments. The 2018 squad featured players born in Bondy, Saint-Denis and Sevran; the 2026 squad added Désiré Doué and Rayan Cherki from the Rennes and Lyon academies. That depth is why Deschamps could lose Paul Pogba and N'Golo Kanté in 2022 and still reach a final.

Rivalries define the calendar: Italy, beaten in 2000 and avenged in 2006; Germany, the 1982 Séville semi-final and the 2014 and 2016 knockouts; and now Spain, who have ended France's last two major campaigns. Zidane's appointment has the public dreaming of the 1998 and 2000 double again, and the first measure of his tenure will be Euro 2028, where France have not won since 2000.

## Managers who shaped them

- Michel Hidalgo: Euro 1984 and the Platini carré magique.
- Aimé Jacquet: the 1998 World Cup.
- Raymond Domenech: the 2006 final and the 2010 implosion.
- [Didier Deschamps](/managers/didier-deschamps): 2012-2026, a World Cup, a Nations League and two further finals.
- [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager): appointed July 2026, three Champions Leagues as [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) coach.

## Current squad and setup

Zidane's first autumn in charge has seen a 4-3-3 with Mbappé, now captain and [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid)'s main man, as the central reference, flanked by Ousmane Dembélé and Bradley Barcola, who moved from [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) to [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) for around 125 million euros in the summer. Aurélien Tchouaméni anchors midfield with Eduardo Camavinga and Désiré Doué, and William Saliba and Jules Koundé lead a defence in front of Mike Maignan.

Next competitions: Euro 2028 qualifying from 2027, the Nations League, and the 2028 Olympics.
