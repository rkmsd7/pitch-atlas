## History

Nigeria won their first [AFCON](/tournaments/afcon) at home in 1980 with Segun Odegbami and Christian Chukwu, but the golden era began in the 1990s. Clemens Westerhof's team won the 1994 AFCON in Tunisia and then dazzled at their first [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), beating Bulgaria and Greece and leading Italy with two minutes left in the round of 16 before Roberto Baggio intervened. Two years later the Dream Team of Kanu, Okocha and Amokachi beat Brazil and Argentina to win Olympic gold in Atlanta.

Jay-Jay Okocha's generation beat Spain 3-2 at France 98 and reached the round of 16 again, and Stephen Keshi, the 1994 captain, won the 2013 AFCON as coach and took the Super Eagles to the 2014 round of 16. The 2018 World Cup brought a narrow group exit against Argentina, and Nigeria then missed Qatar 2022 after losing a play-off to Ghana on away goals.

José Peseiro's team reached the 2023 AFCON final, losing to hosts Ivory Coast, before Finidi George and then Augustine Eguavoen held the job briefly. Éric Chelle, the former Mali coach, was appointed in January 2025 with the brief of reaching the 2026 World Cup. Nigeria won the CAF play-off semi-final against Gabon but lost the final to DR Congo on penalties in November 2025, missing a second straight World Cup. The federation nevertheless renewed Chelle's contract in June 2026 and added the under-23 Olympic Eagles to his remit.

## Philosophy and style

Nigerian football is about athleticism and improvisation, from the Okocha step-overs to [Victor Osimhen](/players/victor-osimhen)'s relentless running. Peseiro's [back three](/tactics/back-three) with [Ademola Lookman](/players/ademola-lookman) and Osimhen as a front pair was pragmatic; Chelle has moved to a 4-3-3 with a higher press and quicker vertical passing through Alex Iwobi and Wilfred Ndidi. Set-piece organisation and defensive concentration have been the recurring weaknesses.

## Managers who shaped them

- Clemens Westerhof: the 1994 AFCON and World Cup.
- Bora Milutinović: the 1998 World Cup.
- Stephen Keshi: the 2013 AFCON title.
- Gernot Rohr: 2016-21, the 2018 World Cup.
- José Peseiro: the 2023 AFCON final.
- Éric Chelle: from January 2025.

## Current squad and setup

Nigeria started AFCON 2027 qualifying in the autumn of 2026 ranked around 26th in the world. Stanley Nwabali is in goal, William Troost-Ekong captains the defence with Calvin Bassey and Ola Aina, Ndidi and Iwobi run midfield, and Osimhen, Lookman, Samuel Chukwueze and Victor Boniface form a fearsome attack. AFCON 2027 in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania and the 2028 Olympics, where Chelle also coaches the under-23s, are the targets, along with 2030 World Cup qualifying.
