## History

Italy played their first match in 1910 and under Vittorio Pozzo became the first great national team, winning the 1934 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) at home, the 1936 Olympics and the 1938 World Cup in France. The Superga air crash of 1949, which killed the Torino side that formed the core of the national team, cut short the next generation, and the 1966 defeat to North Korea marked the nadir.

[Euro 1968](/tournaments/euro) at home, won after a replay against Yugoslavia, and the 1970 World Cup final, reached via the 4-3 "Game of the Century" against West Germany, revived the Azzurri. Enzo Bearzot's 1982 team, written off after three group draws, beat Argentina, Brazil and Poland on the way to a third title, Paolo Rossi scoring six goals in three games and Dino Zoff lifting the trophy at 40.

The next quarter-century brought heartbreak: the 1990 semi-final at home, Roberto Baggio's missed penalty in the 1994 final, the golden goal against France at Euro 2000. Marcello Lippi's 2006 side, forged amid the Calciopoli scandal, beat Germany in the semi-final and France on penalties for a fourth star.

Then the decline. Group-stage exits in 2010 and 2014, failure to qualify in 2018 and 2022, and in between the Euro 2020 title under Roberto Mancini, won on penalties against England at Wembley. Luciano Spalletti was sacked in June 2025 and Gennaro Gattuso took over for the remainder of qualifying. Italy went into the March 2026 play-offs and lost to Bosnia and Herzegovina on penalties, missing a third consecutive World Cup. Gattuso resigned in April 2026 and on 28 July 2026 the FIGC reappointed Roberto Mancini, the Euro 2020 winner, as head coach.

## Philosophy and style

Italy gave the world [catenaccio](/tactics/catenaccio), the sweeper system of Helenio Herrera's Inter, and the national team has always trusted defensive organisation. But the Azzurri have also been innovators in possession: Arrigo Sacchi's zonal pressing, Antonio Conte's 3-5-2 at Euro 2016 and Mancini's fluent 4-3-3 of 2021. Gattuso used a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan)'s Bastoni and Dimarco; Mancini, in his second spell, has returned to the 4-3-3 of his 2021 champions, with Nicolò Barella and Sandro Tonali as ball-winning midfielders, [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) principles in build-up and a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) edge through Mateo Retegui and Moise Kean. Italy remain superb at managing a lead and at [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces).

Serie A's revival, with [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan) winning the 2025-26 title under Cristian Chivu and [Napoli](/clubs/napoli), [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) and [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) investing again, has not yet translated into the national team. Italian clubs field fewer home-grown players than any of Europe's big five leagues, and the FIGC's reform programme, with a new under-23 B-team rule and incentives for Italian starters, is designed to fix a pipeline that produced only a handful of top-level strikers in a decade.

The rivalries are as rich as any: Germany, who Italy beat in 1970, 1982 and 2006 and lost to for the first time in a tournament at Euro 2016; France, Zidane's headbutt and the 2006 shoot-out; Spain, who ended Italy's Euro 2012 and 2024 campaigns; and Brazil, 1970, 1982 and 1994. Mancini, who built the Euro 2020 champions from the wreckage of the 2017 play-off defeat to Sweden, has been asked to do it again, and a strong Euro 2028 would close the chapter.

## Managers who shaped them

- Vittorio Pozzo: two World Cups and an Olympic gold in the 1930s.
- Enzo Bearzot: 1982.
- [Arrigo Sacchi](/managers/arrigo-sacchi): the 1994 final and a pressing revolution.
- Marcello Lippi: 2006.
- [Antonio Conte](/managers/antonio-conte): the Euro 2016 over-achievers.
- [Roberto Mancini](/managers/roberto-mancini): Euro 2020 and a 37-match unbeaten run in his first spell.
- [Gennaro Gattuso](/managers/gennaro-gattuso): June 2025 to April 2026, the failed play-off.
- [Roberto Mancini](/managers/roberto-mancini): reappointed 28 July 2026.

## Current squad and setup

Mancini's Italy in November 2026 plays a 4-3-3: Gianluigi Donnarumma, the captain, behind Calafiori, Bastoni, Dimarco and Andrea Cambiaso; Barella, Tonali and Davide Frattesi in midfield; Retegui flanked by Kean and Giacomo Raspadori. The federation's goal is simple: reach Euro 2028, with qualifying starting in 2027, and then the 2030 World Cup. The Nations League group campaign in autumn 2026 doubled as a rebuilding exercise, and the Olympic under-23 side eyes Los Angeles 2028.
