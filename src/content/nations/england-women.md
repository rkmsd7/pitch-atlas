## History

The Football Association banned women from its grounds in 1921 and only lifted the ban in 1971, so England's modern story starts late. The Lionesses reached the first Euro final in 1984 and lost it to [Sweden](/nations/sweden-women) on penalties, then spent two decades as quarter-finalists while Kelly Smith and Fara Williams carried the team almost alone. Third place at the 2015 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) under Mark Sampson, with [Lucy Bronze](/players/lucy-bronze) emerging as the world's best right-back, was the first sign of a programme that was finally properly funded, and the Women's Super League's shift to full professionalism in 2018 did the rest.

Phil Neville's side finished fourth at the 2019 World Cup, but the transformation came with [Sarina Wiegman](/managers/sarina-wiegman), who arrived in September 2021 having already won Euro 2017 with the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands-women). On home soil at Euro 2022 England beat [Germany](/nations/germany-women) 2-1 after extra time at Wembley in front of 87,192, with [Chloe Kelly](/players/chloe-kelly) scoring the winner and swinging her shirt above her head. A year later they reached the World Cup final in Sydney and lost 1-0 to [Spain](/nations/spain-women).

Euro 2025 in Switzerland was the most dramatic tournament yet: a 2-1 defeat to France to open, then comebacks against Sweden (from 2-0 down, won on penalties), Italy (Michelle Agyemang's 96th-minute equaliser, Kelly's extra-time winner) and finally Spain in Basel, where [Alessia Russo](/players/alessia-russo) cancelled out Mariona Caldentey's opener, [Hannah Hampton](/players/hannah-hampton) saved two penalties and Kelly struck the decisive kick. On 19 October 2026 Wiegman signed a two-year extension taking her through to Euro 2029.

## Philosophy and style

Wiegman's England is a 4-3-3 built on calm. [Keira Walsh](/players/keira-walsh) sets the tempo at the base, Georgia Stanway and Ella Toone carry the ball into the final third, and the wide players, [Lauren James](/players/lauren-james), [Lauren Hemp](/players/lauren-hemp) and Beth Mead, attack full-backs one-v-one. Russo is a complete number nine who drops to link, and [Leah Williamson](/players/leah-williamson) steps out of defence to play like an extra midfielder. The defining trait of the Wiegman era, though, is the bench: Kelly, Agyemang and Toone have repeatedly won games as substitutes, and the manager has never been afraid to change shape to a [back three](/tactics/back-three) mid-match.

## Current squad and setup

England finished second to Spain in [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) qualifying Group A3 with 15 points, beating Spain 1-0 at Wembley in April 2026 but losing 4-0 in the return. That means a play-off route: Greece were beaten 8-1 on aggregate in October 2026, and the decisive round-two tie against Ukraine is on 1 and 5 December 2026. Russo was named England Player of the Year in October 2026 after scoring five goals in ten games; Hampton, the 2025 Yashin Trophy winner, and Bronze, now 35 and England's third most-capped player, remain first choice. Williamson is captain, Agyemang the breakout star of 2025.
