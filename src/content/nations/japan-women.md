## History

Japan's women were a mid-table Asian side for most of the 1990s and 2000s before Norio Sasaki's generation, led by [Homare Sawa](/players/homare-sawa), produced one of sport's great stories. Four months after the Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami, Nadeshiko Japan beat Germany in the 2011 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) quarter-final, Sweden in the semi and the [United States](/nations/united-states-women) in the Frankfurt final on penalties after Sawa's 117th-minute equaliser, becoming the first Asian nation to win a senior World Cup. Sawa won the Golden Ball and the Golden Boot and the 2011 FIFA World Player of the Year award.

The follow-up was almost as strong: Olympic silver in London 2012 and another World Cup final in 2015, lost 5-2 to Carli Lloyd's USA. Asian titles followed in 2014 and 2018 under Asako Takakura, but the 2019 World Cup round-of-16 exit and a home-Olympics quarter-final defeat in Tokyo suggested a cycle had ended. Futoshi Ikeda rebuilt around a younger core and the 2023 World Cup brought a 4-0 demolition of Spain in the group stage before a 2-1 quarter-final loss to Sweden; [Hinata Miyazawa](/players/hinata-miyazawa) won the Golden Boot.

Danish coach Nils Nielsen took over in December 2024 and reached the Paris Olympic quarter-finals, then won the [2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup](/tournaments/asian-cup) in Australia, beating South Korea 4-1 in the semi-final and the hosts 1-0 in front of a record 74,000 crowd at Stadium Australia on 21 March 2026, Maika Hamano scoring from distance. Nielsen nevertheless left when his contract expired in April 2026 and the JFA appointed Michihisa Kano.

## Philosophy and style

Nobody in women's football moves the ball through tight spaces like Japan. Sasaki's 2011 side played a version of [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) built on Sawa's late runs; the modern team is more vertical, with [Yui Hasegawa](/players/yui-hasegawa) dictating from deep, Miyazawa and Mina Tanaka attacking from wide, and Riko Ueki, top scorer at the 2026 Asian Cup with six goals, as the focal point. The press is coordinated and the [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) is ruthless, an evolution forced by a decade of facing bigger, stronger opponents.

## Current squad and setup

Japan qualified for the [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) by reaching the Asian Cup quarter-finals and then won the tournament. Hasegawa of [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city-women) is captain, Hamano, now at [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women), is the rising star, and Ayaka Yamashita, named best goalkeeper of the Asian Cup, is the last line. Two home friendlies against Brazil on 29 November and 5 December 2026 close the year. Kano's appointment has not been widely covered outside Japan and should be treated as the position as of the 2026 Asian Cup aftermath.
