## History

The Super Falcons have been Africa's standard-bearers since the Women's Africa Cup of Nations began. They won the first edition in 1998 and nine of the first eleven, a dominance built on players like Mercy Akide, Florence Omagbemi and Perpetua Nkwocha, who scored 80 international goals and won the African Player of the Year award four times. Nigeria are also the only African side to appear at every [Women's World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) from 1991 to 2023, with a quarter-final in 1999, when they took Brazil to extra time, as the high point.

The modern team added glamour and grit: [Asisat Oshoala](/players/asisat-oshoala) won a record six African Player of the Year awards and a [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) with [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona-women), and at the 2023 World Cup Randy Waldrum's side beat co-hosts Australia 3-2, drew with Canada and Ireland, and held England to 0-0 for 120 minutes in the round of 16 before losing on penalties, with Lauren James sent off for stamping on Michelle Alozie. The Paris 2024 Olympics ended in the group stage.

Justin Madugu's team then completed "Mission X" at WAFCON 2024, held in Morocco in July 2025, coming from 2-0 down to beat the hosts 3-2 in the Rabat final through Jennifer Echegini's 88th-minute winner. The joy did not last. At the rescheduled WAFCON 2026, again in Morocco in July and August 2026, Nigeria lost 3-2 to Malawi in their opening game, recovered to beat Zambia and Egypt, then lost 1-0 to eventual champions Cameroon in the quarter-finals. A play-in defeat to South Africa on 13 August 2026 meant the Super Falcons will miss the 2027 World Cup, the first they have ever failed to reach.

## Philosophy and style

Nigeria's strength is athleticism and defensive resilience. [Chiamaka Nnadozie](/players/chiamaka-nnadozie), the Brighton goalkeeper, is one of the best in the world; Ashleigh Plumptre and Osinachi Ohale defend a [low block](/tactics/low-block) that only England's penalty shootout breached in 2023. Going forward it is [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through [Rasheedat Ajibade](/players/rasheedat-ajibade), the captain, Esther Okoronkwo's hold-up play and the wide running of Alozie. The historic problem is chance creation against deep defences, which the 2026 WAFCON exposed.

## Current squad and setup

The fallout from WAFCON 2026 has left the coaching position unsettled; as of November 2026 the federation lists Augustine Eguavoen, the former men's coach and technical director, as caretaker, though a permanent appointment had not been confirmed at the time of writing. Ajibade of [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) captains, Nnadozie is the undisputed number one, and Oshoala, 32, plays for Bay FC in the [NWSL](/leagues/nwsl). With no World Cup to prepare for, the next target is the 2028 Olympic qualifiers.
