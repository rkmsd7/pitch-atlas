## History

England played the first international match, a 0-0 draw with Scotland in 1872, and stayed aloof from FIFA and the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) until 1950, when a 1-0 defeat to the United States in Belo Horizonte announced that the rest of the world had caught up. The 6-3 loss to Hungary at Wembley in 1953 confirmed it.

Alf Ramsey's "wingless wonders" won the 1966 World Cup at home, beating West Germany 4-2 after extra time with Geoff Hurst's hat-trick and [Bobby Moore](/players/bobby-moore) lifting the trophy. It remains the only major title. The decades that followed were defined by near-misses: the 1970 quarter-final collapse against West Germany, failure to qualify in 1974 and 1978, Maradona's two goals in 1986, the semi-final penalty defeats of Italia 90 and Euro 96 and the golden generation of Beckham, Gerrard, Lampard and Rooney that never got past a quarter-final.

Gareth Southgate changed the mood. England reached the 2018 World Cup semi-final, lost the Euro 2020 final on penalties to Italy at Wembley, and lost the Euro 2024 final to Spain in Berlin. Thomas Tuchel replaced him in January 2025, qualified without conceding a goal, and took England to the 2026 World Cup semi-final, where they lost to Argentina, before beating France 6-4 in a wild third-place match. [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) scored 73 goals in 2025-26 and became the first English Ballon d'Or winner since Michael Owen in 2001; [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka) won the Sócrates Award.

## Philosophy and style

The old English identity, long balls, crosses and second balls, has given way to a more continental mix. Tuchel's team is a 4-2-3-1 that presses in a [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) style with Declan Rice and Adam Wharton screening, [Jude Bellingham](/players/jude-bellingham) and Cole Palmer rotating behind Kane, and Saka providing width and goals from the right. England remain superb at [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), a Southgate-era strength Tuchel has kept, and are now comfortable both in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and in sustained possession.

The club game is both England's blessing and its curse. The [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) is the richest in the world and the national team's players come from [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city), [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) and [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), but a long schedule and a historic tactical conservatism meant England arrived at tournaments tired and predictable. St George's Park, opened in 2012, and the "England DNA" programme changed that: the under-17 and under-20 World Cup wins of 2017 produced Foden, Saka, Guéhi and Rice, and the under-21 Euro wins of 2023 and 2025 added the next wave.

The rivalries are familiar: Germany, from 1966 and 1970 through 1990 and 1996 to the 2-0 at Wembley in 2021; Argentina, from 1986 to the 2026 semi-final; and Scotland, the oldest fixture in the game. Tuchel's own test is whether he can convert a generation that has reached three semi-finals and two finals in four tournaments into champions at Euro 2028 on home soil.

## Managers who shaped them

- Walter Winterbottom: the first full-time manager, 1946-62.
- Alf Ramsey: the 1966 World Cup.
- Bobby Robson: Italia 90 and the restoration of national belief.
- Terry Venables: Euro 96 and a modern tactical vocabulary.
- [Gareth Southgate](/managers/gareth-southgate): two finals and two semi-finals in four tournaments.
- [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel): appointed 2025, third place at the 2026 World Cup.

## Current squad and setup

Tuchel's England in November 2026 lines up in a 4-2-3-1: Jordan Pickford in goal, Trent Alexander-Arnold and Marc Guéhi in a back four, Rice as the anchor, Bellingham and Palmer behind Kane, Saka and Phil Foden wide. Morgan Rogers, Chelsea's 117 million pound British-record signing in the summer of 2026, has forced his way into the group. The nation's attention is already on Euro 2028, which the United Kingdom and Ireland will host, with qualifying beginning in 2027; a first title since 1966 would end sixty-two years of hurt.

## Legends

- [Bobby Charlton](/players/bobby-charlton): 1966 champion, Ballon d'Or winner and Munich survivor.
- [Bobby Moore](/players/bobby-moore): the captain who lifted the Jules Rimet trophy.
- [Gordon Banks](/players/gordon-banks): the save from Pelé in 1970.
- [Gary Lineker](/players/gary-lineker): 1986 Golden Boot, 48 goals.
- [Alan Shearer](/players/alan-shearer): Euro 96 top scorer.
- [Wayne Rooney](/players/wayne-rooney): 53 goals, the record until Kane.
- [David Beckham](/players/david-beckham): 115 caps and the free-kick against Greece.
- [Steven Gerrard](/players/steven-gerrard): captain through the golden generation.
