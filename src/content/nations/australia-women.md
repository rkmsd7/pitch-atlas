## History

Australia left Oceania for the Asian confederation in 2006 and won the [AFC Women's Asian Cup](/tournaments/asian-cup) in 2010, but it was the 2010s that turned the Matildas into a phenomenon. [Sam Kerr](/players/sam-kerr) emerged as the best striker in the world, the team reached World Cup quarter-finals in 2007, 2011 and 2015, and in 2017 they beat the [United States](/nations/united-states-women) for the first time. Tony Gustavsson's side then went to a fourth-place finish at the Tokyo Olympics.

The 2023 home [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) changed Australian sport. With Kerr injured for the group stage, the Matildas still beat Canada 4-0, then France on penalties in a 7-6 quarter-final shootout watched by a record national television audience, before losing the semi-final 3-1 to [England](/nations/england-women) and the bronze match to Sweden. Kerr's long-range goal in the semi-final remains one of the tournament's images.

Gustavsson left after a group-stage exit at Paris 2024 and in June 2025 the federation appointed Joe Montemurro, the Melbourne-born coach who had won titles with [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal-women), Juventus and [Lyon](/clubs/lyon-women). His first big test was a home Asian Cup in March 2026: Australia topped a group that included South Korea, beat China in the semi-final with Kerr scoring the winner on her return from a long ACL lay-off, and lost the final 1-0 to [Japan](/nations/japan-women) in front of 74,000 at Stadium Australia. Alanna Kennedy was named player of the tournament.

## Philosophy and style

The Matildas' default is directness: Kerr or Caitlin Foord running the channels, Ellie Carpenter and Steph Catley bombing on from full-back, Mary Fowler floating between the lines. Montemurro wants more control, a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) build-up and a [high line](/tactics/high-line), and the 2026 Asian Cup showed a team that could hold the ball for longer stretches while still being at its most dangerous on the break. Mackenzie Arnold in goal, the penalty-shootout hero of 2023, is a settled figure.

## Current squad and setup

Australia qualified for the [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) by reaching the 2026 Asian Cup quarter-finals. Kerr, 33, left [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women) in May 2026 as the club's record WSL scorer and joined [Gotham FC](/clubs/gotham-fc) in June, where she helped win the NWSL Shield; she remains captain. Fowler plays for [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city-women), Foord and Catley for Arsenal, Carpenter for Chelsea. Montemurro's contract runs through Brazil 2027.
