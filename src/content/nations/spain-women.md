## History

Spain were a late arrival to the top of the women's game. They did not reach a [Women's World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) until 2015 and went out in the group stage, and a 2019 round-of-16 defeat to the [United States](/nations/united-states-women) was seen as progress. What changed was the club game: [Barcelona Femení](/clubs/barcelona-women) turned professional, won its first [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) in 2021 and gave the national team a ready-made spine of [Alexia Putellas](/players/alexia-putellas), [Aitana Bonmatí](/players/aitana-bonmati), Patri Guijarro and [Mariona Caldentey](/players/mariona-caldentey).

The path to the 2023 title was anything but smooth. Fifteen players withdrew from selection in September 2022 in protest at the environment under coach Jorge Vilda, and several never returned. The team that went to Australia and New Zealand still beat [Sweden](/nations/sweden-women) in the semi-final and [England](/nations/england-women) 1-0 in the Sydney final, Olga Carmona scoring the only goal. Bonmatí won the Golden Ball and [Salma Paralluelo](/players/salma-paralluelo) the young-player award. The celebration was overshadowed by the Luis Rubiales kiss scandal, which forced the federation president out and led to Vilda's sacking. Montse Tomé took charge, won the inaugural Nations League in 2024 by beating [France](/nations/france-women) 2-0, finished fourth at the Paris Olympics and reached the [Euro 2025](/tournaments/womens-euro) final in Basel, where Spain outplayed England but lost 3-1 on penalties after a 1-1 draw.

Tomé's contract was not renewed. On 1 September 2025 former international striker Sonia Bermúdez became head coach, and she immediately won the 2025 Nations League, beating [Germany](/nations/germany-women) 3-0 on aggregate in the final.

## Philosophy and style

Spain play the [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) that Barcelona made famous: a 4-3-3 that turns into a 3-2-5 in possession, short build-up from the goalkeeper, a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and a counter-press to win the ball back within seconds. Guijarro is the pivot, Bonmatí and (until her move to England in 2026) Putellas the interiors. The width now comes from [Vicky López](/players/vicky-lopez) and [Clàudia Pina](/players/claudia-pina), the latter the Liga F Golden Boot winner in 2025-26, while Paralluelo supplies the pace in behind. Bermúdez has kept the structure but asked for quicker ball progression and more direct running once the first line is beaten.

## Current squad and setup

Spain were the first European side other than Germany to book a place at the [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) in Brazil, winning qualifying Group A3 ahead of England with 15 points; a 4-0 home win over the Lionesses in June 2026 settled it. October 2026 friendlies in the United States ended 2-0 and 2-2, with Paralluelo, now at [OL Lyonnes](/clubs/lyon-women), on the scoresheet. [Irene Paredes](/players/irene-paredes) captains the side; Bonmatí, back from the broken leg she suffered at the November 2025 Nations League finals, is the heartbeat; [Alexia Putellas](/players/alexia-putellas), the 2026 Ballon d'Or Féminin winner, continues at 32 after joining London City Lionesses. Friendlies against Chile (28 November) and Venezuela (4 December) close the year. Vicky López, Women's Kopa Trophy winner in 2026, is the face of the next generation.
