## History

Uruguay were the first great international team. Olympic champions in 1924 and 1928, they hosted and won the inaugural [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in 1930, beating Argentina 4-2 in Montevideo. They boycotted the next two tournaments and returned in 1950 to produce the Maracanazo, Obdulio Varela's team beating Brazil 2-1 in front of around 200,000 stunned fans.

Fourth place in 1954 and 1970, with the elegant Juan Schiaffino and then Pedro Rocha, kept Uruguay among the elite, but the 1986 and 1990 teams were notorious for cynicism, and the country missed four of the six World Cups between 1994 and 2006. Óscar Tabárez's second reign, from 2006 to 2021, restored respect: fourth place at the 2010 World Cup, with [Diego Forlán](/players/diego-forlan) winning the Golden Ball and [Luis Suárez](/players/luis-suarez)'s handball against Ghana becoming legend, the 2011 [Copa América](/tournaments/copa-america) title, and quarter-finals in 2018.

Marcelo Bielsa took over in 2023 and reached the 2024 Copa América semi-final, but the 2026 World Cup was a disaster: Uruguay went out at the group stage, the first South American team eliminated. Bielsa departed and on 6 August 2026 the AUF appointed Forlán, the 2010 hero, as interim coach with a contract to March 2027, doubling as under-20 coach.

## Philosophy and style

Garra charrúa, the Uruguayan fighting spirit, is the national trademark: hard tackling, gamesmanship and an obsession with not losing. Tabárez codified it as a 4-4-2 with a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and [counter-attacks](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Suárez and Cavani. Bielsa tried to impose his man-marking, high-pressing style, which produced thrilling wins over Argentina and Brazil in qualifying but fell apart at the World Cup. Forlán has returned to a more conservative 4-3-3, built on the engine of [Federico Valverde](/players/federico-valverde) and the physicality of Ronald Araújo.

For a country of 3.4 million, Uruguay's record is astonishing: two World Cups, fifteen Copa Américas and two Olympic golds, and a conveyor belt of forwards from Scarone and Schiaffino through Francescoli, Forlán, Suárez and Cavani to Darwin Núñez. [Peñarol](/clubs/penarol) and Nacional, both multiple Copa Libertadores winners, remain the twin pillars of a domestic game that exports almost every talent before the age of 21.

The rivalries are Argentina, the first international match outside Britain in 1902 and the 1930 final, and Brazil, the Maracanazo and the 2024 Copa América quarter-final that Uruguay won on penalties. The Bielsa years ended in disappointment but left a younger, more technical squad, and the AUF's decision on a permanent coach in early 2027 will shape whether Forlán's interim 4-3-3 becomes the long-term identity or a bridge to another outside appointment. The centenary World Cup of 2030, with matches scheduled in Montevideo, is the horizon everyone in Uruguay is looking toward.

## Managers who shaped them

- Alberto Suppici: the 1930 World Cup, the youngest-ever winning coach.
- Juan López: the 1950 Maracanazo.
- Óscar Tabárez: two spells, a record 220-plus matches, the 2010 semi-final and 2011 Copa.
- [Marcelo Bielsa](/managers/marcelo-bielsa): 2023-26.
- [Diego Forlán](/managers/diego-forlan-manager): interim from August 2026.

## Current squad and setup

Forlán's Uruguay in November 2026 is a 4-3-3 with Sergio Rochet in goal, Araújo and José María Giménez in central defence, Manuel Ugarte, Rodrigo Bentancur and Valverde, the captain, in midfield, and Darwin Núñez, Facundo Pellistri and Maximiliano Araújo in attack. Suárez and Cavani have retired. The AUF will decide on a permanent coach in early 2027; the targets are Copa América 2028 and the South American under-20 championship in January 2027, which Forlán also coaches.
