## History

Cameroon gave Africa its first great [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) story. Thomas N'Kono's 1982 team went out unbeaten, and Valery Nepomnyashchy's 1990 side beat holders Argentina in the opening match, with [Roger Milla](/players/roger-milla), 38, dancing at the corner flag after his goals against Romania and Colombia. A quarter-final against England was lost 3-2 after extra time, a result that convinced FIFA to expand Africa's allocation.

The [AFCON](/tournaments/afcon) titles of 1984 and 1988 were followed by Olympic gold in Sydney in 2000 and back-to-back AFCONs in 2000 and 2002, with [Samuel Eto'o](/players/samuel-etoo), Patrick Mboma and Rigobert Song. The 2003 Confederations Cup final was overshadowed by the death of Marc-Vivien Foé on the pitch in the semi-final. Eto'o became Africa's greatest striker but his generation never progressed beyond the World Cup group stage, and the 2014 squad's bonus dispute and ill-discipline were a low.

Hugo Broos' outsiders won the 2017 AFCON, and the 2022 World Cup brought a 1-0 win over Brazil, the first by an African team. Song, then Marc Brys, coached a team riven by conflict between the federation president Eto'o and the sports ministry. Cameroon finished second to Cape Verde in 2026 qualifying and lost the CAF play-off to DR Congo in November 2025, missing the World Cup. Brys departed and the identity of the coach in charge as of November 2026 could not be verified at the time of writing.

## Philosophy and style

The Indomitable Lions have always been about power, pace and audacity: Milla's improvisation, Eto'o's finishing, Song's aggression. The modern team plays a 4-3-3 with [André Onana](/players/andre-onana) as a sweeper-keeper, André-Frank Zambo Anguissa and Carlos Baleba running the midfield and Bryan Mbeumo cutting in from the right. Tactically they tend to a mid-block and [counter-attacks](/tactics/counter-attacking); organisational problems off the pitch, not talent, have been the limit.

Cameroonian football has been held back by administration rather than talent. The federation's disputes with the sports ministry, the 2014 bonus strike and Eto'o's turbulent presidency since 2021 have overshadowed a generation that includes Onana, Mbeumo, Zambo Anguissa and Baleba, all starters at major European clubs. The 2021 AFCON at home ended in third place and the 2024 edition in the round of 16. The rivalry with Nigeria, the 2000 AFCON final and the 2019 round of 16, and with Egypt, the 2008 and 2017 finals, are the continental fixtures that matter most.

## Managers who shaped them

- Claude Le Roy: the 1988 AFCON title.
- Valery Nepomnyashchy: Italia 90.
- Pierre Lechantre: the 2000 AFCON.
- Winfried Schäfer: the 2002 AFCON.
- Hugo Broos: the 2017 AFCON.
- Rigobert Song: the 2022 World Cup and the win over Brazil.

## Current squad and setup

Onana is in goal, Christopher Wooh and Nouhou Tolo anchor the defence, Zambo Anguissa and Baleba run midfield, Mbeumo and Georges-Kévin Nkoudou play wide, and Vincent Aboubakar, the captain, shares the striker role with Karl Etta Eyong and Frank Magri. AFCON 2027 in East Africa is the next target, and the federation must also settle its leadership disputes before 2030 qualifying.

## Legends

- [Samuel Eto'o](/players/samuel-etoo): 56 goals, four African Player of the Year awards, three Champions Leagues.
- [Roger Milla](/players/roger-milla): the 1990 hero and the oldest World Cup scorer at 42.
- [Thomas N'Kono](/players/thomas-nkono): the goalkeeper who inspired Gianluigi Buffon.
- [Rigobert Song](/players/rigobert-song): 137 caps and four World Cups.
- [Patrick Mboma](/players/patrick-mboma): 2000 African Player of the Year.
- [Lauren](/players/lauren): the Arsenal Invincible full-back.
- [Geremi](/players/geremi): Real Madrid and Chelsea midfielder, two AFCON titles.
- [Marc-Vivien Foé](/players/marc-vivien-foe): the midfielder lost in 2003.
