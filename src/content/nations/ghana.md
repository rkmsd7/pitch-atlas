## History

Ghana were the first African superpower, winning the [AFCON](/tournaments/afcon) in 1963 and 1965 under Charles Gyamfi and again in 1978 and 1982, the latter with the young Abedi Pelé. The country's enthusiasm for the game, encouraged by Kwame Nkrumah, produced a stream of talent, but a [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) place took until 2006, when Ratomir Dujković's team beat the Czech Republic and the United States to reach the round of 16.

The 2010 World Cup was the high point and the heartbreak: Milovan Rajevac's Black Stars beat the United States in the round of 16 and in the quarter-final against Uruguay had a last-minute penalty, after Luis Suárez's handball, which [Asamoah Gyan](/players/asamoah-gyan) struck against the bar before Ghana lost the shoot-out. They remain, with Cameroon, Senegal and Morocco, one of only a few African sides to reach the last eight.

The 2014 campaign collapsed amid bonus disputes, the 2015 AFCON final was lost on penalties to Ivory Coast, and the 2022 World Cup brought a group-stage exit despite a 3-2 win over South Korea. Otto Addo, who had led the 2022 team, returned in March 2024 and survived a dismal 2023 AFCON exit to qualify Ghana comfortably for 2026, where they lost 1-0 to Colombia in the round of 32.

## Philosophy and style

Ghanaian football is powerful and rhythmic, built on the Right to Dream and Hearts of Oak academies and a tradition of box-to-box midfielders from Essien to Partey. Addo, a Dortmund talent coach, sets up in a 4-2-3-1 with Thomas Partey and Alexander Djiku providing the base, [Mohammed Kudus](/players/mohammed-kudus) as the dribbling creator and Antoine Semenyo and Iñaki Williams running the channels. The team presses in bursts and relies on [counter-attacks](/tactics/counter-attacking); structure in possession has been the long-standing weakness.

Ghana's football culture is one of the richest in Africa, from the Hearts of Oak and Asante Kotoko rivalry to the Right to Dream academy that produced Kudus and Semenyo. The 2010 near-miss against Uruguay still frames the national conversation, and the Black Stars' tendency to produce their best when expectations are lowest, as in 2006 and 2010, and to implode when they are highest, as in 2014 and 2024, is a recurring theme. Addo's task is consistency, and the 2027 AFCON offers the chance to end a drought that stretches back to 1982.

## Managers who shaped them

- Charles Gyamfi: the 1963, 1965 and 1982 AFCON titles.
- Ratomir Dujković: the 2006 World Cup debut.
- Milovan Rajevac: the 2010 quarter-final.
- Avram Grant: the 2015 AFCON final.
- Otto Addo: 2022 and from 2024.

## Current squad and setup

Lawrence Ati-Zigi is in goal, Mohammed Salisu and Djiku partner in defence, Partey and Abdul Fatawu Issahaku share midfield duties, Kudus creates, and Jordan Ayew, the captain, plays alongside Semenyo, Williams and Ernest Nuamah. AFCON 2027 in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania is the next tournament, with the Black Meteors chasing a 2028 Olympic place.
