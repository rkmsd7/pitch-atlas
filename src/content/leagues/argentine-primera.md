## Overview

The Primera Division, run by the Liga Profesional de Futbol under the AFA, is Argentina's top flight, founded in 1891, the oldest league outside the United Kingdom. Since 2025 it has had 30 clubs, split into two zones of 15. Each year there are two tournaments, the Torneo Apertura (February to May) and the Torneo Clausura (July to December): 16 rounds within each zone plus interzonal derbies, then a 16-team single-match knockout from the round of 16 to a final at a neutral venue. The two tournament winners meet in the Trofeo de Campeones, and since November 2025 the AFA also crowns an annual-table "League Champion". Two clubs are relegated, one by the annual table and one by the three-year average.

The format is bewildering even to Argentines, but the football is the most passionate in the world. The Superclasico between [Boca Juniors](/clubs/boca-juniors) and [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate), the Avellaneda derby, Rosario's clasico and the Bombonera and Monumental atmospheres are football's loudest stages, and the league remains the great exporter of forwards and midfielders to Europe.

## History and eras

Alumni and Racing Club dominated the amateur era; professionalism arrived in 1931. [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate)'s La Maquina of the 1940s, Racing's 1967 Intercontinental Cup, Estudiantes' three straight Libertadores (1968-70) under Osvaldo Zubeldia, Independiente's four straight Libertadores (1972-75) and [Boca Juniors](/clubs/boca-juniors)' 1978 Intercontinental set the pattern of global success. [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona) left Argentinos Juniors and Boca for Europe in 1982; Newell's Old Boys sold a teenage [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) to Barcelona.

From 1991 to 2014 the league played Apertura and Clausura; Boca under Carlos Bianchi won four Libertadores and two Intercontinentals (2000, 2003), River under Marcelo Gallardo two Libertadores (2015, 2018, the Madrid final against Boca). Formats then lurched between 30-team single tables, 28-team cups and 26-team leagues.

In 2025 the league produced the strangest year in memory: Platense won their first ever title in the Apertura, beating Huracan 1-0 in the final on 1 June 2025; Rosario Central, with Angel Di Maria home from Europe, were declared 2025 "League Champion" by the AFA for topping the annual table, a title created by a rule change on 20 November 2025 that angered rivals; and Estudiantes won the Clausura on 13 December 2025, beating Racing 5-4 on penalties after 1-1 in Santiago del Estero. None of the five grandes (Boca, River, Racing, Independiente, San Lorenzo) won a title. On 24 May 2026 Belgrano de Cordoba won the Apertura 2026, their first top-flight championship, coming from behind to beat River Plate 3-2 in the Kempes with two late goals from Uvita Fernandez.

## Style and tactical trends

Argentine football is tactical and combative. [Marcelo Bielsa](/managers/marcelo-bielsa) (Newell's, Argentina, now Uruguay) is its great theorist: [man-marking](/tactics/man-marking) pressure, verticality and the 3-3-1-3. The Bianchi-Gallardo school is pragmatic possession with fast wingers and a classic number nine. Most clubs play a 4-3-3 or 4-2-3-1 with an enganche or playmaker in the Riquelme tradition still cherished. The knockout finals of the current format reward [low blocks](/tactics/low-block), [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and penalties; Estudiantes' 2025 title and Belgrano's 2026 comeback both came from tight, transitional games.

The league's young players (Echeverri, Mastantuono, Equi Fernandez, Garnacho's predecessors) leave early; veterans like Di Maria return. Lionel Scaloni's world champions of 2022 and 2026 finalists are mostly Europe-based, but every one of them came through this league.

## Great clubs and dynasties

[River Plate](/clubs/river-plate) have 38 titles and four Libertadores. [Boca Juniors](/clubs/boca-juniors) have 35 and six Libertadores, equal with Independiente's record seven only in reputation. Racing Club (18 titles), Independiente (16), San Lorenzo (15), Velez Sarsfield (10, plus the 1994 Intercontinental), Estudiantes (7), Newell's Old Boys (6), Rosario Central (5), Huracan (5), Argentinos Juniors (3), Lanus (3) and now Platense and Belgrano (one each) make up the roll of honour.

## 2026-27 season storylines

- **Clausura 2026.** The second tournament of 2026 runs to December with the knockout phase in November and December; the Trofeo de Campeones against Belgrano follows. Belgrano also earned a direct 2027 Libertadores place and a Supercopa Argentina final.
- **The grandes' drought.** Boca, River, Racing, Independiente and San Lorenzo went through 2025 without a league title and River lost the 2026 Apertura final; pressure at the Bombonera and the Monumental is immense.
- **Scaloni's future.** Argentina lost the 2026 World Cup final 1-0 to Spain after extra time (Enzo Fernandez sent off) after beating England in the semi; Lionel Scaloni's contract runs to December 2026 and his future was unresolved as of November.
- **The 30-club format** and the AFA's "League Champion" invention remain under fire from clubs and press; the planned reduction of the division is a running story.
- **Estudiantes de Rio Cuarto, Gimnasia de Mendoza and Deportivo Riestra** are among the smaller clubs enjoying top-flight status in the expanded league.
