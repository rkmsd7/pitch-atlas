## Overview

The Première Ligue, long known as Division 1 Féminine and sponsored as the Arkema Première Ligue, is the top tier of French women's football. It has been contested since 1974 and since 2024 has been run by a new professional league body, the Ligue Féminine de Football Professionnel, which introduced a championship play-off: after 22 regular-season rounds the top four meet in semi-finals and a final to decide the title. The top three qualify for the [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league).

The 2025-26 season ended with [OL Lyonnes](/clubs/lyon-women), as the club is now branded, winning a 19th title. Jonatan Giráldez's side topped the regular season with 60 points, then beat Paris FC 5-0 in the play-off final on 29 May 2026. Paris FC were second, [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain-women) third, and PSG's Romée Leuchter won the Golden Boot with 18 goals. Saint-Étienne were relegated, while Dijon were excluded from national competition and Lens reprieved.

## History and eras

Lyon's dominance has no parallel in European football. From 2007 to 2020 they won 14 consecutive titles and, between 2011 and 2022, eight Champions Leagues, with [Wendie Renard](/players/wendie-renard), [Ada Hegerberg](/players/ada-hegerberg), Eugénie Le Sommer, Amandine Henry and [Lucy Bronze](/players/lucy-bronze) among the players who made them the first fully professional superclub in the women's game. Jean-Michel Aulas's investment set the standard every other owner has since tried to follow.

PSG are the only club to have interrupted the sequence, winning the title in 2020-21 under Olivier Echouafni with [Kadidiatou Diani](/players/kadidiatou-diani) and Marie-Antoinette Katoto. Before Lyon, Juvisy (now Paris FC) and Toulouse were the historic powers; Juvisy's six titles came between 1992 and 2006.

## Style and tactical trends

French women's football is technical and quick. Lyon under Giráldez, who arrived from Barcelona in 2025, play a version of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and quick wide combinations, now featuring [Salma Paralluelo](/players/salma-paralluelo), signed in July 2026. PSG's youth project under Paulo César is more vertical; Paris FC, under new American ownership, press aggressively. The gap between the top three and the rest is the league's structural problem, and most mid-table clubs still operate on semi-professional budgets.

## 2026-27 season storylines

- Lyon's attempt to regain the Champions League after the 4-0 final defeat to Barcelona in Oslo in May 2026, with Paralluelo joining Hegerberg, Melchie Dumornay and Renard, now 36.
- PSG without Diani (sold in 2023) and now without Leuchter's goals being a given, as the youth project enters its second year.
- Paris FC's bid to become a genuine third force.
- The full move of Lyon's home games to the 59,000-seat Parc OL and whether crowds follow.
- France's qualification for the [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup), secured in June 2026, and the league's role in supplying Laurent Bonadei's squad.
