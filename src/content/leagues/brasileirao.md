## Overview

The Campeonato Brasileiro Serie A, the Brasileirao, is Brazil's national championship: 20 clubs, 38 rounds, played from late March or April to December across the calendar year. The top four qualify for the Copa Libertadores group stage, fifth and sixth for its preliminaries (with further places through cup winners), seventh to twelfth for the Copa Sudamericana, and the bottom four are relegated to Serie B. Teams also play state championships in January to March and the Copa do Brasil through the year, making Brazilian seasons the most congested in the world.

Since the mid-2010s the league has become the dominant force in South American club football, its clubs winning every Copa Libertadores from 2019 to 2025. Television money, the [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) juggernaut and the return of stars from Europe (Neymar to Santos, Hulk, Arrascaeta, Depay to Corinthians) have raised the standard.

## History and eras

The Taca Brasil began in 1959 and the Roberto Gomes Pedrosa tournament followed; the modern Brasileirao dates from 1971. Formats changed constantly, including play-offs and group stages, until 2003 when the 20-team, double round-robin pontos corridos format was adopted. Santos with [Pele](/players/pele), [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) with Zico, Sao Paulo with Telê Santana (two Intercontinental Cups, 1992 and 1993), and Corinthians with Socrates defined the early decades.

In the points era [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) under Abel Ferreira (2022, 2023) and Flamengo (2019 under Jorge Jesus, 2020, 2024-25) have been the two superpowers, with [Botafogo](/clubs/botafogo)'s 2024 double of league and Libertadores the great upset. In 2025 Flamengo won the Libertadores and then, four days later on 3 December, beat Ceara 1-0 at the Maracana to seal the Brasileirao, their fourth in the points era (eight or nine overall depending on the disputed 1987 title). Arrascaeta was the player of the season, Kaio Jorge of Cruzeiro the top scorer with 21, and Neymar's Santos escaped relegation on the final weekend.

## Style and tactical trends

Brazilian football is in a tactical argument with itself. The European-trained generation (Abel Ferreira, Jorge Jesus, Renato Paiva) brought structured [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), compact mid-blocks and the Brazilian 4-2-2-2 [box midfield](/tactics/box-midfield) inherited from the 1982 side. [Fernando Diniz](/managers/fernando-diniz)' Fluminense, Libertadores winners in 2023, answered with [relationism](/tactics/relationism), the approximation-based, improvised style that European analysts now treat as the main alternative to Guardiola's grid.

Flamengo under Filipe Luis play a modern pressing 4-2-3-1 with a Brazilian looseness in the final third; Palmeiras are the most physically organised; Red Bull Bragantino import the Leipzig pressing model. [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti)'s Selecao, built for the 2026 World Cup, draws most of its squad from Europe but its tactical vocabulary from this league.

## Great clubs and dynasties

[Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) have 12 Brasileiraos and three Libertadores. [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) have eight (nine by their count) and four Libertadores, including 2019, 2022 and 2025. Santos (8, with Pele), Corinthians (7, plus the 2012 Club World Cup), Sao Paulo (6, and three Club World Cups), Fluminense, Cruzeiro, Vasco, [Botafogo](/clubs/botafogo), Internacional, Gremio and Atletico Mineiro complete the twelve great clubs.

- **The 2026 title race.** The 2026 Brasileirao runs to December. The sources available for this page conflicted: Flamengo led after 26 rounds in September with 54 points, Palmeiras led several mid-season tables, and a later report put Red Bull Bragantino top for the first time since April. As of November 2026 the leader should be read from the live table.
- **The newcomers.** Mirassol, the 2025 revelation (their coach Rafael Guanaes was coach of the year), stayed up; Remo, Coritiba and Chapecoense came up from Serie B.
- **Neymar and Santos.** Neymar's return kept Santos up in 2025 and his fitness remains the league's soap opera.
- **Ancelotti's Brazil** reached the 2026 World Cup with the league's best (Estevao and Endrick had already gone to Europe) and the Selecao coach's future after the tournament is tied to the Brasileirao's talent pipeline.
- **Libertadores.** Flamengo, Palmeiras, Botafogo and the 2025 top four are in the 2026 Copa Libertadores, whose final is in late November.
