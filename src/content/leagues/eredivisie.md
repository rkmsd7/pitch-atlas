## Overview

The Eredivisie is the Netherlands' top division: 18 clubs, 34 rounds, August to May. The champion goes straight into the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) league phase, the runner-up into its qualifying rounds, and third and fourth into the Europa and Conference Leagues, with a play-off deciding one further European place. The bottom club is relegated and the 16th- and 17th-placed sides enter promotion/relegation play-offs with the Eerste Divisie.

The league's identity is development. [Ajax](/clubs/ajax)'s De Toekomst, [PSV Eindhoven](/clubs/psv-eindhoven)'s academy and [Feyenoord](/clubs/feyenoord)'s Varkenoord produce more exported players per head than any other league in Europe, and the attacking 4-3-3 is taught from under-8 level. The Eredivisie is a selling league but a watchable one: scoring rates are among the highest in Europe.

## History and eras

Dutch football went professional in 1954 and the Eredivisie began in 1956. [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) under [Rinus Michels](/managers/rinus-michels) and [Johan Cruyff](/players/johan-cruyff) invented [total football](/tactics/total-football) and won three European Cups in a row (1971-73); [Feyenoord](/clubs/feyenoord) had won the first Dutch European Cup in 1970. [PSV Eindhoven](/clubs/psv-eindhoven), with Gullit, Koeman and Romario, won the 1988 treble under Guus Hiddink. Louis van Gaal's Ajax of 1995 (Seedorf, Davids, Kluivert, the de Boers) won the Champions League with a team of academy graduates, the last Dutch side to do so.

The 21st century has been a three-way carousel. Ajax won four in a row from 2011 to 2014 under Frank de Boer, PSV three under Phillip Cocu, Ajax again under Erik ten Hag with the 2019 Champions League semi-final team. Feyenoord won in 2017 and, under Arne Slot, in 2023. Then PSV took three in a row, 2024, 2025 and 2026 under Peter Bosz, the 2025-26 title sealed with five games to spare after Feyenoord failed to win at Volendam, finishing on 84 points with 101 goals. It was PSV's 27th championship.

## Style and tactical trends

The Eredivisie is the cradle of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play). Michels, Cruyff and van Gaal built the five-lane model here; [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) learned it in Barcelona from Dutch coaches. Every top Dutch side still plays a 4-3-3 with wingers who hold width, a pivot who builds, and full-backs who overlap. The [high line](/tactics/high-line) and offside trap are a Dutch inheritance.

Modern trends are more varied. Bosz's PSV played a fast, vertical pressing 4-2-3-1 with Luuk de Jong as a target; Slot's Feyenoord was the best organised possession side in the league; AZ Alkmaar's academy produces structured, intelligent teams; Ajax under successive coaches has struggled to rediscover its identity. The league's open, transition-heavy nature means scorelines of 4-3 are common, which both develops attackers and explains why Dutch defenders are often sold with question marks.

## Great clubs and dynasties

[Ajax](/clubs/ajax) have 36 titles and four European Cups. [PSV Eindhoven](/clubs/psv-eindhoven) have 27 and the 1988 European Cup. [Feyenoord](/clubs/feyenoord) have 16 and the 1970 European Cup plus two UEFA Cups. The three have won every title since 1982 except AZ Alkmaar's in 2009 and FC Twente's in 2010 under Steve McClaren. Sparta Rotterdam, Go Ahead Eagles, Willem II, Heerenveen and FC Utrecht are the historic clubs below them.

## 2026-27 season storylines

- **A four-way race.** As of early November the sources disagree on the order, but Feyenoord, AZ Alkmaar, PSV and Ajax were all within a few points of the top. Feyenoord were reported top with 23 points after nine games by one table; a Flashscore report had AZ taking top spot with a two-point lead over Feyenoord before an international break, after PSV's first defeat. Treat the live table as the authority.
- **Feyenoord under Giovanni van Bronckhorst**, back at De Kuip, have the league's most settled structure.
- **PSV's attempt at a fourth straight title** began with the loss of several key players abroad and a changed coaching staff; the three-peat champions were third in the most recent snapshot.
- **Ajax**, who reportedly hit the post three times and had 33 attempts in a draw with Excelsior, remain the great frustrated giant.
- **Europe.** PSV and Feyenoord are both in the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) league phase. The Dutch coefficient, boosted by recent European runs, now secures the champion a direct place.
- **Promoted.** Telstar and ADO Den Haag are the newcomers; SC Cambuur and Excelsior also feature in the 18.
