## Overview

The Super Lig, officially the Trendyol Super Lig, is Turkey's top division: 18 clubs, 34 rounds, August to May. The champion enters the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) league phase (Turkey's coefficient has risen enough to secure this), the runner-up goes into the qualifying rounds, third and the cup winners go to the Europa and Conference Leagues, and the bottom three are relegated to the 1. Lig.

Turkish football is loud, partisan and Istanbul-centred. [Galatasaray](/clubs/galatasaray), [Fenerbahce](/clubs/fenerbahce) and Besiktas have won all but seven titles in the league's history, and their derbies are among the most hostile in the world. The league has also become a destination for established stars, Osimhen, Icardi, Mourinho (at Fenerbahce in 2024-25), Lukaku's contemporaries, funded by television money and the clubs' vast memberships.

## History and eras

The national league began in 1959; before that, regional championships in Istanbul, Ankara and Izmir. Fenerbahce won the first two titles, Galatasaray and Besiktas followed, and Trabzonspor broke the Istanbul monopoly with six titles between 1976 and 1984. Galatasaray's golden age under Fatih Terim brought four straight titles (1997-2000) and the 2000 UEFA Cup, beating Arsenal on penalties in Copenhagen, the only European trophy won by a Turkish club.

The 2000s were split: Fenerbahce under Daum and Zico, Besiktas, Bursaspor's 2010 shock. Fenerbahce's involvement in the 2011 match-fixing scandal saw them banned from Europe; Galatasaray won four in a row (2012-15) with Sneijder and Drogba. Trabzonspor's 2022 title ended a 38-year wait. Then Galatasaray, under Okan Buruk, won four in a row again from 2023 to 2026, the 2025-26 title secured on 9 May 2026 with a 4-2 win over Antalyaspor. They finished on 77 points to Fenerbahce's 74 and conceded only 30 goals. Victor Osimhen was again the club's top scorer, though sources differ on the league's Golden Boot (Paul Onuachu of Trabzonspor is listed with 18). Antalyaspor, Kayserispor and Fatih Karagumruk were relegated.

## Style and tactical trends

Turkish teams play with emotion and verticality. The big three favour a 4-2-3-1 with a target striker (Osimhen, Dzeko, Icardi) and wide players who cross early, [wing play](/tactics/wing-play) in its most traditional form, with [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and physical centre-backs. Fatih Terim's Galatasaray introduced a more possession-oriented 4-4-2; Buruk's current side is pragmatic and powerful, happy to defend in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) away in Europe and dominate at home.

Foreign coaches have brought variety: Mourinho's Fenerbahce in 2024-25 was a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) machine; Besiktas under a succession of Europeans have tried pressing systems. The Anatolian clubs (Trabzonspor, Samsunspor, Kocaelispor, Eyupspor, Goztepe) tend towards compact, transition football, and the atmosphere in places like Trabzon makes the home game a tactic in itself.

## Great clubs and dynasties

[Galatasaray](/clubs/galatasaray) have 26 titles and the 2000 UEFA Cup and Super Cup. [Fenerbahce](/clubs/fenerbahce) have 19 and the most members of any Turkish club. Besiktas have 16, the last in 2021. Trabzonspor have seven. Bursaspor (2010) and Istanbul Basaksehir (2020, under Okan Buruk) are the only other champions.

## 2026-27 season storylines

- **Galatasaray's fifth in a row.** Buruk's champions began the defence of a title run that already matches the club's 1997-2000 sequence. The November 2026 table could not be verified for this page; the race between Galatasaray and Fenerbahce should be checked live.
- **Fenerbahce's wait.** Without a title since 2014, the Yellow Canaries' 74-point 2025-26 was their best in years and still not enough.
- **Europe.** Both Istanbul giants are in the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) league phase, a measure of how far Turkey's coefficient has climbed.
- **The 18.** Alongside the big three and Trabzonspor: Samsunspor, Kocaelispor, Eyupspor, Goztepe, Basaksehir, Kasimpasa, Konyaspor, Alanyaspor, Rizespor, Gaziantep, Genclerbirligi, Erzurum BB, Amed SFK and Corum FK, the last three promoted from the 1. Lig.
