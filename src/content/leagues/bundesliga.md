## Overview

The Bundesliga is Germany's top division: 18 clubs, 34 rounds, from August to May with a winter break. The top four qualify for the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league), the bottom two are relegated to 2. Bundesliga and the 16th-placed club faces a two-legged play-off against the third-placed second-division side. The 50+1 rule keeps clubs majority-owned by their members, which explains the cheap tickets, standing terraces and the highest average attendances in world football.

It is also a high-scoring, high-pressing league that has exported its coaching ideas across Europe: [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing), [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) and the Red Bull school of [Ralf Rangnick](/managers/ralf-rangnick) and [Julian Nagelsmann](/managers/julian-nagelsmann) all come from here.

## History and eras

Founded in 1963, the league's first champions were 1. FC Koln. Borussia Monchengladbach and [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) traded titles through the 1970s, the Bayern of Beckenbauer, Muller and Maier winning three European Cups (1974-76). Hamburg (1983 European Cup), Werder Bremen, Kaiserslautern (champions in 1998 as a promoted side) and VfB Stuttgart all took titles before the Bayern era properly began.

[Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) won the 1997 Champions League and, under [Jurgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), back-to-back titles in 2011 and 2012 with the gegenpressing that changed the sport. Then Bayern won eleven in a row from 2013 to 2023, under Heynckes (the 2013 treble), [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), Niko Kovac, [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick) (the 2020 treble) and Nagelsmann. [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso)'s [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) broke the run in 2024 with an unbeaten season. Vincent Kompany restored Bayern's grip in 2025 and 2026, with [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) scoring at a rate no one had seen since Gerd Muller: 73 goals in all competitions in 2025-26, enough to win the 2026 Ballon d'Or.

## Style and tactical trends

The Bundesliga is the pressing league. Rangnick's Hoffenheim and [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig), Klopp's Dortmund, Nagelsmann's Hoffenheim and Leipzig, Thomas Tuchel's Mainz and Dortmund all built on winning the ball high and attacking within seconds. Games are open, transitions frequent, and goals plentiful (the league averages more than three a game). The [high line](/tactics/high-line) is standard; Flick's Bayern of 2020 played the highest in Europe.

Possession football came with Guardiola's Bayern (the [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) of Lahm and Alaba) and Alonso's Leverkusen (a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with Grimaldo and Frimpong as wing-backs). Kompany's Bayern combine positional control with Kane as a classic target nine. Among the chasing pack, [Eintracht Frankfurt](/clubs/eintracht-frankfurt) and [VfB Stuttgart](/clubs/vfb-stuttgart) play vertical, pressing football; Dortmund under Niko Kovac have returned to transition speed.

## Great clubs and dynasties

[Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) have 35 Bundesliga titles and six European Cups, the German superclub against which all others are measured. [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) have five Bundesliga titles, the 1997 Champions League and the Westfalenstadion's Yellow Wall. Monchengladbach (5), Werder Bremen (4), Hamburg and Stuttgart (3 each) are the historic rivals; [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) (2024) and Wolfsburg (2009) are the single-title outliers. [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig), founded in 2009, are the controversial modern contender, two German Cups and regular Champions League football.

## 2026-27 season storylines

- **Bayern unbeaten.** Kompany's champions were top in early November, unbeaten after eight matches (five wins, three draws, 27 goals scored, eight conceded), with Dortmund their closest challengers.
- **Kane's Ballon d'Or.** The 2026 award on 26 October in London crowned Kane's 73-goal season; he began 2026-27 as the league's top scorer again.
- **New coaches.** Niko Kovac at Dortmund, Kasper Hjulmand at Leverkusen after Alonso's departure for Real Madrid and then Chelsea, and Ole Werner at RB Leipzig, who sold Yan Diomande to Real Madrid for around 125 million euros in the summer.
- **Klopp's Germany.** Jurgen Klopp took the national job on 24 July 2026 after Germany's early World Cup exit under Nagelsmann; his first squads leaned heavily on Bayern, Leverkusen and Stuttgart players.
- **Upcoming.** Bayern face Koln on 21 November and Hamburg on 28 November, with the Champions League trip to Lille on 25 November in between.
