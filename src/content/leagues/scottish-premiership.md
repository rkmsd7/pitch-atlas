## Overview

The Scottish Premiership is the top division of the Scottish Professional Football League, formed in 2013 from the merger of the Scottish Premier League and the Football League; the national championship dates to 1890. Twelve clubs play each other three times (33 rounds), then split into a top six and a bottom six for five further matches, making 38. The champion enters the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) qualifying rounds (the Scottish coefficient has at times given direct entry), second goes to Champions League qualifying or the Europa League, the bottom club is relegated and the 11th-placed side plays off against the Championship.

The league is defined by Glasgow's Old Firm, [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) and [Rangers](/clubs/rangers), who have won every title since Aberdeen's in 1985, and by crowds and atmosphere out of proportion to the country's size: Celtic Park and Ibrox each hold around 50,000 to 60,000 and sell out.

## History and eras

Dumbarton and Rangers shared the first title in 1891. Celtic, founded by Irish immigrants in 1888, and Rangers quickly became the dominant pair. Celtic's Lisbon Lions under Jock Stein won the 1967 European Cup, the first British club to do so, with a team born within 30 miles of Glasgow, and nine titles in a row (1966-74). Rangers under Graeme Souness and Walter Smith matched the nine from 1989 to 1997, signing Gascoigne and Laudrup. Aberdeen under [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) won three titles and the 1983 Cup Winners' Cup; Dundee United won in 1983 and reached the 1987 UEFA Cup final.

Rangers' financial collapse in 2012 sent them to the fourth tier; Celtic won nine in a row again (2012-20) before Steven Gerrard's Rangers took the 2021 title. Celtic then won five straight under Ange Postecoglou and Brendan Rodgers. The 2025-26 season was extraordinary: Rodgers resigned in October 2025, his successor Wilfried Nancy lasted 33 days, a third manager took over, and Hearts led for much of the campaign before Celtic beat them 3-1 at Celtic Park on the final day to win by two points, a record 56th Scottish title and their fifth in a row.

## Style and tactical trends

Scottish football has shifted from its physical, [direct-play](/tactics/direct-play) reputation towards continental coaching. Postecoglou's Celtic played a relentless, high-possession 4-3-3 with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs); Rodgers refined it. Rangers under Gerrard, Giovanni van Bronckhorst and Philippe Clement used a pressing 4-3-3 and [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing). Hearts' 2025-26 challenge was built on a disciplined [low block](/tactics/low-block) and [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces). Outside the top two, [man-marking](/tactics/man-marking) and mid-blocks remain common, and winter pitches still reward long throws and second balls.

## Great clubs and dynasties

[Celtic](/clubs/celtic) have 56 titles, the 1967 European Cup and two nine-in-a-row runs. [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) have 55, the 1972 Cup Winners' Cup and the 2008 and 2022 UEFA Cup/Europa League finals. Aberdeen (4 titles), Hearts (4), Hibernian (4), Dundee United, Dundee, Motherwell, Kilmarnock and Third Lanark are the other champions. Heart of Midlothian's near miss in 2026 was the closest any club had come to breaking the duopoly since Aberdeen in 1985.

## 2026-27 season storylines

- **Celtic's sixth in a row.** The champions began the season with the manager who finished 2025-26 in charge; the exact identity of Celtic's and Rangers' 2026-27 managers could not be verified for this page and should be checked against the clubs' sites.
- **Hearts' response.** After finishing second by two points, Tynecastle's ambition is the league's biggest sub-plot.
- **Rangers' rebuild.** Under American ownership since 2025, Rangers are trying to close the gap and return to the Champions League.
- **The Scotland job.** Sebastien Pocognoli, formerly of Union Saint-Gilloise, coaches Scotland after the 2026 World Cup cycle.
- **The 12.** Celtic, Rangers, Hearts, Hibernian, Aberdeen, Dundee United, Dundee, Motherwell, Kilmarnock, St Mirren, St Johnstone and Falkirk make up the division.
