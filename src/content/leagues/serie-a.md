## Overview

Serie A is the top division of Italian football, contested by 20 clubs over 38 rounds. The champion receives the Scudetto, the little shield worn on the shirt the following season. The top four qualify for the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) league phase (Italy took a fifth place via coefficient in 2024-25), fifth goes to the Europa League and the bottom three are relegated to Serie B.

Italian football is the world's great school of defending and of tactical flexibility. Its coaches export everywhere: [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) to Brazil, [Antonio Conte](/managers/antonio-conte) and [Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi) to England, [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca) to Manchester City. The league also produced the 2026 Ballon d'Or's Socrates Award winner's opponents in the Champions League final: Inter lost the 2025 final 5-0 to PSG, the moment that prompted Simone Inzaghi's exit and Cristian Chivu's promotion.

## History and eras

The national championship dates to 1898; the single round-robin Serie A began in 1929. [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) won five in a row in the 1930s, Torino's Grande Torino five more in the 1940s before the Superga air disaster of 1949. The 1960s were Milan's: [Helenio Herrera](/managers/helenio-herrera)'s [catenaccio](/tactics/catenaccio) Inter won two European Cups, Nereo Rocco's [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) two more.

The 1980s and 1990s were Serie A's golden age, when it was the best league in the world. Diego Maradona took [Napoli](/clubs/napoli) to two titles (1987, 1990). [Arrigo Sacchi](/managers/arrigo-sacchi)'s Milan, with Gullit, van Basten and Rijkaard, revolutionised pressing and won back-to-back European Cups (1989, 1990); Fabio Capello's Milan went 58 games unbeaten. Juventus under Lippi, Inter with Ronaldo, [Lazio](/clubs/lazio) and [Roma](/clubs/roma) with Totti all won titles around the turn of the century.

Calciopoli in 2006 stripped Juventus of two titles and sent them to Serie B. Inter won five in a row, including [Jose Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s 2010 treble. Juventus then won nine straight from 2012 to 2020 under Conte and Allegri. Inter (2021), Milan (2022), Napoli (2023, with Spalletti), Inter (2024) and Conte's Napoli (2025) made the 2020s the most open decade in memory. In 2025-26 Chivu's Inter won their 21st Scudetto.

## Style and tactical trends

Serie A is where the [back three](/tactics/back-three) never went out of fashion. Conte's 3-5-2 at Juventus and Inter, Gasperini's man-marking 3-4-3 at [Atalanta](/clubs/atalanta), Inzaghi's fluent 3-5-2 at Inter and [Cesc Fabregas](/managers/cesc-fabregas)' positional 3-4-3 at Como are the league's signature shapes. [Man-marking](/tactics/man-marking) survives here more than anywhere, both in high pressing and in the deep [low blocks](/tactics/low-block) every mid-table side can deploy.

The other Italian art is the transition. Napoli's 2025 title was built on [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) to Lukaku and McTominay's second-ball runs; Inter's European runs on quick wing-back overlaps. Italian goalkeeping and [set-piece](/tactics/set-pieces) organisation (Gianni Vio's routines) remain world-leading. The tempo is slower than England's, the structures tighter, and the margin for error smaller.

## Great clubs and dynasties

[Juventus](/clubs/juventus) have 36 titles (38 by their own count) and two European Cups. [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan) have 21 and three European Cups, and have never been relegated. [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) have 19 titles and seven European Cups, second only to Real Madrid. [Napoli](/clubs/napoli) have four, two with Maradona and two in the 2020s. [Roma](/clubs/roma) and [Lazio](/clubs/lazio) have three and two; [Fiorentina](/clubs/fiorentina) and [Atalanta](/clubs/atalanta), with the 2024 Europa League, are the great provincial powers.

## 2026-27 season storylines

- **A five-way race.** Inter led after seven rounds on 19 points, with Lazio, Milan, Napoli and Juventus all close; Juventus beat Napoli 2-0 on 1 November. Early-season analysis said no team had yet pulled clear.
- **New benches.** Luciano Spalletti is in charge at Juventus; Daniele De Rossi at Genoa; Ivan Juric at Monza; Carlos Cuesta, the former Arsenal assistant, at Parma; Eusebio Di Francesco at Lecce. Fiorentina sacked Fabio Grosso in September and Venezia sacked Paolo Stroppa in October.
- **Como and Fabregas.** [Cesc Fabregas](/managers/cesc-fabregas)' Como are the league's most watched project, a positional-play team funded by Indonesian owners.
- **Inter after Chivu's title.** The champions kept the Inzaghi structure and lead the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) group of Italian hopefuls alongside Juventus.
- **Gattuso's Italy.** Gennaro Gattuso coaches the national team after the 2026 World Cup; Serie A's players remain the Azzurri's base.
