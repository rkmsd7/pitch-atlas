## Overview

The Primeira Liga, branded Liga Portugal Betclic, is Portugal's top division: 18 clubs, 34 rounds, August to May. The top two qualify directly for the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) league phase, third enters the qualifiers, fourth and the cup winners go to the Europa League, the bottom two are relegated and the 16th-placed club plays off.

It is a league of three giants and fifteen others, and of exceptional coaches and scouts. [Jose Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) (Porto 2004), Andre Villas-Boas, Leonardo Jardim, Ruben Amorim and Sergio Conceicao all built their names here; the clubs' Brazilian and African scouting networks turn modest fees into huge profits (Haaland's predecessors at Benfica, Darwin, Enzo Fernandez and Joao Felix among the biggest sales).

## History and eras

The national championship began in 1934 (Porto won the first) and Benfica, Porto and Sporting have won every title except two: Belenenses in 1946 and Boavista in 2001. [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) dominated the 1960s with Eusebio, winning the European Cup in 1961 and 1962 and reaching three more finals. [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) won the 1964 Cup Winners' Cup and most of their titles before 1982. [Porto](/clubs/porto) became the modern power from the 1980s: European Cup in 1987 under Artur Jorge, then the Champions League in 2004 under Mourinho, with five straight titles from 1995 to 1999 and five more from 2006 to 2009.

The 2010s were Benfica's (seven titles, four in a row 2014-17) and Porto's; Sporting broke a 19-year drought under Amorim in 2021 and won again in 2024 and 2025 with Viktor Gyokeres scoring at absurd rates. In 2025-26 Porto, under the former Ajax coach Francesco Farioli and with Villas-Boas as president, won their 31st title, clinching it with a 1-0 home win over Alverca and finishing on 88 points (28 wins, four draws, two defeats). Luis Suarez, the Colombian striker, was the league's top scorer with 26.

## Style and tactical trends

Portuguese football is a coaching culture. Its clubs play structured, possession-based 4-3-3 and 4-2-3-1 systems with aggressive full-backs and technically polished midfielders, and the national coaching schools (the Portuguese FA's courses and the universities of Porto and Lisbon) emphasise tactical periodisation, Vitor Frade's method of training everything through the game model. Mourinho, Villas-Boas and Amorim are its products.

Amorim's Sporting popularised the [back three](/tactics/back-three) in Portugal; Farioli's Porto play a Dutch-influenced [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps); Benfica under a succession of coaches have swung between Jorge Jesus' attacking 4-4-2 and more pragmatic transition football. Smaller clubs, Braga, Vitoria de Guimaraes, Famalicao, Gil Vicente, play an open game that produces goals and exportable talent, while the Brazilian contingent (hundreds of players) gives the league a South American flavour.

## Great clubs and dynasties

[Benfica](/clubs/benfica) have 38 titles and two European Cups. [Porto](/clubs/porto) have 31, two European Cups and two UEFA Cups/Europa Leagues (2003, 2011). [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) have 21 and the 1964 Cup Winners' Cup. Braga are the perennial fourth force, regular Europa League participants; Boavista (2001) and Belenenses (1946) are the only other champions.
