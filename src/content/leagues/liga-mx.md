## Overview

Liga MX is Mexico's top division: 18 clubs playing two separate tournaments each year, the Apertura (July to December) and the Clausura (January to May). Each is a 17-round single round-robin followed by the Liguilla, a knockout play-off; since 2023 the top eight go straight through (earlier formats used a play-in). The Liguilla final is two-legged. Relegation was suspended in 2020 and replaced by fines, with a return to promotion and relegation promised for later in the decade.

It is the richest and best-attended league in the Americas, with huge television audiences in Mexico and the United States, and the base of the [Mexico](/nations/mexico) national team. Clubs are often owned by television networks (Televisa's America) or conglomerates (Grupo Pachuca, Grupo Orlegi), and the Estadio Azteca, host of the 2026 World Cup's opening match, is its cathedral.

## History and eras

The professional league began in 1943-44, won by Asturias. The 1950s and 1960s belonged to Chivas de Guadalajara's "Campeonisimo", seven titles in nine years with an all-Mexican squad, a policy the club still keeps. Club America, Chivas's great rival, won repeatedly from the 1960s with foreign stars. Cruz Azul won six in the 1970s; Pumas UNAM produced Hugo Sanchez; Toluca and Pachuca emerged in the late 1990s. The short-tournament format was introduced in 1996, doubling the number of champions.

The 2010s brought Tigres and Monterrey, funded by Cemex and FEMSA, into the elite, and America's dominance under Andre Jardine with three straight titles (Apertura 2023, Clausura 2024, Apertura 2024) made them the first team to achieve a tricampeonato in the short-tournament era. Toluca won the Apertura 2025. On 24 May 2026, Cruz Azul won the Clausura 2026 final 2-1 on aggregate against Pumas, a goalless first leg followed by Rodolfo Rotondi's stoppage-time winner at the Olimpico Universitario, their tenth league title; Joel Huiqui became the first Mexican coach to win the league since Ignacio Ambriz in 2020. Cruz Azul then beat Toluca for the Campeon de Campeones in Carson, California on 25 July.

## Style and tactical trends

Liga MX is technical and attacking, with a strong South American influence: Argentine and Uruguayan strikers and coaches (Jardine is Brazilian, Robert Dante Siboldi Uruguayan, Martin Anselmi and Antonio Mohamed Argentine) run most of the big clubs. The default is a 4-2-3-1 or 4-3-3 with overlapping full-backs; the altitude of Mexico City (2,200 metres), Toluca (2,600) and Pachuca makes the home-away dynamic brutal and rewards [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) from visiting teams tired in the second half.

The Liguilla culture favours [low blocks](/tactics/low-block) and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) in two-legged ties, which is why the regular-season leader so often falls. Set pieces and big centre-forwards remain decisive. Young Mexicans are pulled towards MLS and Europe, but the league's wage levels keep more at home than in most of Latin America.

## Great clubs and dynasties

Club America have 16 titles and seven CONCACAF Champions Cups. Chivas have 12 and the all-Mexican policy. Cruz Azul reached ten in 2026; Toluca have 11, Tigres eight, Leon eight, Pumas seven, Monterrey five, Pachuca seven. Pumas, Cruz Azul and America are Mexico City's three.

## 2026-27 season storylines

- **Apertura 2026 three-way tie.** After 15 of 17 rounds, America, Chivas and Cruz Azul were level on 29 points and all qualified for the Liguilla, with Atlas, Toluca, Leon, Monterrey and Tijuana filling the eight direct play-off places. Tigres were eliminated from Liguilla contention. The Liguilla runs through November and December 2026.
- **World Cup glow.** The Azteca staged the 2026 World Cup's opening match in June; the tournament's success in Mexican cities (Guadalajara, Monterrey) lifted attendances in the Apertura.
- **Mexican coaches.** Huiqui's Clausura triumph revived the debate about domestic managers in a league run by South Americans.
- **Relegation return.** The league's promise to restore promotion and relegation remains unfulfilled; Atlante are among the sides in the 18 who benefited from the freeze.
- **Leagues Cup.** The cross-border competition with MLS, held in July and August, gives Liga MX clubs a route to the CONCACAF Champions Cup.
