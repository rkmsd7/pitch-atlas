## Overview

The Women's Super League is the top division of English women's football and, by most measures, the strongest league in the world. It began in 2011 as an eight-team summer competition, switched to a winter calendar in 2017-18, became fully professional in 2018-19 and, since 2024, has been run by Women's Professional Leagues Ltd, a company owned by the clubs and independent of the FA. Expansion to 14 teams for 2026-27 brought Birmingham City, Crystal Palace and Charlton Athletic up from WSL2, with Leicester City going down after losing a relegation play-off on penalties.

Attendances have transformed the league. [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal-women) play at the Emirates, [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women) moved all home league games to Stamford Bridge for 2026-27, and [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united-women) use Old Trafford for big fixtures. Broadcast deals with Sky Sports and the BBC run to 2030.

## History and eras

The first decade belonged to Arsenal and then [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city-women), who won the 2016 title in their third season. Emma Hayes's Chelsea then built the greatest dynasty the league has seen: seven titles in eight seasons from 2017-18 to 2024-25, including the 2024-25 campaign in which [Sonia Bompastor](/managers/sonia-bompastor), Hayes's successor, went the entire season unbeaten in her first year. [Sam Kerr](/players/sam-kerr), [Fran Kirby](/players/fran-kirby), [Lauren James](/players/lauren-james) and [Hannah Hampton](/players/hannah-hampton) were the faces of that era.

Arsenal, meanwhile, won the [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) in 2025 under Renée Slegers, beating Barcelona in Lisbon, and the 2026 FIFA Women's Champions Cup, but had not won the league since 2018-19. The 2025-26 season belonged to Manchester City: Andrée Jeglertz's side won 18 of 22, scored 62 goals, finished four points clear of Arsenal and completed a double with the FA Cup, their first league title since 2016. [Khadija Shaw](/players/khadija-shaw) won the Golden Boot with 21 goals and [Vivianne Miedema](/players/vivianne-miedema) became the first player to score ten or more in five different WSL seasons.

## Style and tactical trends

The WSL is a league of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with English intensity. Chelsea under Bompastor press high from a 4-2-3-1 and attack through wide overloads; Arsenal under Slegers build patiently and rely on [Mariona Caldentey](/players/mariona-caldentey) and [Alessia Russo](/players/alessia-russo) in the half-spaces; City play through Yui Hasegawa and attack with Lauren Hemp and Shaw. The arrival of [Alexia Putellas](/players/alexia-putellas) and [Kadidiatou Diani](/players/kadidiatou-diani) at London City Lionesses in summer 2026 shows how far the money and ambition have spread beyond the top three. Physicality and [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) still decide more games here than in [Liga F](/leagues/liga-f).

## 2026-27 season storylines

- Can City retain the title against a Chelsea side that has lost Kerr but added Alyssa Thompson and Naomi Girma, and an Arsenal squad strengthened by Olivia Smith and Michelle Agyemang's emergence?
- Manchester United's new era under Eva Olid, appointed in August 2026 after Marc Skinner's departure, with Fridolina Rolfö and Lea Schüller among the signings.
- London City Lionesses, with Putellas as captain, trying to break into the Champions League places.
- The 14-team league means 26 rounds and a tighter winter schedule alongside the expanded Champions League league phase.
- Three promoted clubs, including a Birmingham side backed by the NFL's Tom Brady, trying to avoid becoming the first relegated team of the new format.
