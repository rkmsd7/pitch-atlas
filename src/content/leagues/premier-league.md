## Overview

The Premier League is English football's top division, formed in 1992 when the First Division clubs broke away from the Football League to sell their own television rights. Twenty clubs play 38 rounds from August to May. The top five now qualify for the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) league phase (England earned the extra place through UEFA's coefficient system in 2024-25 and again in 2025-26), the sixth goes to the Europa League, and the bottom three are relegated to the Championship.

Its wealth is the defining fact. Domestic and overseas broadcast deals worth more than 12 billion pounds over the 2025-2029 cycle let even relegated clubs outspend most European champions. Manchester City spent around 458 million pounds in the summer of 2026 alone; Chelsea paid a British record 117 million pounds for Morgan Rogers. The league's global audience, the intensity of its stadiums and the sheer number of competitive matches make it the sport's centre of gravity.

## History and eras

The early Premier League belonged to [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson)'s [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united), who won 13 of the first 21 titles including the 1999 treble. [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) under Arsene Wenger were the challengers, with the 2003-04 Invincibles going unbeaten through a whole season. Roman Abramovich's money turned [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) into champions under [Jose Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) in 2005 and 2006, the first of five titles in twelve years.

The Abu Dhabi takeover of [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) in 2008 opened a new era. Sergio Aguero's last-minute goal in 2012 won City's first title; [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola)'s arrival in 2016 brought six in seven seasons, a 100-point campaign in 2017-18 and four in a row from 2021 to 2024. Leicester City's 5,000-1 title in 2016 was the miracle in between. [Jurgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) won the club's first title in 30 years in 2020, and Arne Slot repeated it in 2025 in his first season.

Then came 2025-26. [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s Arsenal, runners-up three times, finally won the title, their first in 22 years, built on the best defence and the best [set-piece](/tactics/set-pieces) record in the division. Guardiola left City that summer after ten years and 20 trophies.

## Style and tactical trends

The Premier League is where every tactical idea arrives to be stress-tested at full speed. Guardiola imported [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs); Klopp brought [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) and the [high line](/tactics/high-line); Mourinho and [Diego Simeone](/managers/diego-simeone)'s imitators defined the [low block](/tactics/low-block). By 2026 the dominant shape in possession is the 3-2-5, with a full-back or centre-back stepping into a [box midfield](/tactics/box-midfield), while out of possession teams split between man-oriented pressing ([Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola), now at Liverpool) and compact mid-blocks ([Unai Emery](/managers/unai-emery) at Aston Villa).

Two trends define the mid-2020s. First, set pieces: Arsenal's title and the rise of specialist coaches made dead balls worth a quarter of all goals. Second, [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) over the press: with everybody pressing high, goalkeepers go long more often than at any time since the 2000s, and target strikers (Haaland, Isak) are back in fashion.

## Great clubs and dynasties

[Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) hold the record with 13 Premier League titles and 20 English championships in all, matched on the overall count by [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) in 2025. [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) (10 Premier League titles) built the most dominant dynasty of the modern era. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) have five, [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) four, and Blackburn Rovers and Leicester City one each. [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur), [Newcastle United](/clubs/newcastle-united) and [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa) are the great under-achievers, each with a title drought stretching back decades.

## 2026-27 season storylines

- **A new managerial map.** Five of the big six changed managers in a year. [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca) replaced Guardiola at City. [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) took over Chelsea in July 2026 after Liam Rosenior's brief spell (Rosenior had replaced Maresca). [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola) replaced Arne Slot at Liverpool in June. Michael Carrick has been United's head coach since 13 January 2026, after Ruben Amorim was sacked. [Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi) arrived at Tottenham. Newcastle appointed Matthias Jaissle on 5 August after Eddie Howe left.
- **Arsenal's defence of the title.** The champions, still under Arteta, led the table after nine rounds in early November with 24 points, three ahead of Maresca's City and five ahead of Fabian Hurzeler's Brighton. Chelsea (16), Liverpool (15) and Keith Andrews' Brentford (14) followed.
- **The money.** City's 458 million pound summer, Rogers' British-record move to Chelsea, Bradley Barcola joining Liverpool from PSG for around 125 million euros alongside [Alexander Isak](/players/alexander-isak), Sandro Tonali's 100 million pound switch from Newcastle to Spurs, and Andrey Santos going from Chelsea to United for 48 million pounds. The window closed on 1 September.
- **The promoted trio.** Frank Lampard's Coventry City, Gary O'Neil's Ipswich Town (Kieran McKenna stepped down) and Sergej Jakirovic's Hull City, up via the play-offs, replaced Burnley, Wolves and West Ham.
- **Struggles.** Spurs began with two points from five games before De Zerbi's methods took hold; Sunderland, under Regis Le Bris, were bottom on five points in early November, with Oliver Glasner's Nottingham Forest and Pierre Sage's Crystal Palace just above.
- **The Ballon d'Or effect.** [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) won the 2026 Ballon d'Or with Bayern, the first Englishman since Michael Owen in 2001, a reminder of the one trophy the Premier League's best English striker never won at home.
