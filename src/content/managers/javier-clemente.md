## Biography

Javier Clemente Lázaro was born in Barakaldo, Biscay, on 12 March 1950. A promising winger whose career was ended by injury at 23, he became the most combative coach in Spanish football: the man who won two league titles and a double with an [Athletic Bilbao](/clubs/athletic-bilbao) side made entirely of Basque players, feuded with Barcelona, Johan Cruyff and the Madrid press for a decade, coached [Spain](/nations/spain) through two tournaments, and then, in his sixties, found a late calling with Serbia, Cameroon and Libya, whom he led to the 2014 African Nations Championship. He retired after coaching the unofficial Basque Country team until 2020.

## Playing career

Clemente was a quick left-winger for Athletic Bilbao, making his debut at 19 in 1969 and winning the 1973 Copa del Rey. A brutal tackle in 1971 damaged his knee, and after years of attempted comebacks he retired in 1974 with fewer than 50 league games. He coached Athletic's youth and reserve sides while still in his twenties.

His Athletic of Andoni Zubizarreta, Andoni Goikoetxea, Dani and Manu Sarabia won the title in 1983 on the final day and the double in 1984, and the Copa final against Maradona's Barcelona ended in a mass brawl that defined the era's rivalry. Espanyol lost the 1988 UEFA Cup final to Bayer Leverkusen on penalties after winning the first leg 3-0. Spain under him went 31 matches unbeaten, lost the 1994 quarter-final to Italy after Mauro Tassotti's unpunished elbow on Luis Enrique, lost to England on penalties at Euro 1996, and went out in the group at France 98, after which he was sacked. Libya's triumph in 2014, in the middle of a civil war, was perhaps his most remarkable achievement.

## Tactical identity

Clemente is the great exponent of Spain's "furia": organised, physical, [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) in a rigid 4-4-2, a [low block](/tactics/low-block) built on a dominant centre-back pairing and a goalkeeper, and quick [counter-attacks](/tactics/counter-attacking) to a big striker, Dani at Athletic, Julio Salinas with Spain. He prized fitness, commitment and the collective over individual talent, which is why he left out gifted but inconsistent players and why his Spain was so criticised by the Catalan press. He was, in every way, the opposite of the [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) that [Luis Aragonés](/managers/luis-aragones) would build a decade later, and he has never stopped saying so.

## Achievements

- La Liga 1983 and 1984 and the 1984 double with an all-Basque Athletic Bilbao, the club's last titles
- UEFA Cup final with Espanyol in 1988
- Spain's longest unbeaten run at the time (31 matches, 1994-95) and two tournament quarter-finals
- African Nations Championship 2014 with Libya, their first continental title

## Storyline

Retired. Clemente's Athletic titles are the last of the club's eight, and the romance of an all-Basque champion has only grown as the gap has widened; Athletic's Copa del Rey win in 2024 and Europa League final on home soil in 2025 under Ernesto Valverde were the closest the club has come since. He remains a voice on Spanish radio, blunt, Basque and unrepentant.

The torch is carried by Athletic's enduring policy and by the coaches who defend the right to be pragmatic in a possession-obsessed country: Diego Simeone at Atlético, where Clemente also worked, is the modern master of the organised low block. Spain's 2026 World Cup winners under [Luis de la Fuente](/managers/luis-de-la-fuente) play a very different game, but De la Fuente was a left-back in Clemente's 1983 and 1984 teams, and the discipline came from somewhere.
