## Biography

Derek McInnes, born in Paisley on 5 July 1971, is one of Scottish football's most experienced managers and a former [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) player whose return to Ibrox as manager was reported in June 2026. According to the SPFL and other outlets, he replaced Danny Röhl on a three-year contract; Röhl had himself been appointed after Russell Martin was sacked only 17 games into his tenure in late 2025. Some reporting around the Rangers succession in 2026 was contradictory, so the precise sequence should be treated with care, but McInnes is understood to be the man in charge for 2026-27.

## Playing career

A tenacious central midfielder, McInnes came through at Greenock Morton before joining Rangers in 1995, where he was part of the squad that completed nine-in-a-row under Walter Smith, although his appearances were limited. He went on to Toulouse, West Bromwich Albion, where he captained the club to Premier League promotion in 2002, Dundee United, Millwall and finally St Johnstone, where he was player-coach. He won two [Scotland](/nations/scotland) caps in 2002.

## Coaching career

McInnes took over at St Johnstone in 2007 and won promotion to the top flight in 2009, keeping them there comfortably. A spell at Bristol City in the Championship ended in relegation in 2013, but [Aberdeen](/clubs/aberdeen) hired him that spring and he built the most consistent Scottish side outside the Old Firm for a generation. The 2014 League Cup, won on penalties against Inverness, ended Aberdeen's 19-year wait for a trophy; the Dons finished second four times and reached several cup finals, usually falling to [Celtic](/clubs/celtic). He left in March 2021 after eight years.

Kilmarnock were in the Championship when he arrived in January 2022; he won the title within months and in 2023-24 took them to fourth and European football. He moved to Hearts in 2025, and his success in Edinburgh over the 2025-26 season, with Hearts challenging in the upper reaches of the [Scottish Premiership](/leagues/scottish-premiership), made him an obvious candidate when Rangers' search for stability became urgent.

Rangers' 2025-26 was chaotic. Martin was dismissed after a poor start, a pursuit of Kevin Muscat foundered because of his commitments in China, and Röhl, the former Sheffield Wednesday coach, took over but could not close the gap on Celtic, who completed a double under [Martin O'Neill](/managers/martin-oneill). McInnes was reportedly confirmed on 17 June 2026.

## Tactical identity

McInnes is a pragmatist who builds from defensive organisation outwards. His favoured 4-2-3-1 has a hard-working double pivot, wide players who track back and a target forward; Aberdeen's best seasons were built on Adam Rooney's finishing, Niall McGinn's delivery and a settled back four. The team defends in a mid or [low block](/tactics/low-block), presses in bursts rather than continuously, and attacks quickly on the [counter](/tactics/counter-attacking) with crosses and [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into the box. [Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) are a major weapon; his sides are consistently among the best in Scotland from dead balls.

At Rangers, where possession and dominance are expected, he has had to adapt, and early reports suggest a higher defensive line and more emphasis on sustained pressure in domestic games while keeping the counter-attacking shape for Europe. Critics have questioned whether his style is ambitious enough for Ibrox; supporters, after a year of upheaval, have mostly welcomed the competence.

## Achievements

- Aberdeen's 2014 League Cup, the club's first trophy since 1995, and four second-place finishes.
- Promotions with St Johnstone (2009) and Kilmarnock (2022), and a top-four finish with Kilmarnock in 2024.
- One of the longest unbroken records of top-flight management in modern Scottish football.

## Storyline

McInnes inherits a Rangers squad reshaped by the club's American ownership group and their recruitment staff, and a support that has watched Celtic win the title in most recent seasons. The 2026-27 campaign is about closing the gap on O'Neill's Celtic and restoring respectability in Europe. The early-season Old Firm matches have been tight, and Rangers' progress should be judged cautiously while the season is young.

What McInnes offers is experience of the Scottish game, a clear structure and a reputation for improving players such as Scott McKenna, Ryan Jack and Kenny McLean. What he must prove is that he can win the big games, something his Aberdeen sides did too rarely against Celtic. The three-year contract suggests the board wants a settled period after cycling through Philippe Clement, Martin and Röhl in quick succession. As of November 2026 the project is steady rather than spectacular, which at Ibrox is at least a starting point.
