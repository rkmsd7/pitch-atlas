## Biography

Keith Joseph Andrews was born in Dublin on 13 September 1980 and left for England at 16 to join Wolverhampton Wanderers' academy. He is thoughtful and articulate, a familiar voice on Irish television during his years as a pundit, and his appointment as a [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) head coach in 2025 without a previous number-one job anywhere was one of the boldest decisions of that summer. He is one of a small number of Irish managers to have led a top-flight English club.

## Playing career

Andrews was a tireless central midfielder with a long-range shot and a knack for scoring important goals. He spent years in the lower leagues with Wolves, Oxford, Stoke, Walsall and Hull, before his career took off at MK Dons under Paul Ince, with whom he won League Two in 2008 as captain. Ince took him to Blackburn Rovers, and Andrews played in the Premier League for Blackburn and West Bromwich Albion. He later had spells with Ipswich, Bolton, Brighton and Watford, retiring in 2015.

For the [Republic of Ireland](/nations/ireland) he won 35 caps and scored three goals, including a memorable strike against Italy at Euro 2012, and was part of Giovanni Trapattoni's squad at that tournament.

## Coaching career

Andrews began at MK Dons as a coach and then assistant under Robbie Neilson and Dan Micciche, followed by a short spell as caretaker. In 2019 he joined Stephen Kenny's Ireland staff, first with the U21s and then with the senior team, where he was credited with a more progressive style than the country had been used to. After Kenny's exit he worked as a first-team coach at Sheffield United under Chris Wilder during the 2023-24 Premier League season.

[Brentford](/clubs/brentford) hired him in 2024 as set-piece coach, a role the club takes as seriously as any in football, and Brentford scored more goals from dead balls than anyone that season. When Thomas Frank left for [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) in June 2025, the club chose Andrews from inside the building rather than an established name. He guided Brentford to safety in 2025-26 despite the departures of Bryan Mbeumo and Christian Nørgaard, and is now in his second season.

## Tactical identity

Andrews' Brentford is recognisably the club of the Frank years, built on efficiency rather than possession. The shape alternates between a 4-2-3-1 and a [back three](/tactics/back-three) depending on the opponent, and Brentford are happy to play [direct](/tactics/direct-play) from goalkeeper to striker, with long throws and quick restarts a staple. Yoane Wissa's and Igor Thiago's running in behind gave the team its [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) threat, and midfielders arrive late into the box rather than dominating the ball.

The defining feature is, unsurprisingly, [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces). Andrews' routines use blocking, decoy runners and near-post flicks, and the kick-off routine that produced several goals within the first minute of games in 2025-26 became a talking point across the league. Out of possession the team defends in a compact mid-block and springs forward on turnovers.

## Achievements

- Premier League survival in his first season as a head coach (2025-26)
- Brentford's set-piece record under his coaching in 2024-25
- League Two title as a player with MK Dons in 2008
- 35 caps for the Republic of Ireland, including Euro 2012

## Storyline

Brentford's model, as much as any club's, is a test of whether structure can outlast individuals. The club has lost Ivan Toney, Mbeumo, Nørgaard and Frank in successive summers, and each time the data-driven recruitment department has found replacements at a fraction of the cost. Andrews is part of that logic: the head coach as one component in a system owned by Matthew Benham and run by Phil Giles.

His first season proved he could keep the team competitive. The second is about proving the ceiling can rise. With three promoted clubs in the division in 2026-27, [Coventry City](/clubs/coventry-city), [Hull City](/clubs/hull-city) and [Ipswich Town](/clubs/ipswich-town), the bottom of the table has an obvious shape, and Brentford's ambition is the comfortable mid-table of the Frank era with occasional cup runs.

Andrews' quiet manner can mislead. He has been firm about the standards he inherited and has given significant minutes to academy players and low-cost signings. As of November 2026 the squad is one of the youngest in the league, and the one question nobody in west London has yet had to answer is what happens if a bigger club notices the coach rather than the players.
