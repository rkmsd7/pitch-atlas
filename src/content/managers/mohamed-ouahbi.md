## Biography

Mohamed Ouahbi is a Belgian-born Moroccan coach who spent most of his working life in youth development in Belgium before the Royal Moroccan Football Federation made him the head of its under-20 programme. In October 2025 his under-20s won the [Under-20 World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in Chile, Morocco's first global title at any level, and on 5-6 March 2026 the FRMF appointed him head coach of the senior [Morocco](/nations/morocco) team after Walid Regragui resigned. He led the Atlas Lions at the 2026 World Cup; his status after the tournament is not verified here and this page reflects his position as of the 2026 World Cup.

## Playing career

Ouahbi's playing career was in the Belgian lower leagues, and he moved into coaching early, working through the academies of Belgian clubs including Anderlecht's youth structure and the Belgian federation's development programmes. He never played for Morocco, but his background as a diaspora footballer made him a natural fit for a federation whose squad is drawn largely from players raised in Europe.

The under-20 triumph in Chile, beating Argentina in the final, was a landmark for African football and made Ouahbi a national figure. When Regragui, exhausted by the fallout from Morocco's defeat in the final of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations, which they hosted, stepped down in early March 2026, the federation chose the man who had built the pipeline over an established foreign name. He had less than three months to prepare for the World Cup, and his camp in the United States was notable for the individual training programmes he gave to several senior players.

## Tactical identity

Ouahbi's under-20s played a confident, possession-based 4-3-3 with a high defensive line, patient build-up and quick combinations between the lines, which places him in the [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) tradition rather than the compact, transition-based model that Regragui used to reach the 2022 World Cup semi-finals. With the seniors he has kept Regragui's defensive solidity, Morocco press in organised [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) and remain lethal on the [counter](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Brahim Díaz and Achraf Hakimi's overlaps, while asking for more control of the ball through Sofyan Amrabat and Bilal El Khannouss. His great advantage is that he knows the next generation personally.

## Storyline

Morocco arrived at the 2026 World Cup as Africa's leading nation: semi-finalists in 2022, AFCON 2025 finalists at home, under-20 world champions, and co-hosts of the 2030 World Cup with Spain and Portugal. Ouahbi's appointment was a bet on continuity between the youth and senior programmes. Morocco's results at the tournament and the federation's decisions afterwards are not verified on this page; readers should check the live data on this site.

Beyond 2026, the calendar is clear. [AFCON 2027](/tournaments/afcon) in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania is the next trophy Morocco want, having lost the 2025 final on home soil, and the qualifiers run through 2026. The 2030 World Cup will be played partly in Casablanca, Rabat, Tangier and Marrakech, with a new 115,000-seat stadium planned in Benslimane, and the federation's whole project is built around being a genuine contender in a home tournament. Hakimi, Díaz and Amrabat will be in their thirties by then; the under-20 champions Ouahbi coached in 2025 will be in their prime.
