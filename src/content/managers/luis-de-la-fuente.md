## Biography

Luis de la Fuente Castillo was born in Haro, in La Rioja, on 21 June 1961. For most of his career he was invisible to the wider public: a youth coach at Athletic Bilbao and then at the Spanish federation, where he won European titles with the under-19s and under-21s and an Olympic silver medal. When he replaced Luis Enrique in December 2022 the reaction in Spain ranged from indifference to derision. Four years later he has won the [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league), [Euro 2024](/tournaments/euro) and the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), and the federation extended his contract after the final in New Jersey.

## Playing career

De la Fuente was a left-back who came through Athletic Bilbao's academy and won two La Liga titles with [Athletic](/clubs/athletic-bilbao) in 1983 and 1984 under Javier Clemente, plus the 1984 Copa del Rey. He later played for [Sevilla](/clubs/sevilla) and Alavés before retiring in 1994. He won no senior caps.

His senior tenure began badly, with a home defeat to Scotland in March 2023, but Spain won the Nations League three months later on penalties against Croatia. At Euro 2024 they won all seven games, beating Italy, Germany, France and England, with [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal), 16 at the time, and Nico Williams providing the width. In 2026 they went further still, winning the World Cup with a knockout run through Austria, Portugal, France and Argentina.

## Tactical identity

De la Fuente's Spain play [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with a modern edge. The passing structure is inherited from the [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) era, but the side is more vertical and more willing to use genuine wingers who stay wide and isolate full-backs. [Rodri](/players/rodri) anchors the 4-3-3 and dictates when to slow down and when to break; [Pedri](/players/pedri) and Fabián Ruiz carry the ball between the lines; Yamal and Williams attack one-on-one. Without the ball the team sets [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the first pass out from a centre-back, and the defence, with [Pau Cubarsí](/players/pau-cubarsi) and Unai Simón behind it, holds a bold line. The coach's gift is clarity of selection: he picks the best players in form and gives them simple, repeatable roles.

## Storyline

The 2026 World Cup final at MetLife Stadium on 19 July was a tight, tense game that turned when [Enzo Fernández](/players/enzo-fernandez) was sent off, and ended when Ferran Torres scored in the 106th minute. Spain had beaten the hosts of 2030's centenary celebrations, [Argentina](/nations/argentina), the defending champions, after knocking out Austria, [Portugal](/nations/portugal) and [France](/nations/france). Simón's seven clean sheets were the defining statistic; Rodri's Golden Ball confirmed that his comeback from a serious knee injury was complete. The federation offered De la Fuente a new contract before the players had finished celebrating.

What comes next is the attempt to do what no men's team has done since Spain themselves in 2008-12: hold the continental and world titles together. The 2026-27 Nations League opened the new cycle, and qualification for [Euro 2028](/tournaments/euro) follows. The squad is young enough that the core of Yamal, Cubarsí, Pedri and Nico Williams should be at their peak in 2028 and at the 2030 World Cup that Spain co-host with Portugal and Morocco. De la Fuente's challenge is the one every great coach eventually faces: keeping a champion team hungry. Fixtures since the World Cup should be checked against the live data on this site.
