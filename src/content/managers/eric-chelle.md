## Biography

Eric Sékou Chelle was born in Abidjan, Ivory Coast, on 15 November 1977, to a Malian father and a French mother, and grew up in France. A journeyman defender in the French lower leagues, he became a respected coach with Mali, taking them to the quarter-finals of the 2023 Africa Cup of Nations, where they lost to the hosts Ivory Coast in extra time. The Nigeria Football Federation appointed him in January 2025 to rescue a World Cup qualifying campaign; the Super Eagles fell in the play-offs to DR Congo in November 2025, but the NFF kept faith, agreeing in principle to a new contract in June 2026 and adding the under-23 team to his portfolio.

## Playing career

Chelle was a centre-back for Martigues, Valenciennes, Istres, Lens, Chamois Niortais and Boulogne between 1997 and 2013, mostly in Ligue 2 and the Championnat National, with a few Ligue 1 appearances for Lens and Valenciennes. He won eight caps for [Mali](/nations/mali) between 2008 and 2009.

Mali under Chelle were organised, physical and unlucky, leading Ivory Coast until the 90th minute of the 2023 AFCON quarter-final. He was sacked in June 2024 after a draw with Madagascar. Nigeria, who had been through Finidi George and an interim spell, hired him as the first non-Nigerian in the job since Gernot Rohr; he inherited a group where Nigeria sat fifth, dragged them into the play-offs, and lost the African play-off final to DR Congo.

## Tactical identity

Chelle's teams are pragmatic and transitional. He has used a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with Nigeria to accommodate Calvin Bassey, William Troost-Ekong and Ola Aina, with wing-backs providing width, and the attacking plan is simple: get Victor Osimhen and Ademola Lookman into space quickly. That makes Nigeria a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) side with a strong element of [direct play](/tactics/direct-play), which suits the squad's athleticism. He has demanded more defensive discipline than his predecessors and has tried to settle a midfield built on Alex Iwobi and Wilfred Ndidi. His press conferences have been frank, including open criticism of the federation's logistics.

## Storyline

Nigeria's absence from the 2026 World Cup was the great failure of the cycle: a nation with Osimhen, Lookman, the 2025 Serie A top scorer at Atalanta, and a squad drawn from Europe's top leagues failed to reach a 48-team tournament with nine and a half African places. The play-off defeat to DR Congo in November 2025 triggered an inquest, but the NFF decided that the chaos predated Chelle and, on 24 June 2026, agreed to extend him and to put the Olympic team under his control as well.

What comes next is [AFCON 2027](/tournaments/afcon), hosted by Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania, with qualifiers against Tanzania in November 2026 (reports gave 13 and 17 November, with earlier schedules showing 11 and 15), and the 2028 Olympic campaign with the under-23s. Nigeria have not won the AFCON since 2013 and have lost two of the last three finals they reached. The NFF had two squads to name and a coaching arrangement to confirm before the November window as of late October 2026; results and the final staffing should be checked against the live data on this site.
