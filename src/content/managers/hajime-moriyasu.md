## Biography

Hajime Moriyasu was born in Kakegawa, Shizuoka, on 23 August 1968 and grew up in Nagasaki. A tireless midfielder for Sanfrecce Hiroshima and the first great Japan side of the 1990s, he became a title-winning club coach and, in 2018, head coach of [Japan](/nations/japan). He has held the job longer than anyone, took Japan to the round of 16 at the 2022 World Cup with wins over Germany and Spain, and after the 2026 World Cup signed a short extension to lead the team at the 2027 [Asian Cup](/tournaments/asian-cup), after which under-21 coach Go Oiwa will take over.

## Playing career

Moriyasu made more than 300 appearances for Sanfrecce Hiroshima between 1987 and 2002, with a spell at Kyoto Purple Sanga and a finish at Vegalta Sendai. He won 35 caps for Japan and was a regular under Hans Ooft in the side that lost the 1993 "Agony of Doha" qualifier and won the 1992 Asian Cup.

His Hiroshima sides won three titles in four years with a distinctive 3-4-2-1 inherited from Mihailo Petrović and refined into a system of overloads and quick switches. Appointed Japan coach after the 2018 World Cup, he reached the 2019 Asian Cup final, survived heavy criticism in 2021-22 and produced the two great upsets of Qatar 2022, coming from behind to beat both Germany and Spain, before losing to Croatia on penalties. Japan were the first team to qualify for 2026 and lost to Brazil 2-1 in the round of 32 in stoppage time.

## Tactical identity

Moriyasu is the most prominent exponent of the [back three](/tactics/back-three) in Asian football. His 3-4-2-1 in possession becomes a 4-4-2 or 5-4-1 without the ball, and the 2022 side was built on this flexibility: a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block) for the first hour and then a switch to a front-foot 3-4-3 with wing-backs attacking, which overwhelmed Germany and Spain. He uses [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the opponent's wide centre-backs and attacks in transition with Kaoru Mitoma, Ritsu Doan and Takefusa Kubo, which makes Japan a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) side at their most dangerous. He is also a careful, consensual manager who rotates heavily and has given the team a deep bench.

## Achievements

- Three J1 League titles with Sanfrecce Hiroshima
- World Cup 2022: group winners above Germany and Spain, round of 16
- Asian Cup 2019 runner-up
- EAFF E-1 Championship 2022 and 2025
- Japan's longest-serving head coach and first to lead them at two World Cups

## Storyline

The 2026 World Cup was supposed to be Japan's breakthrough into the quarter-finals, a target the JFA had stated publicly, and a stoppage-time defeat to [Brazil](/nations/brazil) in the round of 32 was a bitter way to miss it. Moriyasu expressed regret and said he would accept whatever the federation decided. On 23 July 2026 the JFA announced a six-month extension: he stays for the Asian Cup in Saudi Arabia from 7 January to 5 February 2027, and Oiwa, who coaches the under-21s, takes charge of the senior team from March 2027, holding both roles for a short overlap.

So the next chapter is a farewell tour with a trophy at the end of it. Japan have not won the Asian Cup since 2011, and the squad, with Mitoma, Kubo, Wataru Endo, Daichi Kamada, Hiroki Ito and Ko Itakura, is probably the best they have ever sent. Beyond February 2027 Oiwa inherits the programme and the job of building for the 2030 World Cup. Results since July 2026 should be checked against the live data on this site.
