## Biography

Tony Popovic was born in Sydney on 4 July 1973 to Croatian parents and became one of the defining defenders of the Socceroos' first golden generation. As a coach he built Western Sydney Wanderers from nothing into Asian champions within two years, and in September 2024, after Graham Arnold resigned mid-qualifying, Football Australia turned to him on a deal running to the end of the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup). He got [Australia](/nations/australia) there, and before the opening match against Türkiye signed an extension through the 2027 [Asian Cup](/tournaments/asian-cup).

## Playing career

Popovic was a centre-back for Sydney United, Sanfrecce Hiroshima, [Crystal Palace](/clubs/crystal-palace), where he spent five seasons and was a Premier League regular in 2004-05, and Al Arabi, before finishing at Sydney FC with the inaugural A-League title in 2006. He won 58 caps for Australia, played at the 2004 Olympics, was part of the 2006 World Cup squad and retired in 2008.

The Wanderers were founded in 2012 and Popovic took them to the Premiership in their first season and the Asian title in their third, beating Al Hilal over two legs in 2014, the only Australian club to have won the competition. He won another Premiership at Perth, had short spells abroad, and took Melbourne Victory to three cup finals. Hired by Football Australia with a crucial qualifier in China days away, he turned a stalling campaign into direct qualification.

## Tactical identity

Popovic is a [back three](/tactics/back-three) coach by instinct. His Socceroos play a 3-4-2-1 that becomes a 5-4-1 without the ball, with Harry Souttar and Cameron Burgess the physical core, wing-backs Jordan Bos and Lewis Miller providing width, and Jackson Irvine and Connor Metcalfe as the engine. Australia are [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) opponents with a heavy emphasis on [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), where their height makes them one of the most dangerous teams in Asia. He demands structure, fitness and defensive discipline, and his teams concede very few goals from open play.

## Achievements

- AFC Champions League 2014 with Western Sydney Wanderers, the only Australian club to win it
- Two A-League Premierships (2013, 2019) and the Australia Cup 2021
- Qualified Australia for the 2026 World Cup, their sixth in a row
- Asian Coach of the Year 2014

## Storyline

Australia went to the 2026 World Cup aiming, in Popovic's words, for their best-ever result, which would mean bettering the round of 16 reached in 2006 and 2022. The outcome of the tournament for the Socceroos is not verified on this page. What is confirmed is that Football Australia's chief executive Martin Kugeler announced the contract extension before the opener, saying it gave the federation clarity to plan for the period after the tournament.

What comes next is the Asian Cup in Saudi Arabia from 7 January to 5 February 2027, a trophy Australia won in 2015 at home and have not reached the final of since, and then the Asian qualifiers for 2030. The squad is at a crossroads: Mathew Ryan, Irvine and Souttar are in their thirties, and the next wave of Nestory Irankunda, Garang Kuol, Bos and Patrick Yazbek must take over. Popovic's deal expires after the Asian Cup, so the tournament doubles as his audition for the next cycle. Results since June 2026 should be checked against the live data on this site.
