## Biography

Leonardo Ponzio, born in Las Rosas, Santa Fe on 29 January 1982, is the most decorated player in [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate)'s history and the man the club turned to when its 2026 season unravelled. He took charge as interim head coach in October 2026 after Eduardo Coudet departed following elimination from the Copa Sudamericana by Independiente Santa Fe, and Argentine reporting at the end of October made clear that his continuation into 2027 depends on whether River win the Clausura. If they do not, the board intends to start a new search in January.

## Playing career

Ponzio was a defensive midfielder of uncommon intelligence rather than physical dominance. He came through at Newell's Old Boys, joined River Plate in 2003 and moved to Real Zaragoza in 2004, where he won the Copa del Rey in his first season and spent six years in Spain, including a loan to Granada. He returned to River in 2012, just as the club climbed back from its only relegation, and became the heartbeat of the greatest era in its modern history under [Marcelo Gallardo](/managers/marcelo-gallardo).

Between 2014 and 2021 he won 17 trophies, including the Copa Sudamericana in 2014 and the [Copa Libertadores](/tournaments/copa-libertadores) in 2015 and 2018, the latter against [Boca Juniors](/clubs/boca-juniors) in the Madrid final. He captained the side, coming off the bench to settle games and reading transitions before they happened. He retired in December 2021 after a final league title and won two caps for [Argentina](/nations/argentina).

## Coaching career

After retiring he stayed close to the club, working in an institutional and advisory capacity and studying for his coaching badges. He was part of the conversations around Gallardo's return in 2024, and when Gallardo, who had renewed to December 2026, decided to leave in February 2026, Ponzio remained a bridge between the dressing room and the directors.

Coudet was appointed on 4 March 2026 with a contract to December 2027 and debuted a week later, but the Sudamericana exit in the autumn ended his tenure. River announced that Ponzio would assume the first team on an interim basis for the remainder of the Clausura. It is his first job as a head coach at any level, though he has been surrounded by experienced assistants from the Gallardo years.

## Tactical identity

Ponzio's reference is the Gallardo school in which he played: a 4-3-1-2 or 4-2-3-1 with a deep organiser, a creative number ten (Juan Fernando Quintero in his own era and again now), and forwards who attack the channels. The principles are vertical: win the ball through [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) in midfield, play forward quickly, [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) with numbers and use [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) when the opponent sits deep. [Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), a Gallardo hallmark, have been a focus of his training sessions according to the Argentine press.

As a caretaker his priority has been simplicity: a settled eleven, clear roles and an appeal to the club's identity. He has spoken of wanting River to recover the intensity and aggression of the 2018 side, and of trusting senior players such as Franco Armani and Quintero. It is too early, and the sample too small, to describe a Ponzio style with any confidence.

## Achievements

- As a player: two Copa Libertadores, a Copa Sudamericana, two Recopas and multiple domestic titles, the most trophies of any River player.
- Copa del Rey with Zaragoza in 2004.
- Captain of the 2018 Libertadores-winning side.

## Storyline

River's 2026 has been a season of transition and disappointment. Gallardo's exit, Coudet's brief and unhappy reign, a Sudamericana elimination and a squad still being reshaped around younger arrivals such as Kendry Páez have left the club searching for direction. Ponzio's interim spell carries an explicit condition: an October report said that if River are crowned Clausura champions, the possibility of him continuing will remain open; otherwise the directors will begin their search in January 2027.

That makes the closing weeks of the [Argentine Primera](/leagues/argentine-primera) season a referendum on both the team and its caretaker. Across the city, Boca are also between coaches, which has made the Superclásico rivalry unusually volatile. Ponzio has the goodwill of the Monumental, which in Argentine football is a currency that depreciates quickly. As of November 2026, the outcome of the Clausura is undecided and so is his future.
