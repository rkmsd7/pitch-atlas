## Biography

Francesc Fàbregas Soler was born in Arenys de Mar, Catalonia, on 4 May 1987, and his life has been a series of early arrivals: at [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona)'s La Masia at ten, at [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) at 16, in the Premier League at 16 years and 177 days, and in management at 36. He is articulate in four languages, a trained observer of coaches from Arsène Wenger to [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) and José Mourinho, and part-owner of the club he manages, having invested in [Como](/clubs/como) in 2022 alongside the Indonesian Hartono family.

## Playing career

Fàbregas left La Masia for Arsenal in 2003 and became the youngest player and scorer in the club's history. By 20 he was captain and the creative centre of Wenger's post-Invincibles side, a deep-lying playmaker with an unusual gift for the through ball. He returned to Barcelona in 2011, won the 2013 La Liga title and a Copa del Rey under Guardiola and Tito Vilanova, then joined [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in 2014 and won two Premier League titles, the first with 18 assists under Mourinho in 2014-15 and the second under Antonio Conte in 2016-17.

For [Spain](/nations/spain) he won Euro 2008, the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in 2010 and Euro 2012, delivering the assist for Andrés Iniesta's winning goal in Johannesburg. After spells at [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) and Como, he retired in 2023 with 110 caps.

## Coaching career

Fàbregas signed for Como in Serie B in 2022 as a player, with an understanding that he would move into coaching and ownership. He coached the Primavera, took the first team as caretaker in November 2023 while completing his UEFA Pro licence, and was formally appointed head coach in the summer of 2024 after Como won promotion to [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a) as runners-up.

His first top-flight season in 2024-25 surprised the division: Como stayed up comfortably playing possession football, and the club's spending power, combined with Fàbregas's network, attracted a succession of young talents, among them [Nico Paz](/players/nico-paz), signed from [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), and Assane Diao. In 2025-26 Como pushed into the top half and became a regular feature in discussions about Italian football's future, with Fàbregas repeatedly linked with bigger clubs and repeatedly choosing to stay.

## Tactical identity

Fàbregas coaches the football he played. Como build from the back in a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, with one full-back inverting into midfield in the manner of Guardiola's [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) and the other pushing high. The goalkeeper is a passing option; the centre-backs split wide; the double pivot rotates so that one player is always free to receive. It is [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in the purest sense, with elements of the short-passing [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) he learned at La Masia.

Out of possession Como press high with a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and a front four that hunts in pairs, a demand that made the 2024-25 side vulnerable on transitions and that Fàbregas addressed in 2025-26 with a more disciplined rest-defence. Nico Paz, operating as the number ten, is the creative hub, and the wide forwards are asked to stay high and wide to stretch defences before cutting inside.

The evolution has been towards control. The early Como sides were brave and open; the current version keeps the ball longer, presses more selectively and has become harder to beat, a sign of a coach learning that in Serie A the second half of the game is often decided by what you do not concede.

## Achievements

- Promotion to Serie A with Como in 2023-24 as caretaker-turned-head coach.
- Consecutive Serie A survivals and a top-half push in 2025-26 with one of the youngest squads in the league.
- As a player: World Cup 2010, two European Championships, two Premier League titles and a La Liga title.

## Storyline

Como are Italian football's most interesting outsider, a lakeside club backed by enormous wealth, run by a former superstar, and playing a style that sits closer to Barcelona than to the traditions of [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a). Fàbregas has turned down approaches from larger clubs, and his loyalty to a project in which he holds a stake has become a selling point for recruitment.

The 2026-27 ambition is European qualification. The competition is severe: [Cristian Chivu](/managers/cristian-chivu)'s [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) are champions, [Massimiliano Allegri](/managers/massimiliano-allegri) has taken [Napoli](/clubs/napoli), [Luciano Spalletti](/managers/luciano-spalletti) is at [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), [Ruben Amorim](/managers/ruben-amorim) at [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan), [Maurizio Sarri](/managers/maurizio-sarri) at [Atalanta](/clubs/atalanta) and [Gian Piero Gasperini](/managers/gian-piero-gasperini) at [Roma](/clubs/roma). Keeping Nico Paz, the subject of a Real Madrid buy-back clause, has been a recurring summer theme.

How the campaign stands in November 2026 cannot be verified here. What is settled is that Fàbregas has become a coach in his own right rather than a famous name in a tracksuit, and that Como are the club the rest of Europe's scouts now watch.
