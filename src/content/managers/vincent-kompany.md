## Biography

Vincent Jean Mpoy Kompany was born in Uccle, Brussels, on 10 April 1986, to a Congolese father who had been a political refugee and a Belgian mother. He is a graduate in business administration, an articulate public speaker on racism and social mobility, and a leader whose authority in dressing rooms has never depended on volume. He was the captain of [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city)'s first Premier League titles, and he has brought the same seriousness to coaching that he showed as a player.

## Playing career

Kompany came through at Anderlecht and was a Belgian champion at 17. Hamburg signed him in 2006, and in August 2008 Manchester City paid around £6m for a defender who would become the face of the club's transformation. He captained City to four Premier League titles, including the first in 2012 and the 2019 title his thunderous winner against Leicester effectively settled, and lifted two FA Cups and four League Cups. He won 89 caps for [Belgium](/nations/belgium) and was part of the side that finished third at the 2018 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup). He left City in 2019 as a club legend.

## Coaching career

He returned to Anderlecht as player-manager in 2019 and became full-time head coach in 2020, taking the club to the 2022 Belgian Cup final while developing young talent. Burnley hired him in June 2022 after relegation, and he won the Championship with 101 points playing a possession style unusual in the second tier. Burnley's Premier League return ended in relegation in 2023-24, but Kompany's reputation survived the drop because the principles were visible.

[Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), having been rejected by several targets, took the risk in May 2024. It paid off. Bayern won the 2024-25 Bundesliga back from [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) and retained it in 2025-26, a season in which [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) scored 73 goals in all competitions and won the 2026 Ballon d'Or in London on 26 October 2026, the first Englishman since Michael Owen in 2001.

## Tactical identity

Kompany's Bayern play a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, with one full-back inverting into midfield in the manner of the [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) he played alongside at City. Build-up is patient and positional, with [Joshua Kimmich](/players/joshua-kimmich) as the controlling pivot and [Jamal Musiala](/players/jamal-musiala) and [Michael Olise](/players/michael-olise) occupying half-spaces. It is [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with a German intensity: Bayern press with a [high line](/tactics/high-line), the centre-backs often standing near halfway, and counter-press ferociously the moment possession is lost, a clear [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) inheritance.

The criticism in his first season was the exposure of that line to runners in behind, and the evolution over two years has been towards control without sacrificing aggression: a deeper rest-defence, better coordination between the two pivots, and Kane dropping to link play before arriving in the box. The result in 2025-26 was a side that scored freely and conceded little in the league.

[Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) have been outsourced to specialist coaches, and Bayern's delivery to Kane and the centre-backs has become a reliable source of goals.

## Achievements

- Two Bundesliga titles with Bayern (2024-25, 2025-26) and the DFL-Supercup.
- EFL Championship title with Burnley in 2022-23 with 101 points.
- Belgian Cup finalist with Anderlecht in 2022.
- As a player: four Premier League titles, two FA Cups, four League Cups and 89 Belgium caps.

## Storyline

Kompany enters the 2026-27 season as the most secure coach in Germany, a two-time champion whose striker holds the Ballon d'Or and whose club dominates the [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) domestically. The missing piece is Europe. [PSG](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) retained the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history) in Budapest on 30 May 2026 by beating [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) on penalties, and Bayern's failure to reach that final despite their league form is the standard by which Kompany's third season will be measured.

The context around him shifted in the summer. [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) became [Germany](/nations/germany) coach on 24 July 2026 after the national team's early World Cup exit, and the relationship between Bayern's staff and the new national set-up will matter for the German core of the squad. [Niko Kovač](/managers/niko-kovac)'s [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund), [Kasper Hjulmand](/managers/kasper-hjulmand)'s Leverkusen and [Ole Werner](/managers/ole-werner)'s [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig) are the domestic challengers.

The state of the table as of November 2026 cannot be confirmed here; early-season reports suggest Bayern have again set the pace. The narrative is Kompany's: he was the coach nobody wanted in 2024 and he is now the coach everyone studies.
