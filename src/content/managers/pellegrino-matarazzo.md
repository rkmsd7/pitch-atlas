## Biography

Pellegrino Matarazzo was born in Wayne, New Jersey, on 28 November 1977 to Italian parents and holds both nationalities, but his football education is entirely German. He played in the lower leagues there, coached in the academies of Nürnberg and Hoffenheim, and became one of the Bundesliga's most respected young coaches at [VfB Stuttgart](/clubs/vfb-stuttgart). [Real Sociedad](/clubs/real-sociedad) appointed him in December 2025, making him the first American head coach in [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga).

## Playing career

Matarazzo studied mathematics at Columbia University and played for the Lions before moving to Germany in 2000. He was a centre-back for a string of lower-league clubs, Eintracht Braunschweig and Nürnberg's reserves among them, in the regional divisions. He retired in 2010 at 32 and went straight into youth coaching, where his analytical bent found a home.

## Coaching career

He coached Nürnberg's youth and reserve sides, then joined Julian Nagelsmann's staff at Hoffenheim in 2017, part of the generation of German coaches who think in structures and principles. Stuttgart appointed him in December 2019 in the second tier; he won promotion in 2020 and finished ninth in 2020-21 with a bold, young team featuring Silas, Sasa Kalajdzic and Borna Sosa. A difficult 2021-22 ended in dramatic last-day survival and he was sacked in October 2022.

Hoffenheim brought him back in February 2023 and he kept them up, then steered them into Europe with a seventh-place finish in 2023-24 before leaving in November 2024. Real Sociedad, having sacked Sergio Francisco in December 2025 after a poor start, appointed Matarazzo within a week on a contract to the end of 2026-27. He went five games unbeaten after taking over and steadied a club that had been drifting toward the relegation zone.

## Tactical identity

Matarazzo favours a [back three](/tactics/back-three), typically a 3-4-2-1 with aggressive wing-backs and two creative players behind a striker, though at Hoffenheim he switched regularly to a 4-2-3-1. His teams build from the back with the central centre-back stepping into midfield, a touch of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), and press high in coordinated waves, a legacy of his Nagelsmann years and the German [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) school.

He is flexible in-game, often changing shape between halves, and his Stuttgart side was one of the Bundesliga's most dangerous [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) teams. At Real Sociedad he inherited a technically gifted squad used to Imanol Alguacil's possession game and has tried to combine that control with more vertical attacks and a sturdier defensive block, especially away from Anoeta. Mikel Oyarzabal remains the reference point in attack and Martín Zubimendi's departure in 2025 left a midfield he has been rebuilding.

## Achievements

- Promotion to the Bundesliga with Stuttgart in 2020 and a ninth-place finish in 2020-21.
- Guided Hoffenheim into European qualification with seventh place in 2023-24.
- The first American to manage a La Liga club, from December 2025.
- Stabilised Real Sociedad in 2025-26 after a relegation-threatened start.

## Storyline

Real Sociedad's 2026-27 season is Matarazzo's first full one, and it is the test of whether a mid-season firefighter can become a long-term builder. His contract runs to the end of this season, which makes the campaign an extended audition. The club lost ground on the Basque rival [Athletic Bilbao](/clubs/athletic-bilbao), now coached by [Edin Terzić](/managers/edin-terzic), and the derby is the yardstick for supporters who expect European football as standard.

The broader La Liga context is dominated by [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), champions in 2025 and 2026 under [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick), and by [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s return to [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid). Real Sociedad's ambitions are more modest: a top-six finish, a cup run and the continued emergence of Zubieta academy products. Oyarzabal, scorer of the Euro 2024 winner and a member of the [Spain](/nations/spain) squad that won the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), is the captain and the link between the Alguacil era and this one. As of November 2026 the mood at Anoeta is cautiously positive, with Matarazzo's calm and his willingness to trust young players earning him credit, and the club's hierarchy has indicated it will decide on an extension based on the season's trajectory rather than any single result.
