## Biography

Ottmar Hitzfeld was born in Lörrach, on the Swiss border, on 12 January 1949, and trained as a mathematics and PE teacher. "Der General" became the most successful German club coach of his generation, the first to win the Champions League with two clubs, [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) in 1997 and [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) in 2001, and the winner of seven Bundesliga titles. He finished his career with six years as coach of Switzerland, retiring after the 2014 World Cup.

## Playing career

Hitzfeld was a prolific striker in Switzerland for Basel, where he won two titles, Lugano and Luzern, with a spell at Stuttgart in the Bundesliga. He scored 72 goals in 112 games for Basel and was the Swiss league's top scorer in 1973. He played for West Germany at the 1972 Olympics, scoring five goals, and retired in 1983.

His Dortmund, with Matthias Sammer, Andreas Möller and Karl-Heinz Riedle, won back-to-back titles and beat Juventus 3-1 in Munich in the 1997 final. At Bayern he lost the 1999 final to Manchester United's stoppage-time goals, won three straight titles, and beat Valencia on penalties in Milan in 2001 with Oliver Kahn's saves. He returned in 2007-08 to win a double. Switzerland under him beat Spain in the opening game of the 2010 World Cup and reached the last 16 in 2014.

## Tactical identity

Hitzfeld's teams were compact, organised and devastating in transition. His preferred shape was a 4-4-2 that could become a 3-5-2 or a 3-4-3 with a libero, Sammer at Dortmund and Lothar Matthäus at Bayern, in a [back three](/tactics/back-three) that gave the defence a spare man and the attack a deep playmaker. The 2001 Bayern side defended in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) with Kahn, Thomas Linke and Samuel Kuffour and attacked on the [counter](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Mehmet Scholl, Giovane Élber and Hasan Salihamidžić. He was a calm, analytical presence, famous for rotating squads before rotation was fashionable and for managing egos with the patience of a schoolteacher.

## Achievements

- Champions League with Dortmund (1997) and Bayern (2001), the first coach to win it with two clubs in the Champions League era
- Seven Bundesliga titles, a record shared with Udo Lattek until Guardiola and Flick's Bayern
- Two Intercontinental Cups (1997, 2001)
- World Coach of the Year 1997 and 2001

## Storyline

Retired. Hitzfeld bridged the gap between the libero era and the modern Bundesliga, and his two European Cups remain the standard for German coaches. Bayern's 2025-26 title under Vincent Kompany made it a club record run of dominance, built on foundations Hitzfeld laid with Kahn, Scholl and Uli Hoeneß in the boardroom.

The torch is carried by the pragmatists of the Bundesliga and by coaches who win in Europe with organisation rather than possession: [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti), who later matched his feat of European titles with different clubs, [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) and, in a different register, [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), who took Dortmund back to a Champions League final in 2013. Hitzfeld lives quietly in Lörrach and the Swiss countryside, occasionally commenting on Bayern, never returning to the bench.
