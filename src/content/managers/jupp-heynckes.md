## Biography

Josef Heynckes was born in Mönchengladbach on 9 May 1945. "Osram", for the way his face reddened under pressure, was one of the great strikers of the Bundesliga's first decades and then a coach whose reputation was finally settled in his late sixties: the 2013 treble with [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), including a 7-0 aggregate against Barcelona and a Wembley final against Dortmund, remains the finest season by a German club. He had already won the Champions League with [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) in 1998, the club's first in 32 years, and been sacked a week later. He came out of retirement at 72 in 2017 to win Bayern another title and then retired for good.

## Playing career

Heynckes scored 220 Bundesliga goals, the fourth most in history, for [Borussia Mönchengladbach](/clubs/borussia-monchengladbach) and Hannover 96, and won four Bundesliga titles, the 1975 UEFA Cup and the 1973 DFB-Pokal with Gladbach's great side of Günter Netzer and Berti Vogts. For West Germany he won Euro 1972 and the 1974 World Cup, scoring 14 goals in 39 caps. He retired in 1978 and became Udo Lattek's assistant at Gladbach within months.

Real Madrid beat Juventus 1-0 in Amsterdam with Predrag Mijatović's goal; Lorenzo Sanz sacked Heynckes anyway for finishing fourth in La Liga. His 2011-13 Bayern, with Arjen Robben, Franck Ribéry, Thomas Müller, Bastian Schweinsteiger and Philipp Lahm, lost the 2012 final on penalties to Chelsea in their own stadium and responded with a 91-point league, the cup and the Champions League; Pep Guardiola inherited the squad. Bayern called him back when Carlo Ancelotti was sacked in 2017 and he won the double at 73, before handing over to Niko Kovač.

## Tactical identity

Heynckes's 2013 Bayern was the model of German [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) married to possession: a 4-2-3-1 with Robben and Ribéry cutting in from the wings, Müller as the roaming raumdeuter behind Mario Mandžukić, Javi Martínez and Schweinsteiger as a pressing double pivot and full-backs Lahm and David Alaba pushing high. The team pressed in packs the instant the ball was lost, held a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and controlled the ball with [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) principles, though it was more direct than Guardiola's version a year later. His 1998 Madrid was a more cautious [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) side built on Fernando Hierro and Fernando Redondo. His gift was calm: players spoke of a coach who demanded everything without shouting.

## Achievements

- Champions League 1998 (Real Madrid) and 2013 (Bayern), plus the 2012 final
- The 2013 treble, the first by a German club
- Four Bundesliga titles across three decades
- FIFA World Coach of the Year 2013; UEFA Coach of the Year 2013
- As a player: World Cup 1974, Euro 1972, 220 Bundesliga goals

## Storyline

Retired. Heynckes's 2013 team is the benchmark for Bayern, whose 2025-26 title under Vincent Kompany restored the club's dominance, and for every German coach who talks about the balance between pressing and possession. His 1998 sacking became a cautionary tale about Real Madrid's presidents, revisited when [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) returned to the Bernabéu in 2026.

The torch is carried by [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), whose Dortmund lost the Wembley final and whose pressing drew on the same German sources, by Hansi Flick, whose 2020 treble was consciously modelled on 2013, and by Guardiola, who inherited the squad and called it the best he had received. Heynckes lives on a farm in the Lower Rhine and still declines interviews about tactics.
