## Biography

Oliver Glasner was born in Salzburg on 28 August 1974 and spent almost his entire adult life inside one club, SV Ried, first as a player and then as its sporting director and coach. That long apprenticeship in provincial Austria shaped a manager who is unusually calm, unusually direct with players and unusually good at making a limited squad play above its means. He arrived in England in February 2024 with a reputation as a European specialist and left [Crystal Palace](/clubs/crystal-palace) in 2026 as the man who had delivered the club's first major trophy.

## Playing career

Glasner was a composed centre-back who played more than 500 matches for Ried between 1992 and 2011. He captained the side, won the Austrian Cup twice (1998 and 2011) and was known for reading the game rather than for pace or power. A serious head injury suffered in 2011 effectively ended his playing days and pushed him into the club's football administration, where he worked under the Red Bull-era thinkers who were then reshaping Austrian football.

## Coaching career

He took charge of Ried in 2014 and then LASK in 2015, where he built one of the most admired teams in Austrian history: promotion in 2017, a run to the Europa League and a brand of aggressive, organised football that made Europe notice. [Wolfsburg](/leagues/bundesliga) hired him in 2019 and he took them into the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league) in 2021 before moving to [Eintracht Frankfurt](/clubs/eintracht-frankfurt), where the 2022 Europa League triumph (beating [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) at Camp Nou, West Ham, and Rangers on penalties in Seville) became the defining night of his career.

Crystal Palace appointed him in February 2024. The second half of that season, with Michael Olise and Eberechi Eze flying, was the best football Selhurst Park had seen in a generation. In May 2025 Palace beat [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) 1-0 at Wembley to win the FA Cup, the club's first major honour, and added the Community Shield against [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool). Glasner left Palace in the summer of 2026 and was appointed by [Nottingham Forest](/clubs/nottingham-forest) ahead of the 2026-27 [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) season, with Pierre Sage taking over at Palace.

## Tactical identity

Glasner's football is built on a [back three](/tactics/back-three) that becomes a back five without the ball and a 3-2-5 with it. Wing-backs provide the width; two number tens drift inside behind a physical centre-forward. The build-up is patient from the three centre-backs but the moment possession is won high up the pitch the instruction is to go forward in three passes or fewer, which makes his sides among the most dangerous [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) teams in Europe.

Out of possession he uses a mid-block with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps): the front three show opponents into the wide channels, the wing-back jumps, and the nearest central midfielder closes the passing lane. He is also a meticulous set-piece coach, with Frankfurt and Palace both scoring heavily from rehearsed corners. The approach evolved in England toward more [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into the strikers because Premier League defences give less time, and at Forest he inherited a squad already comfortable in that mould.

## Achievements

- Europa League winner 2022 with Eintracht Frankfurt, the club's first European trophy since 1980.
- FA Cup winner 2025 and Community Shield winner 2025 with Crystal Palace.
- Promotion with LASK in 2017 and Austrian Cup wins as a player with Ried.
- Took Wolfsburg into the Champions League in 2021.

## Storyline

Forest hired Glasner because he has done the hard thing twice: win a knockout trophy with a club that was not supposed to. The 2026-27 season began with Forest looking to consolidate as a European-level club after their recent top-half finishes. His honeymoon has been a question of fit rather than style, since Forest already had the pace in wide areas that his 3-4-2-1 needs, and early-season reports suggested the squad took to the back three quickly.

The context around him is a Premier League that has changed shape. [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) are champions under [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta), [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) have moved on from [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) to [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca), and his former club Palace are now coached by Pierre Sage. Glasner's calm, no-drama press conferences have made him a favourite with Forest supporters, and his record suggests that if the club reaches a cup semi-final in the spring, nobody will want to draw them. As of November 2026 the pressure is low and the expectation is simply a stable, well-drilled side; whether that becomes something more depends on January and on how quickly the forwards learn his transitions.
