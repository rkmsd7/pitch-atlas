## Biography

Marcello Lippi was born in Viareggio on 12 April 1948. The silver-haired, cigar-smoking Tuscan was the most successful Italian club coach of the 1990s, the man who made [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) champions of Europe in 1996 and reached three consecutive Champions League finals, and then the coach of [Italy](/nations/italy)'s fourth World Cup win in Berlin in 2006, in the middle of the Calciopoli scandal. He later became the first coach to win the Champions League and the AFC Champions League, with Guangzhou Evergrande, and retired for good in 2019 after a spell with China.

## Playing career

Lippi was a libero for Sampdoria, where he played 274 games between 1969 and 1979, and finished with Pistoiese and Lucchese. He was not capped by Italy. He began coaching in Sampdoria's youth system in 1982.

His first Juventus side, with Gianluca Vialli, Fabrizio Ravanelli, Alessandro Del Piero and Antonio Conte, won the 1995 double and beat Ajax on penalties in Rome in 1996, then lost finals to Dortmund and Real Madrid with Zinedine Zidane in the team. The second spell brought two more titles and the 2003 final lost to Milan. The 2006 Italy, with Fabio Cannavaro, Gianluigi Buffon, Andrea Pirlo and Gennaro Gattuso, conceded two goals all tournament, beat Germany in extra time in Dortmund and France on penalties in the final. He resigned after a group-stage exit in 2010 and found a late renaissance in China.

## Tactical identity

Lippi was a pragmatist with an eye for balance. At Juventus he used a 4-3-3 that became a 4-4-2 or a 4-3-1-2 with Zidane behind the strikers, built on a fierce defensive block and rapid transitions, in the Italian [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) tradition but with more pressing than the catenaccio era. With Italy in 2006 he changed shape almost every match, moving between a 4-4-2, a 4-3-1-2 with Francesco Totti and a [back three](/tactics/back-three) late in games, and used [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) decisively: both semi-final goals against Germany and Marco Materazzi's equaliser in the final came from dead-ball situations. His strength was selection and squad harmony, a group that he convinced was being persecuted by the Calciopoli investigation.

## Storyline

Retired. Lippi's Juventus and Lippi's Italy are the models of the Italian school at its best: organised, ruthless, flexible and unbeatable in the moments that matter. His 2006 team is the last Italian side to win anything at a World Cup; the Azzurri missed 2018, 2022 and 2026, and [Roberto Mancini](/managers/roberto-mancini), reappointed in July 2026, inherits the search for that ruthlessness.

The torch is carried by his players turned coaches: [Antonio Conte](/managers/antonio-conte), his captain in 1996, who won five Scudetti of his own; Gennaro Gattuso, who coached Italy until 2026; Andrea Pirlo and Fabio Cannavaro. [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti), who followed him at Juventus, has described Lippi's dressing-room authority as a model. His son Davide Lippi is a players' agent who represents several Italian internationals.
