## Biography

Manuel Luis Pellegrini Ripamonti was born in Santiago on 16 September 1953 and trained as a civil engineer, a qualification that gave him his nickname and his temperament: unhurried, precise, never performative. He has coached for nearly four decades across South America, Spain, England and China, and at 73 he is the oldest head coach in [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga), still producing attractive football at [Real Betis](/clubs/real-betis), where he is under contract until June 2027.

## Playing career

Pellegrini was a one-club centre-back for Universidad de Chile from 1973 to 1986, playing more than 450 matches and winning 28 caps for Chile. He was known for his positional sense and composure rather than for aggression, and he completed his engineering degree while playing, a detail that tells you everything about his approach to preparation.

## Coaching career

He coached across Chile, Ecuador and Argentina through the 1990s, winning titles with LDU Quito, San Lorenzo and [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate). [Villarreal](/clubs/villarreal) brought him to Europe in 2004 and he took a provincial club to a [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league) semi-final in 2006 and second in La Liga in 2008. A single season at [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) in 2009-10 produced 96 points and no title, as Guardiola's Barcelona set a record. At Málaga he reached the 2013 Champions League quarter-final, losing at the death to Dortmund.

[Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) appointed him in 2013 and he won the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) and League Cup in his first season, scoring 102 league goals, and a second League Cup in 2016 before handing over to [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola). After Hebei in China and a season and a half at West Ham, he took over Betis in 2020. There he has delivered the 2022 Copa del Rey, the club's first trophy since 2005, consistent European qualification and the 2025 Conference League final. In August 2026 he became the first Betis coach to reach 100 La Liga wins.

## Tactical identity

Pellegrini's teams are built on a 4-2-3-1 and a possession game that owes something to [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) but is less rigid than the Guardiola school. He wants the ball moved quickly through the double pivot, a creative number ten (Isco at Betis, David Silva at City) linking with inside-cutting wingers, and full-backs providing width. His sides are not pressing machines; they defend in a mid-block with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) and break with pace, a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) threat that was deadly at Villarreal with Riquelme and Forlán.

[Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) are a Betis strength and his substitutions are famously late and calm. The evolution over four decades has been minimal in philosophy and constant in detail: he adapts the team to the players, trusts experience, and refuses to abandon attacking principles when results dip. At Betis he has made the Villamarín a place where opponents are made to chase the ball.

## Achievements

- Premier League champion 2013-14 and two League Cups with Manchester City.
- Copa del Rey 2022 with Real Betis, ending a 17-year wait.
- UEFA Conference League finalist 2025 with Betis.
- League titles in Argentina and Ecuador, a Champions League semi-final with Villarreal, and 100 La Liga wins for Betis (August 2026).

## Storyline

Betis's 2026-27 season is the seventh of the Pellegrini era, an extraordinary span for a Spanish club that once changed managers every year. His contract was extended in November 2025 to June 2027, but speculation about succession surfaced in the spring of 2026, with reports that the board had sounded out candidates including [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola) before he chose [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool). Pellegrini's response was the usual one: keep winning. The 100th league win milestone in August 2026 came with the team again competing for Europe.

The context is a La Liga in which [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) have won two straight titles and [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) has returned to Real Madrid, while Betis's Seville rivals, under Luis García Plaza, are rebuilding from a relegation scare. Isco's longevity and the production line of young Spaniards remain the heart of the project. As of November 2026 the question in Heliópolis is not whether Pellegrini still has it, but how the club manages the transition when a 73-year-old finally decides to stop; for now, he shows no inclination to.
