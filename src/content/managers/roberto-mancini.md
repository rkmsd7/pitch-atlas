## Biography

Roberto Mancini was born in Jesi, in the Marche, on 27 November 1964. He was the languid genius of Sampdoria's one great era and became a coach who won titles with Inter and Manchester City before giving [Italy](/nations/italy) the most joyful period in its modern history, the 2021 European Championship won at Wembley at the end of a record 37-match unbeaten run. He left the Azzurri acrimoniously in 2023 for Saudi Arabia. On 28 July 2026, with Italy having missed a third consecutive [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) and the federation under new president Giovanni Malagò, Mancini was reappointed head coach, with Claudio Ranieri as technical director.

## Playing career

Mancini debuted for Bologna at 16 and joined Sampdoria in 1982, where over 15 seasons he and Gianluca Vialli took the club to its only Scudetto in 1991, four Coppa Italia wins, the 1990 Cup Winners' Cup and the 1992 European Cup final. He later won another title with Lazio in 2000, played briefly for Leicester, and won 36 caps for Italy, playing at Euro 1988. He retired in 2001 as a Lazio player and was head coach of Fiorentina within weeks.

At [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) he won three straight titles, the first awarded after Calciopoli and the next two with a record 97 points. At [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) he won the club's first FA Cup in 42 years and then, on the last kick of the 2011-12 season, their first league title in 44. His first Italy spell took a team that had missed the 2018 World Cup to Euro 2020 glory within three years, before a play-off defeat to North Macedonia cost them 2022. He walked out in August 2023 to coach [Saudi Arabia](/nations/saudi-arabia) and was sacked after the 2024 Asian Cup. He spent part of 2025-26 at Al Sadd in Qatar, leaving in June 2026.

## Tactical identity

Mancini is a believer in attacking, ball-dominant football: his Italy played a 4-3-3 built on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), with Jorginho as the metronome, Marco Verratti and Nicolò Barella as the eights, and wingers Federico Chiesa and Lorenzo Insigne attacking inside. The team held a [high line](/tactics/high-line), pressed in organised [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) and was, for a while, the most entertaining national side in Europe. At City he was more pragmatic, often using a 4-2-3-1 or a back three and giving his best players, Sergio Agüero, David Silva and Yaya Touré, freedom. His great gift is to make players enjoy themselves, and his great weakness is a tendency to fall out with employers.

## Storyline

Italy's 2026 World Cup campaign ended in April 2026 with a play-off defeat to Bosnia and Herzegovina on penalties, after which [Gennaro Gattuso](/managers/gennaro-gattuso) resigned. What followed was chaos: Paolo Maldini quit as technical director, the federation's leadership changed, and a succession of candidates, including [Antonio Conte](/managers/antonio-conte), were linked with the job. Malagò's choice was the man who had won Italy's last trophy, with Ranieri above him to provide the stability the first Mancini era had lacked.

The task is the 2026-27 [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league), with [Belgium](/nations/belgium) among the opponents, and then qualifying for [Euro 2028](/tournaments/euro), where Italy will defend the title they won under Mancini in 2021 and lost in 2024. The squad is better than its results: Gianluigi Donnarumma, Alessandro Bastoni, Riccardo Calafiori, Barella at champions Inter, Sandro Tonali at Tottenham, Moise Kean and Mateo Retegui. Mancini said on his return that Italy's problem was confidence, not talent, which is what he said in 2018, too. Results since July 2026 should be checked against the live data on this site.
