## Biography

Gennaro Ivan Gattuso was born in Corigliano Calabro, Calabria, on 9 January 1978, the son of a sports-mad family from Italy's deep south. As a player he was the embodiment of grinta, the Italian word for grit, a snarling midfield destroyer who won everything with [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) and lifted the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in 2006. As a coach he is more thoughtful than his caricature suggests, a believer in intensity and honesty who has been candid about the cost of the job on his health and mood.

## Playing career

Gattuso began at Perugia before a formative spell at [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) in Glasgow from 1997 to 1998, where he fell in love with British football's physicality and met his wife. After a season at Salernitana he joined Milan in 1999 and stayed 13 years, forming one of the great midfield partnerships with Andrea Pirlo under Carlo Ancelotti. He won two Scudetti, two [Champions Leagues](/tournaments/champions-league-history) (2003 and 2007), the Coppa Italia and the Club World Cup, and earned 73 caps for [Italy](/nations/italy), winning the World Cup in Berlin in 2006. He retired at Sion in 2013.

## Coaching career

His coaching career began chaotically: a few months as player-manager at Sion, six games at Palermo, a season at OFI Crete, and then the job that taught him most, Pisa, where he won promotion from Lega Pro in 2016 and endured an unpaid, unstable season in Serie B. Milan brought him home to coach the Primavera in 2017 and promoted him to the first team that November. He took the Rossoneri to fifth in 2018-19, one point from the Champions League, and left on principle.

[Napoli](/clubs/napoli) appointed him in December 2019 and he won the 2020 Coppa Italia against [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) on penalties, then finished fifth in 2020-21 by a single point, losing the last game to Verona. A brief, unhappy stint at [Valencia](/clubs/valencia) in 2022, a turbulent season at [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) and a year at Hajduk Split followed.

The Italian federation appointed him national coach in 2025 after [Luciano Spalletti](/managers/luciano-spalletti)'s exit, tasked with reaching the 2026 World Cup. Italy fell into the play-offs and lost to Bosnia and Herzegovina, a third successive failure to qualify, and Gattuso resigned on 3 April 2026. [Lazio](/clubs/lazio) gave him a route back in June 2026 on a two-year deal reportedly worth €1.5m net a year, succeeding [Maurizio Sarri](/managers/maurizio-sarri), who had left for [Atalanta](/clubs/atalanta). His first competitive match was a shock: a 2-0 home defeat to Serie B side Mantova in the Coppa Italia.

## Tactical identity

Gattuso's teams press. His Napoli played a 4-3-3 with a high, aggressive first line and midfielders stepping into [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) triggers the moment the ball was lost. He likes a physical holding midfielder, quick wingers who attack the inside channels and a striker who occupies both centre-backs. The influence of his Ancelotti years shows in a preference for a rhombus or 4-3-3 that keeps two central creators close together.

He has become more pragmatic with time. At Marseille and with Italy he used a 4-2-3-1 and a deeper block, accepting that elite [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) is sometimes the better route, and he values [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) as a cheap source of goals. At Lazio he inherited a squad built for Sarri's rigid positional 4-3-3, and he has openly said the team must improve in many areas, hinting at a transition towards more [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) and vertical transitions through the wingers.

## Achievements

- Coppa Italia winner with Napoli, 2019-20.
- Promotion with Pisa from Lega Pro in 2016.
- Fifth-place finishes with Milan (2018-19) and Napoli (2020-21).
- As a player: World Cup 2006, two Champions Leagues and two Serie A titles with Milan.

## Storyline

Gattuso's Lazio story opened badly and has been about recovery ever since. The Mantova defeat in August 2026 brought the familiar Roman pressure immediately, and the Biancocelesti's ownership under Claudio Lotito is not known for patience. The club missed European football under Sarri's final season and wants a return to the top six, with [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a) more crowded than ever in 2026-27 under [Cristian Chivu](/managers/cristian-chivu)'s [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), [Massimiliano Allegri](/managers/massimiliano-allegri)'s Napoli and Spalletti's Juventus.

The derby with [Roma](/clubs/roma), now coached by [Gian Piero Gasperini](/managers/gian-piero-gasperini), is the fixture that will define his standing with the Curva Nord. There is also the personal context: Gattuso carries the burden of Italy's World Cup absence while [Spain](/nations/spain) lifted the trophy in New Jersey in July 2026, and the Lazio job is his chance to rebuild a reputation that the national team damaged. How the league campaign stands as of November 2026 cannot be verified here; what is known is that he remains in post and that his honesty, as ever, has won him supporters even when results have not.
