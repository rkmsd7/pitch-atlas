## Biography

Luis Enrique Martínez García was born in Gijón on 8 May 1970 and has never done anything by half: he ran marathons and Ironman triathlons in retirement, left [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) for [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) as a player and treated the resulting hostility as fuel, and as a coach has won the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league) three times. At [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) since 2023 he has built the first PSG side to win Europe, and then the first to retain it.

## Playing career

He began at Sporting Gijón and joined Real Madrid in 1991, winning La Liga in 1995, before moving to Barcelona on a free in 1996, where he became captain and a club icon, scoring more than 100 goals from midfield and wide positions. He won two La Liga titles and two Copas del Rey at Barcelona, retired in 2004 and earned 62 caps for [Spain](/nations/spain), playing at three [World Cups](/tournaments/world-cup).

## Coaching career

He coached Barcelona B into the Segunda División (2008-11), had a rough year at [Roma](/clubs/roma) and a good one at Celta Vigo before Barcelona appointed him in 2014. The 2014-15 season was the second treble in the club's history, with [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi), Luis Suárez and Neymar scoring 122 goals between them and the Champions League won in Berlin against [Juventus](/clubs/juventus). He left in 2017 with nine trophies in three years.

As Spain coach (2018-22, with a pause in 2019 for family reasons) he reached the Euro 2020 semi-final and the 2021 Nations League final and laid the ground for the Luis de la Fuente era. PSG hired him in July 2023. After [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe)'s departure in 2024 he rebuilt the team around [Ousmane Dembélé](/players/ousmane-dembele), Vitinha and a group of young signings, and in May 2025 PSG beat [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) 5-0 in Munich for their first Champions League. On 30 May 2026 in Budapest they retained it, beating [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) 4-3 on penalties after a 1-1 draw. Ligue 1 has been won every season of his tenure, the club's fifth straight in 2025-26.

## Tactical identity

Luis Enrique is a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) coach with an aggressive edge. PSG build from a 4-3-3 in which the full-backs push into midfield, Vitinha runs the game from the base and the front three rotate constantly, with Dembélé often operating as a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine). The defensive line is extremely high ([high line](/tactics/high-line)) and the counter-press is immediate and coordinated, [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) learned from the Barcelona of his playing days and sharpened in Paris.

What distinguishes him is the insistence on the collective over the star. He sold or sidelined big names, dropped players who did not press, and built a squad of interchangeable, hard-running technicians. He also coaches the details: PSG's pressing triggers, their rest defence against counters, and their [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) are all notably well drilled. The evolution from Barcelona to Paris is toward a more intense, less possession-obsessed version of the same idea.

## Achievements

- Three Champions League titles as a coach: Barcelona 2015, PSG 2025 and 2026.
- Treble with Barcelona in 2015 and a domestic treble with PSG in 2025.
- Three Ligue 1 titles and two Coupes de France with PSG; two La Liga titles and three Copas del Rey with Barcelona.
- Nations League finalist and Euro semi-finalist with Spain.

## Storyline

PSG's 2026-27 season is a test of appetite. Having won everything there is to win in Europe, Luis Enrique faced the loss of Bradley Barcola to [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) for around €125m in a summer window that closed on 1 September 2026, and the question of how long a hungry young team can stay hungry. The club's fifth straight [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1) title in 2025-26 was taken for granted; the Champions League, a three-peat that no club has achieved since Real Madrid in 2018, is the stated target.

Luis Enrique himself has become one of the most respected figures in the game. His press conferences, by turns combative and funny, are must-watch, and his handling of the Mbappé era, which looked like a gamble in 2024, now looks like a masterstroke. The challengers are familiar: Arsenal, who took PSG to penalties in Budapest, a resurgent [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho), and [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick)'s Barcelona. As of November 2026 PSG are again the team everyone measures themselves against, and Luis Enrique, who has spoken of coaching only as long as the fire lasts, shows every sign of still burning.
