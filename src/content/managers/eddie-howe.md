## Biography

Edward John Frank Howe was born in Amersham on 29 November 1977 and raised in Verwood, Dorset. He is the most successful English manager of his generation: the man who saved AFC Bournemouth from extinction and took them to the Premier League, and who gave [Newcastle United](/clubs/newcastle-united) their first domestic trophy since 1955 with the 2025 League Cup. On 30 July 2026, four weeks before the new season, he informed Newcastle of his decision to step down after nearly five years; Matthias Jaissle replaced him on 5 August. Howe has no job lined up and is expected to take a break from football.

## Playing career

Howe was a centre-back who played over 200 games for Bournemouth between 1995 and 2002, earned England under-21 caps, and had a £400,000 move to Portsmouth that was wrecked by a knee injury on his debut. He returned to Bournemouth on loan and permanently, and retired in 2007 at 29 because of the knee, moving straight into the club's coaching staff.

Appointed at 31 with Bournemouth bottom of League Two and docked 17 points, he kept them up, won two promotions and, after a brief spell at Burnley, returned to take the club to the top flight in 2015. Newcastle's new owners hired him in November 2021 with the club in the relegation zone; he finished 11th, then fourth, then won the League Cup against Liverpool in March 2025 and qualified for the Champions League again. The 2025-26 season was more difficult, with Alexander Isak sold to Liverpool and a heavy injury list, and he left with the club's thanks and a statue's worth of goodwill on Tyneside.

## Tactical identity

Howe is an English coach with a continental pressing identity. His Newcastle played a 4-3-3 with a ferocious high press, in the [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) family, a [high line](/tactics/high-line) with Sven Botman and Fabian Schär, and a midfield of Bruno Guimarães, Joelinton and Sandro Tonali that out-ran almost everyone. The attack relied on quick transitions, with [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Anthony Gordon and Isak, and on set pieces drilled by his long-time staff. At Bournemouth he had been more of a possession coach; at Newcastle he adapted to the squad and to the Premier League's physical demands. He is known for extreme attention to detail, individual player programmes and a quiet, courteous public manner that hides a very demanding training ground.

## Achievements

- League Cup 2024-25 with Newcastle, the club's first domestic trophy in 70 years
- Two Champions League qualifications with Newcastle (2023, 2025)
- Three promotions with Bournemouth, including the 2015 Championship title
- LMA Manager of the Year 2015 and 2023; Premier League Manager of the Season 2023

## Storyline

Howe's exit, on the eve of the 2026-27 season, surprised Tyneside because it came from him. Reports in the days afterwards agreed that he was tired after five intense years and wanted time away, and that with every Premier League job filled there was no immediate alternative. Speculation about his future has centred on England, where [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel) is contracted to Euro 2028 and Howe has always been the leading English candidate, on the Bundesliga, and on [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united), who in November 2026 were a point above the relegation zone under Michael Carrick.

Whatever he chooses, his place in English coaching is secure. He has built two clubs, won with both, and done it with a style that persuaded Premier League owners that an English manager could play modern, intense football. The next job will be his choice rather than his necessity. His status after November 2026 should be checked against the live data on this site.
