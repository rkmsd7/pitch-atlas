## Biography

Lionel Sebastián Scaloni was born in Pujato, Santa Fe, on 16 May 1978. He was the most unlikely of World Cup-winning coaches: an assistant promoted on an interim basis in 2018 with no senior management experience, who then built the most successful [Argentina](/nations/argentina) team since 1986. He won the 2021 [Copa América](/tournaments/copa-america), the 2022 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in Qatar, the 2024 Copa América and took Argentina to the 2026 World Cup final, where they lost 1-0 to Spain after extra time. His contract runs to December 2026, and the AFA confirmed renewal talks after the final.

## Playing career

Scaloni was a right-back and later a right midfielder who came through Newell's Old Boys, won the Clausura 1998 with Estudiantes, and then spent nine years in Spain with Deportivo La Coruña, winning the 2002 Copa del Rey. He had loan spells at West Ham, where he played in the 2006 FA Cup final, Racing Santander, Lazio and Mallorca, and finished at Atalanta in 2015. He won seven caps for Argentina and was in the 2006 World Cup squad.

Appointed on a temporary basis after the 2018 World Cup, with Pablo Aimar, Walter Samuel and Roberto Ayala as his staff, Scaloni won a dressing room by defending it. The 2021 Copa América at the Maracanã ended a 28-year wait for a senior title and [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi)'s wait for one with Argentina. The 2022 World Cup final against France, 3-3 and won on penalties, is widely regarded as the greatest final ever played. The 2024 Copa América followed, and in 2026 Argentina reached a second successive final.

## Tactical identity

Scaloni's Argentina are a modern [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) team with the ball-security of a possession side. The base shape is a 4-3-3 or 4-4-2 depending on whether Messi plays as a false nine or a right-sided free role; without the ball they drop into a disciplined [low block](/tactics/low-block) with Rodrigo De Paul and [Enzo Fernández](/players/enzo-fernandez) shuttling, and in possession they build with [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) patterns through Alexis Mac Allister. He has been unusually flexible for a national coach, changing shape between matches and even within them, and has rebuilt the defence twice, around Cristian Romero and Lisandro Martínez. His greatest skill is emotional: he kept a group with Messi at its centre humble and hungry for eight years.

## Storyline

The 2026 World Cup final at MetLife Stadium was Argentina's chance to retain the trophy, something no team has done since Brazil in 1962. They beat [England](/nations/england) 2-1 in the semi-final and [Egypt](/nations/egypt) in the round of 16, but in the final Enzo Fernández was sent off and Ferran Torres scored for [Spain](/nations/spain) in the 106th minute. Scaloni questioned his own future on the pitch afterwards, as he has after every tournament; the AFA confirmed talks with president Claudio Tapia within days. In October 2026 Argentine outlets reported a verbal agreement on a new deal that could run to the 2030 World Cup, but no signing had been announced as of November 2026, and Walter Samuel was reported to be leaving the staff.

Whatever the paperwork says, the task is the same: life after Messi. His international retirement after the 2026 final was reported as the end of the greatest career in the sport, and the next cycle belongs to Julián Álvarez, Fernández, Mac Allister, Nico Paz, Franco Mastantuono and the Boca and River academies. The 2027 [Copa América](/tournaments/copa-america) and the 2030 qualifiers come first; the 2030 World Cup's centenary matches in Uruguay, Argentina and Paraguay give the project an emotional deadline. Results since the final should be checked against the live data on this site.
