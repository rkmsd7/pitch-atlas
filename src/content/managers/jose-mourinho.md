## Biography

José Mário dos Santos Mourinho Félix was born in Setúbal on 26 January 1963, the son of a goalkeeper. He is the most quotable, most combative and, for a decade, most successful manager of his generation, the only coach to win the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league) with two clubs who were not favourites and to win all three UEFA club competitions. His return to [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) on 13 July 2026, thirteen years after leaving, was one of the stories of the summer.

## Playing career

Mourinho was a midfielder of limited gifts who played in the Portuguese lower tiers for Rio Ave, Belenenses and Sesimbra, and he knew early that his future was on the touchline. He studied sport science in Lisbon, coached youth teams, and became Bobby Robson's interpreter and then assistant at Sporting, Porto and Barcelona, before working under Louis van Gaal at Camp Nou.

## Coaching career

After short spells at Benfica and Leiria he took over [Porto](/clubs/porto) in 2002 and won the UEFA Cup in 2003 and the Champions League in 2004. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) hired him that summer; he won back-to-back [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) titles with a record 95 points in 2005. At [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) he won the 2010 treble, beating [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in the semi-final and Bayern in the final. His first Madrid spell (2010-13) produced the 100-point La Liga of 2012 and the fiercest Clásico rivalry of the modern age with [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola).

A second Chelsea title in 2015 was followed by a steep fall. [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) brought a Europa League and League Cup in 2017; [Tottenham](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) ended without a trophy; [Roma](/clubs/roma) gave him the inaugural Conference League in 2022 and a Europa League final. Fenerbahçe (2024-25) ended acrimoniously, and a second spell at [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) in 2025-26 restored his standing enough for Madrid, who had parted with [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) midway through 2025-26, to bring him back.

## Tactical identity

Mourinho is the modern master of the reactive game. His teams defend in a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block) or mid-block, deny central space, and attack with devastating [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) speed. At Madrid in 2011-12 this meant [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo), Di María and Özil breaking from a 4-2-3-1 with Xabi Alonso launching [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) from deep. At Inter in 2010 it meant a famous ten-man rearguard at Camp Nou.

He uses [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) rather than a sustained high press, choosing moments to jump, and he has always been strong at set pieces and in-game management. His later teams have been accused of being too passive, but at Benfica he showed a more front-footed version, and the Madrid squad he inherited, with [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe), [Vinícius Júnior](/players/vinicius-junior) and [Jude Bellingham](/players/jude-bellingham), is built for transitions.

## Achievements

- Champions League winner with Porto (2004) and Inter (2010), plus the UEFA Cup, Europa League and Conference League.
- League titles in Portugal, England (3), Italy (2) and Spain, 26 major trophies in all.
- Record 95-point Premier League (2005) and 100-point La Liga (2012).
- Inter's 2010 treble, the only one in Italian history.

## Storyline

Madrid's 2026-27 season is a Mourinho season in every sense. He arrived on a contract to 2029 after Alonso's mid-season exit, into a squad that had just watched [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) win a second straight La Liga and [PSG](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) retain the Champions League. The club spent around €125m on Yan Diomande from [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig), and Mbappé arrived back from the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) as Golden Boot winner with ten goals, a runner-up medal and, in October, second place in the Ballon d'Or behind [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane).

The storyline is a familiar one: Mourinho versus the establishment. His press conferences have already reignited the feud with the Spanish media, and the Clásico against [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick)'s Barcelona is the fixture of the season. Early-season reports suggested a tighter, more organised Madrid than the one he inherited, built around Bellingham's running and Mbappé's finishing, though with the season in progress it is too early to say where it ends. What is certain is that the Bernabéu wanted a winner and a fighter, and at 63 Mourinho is determined to prove that the third act can be as good as the first.
