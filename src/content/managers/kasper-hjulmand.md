## Biography

Kasper Hjulmand was born in Aalborg on 9 April 1972 and grew up in a Danish football culture that has long prized education over celebrity. He is a thinker, a coach who talks about human relationships before formations and who became a national figure in the summer of 2021 for the dignity with which he led [Denmark](/nations/denmark) after Christian Eriksen's cardiac arrest at Euro 2020. He is also, less noticed, one of Europe's more sophisticated positional coaches, whose Nordsjælland sides were small-club laboratories for the ideas now mainstream.

## Playing career

Hjulmand was a midfielder in Denmark's lower divisions, playing for Frem, Hvidovre, Lyngby and Brønshøj, until a knee injury ended his career at 26. He moved immediately into coaching, taking charge of Lyngby's youth teams while still in his twenties.

## Coaching career

He became Lyngby's head coach in 2008 and won promotion to the Superliga in 2010. Nordsjælland hired him in 2011 and he won the club's first and only Danish title in 2011-12, qualifying for the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history) group stage. Mainz took him to the [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) in 2014, but a difficult season ended with his dismissal in February 2015, an experience he has described as formative. He returned to Nordsjælland in 2016 and built a side from the club's Right to Dream academy pipeline, developing players such as Mikkel Damsgaard and Mohammed Kudus.

Appointed Denmark coach in 2020, he took the national side to the semi-finals of Euro 2020, beating Wales and the Czech Republic before losing to [England](/nations/england) in extra time at Wembley, and to the 2022 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) and Euro 2024. He resigned after the latter, a round-of-16 exit to [Germany](/nations/germany).

[Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) appointed him in 2025 after a summer of upheaval in which [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) left for [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), [Florian Wirtz](/players/florian-wirtz) and others departed, and Alonso's initial successor was quickly replaced. Hjulmand's task was to stabilise a club that had won the 2023-24 double and to rebuild a competitive squad. He did so through the 2025-26 season, finishing sixth behind [Vincent Kompany](/managers/vincent-kompany)'s champions [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) and qualifying for the Europa League, but the board wanted more, and he was sacked at the end of the season. [Carles Martínez Novell](/managers/carles-martinez-novell), the former Toulouse coach, replaced him from 1 July 2026.

## Tactical identity

Hjulmand is a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) coach with Scandinavian pragmatism. His Denmark side moved between a 3-4-2-1 and a 4-3-3 depending on opponent, and at Leverkusen he used the [back three](/tactics/back-three) that Alonso made the club's identity, with wing-backs providing width and two creators behind a striker. Build-up is patient, with the goalkeeper involved and the central midfielders forming a box with the two inside forwards.

Out of possession his teams hold a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and set structured [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) that steer the ball wide before a coordinated jump. He is especially attentive to the emotional side of a dressing room, and his man-management is often cited as his greatest strength: Denmark's players spoke openly about the trust he built, and Leverkusen's younger signings were given time to grow rather than judged on early results.

The evolution of his work has moved from the romantic, open Nordsjælland sides to the more controlled, risk-aware football of the national team and finally a Leverkusen that tried to combine Alonso's structural legacy with a quicker transition game.

## Achievements

- Danish Superliga champion 2011-12 with Nordsjælland, the club's only title.
- Euro 2020 semi-finalist with Denmark; World Cup 2022 and Euro 2024 qualification.
- Promotion to the Superliga with Lyngby in 2010.
- Danish Coach of the Year honours in 2012 and 2021.

## Storyline

Leverkusen's story since 2025 was one of managed decline avoided, and Hjulmand was the man who avoided it. The club lost its coach, its best player and its aura in one summer, and his calm presence was a deliberate choice by sporting director Simon Rolfes to rebuild without drama. By the end of 2025-26 the team had re-established itself in the [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga)'s upper tier, finishing sixth and qualifying for the Europa League. It was not enough. With [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane)'s Bayern pulling away and the Champions League places missed, Leverkusen sacked Hjulmand at the end of the season and appointed Carles Martínez Novell from Toulouse on a contract to June 2028.

As of November 2026 Hjulmand is without a club, and the verdict on his year in Germany is kinder than the decision suggests. He inherited a dressing room in shock, kept it together, and handed over a European side. His stock in Scandinavia remains high, his Mainz dismissal in 2015 has long since stopped defining him, and the pattern of his career, a rebuild, a steadying hand, then a move, suggests another job will come: national teams with a settled squad and clubs that need calm after chaos have always suited him best. [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s Germany and [Niko Kovač](/managers/niko-kovac)'s [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) move on without him; the question is which federation or club next decides that a thoughtful Dane is exactly what they need.
