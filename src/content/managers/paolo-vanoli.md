## Biography

Paolo Vanoli was born in Varese, Lombardy, on 12 August 1972. He is a coach shaped by apprenticeship rather than stardom: a decade with Italy's youth national teams, three years beside Antonio Conte at [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) and [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), and then a sequence of difficult jobs taken on because they were available. He is calm in public, methodical in training and known among players for the clarity of his instructions. His 2026 at [Fiorentina](/clubs/fiorentina) was one of the strangest years any Italian coach has lived through: saviour in spring, sacked in May, rehired in September.

## Playing career

Vanoli was a left-back who came through at Varese and made his name at Verona, Parma and Fiorentina, where he played from 1998 to 2000 under Giovanni Trapattoni and alongside Gabriel Batistuta and Rui Costa. He later played for Bologna, Catania and Venezia, retiring in 2006. He was a modest, reliable defender rather than a star, and his understanding of wide defending has informed how he coaches wing-backs.

## Coaching career

He spent seven years with the Italian federation coaching the under-18s, under-19s and under-20s, and was part of the staff that reached the 2017 Under-20 World Cup semi-finals. Conte took him to Chelsea as an assistant in 2018, then to Inter in 2019, where Vanoli was a key figure in the 2020-21 Scudetto staff. Spartak Moscow gave him his first senior job in 2021, and he won the Russian Cup in May 2022 before leaving.

Venezia hired him in 2023, and he took them from mid-table in Serie B to promotion via the play-offs in 2024. Torino followed for a solid 2024-25 season. In early 2026 Fiorentina, drifting towards relegation after Stefano Pioli's spell had broken down, turned to Vanoli and he kept them in [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a). The club nevertheless sacked him in May 2026, preferring Fabio Grosso, the 2006 World Cup winner who had left Sassuolo. Grosso lasted three Serie A matches, all defeats, and was dismissed on 6 September 2026 with Fiorentina bottom of the table. A day later, on 7 September, the club brought Vanoli back, confirming his return in the small hours after reports had initially pointed to Thiago Motta. His first game back was away at Venezia, his old club.

## Tactical identity

Vanoli is a Conte disciple, and the fingerprints are obvious: a [back three](/tactics/back-three), wing-backs who provide all the width, a compact midfield three and two forwards who stay close together. His teams defend in a 5-3-2 block that is lower than Conte's and attack with quick, [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into the strikers, with the wing-backs arriving as the third and fourth runners. [Counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) transitions are the first weapon rather than sustained possession.

What distinguishes him from his mentor is temperament. Where Conte demands relentless intensity, Vanoli is a pragmatist who tailors the shape to the squad: Venezia played a 3-4-2-1 with a fluid front three, Torino a 3-5-2 built around a target striker. He is a strong [set-piece](/tactics/set-pieces) coach, a habit from his youth-team years, and his sides have scored a high proportion of goals from dead balls.

At Fiorentina the first spell was about emergency defence, with Moise Kean as the focal point. The second has begun with the same instincts: tighten the block, restore confidence, and worry about aesthetics later.

## Achievements

- Russian Cup winner 2021-22 with Spartak Moscow.
- Promotion to Serie A with Venezia via the 2024 play-offs.
- Kept Fiorentina in Serie A in the spring of 2026 after a mid-season appointment.
- As an assistant: Premier League 2016-17 and Serie A 2020-21 with Conte.

## Storyline

Few coaches get a second chance at the same club within a single calendar year, and Vanoli's return is both a vindication and a warning. Fiorentina's ownership under Rocco Commisso has become notorious for hiring and sacking, and the decision to replace Vanoli with Grosso and then reverse it within three months was widely criticised in the Italian press as emblematic of the club's drift. Vanoli returned to a dressing room he knew, which was the point: the players had responded to him before and the club needed immediate results rather than a new philosophy.

The 2026-27 season began with zero points from three and the task is survival first, with any ambition beyond mid-table a bonus. The division around him is unusually strong at the top, with [Cristian Chivu](/managers/cristian-chivu)'s [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) as champions, and unusually volatile in the middle, where [Raffaele Palladino](/managers/raffaele-palladino), another ex-Fiorentina coach, has taken over [Bologna](/clubs/bologna) after Domenico Tedesco's sacking in the same September week.

Where Fiorentina sit as of November 2026 cannot be confirmed here. The storyline is simple: a coach proving, for the second time, that the club should never have let him go.
