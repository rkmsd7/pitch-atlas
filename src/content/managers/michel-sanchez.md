## Biography

Miguel Ángel Sánchez Muñoz, known to everyone simply as Míchel, was born in Madrid on 30 October 1975 and grew up in the working-class district of Vallecas, where he became a one-club man in spirit if not quite in deed. He is one of the most quietly influential Spanish coaches of his generation: a manager who, with a small budget and a borrowed squad, took [Girona](/clubs/girona) to third place in [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) in 2023-24, above [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid), and into the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) for the first time in the club's history.

Having left Girona in 2025 after four transformative years, he was appointed head coach of [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) ahead of the 2026-27 season on a contract running to the summer of 2028, succeeding a chaotic 2025-26 that saw three men occupy the dugout.

## Playing career

Míchel was a combative, intelligent central midfielder who spent the vast bulk of his career at Rayo Vallecano, making well over 300 appearances across two spells between 1994 and 2013, interrupted by a season at Real Zaragoza. He captained Rayo through promotions and relegations, and his understanding of a club that lives in the shadow of [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) and Atlético shaped the pragmatism beneath his later idealism. He retired in 2013 and moved straight into Rayo's youth system.

## Coaching career

He worked his way through Rayo's academy before being handed the first team in November 2017 and promptly won promotion to La Liga via the 2018 play-offs. Relegation followed, as it often does with Rayo, and after a brief spell at Huesca in 2020-21 he arrived at Girona in July 2021.

Girona were a Segunda club backed by the City Football Group, and Míchel built something unusual: a side that played [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with the conviction of a [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) team but with loanees and bargains. Promotion came via the 2022 play-offs, a comfortable tenth place in 2022-23 followed, and then the remarkable 2023-24 season in which Girona led La Liga for stretches of the autumn, beat [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) 4-2 at Montjuïc, and finished third with 81 points. The Champions League campaign of 2024-25 was harder; key players such as Dovbyk, Aleix García and Savinho had moved on, and he left in 2025 with his reputation intact.

Ajax, meanwhile, had sacked John Heitinga in November 2025 after a dismal Champions League campaign, used Fred Grim and then Óscar García as caretakers, and finished 2025-26 some way behind [PSV](/clubs/psv-eindhoven). Míchel's appointment was announced on a deal to mid-2028.

## Tactical identity

Míchel's Girona were a laboratory for modern [positional play](/tactics/positional-play). The nominal 4-3-3 became a 3-2-5 in possession: one full-back (often Miguel Gutiérrez or Daley Blind) tucked into the back three while the other, usually Yan Couto, pushed high on the wing, and the pivot was joined by an [inverted full-back](/tactics/inverted-full-backs). Build-up was patient and deliberately provocative, drawing a press before releasing a third-man run.

Out of possession he favoured a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and a coordinated press that used [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the touchline. Set pieces were a quietly important source of goals, with Daley Blind's delivery and a clutch of tall centre-backs.

At Ajax, the logic is natural: the club's [Total Football](/tactics/total-football) heritage and Míchel's Guardiola-influenced principles belong to the same family. Early-season reports suggest he has kept the 4-3-3 shape and emphasised ball retention and the recruitment of ball-playing defenders, though it is too soon to judge the results.

## Achievements

- Promotion to La Liga with Rayo Vallecano (2018) and Girona (2022).
- Girona's best-ever finish, third in La Liga 2023-24, and their first Champions League qualification.
- Widely regarded as one of the best tactical coaches of the 2020s outside the elite clubs.

## Storyline

The Ajax job is a rebuilding exercise as much as a sporting one. The club have churned through sporting directors and coaches since Erik ten Hag left in 2022, and Jordi Cruyff's arrival in a football leadership role in early 2026 was followed by Míchel's appointment as the long-term coach. Expectations are to re-establish Ajax as [Eredivisie](/leagues/eredivisie) champions and a credible European side, ending PSV's run of titles under [Peter Bosz](/managers/peter-bosz).

As of November 2026 the project is in its early months. Míchel has said he wants a young, possession-based team in the Ajax tradition; supporters, scarred by two chaotic seasons, want results against PSV and [Feyenoord](/clubs/feyenoord), now coached again by [Giovanni van Bronckhorst](/managers/giovanni-van-bronckhorst). The Dutch title race in 2026-27 is a three-way contest between three new or returning coaches, and it will define whether Míchel's Girona miracle was a one-off or a method.
