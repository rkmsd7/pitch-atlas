## Biography

Davide Ancelotti was born in Parma on 22 July 1989, the son of [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti), and has spent his adult life learning the trade beside one of the most decorated coaches in history. He was his father's assistant at [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), Napoli, Everton and [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), where he was increasingly credited with the tactical detail of the 2022 and 2024 [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league) wins. [Lille](/clubs/lille) appointed him head coach on 1 June 2026 on a two-year contract, his first senior job in Europe.

## Playing career

Davide was a midfielder in the youth systems of Milan and then Borgomanero, and played briefly for Genoa's academy, but a serious injury and modest ability ended his playing ambitions in his early twenties. He studied sport science and took his coaching badges while still in his twenties, and by 2016 was working alongside his father in Munich as a fitness and tactical coach.

## Coaching career

His decade as an assistant covered an unusual range: Bayern (2016-17), Napoli (2018-19), Everton (2019-21) and Real Madrid (2021-25). At Madrid he ran large parts of training, led set-piece and pressing work and was widely described by players as the staff member who explained the detail. When Carlo left for [Brazil](/nations/brazil) in 2025, Davide chose to go his own way and was appointed head coach of Botafogo in July 2025. The spell lasted five months: a reasonable record in the Brasileirão, no silverware, and a parting in the winter.

Lille, having finished third in 2025-26 under Bruno Génésio and qualified for the Champions League, needed a successor when Génésio's contract expired and he chose [Marseille](/clubs/marseille). They announced Davide on 1 June 2026 on a two-year deal, a bet on a 36-year-old with a famous surname and a reputation among players as an excellent communicator.

## Tactical identity

Davide Ancelotti's approach echoes his father's: adaptable, player-first, built on a solid defensive shape and quick transitions. His Botafogo used a 4-3-3 that became a 4-4-2 without the ball, with [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through fast wingers and a compact mid-block with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) rather than relentless high pressing. He is more structured in possession than Carlo, with clear [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) principles in build-up, a reflection of his generation.

Set pieces are a particular strength; he ran Madrid's dead-ball programme and the club's improvement in that area between 2022 and 2024 was partly his. At Lille he inherited a side with a strong defensive base and quick forwards, and his early work has focused on keeping the solidity that won Génésio third place while adding more combination play in the final third. He speaks French, Spanish, English and Italian, which has helped a dressing room that is as multinational as any in [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1).

## Achievements

- Assistant coach for two Champions League wins (2022, 2024) and two La Liga titles with Real Madrid.
- Assistant at Bayern (Bundesliga 2017) and Napoli, and part of Everton's staff.
- Head coach of Botafogo in 2025, his first senior post.
- Appointed Lille head coach at 36 for the 2026-27 Champions League campaign.

## Storyline

Lille's 2026-27 season is a Champions League season with a novice head coach, and the club is comfortable with that. Lille's model is young coaches, young players and smart sales, and Davide fits it exactly. The club returned to the Champions League league phase after its third-place finish, and a ligue1.com preview in August 2026 framed the campaign as the start of a new era.

The surname is both help and burden. Carlo, now coaching Brazil, is a constant point of comparison, and every tactical decision at Lille will be read against the father's record. Davide has handled it with good humour so far, pointing out that he has been in the dugout for ten years. The domestic picture is the usual one: [PSG](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) under [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique) are five-time consecutive champions and double European champions, and the battle is for second with Marseille, [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) under Filipe Luís and [Lyon](/clubs/lyon). As of November 2026 Lille are, by early reports, competitive and organised, and the Champions League nights at the Stade Pierre-Mauroy will tell whether a son can write his own story.
