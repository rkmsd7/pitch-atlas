## Biography

Khalid Jamil was born in Kuwait on 5 June 1977 to Indian parents and grew up between the Gulf and Mumbai. He is the most successful Indian-born club coach of the modern era, the man behind the fairy tale of Aizawl FC's 2017 I-League title and Jamshedpur's 2022 [Indian Super League](/leagues/indian-super-league) Shield. On 1 August 2025 the All India Football Federation's executive committee appointed him head coach of [India](/nations/india), the first Indian in the role since Savio Medeira in 2012, ahead of Stephen Constantine and Štefan Tarkovič. His contract is for two years with an option for a third; his status as of November 2026 is not verified here.

## Playing career

Jamil was a hard-working midfielder for Mahindra United and Mumbai FC in the National Football League and I-League, winning the Federation Cup with Mahindra in 2003 and 2005. He won 11 caps for India between 2001 and 2006 and was in the squad for the 2002 LG Cup, which India won in Vietnam. He retired in 2009 and moved straight into coaching at Mumbai FC.

The Aizawl title, won with a budget a fraction of the Kolkata giants', is the great romantic story of Indian club football. At Jamshedpur he became the first Indian coach to win the ISL's league stage. At NorthEast United he ended the club's trophy drought with the Durand Cup. His first assignments with the national team were the CAFA Nations Cup in Central Asia in 2025 and the Asian Cup qualifiers, including a double-header against Singapore.

## Tactical identity

Jamil is a pragmatist. His sides defend in a disciplined [low block](/tactics/low-block), usually a 4-4-2 that becomes a 4-5-1, concede little space between the lines and attack on the [counter](/tactics/counter-attacking) through fast wide players and a target man. [Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) are a major weapon; Aizawl and Jamshedpur both scored a high proportion of their goals from dead balls. With India he has leaned on Sandesh Jhingan's organisation at the back, the energy of Anirudh Thapa and Brandon Fernandes in midfield, and the question of who replaces Sunil Chhetri, who came out of retirement in 2024 and was still scoring in 2025, as the focal point. He rarely talks about philosophy; he talks about points.

## Storyline

India did not come close to the 2026 World Cup; the third round of Asian qualifying was beyond them, and Manolo Márquez's short reign ended in the summer of 2025. The AIFF's decision to appoint an Indian, after a dozen years of foreign coaches, was framed by the federation as a statement that Indian coaches deserve a fair chance. Jamil's early mandate was simple: qualify for the 2027 [Asian Cup](/tournaments/asian-cup) in Saudi Arabia, which India reached in 2019 and 2023 but have never progressed from.

The qualifiers ran through 2025 and 2026, with Singapore, Hong Kong and Bangladesh in India's group, and whether India made it to Saudi Arabia, and whether Jamil was still in charge in November 2026, should be checked against the live data on this site. Beyond that lies the 2030 World Cup qualifying cycle and the perennial problems of Indian football: a shrinking pool of strikers, an ISL calendar at odds with the national team, and the search for the next Chhetri in a generation led by Lallianzuala Chhangte and Manvir Singh.
