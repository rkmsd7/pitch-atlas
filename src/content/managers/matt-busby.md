## Biography

Alexander Matthew Busby was born in Orbiston, Lanarkshire, on 26 May 1909, in a miner's cottage; his father was killed on the Somme when he was six. He became [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united)'s manager in 1945, when Old Trafford was a bombed-out shell, and over 24 years created the club's identity: youth, attacking football and European ambition. He built the Busby Babes, lost eight of them in the Munich air disaster of February 1958, nearly died himself, and rebuilt a team that won the European Cup at Wembley in 1968. He was knighted that year and died in Cheadle on 20 January 1994.

## Playing career

Busby was a half-back for Manchester City, where he won the 1934 FA Cup, and for Liverpool, where he was captain before the Second World War interrupted his career. He won one cap for Scotland in 1933 and served as an Army PT instructor during the war, coaching services teams.

Busby demanded, and got, control over team selection and transfers, unprecedented at the time, and insisted on an academy. His first side, with Johnny Carey and Jack Rowley, won the 1948 FA Cup and the 1952 title. The Babes, Duncan Edwards, Tommy Taylor, Roger Byrne and Bobby Charlton, won the title in 1956 with an average age of 22 and again in 1957, and defied the Football League to enter the European Cup. On 6 February 1958, returning from Belgrade, their aircraft crashed on takeoff at Munich; 23 died, including eight players. Busby, twice given the last rites, returned to work within months. With Charlton, Denis Law and George Best he won the title in 1965 and 1967 and beat Benfica 4-1 at Wembley in 1968, the first English club to win the European Cup.

## Tactical identity

Busby's teams attacked. He played the W-M of the era and then a 4-2-4 and a 4-3-3 in the 1960s, with wingers, Best and John Aston, a creative inside-forward in Charlton and Law as a predatory centre-forward. The approach was less a system than a philosophy of freedom and pace: play the ball quickly to the forwards, [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) through the wide players, and trust individual talent. There were elements of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in the way his sides passed through midfield and of [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) in the crosses to Taylor and Law, but the main idea was that the game should be entertaining. He was also the first British manager to take European football seriously, and the Babes' adventures in the early European Cups made United a continental club.

## Storyline

Died 1994. Busby defined what Manchester United means: attacking football, homegrown players and the European Cup as the measure of greatness. The Munich clock at Old Trafford, the statue of Busby outside, the Busby Babes and the 1968 team are the club's founding myths, and [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson), who found a club haunted by him, eventually matched and surpassed him in the same spirit, with the Class of '92 as his Babes and the 1999 treble as his Wembley.

The torch has flickered since. United's decline after 2013, through David Moyes, Louis van Gaal, José Mourinho, Erik ten Hag and Ruben Amorim to Michael Carrick in 2026, with the club fighting relegation in November 2026, is painful to anyone who knows the story. The academy still produces players, Marcus Rashford and Kobbie Mainoo among them, and the belief that United should attack and trust youth is Busby's enduring gift.
