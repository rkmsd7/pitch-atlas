## Biography

José Luis Aragonés Suárez was born in Hortaleza, then a village outside Madrid, on 28 July 1938. He was [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid)'s record scorer as a player and coached the club four times; he coached nearly every major club in Spain; and at 66, with a reputation as a shouting, superstitious traditionalist, he reinvented the [Spain](/nations/spain) national team as a possession side built around small midfielders and won [Euro 2008](/tournaments/euro), the country's first title in 44 years and the start of a run that brought the 2010 World Cup and Euro 2012. He died in Madrid on 1 February 2014.

## Playing career

Aragonés was a tall, left-footed attacking midfielder and free-kick specialist who played for Getafe, Recreativo, Hércules, Oviedo, Betis and, from 1964, Atlético Madrid, where he scored 172 goals in 370 games and won three league titles and two Copas del Rey. He scored Atlético's goal in the 1974 European Cup final, which Bayern won in a replay. He won 11 caps and retired in 1974, taking over as Atlético's coach immediately.

He was Atlético's manager for 11 seasons across four spells, winning the title in 1977 and bringing the club back from the second division in 2002. With Spain he took over after Euro 2004, survived the 2006 World Cup exit to France and a racism controversy over remarks about Thierry Henry, dropped Raúl, the captain and national icon, in 2006, and built the team around Xavi, Andrés Iniesta, Cesc Fàbregas, David Silva and Marcos Senna. Spain won all their matches at Euro 2008, beating Italy on penalties and Russia and Germany in the knockouts, and Fernando Torres's goal in the final in Vienna ended the drought. He left for Fenerbahçe straight afterwards.

## Tactical identity

Aragonés was the midwife of [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka). His insight in 2006-08 was that Spain's best players were small, technical midfielders and that the team should keep the ball rather than compete physically: a 4-1-4-1 with Senna as the pivot, Xavi and Iniesta as the interiors, Silva and Iniesta wide and Torres and David Villa alternating as the striker. The passing structure was [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in all but name and the team pressed in [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) immediately after losing possession, which [Vicente del Bosque](/managers/vicente-del-bosque) kept intact. Before that, his club sides were organised, hard-working 4-4-2 teams in the Atlético tradition; the transformation at 66 was an act of intellectual courage.

## Achievements

- Euro 2008, Spain's first major title since 1964 and the start of their 2008-12 treble
- La Liga 1977 and three Copas del Rey with Atlético Madrid; the 1974 Intercontinental Cup
- Copa del Rey with Barcelona in 1988
- Promotion with Atlético in 2002 and a Champions League place with Mallorca in 2001
- Atlético's record scorer; a statue outside the Metropolitano

## Storyline

Died 2014. Aragonés is the hinge of Spanish football: before him, "La Furia", the belief that Spain's identity was fight and failure; after him, the most successful national team of its era and a style exported to every academy in the country. Spain's 2026 World Cup win under [Luis de la Fuente](/managers/luis-de-la-fuente), with Rodri, Pedri and Lamine Yamal, is the direct descendant of 2008; De la Fuente dedicated the Euro 2024 title to him.

The torch is carried by Del Bosque, De la Fuente, [Xavi](/managers/xavi-manager), [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) and [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique), all of them products of the system he had the nerve to build, and by Atlético, where Diego Simeone, a different kind of Atlético man, has kept the fighting spirit alive. The Wise Man of Hortaleza's statue stands outside the Metropolitano with his arms folded, as if waiting to be proved right.
