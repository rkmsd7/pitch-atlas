## Biography

Sonia Bermúdez Tribano was born in Madrid on 18 November 1984. One of the best Spanish strikers of her era, she scored 35 goals in 63 internationals and won league titles with Rayo Vallecano, Barcelona and Atlético Madrid before moving into the federation's coaching structure. In August 2025, after [Spain](/nations/spain-women) lost the Euro 2025 final to England and parted with Montse Tomé, the RFEF board appointed her head coach of the world champions on a two-year contract. She took charge in October 2025.

## Playing career

Bermúdez began at Atlético Madrid's women's section, moved to Rayo Vallecano, where she won three league titles between 2009 and 2011, and then joined [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona-women) in 2012, winning the league in 2013 and 2014 and a hat-trick of Copa de la Reina titles. She finished at Valencia, Atlético, Levante and Madrid CFF, retiring in 2021 after a short spell in Mexico. With Spain she scored in the team's first World Cup, in 2015, and played at Euro 2013 and 2017.

The federation appointed her to the under-23 team in September 2024 as part of the restructuring that followed the 2023 World Cup and the Rubiales scandal, and promoted her to the senior job 11 months later. Her first assignment was the 2025-26 Nations League group; in April and May 2026 she named squads for matchdays three to six of World Cup qualifying, including the fixtures against England and Iceland.

## Tactical identity

Bermúdez inherited the most sophisticated possession team in women's football and has kept its principles. Spain play [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in a 4-3-3, descended from the Barcelona [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) model that Alexia Putellas, Aitana Bonmatí and Patri Guijarro grew up in, with a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and the full-backs pushing up to provide width while the wingers come inside. Her own emphasis as a former striker has been on arriving in the box with more bodies and on finishing: she has spoken publicly about Spain needing to convert their dominance of possession into more goals in the biggest matches, a reference to the penalty shoot-out defeat at Euro 2025. She has also widened the squad, bringing in younger players from Barcelona, Real Madrid and Atlético.

## Storyline

Spain are the 2023 world champions, won the inaugural Women's Nations League in 2024 and reached the Euro 2025 final, where they lost to [Sarina Wiegman](/managers/sarina-wiegman)'s England on penalties. Bermúdez's job is to keep the greatest generation of Spanish players, [Alexia Putellas](/players/alexia-putellas), who won her third Ballon d'Or in October 2026, Bonmatí, Guijarro, Mariona Caldentey and Vicky López, winner of the 2026 women's Kopa Trophy, on course for the 2027 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) in Brazil and the 2028 [Olympics](/tournaments/olympics-women).

The qualification campaign in 2026 placed Spain in a group with England, a rematch of two consecutive finals, and Bermúdez said before the May fixtures that the objective was to finish top. [Barcelona Women](/clubs/barcelona-women), who won their fourth [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) in Oslo in 2026, supply most of her starting XI. Her contract runs to 2027; her future beyond it depends on Brazil. Results after May 2026 should be checked against the live data on this site.
