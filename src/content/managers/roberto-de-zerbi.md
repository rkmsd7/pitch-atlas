## Biography

Roberto De Zerbi was born in Brescia on 6 June 1979 and grew up in the Milan academy, which explains the stubborn idealism that runs through his coaching. He is a man who would rather lose playing his way than win badly, and he says so, often. [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) called him one of the most influential coaches of the last twenty years after Brighton's 2022-23 season, and that praise followed him through Marseille to north London, where [Tottenham](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) appointed him in the summer of 2026.

## Playing career

De Zerbi was an attacking midfielder with real technique and not quite enough of anything else to play for the big clubs that courted him as a teenager. He left Milan without a first-team appearance and spent his career in Serie B and the lower top flight, with Napoli (in Serie C and B), Catania, Brescia, Avellino and finally a spell in Romania with CFR Cluj. He retired in 2013 at 34 and immediately became a coach.

## Coaching career

He started at Darfo Boario in Serie D and made his name at Foggia, winning promotion from Lega Pro in 2016 with a possession game that was unheard of at that level. Palermo sacked him after a few months; Benevento were relegated with him but earned admirers for how they played. Sassuolo (2018-21) is where the De Zerbi style was fully formed: eighth-place finishes, Manuel Locatelli and Domenico Berardi flourishing, and a build-up so provocative that opponents did not know whether to press or wait.

He took Shakhtar Donetsk to the top of the Ukrainian league before war forced his departure in 2022. In September 2022 [Brighton](/clubs/brighton) hired him to replace Graham Potter and he produced the club's greatest season, sixth place and a first-ever European qualification, with Alexis Mac Allister, Moisés Caicedo and Kaoru Mitoma elevated to stars. He left in May 2024, joined [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) and took them into the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league), but the relationship frayed and he departed by mutual consent in February 2026. Tottenham, needing a defined identity, turned to him for 2026-27.

## Tactical identity

De Zerbi's teams are built on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with a signature: the centre-backs and goalkeeper hold the ball on purpose, sometimes standing on it, to pull the opposition press onto them before passing through it. The structure is a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, with one full-back inverting into midfield ([inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs)) and the two pivots forming a [box midfield](/tactics/box-midfield) with the number ten and the dropping striker.

Without the ball his teams press high but intelligently, using [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) to force the goalkeeper into long balls. At Brighton he was criticised for a weak transition defence; at Marseille he adjusted with a slightly deeper rest defence. At Spurs he inherited quick wide players and has added structure in the midfield base, including the summer signing of Sandro Tonali from [Newcastle](/clubs/newcastle-united) for around £100m, a deal that tells you how seriously the club has taken the project.

## Achievements

- Brighton's sixth place in 2022-23 and first European campaign, reaching the Europa League last 16.
- Promotion with Foggia in 2016 and a Ukrainian Super Cup with Shakhtar in 2021.
- Champions League qualification with Marseille in 2025.
- Widely cited by Guardiola and others as the coach who changed how build-up play is taught.

## Storyline

Tottenham's 2026-27 season is a bet on identity. After years of churn the club has committed to a coach with a clear method and spent to support it, Tonali being the headline arrival in a window that closed on 1 September 2026. De Zerbi's early months have been about teaching, and he has been candid that the first weeks of his style are always uncomfortable while players learn to hold the ball under pressure.

The [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) around him is fiercely competitive: champions [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), a rebuilt [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) under [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) with Morgan Rogers, [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) under [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca), and [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) under [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola) with Bradley Barcola. North London derbies with Arsenal, the reigning champions, are the matches by which his first season will be judged. As of November 2026 the supporters have bought in; the board's patience, historically short, is the variable. If his Spurs side plays like his Brighton did by the spring, it will be one of the most watchable teams in Europe.
