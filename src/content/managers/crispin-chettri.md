## Biography

Crispin Chettri is a coach from Darjeeling who played for India's youth teams before building a reputation in women's club football. He led Odisha FC to the Indian Women's League title in 2023, took charge of the [India women's team](/nations/india-women) in February 2025, guided the qualifying campaign for the 2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup, stepped aside to be assistant when the AIFF brought in Costa Rica's Amelia Valverde for the tournament itself, and was reappointed head coach in April 2026 after her departure. He has since led the Blue Tigresses at the SAFF Women's Championship in Goa and the FIFA Series in Kenya in October 2026.

## Playing career

Chettri was a midfielder who represented India at under-16 and under-19 level and played club football in the Kolkata and Sikkim leagues. His playing career was short, and he moved into coaching while still in his twenties, working his way through the AFC licence structure and the AIFF's development programmes, where he coached junior national sides before entering the women's club game.

His first assignment as national coach was the Pink Ladies Cup in Dubai in February 2025, and the highlight of the first spell was qualification for the 2026 Asian Cup in Australia, achieved in the summer of 2025. The AIFF's Technical Committee recommended his return in early April 2026, and he named a 23-player squad for the SAFF Women's Championship, played in Goa from 25 May to 6 June 2026, with Sujata Kar and Nivetha Ramadoss as assistants.

## Tactical identity

Chettri's Odisha side was known for a brisk 4-3-3 that pressed in the opposition half and attacked at pace through Pyari Xaxa and Lynda Kom, but with the national team, facing stronger Asian opponents, the approach is more conservative: a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block), quick transitions and a reliance on [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into the forwards, which makes India a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) team at this level. He builds around the experience of Sangita Basfore and Anju Tamang in midfield, Manisha Kalyan, who plays in Cyprus, as the main attacking threat, and a young defence drawn from the Indian Women's League. He has pushed the federation to schedule more friendlies against higher-ranked sides.

## Storyline

Indian women's football has had a turbulent few years: the 2022 Asian Cup on home soil ended with the squad withdrawn after a Covid outbreak, coaches have come and gone, and the Indian Women's League remains semi-professional. Chettri's reappointment in April 2026 was a vote for continuity. The SAFF Championship in Goa, where India are historically the dominant force alongside Nepal and Bangladesh, was the first test; the result is not verified on this page.

What comes next is the FIFA Series window and the longer project towards the 2029 Asian Cup, the 2028 Olympic qualifiers and, eventually, a first Women's World Cup. The AIFF has spoken of a full-time professional women's league and a residential national programme as the preconditions; Chettri's job is to keep the team competitive while those foundations are built.
