## Biography

David William Moyes was born in Glasgow on 25 April 1963 and grew up in Bearsden, where his father ran a boys' club. He took his coaching badges at 22 while still playing, and he has now managed more than 1,000 professional games. Moyes is famously demanding and once a byword for a certain kind of grim Scottish authority; his later career, with a European trophy and a second [Everton](/clubs/everton) spell, has softened the picture into something closer to elder statesman.

## Playing career

A centre-half, Moyes began at [Celtic](/clubs/celtic), winning the Scottish title in 1982 under Billy McNeill, and then had a long, unglamorous career in England and Scotland with Cambridge United, Bristol City, Shrewsbury, Dunfermline and Hamilton before finishing at Preston North End, where he was player-coach. He made well over 500 league appearances.

## Coaching career

Preston appointed him in January 1998, and he took them to the Second Division title in 2000 and the play-off final the following year. Everton came calling in March 2002. Over 11 years he turned a club that had been flirting with relegation into a perennial top-eight side on a modest budget, finishing fourth in 2004-05, reaching the 2009 FA Cup final and developing Wayne Rooney, [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta), Tim Cahill and Leighton Baines. He won the LMA Manager of the Year three times.

Sir [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) chose him as his successor at [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in 2013; the 10 months that followed were a public failure and have shadowed him since. Spells at [Real Sociedad](/clubs/real-sociedad) and Sunderland, where he was relegated, were unhappy. West Ham, twice, revived him. He kept them up in 2018, returned in 2019 and took them to sixth, seventh, a Europa League semi-final and then the 2023 Europa Conference League, the club's first major trophy for 43 years, beating Fiorentina 2-1 in Prague. He left in 2024 and came back to Everton in January 2025 with the club 16th, steering them clear of trouble and then overseeing the move from Goodison Park to the new stadium on the Bramley-Moore Dock in 2025-26.

## Tactical identity

Moyes is a pragmatist whose teams are hard to beat first and expressive second. His default is a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 4-5-1 or 4-4-1-1 [low block](/tactics/low-block) without the ball, compact between the lines, with the wingers tracking full-backs diligently. From there his sides attack with pace in transition: at West Ham Jarrod Bowen and Michail Antonio made the [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) a weapon, and at Everton Iliman Ndiaye and Jack Grealish, signed on loan in 2025, have played a similar role.

[Set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) have always been central to his teams, from the Cahill era onwards, and he is comfortable with [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) when the game calls for it. The criticism, that he is cautious, has some truth, but his first Everton tenure was adventurous by the standards of the time, and his record of over-performing budgets is matched by few in English football.

## Achievements

- UEFA Europa Conference League 2023 with West Ham
- Everton's fourth-place finish in 2004-05 and FA Cup final in 2009
- Second Division title with Preston in 2000
- Three LMA Manager of the Year awards (2003, 2005, 2009)
- More than 1,000 games as a manager

## Storyline

Moyes' return to Everton in 2025 was partly sentimental and entirely practical: the club under new owners The Friedkin Group needed stability after years of points deductions, financial crisis and relegation scraps, and no one knew the place better. He delivered safety with room to spare and then managed the emotional farewell to Goodison Park in May 2025.

The 2025-26 season was the first at the Hill Dickinson Stadium, and Everton settled into mid-table with a more organised, less chaotic identity. The Grealish loan gave the attack creativity it had lacked for years, and Moyes has spoken of wanting to leave a squad in better shape than he found it, with recruitment under Angus Kinnear's executive team now focused on younger profiles.

As of November 2026 he is in his second full season back and, at 63, one of the oldest managers in the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league). The Merseyside derby has a new flavour with [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola) across Stanley Park, and the arrival of three promoted sides means Everton's realistic ambitions are a comfortable top-half finish and a cup run. Moyes has said he will not manage forever. For now he is doing what he always did best: making a club with limited means feel competitive, and doing it at the place where his reputation was first made.
