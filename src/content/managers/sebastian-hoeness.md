## Biography

Sebastian Hoeneß was born in Munich on 12 May 1982 into German football's most famous family: his father Dieter won the 1980 European Championship and his uncle Uli built the modern [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich). He has spent his career trying to be judged on his own work, and at [VfB Stuttgart](/clubs/vfb-stuttgart) he has succeeded. He is thoughtful, media-friendly and modern in his tactical vocabulary, a coach who talks about rest-defence and third-man runs rather than fighting spirit.

## Playing career

Hoeneß was a midfielder who came through at Bayern's academy and Hertha Berlin's reserves, then played for Hoffenheim in the Regionalliga from 2007 to 2010, a period that coincided with the club's rise to the Bundesliga under Ralf Rangnick. He never played in the top flight and retired in 2010 at 28 to begin coaching.

## Coaching career

He coached Hertha's academy and then joined [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig)'s youth set-up in 2017, where he absorbed the Red Bull pressing school. Bayern appointed him to coach their reserve side in 2019 and he won the 3. Liga title in 2019-20, the first reserve team to do so, developing players such as Joshua Zirkzee and Jamal Musiala's generation.

Hoffenheim hired him in 2020 and he delivered mid-table finishes, with a memorable 4-1 win over Bayern in his first months, before leaving in 2022. [VfB Stuttgart](/clubs/vfb-stuttgart) came calling in April 2023 with the club 18th and heading for relegation under Bruno Labbadia. Hoeneß kept them up through the relegation play-off against Hamburg and then produced one of the Bundesliga's great seasons: second place in 2023-24, behind only [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso)'s unbeaten Leverkusen, with Serhou Guirassy scoring 28 league goals and Deniz Undav 18. He was named Bundesliga Coach of the Year.

The 2024-25 season brought [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history) football and a DFB-Pokal triumph, Stuttgart beating Arminia Bielefeld 4-2 in the Berlin final on 24 May 2025 for the club's first trophy since 2007. He signed a contract extension announced in March 2025, and he led Stuttgart through 2025-26 behind [Vincent Kompany](/managers/vincent-kompany)'s champions Bayern.

## Tactical identity

Hoeneß plays a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, with one full-back inverting into midfield and the other pushing high, a structure borrowed from the [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) of Pep Guardiola's City. The build-up is brave, with the goalkeeper and centre-backs expected to play through the first line, and the attacking midfielders occupy the half-spaces to receive between lines. It is [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with a German pressing edge: Stuttgart hold a [high line](/tactics/high-line), counter-press aggressively in the [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) tradition and are happy to play a high-risk, high-reward game.

His great gift is the improvement of strikers. Guirassy, Undav and later signings have all scored at rates far above their previous careers, a product of the volume of crosses and cut-backs Stuttgart create. The weakness is the same as any high-line side: exposure to pace in behind, which Stuttgart have mitigated by recruiting quicker centre-backs.

The evolution has been from the survival football of spring 2023 to one of the most consistently attractive sides in Germany, and then to a more balanced version that can manage European midweeks.

## Achievements

- DFB-Pokal winner 2024-25, Stuttgart's first trophy in 18 years.
- Second place in the Bundesliga in 2023-24 and Champions League qualification.
- Bundesliga Coach of the Year 2024.
- 3. Liga champion with Bayern Munich II in 2019-20.

## Storyline

Stuttgart under Hoeneß are the Bundesliga's model of a well-run upper-mid club: sell high, recruit smart, play well. Their challenge in 2026-27 is to stay in the European places behind [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), where [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) won the 2026 Ballon d'Or on 26 October after a 73-goal season, and alongside [Niko Kovač](/managers/niko-kovac)'s [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund), [Kasper Hjulmand](/managers/kasper-hjulmand)'s [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) and [Ole Werner](/managers/ole-werner)'s [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig).

Hoeneß has been linked with bigger jobs every summer, and the appointment of [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) as [Germany](/nations/germany) coach on 24 July 2026, after the national team's early World Cup exit, has kept the question of the next generation of German coaches in the air. His position at Stuttgart as of November 2026 could not be independently verified beyond the most recent available reports, which still listed him in charge; no change was reported in the sources consulted.

The storyline is of a coach who turned a crisis into a project and now has to prove the project can last beyond a single golden generation of players.
