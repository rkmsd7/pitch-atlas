## Biography

Vincenzo Maresca was born in Pontecagnano Faiano, near Salerno, on 10 February 1980 and left Italy at 18 to join West Bromwich Albion, an unusual path for an Italian teenager that made him comfortable in England long before he coached there. He is intense, principled to the point of stubbornness about his football, and has described himself as a Guardiola disciple without embarrassment. He is the first Italian to manage [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) since Roberto Mancini.

## Playing career

Maresca was a cultured midfielder who played for West Brom, [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), Bologna, Piacenza, Fiorentina and most memorably [Sevilla](/clubs/sevilla), where he scored twice in the 2006 UEFA Cup final against Middlesbrough and won the trophy again in 2007 alongside the Super Cup. Later years took in Olympiacos, Málaga, Sampdoria, Palermo and Hellas Verona. He played for [Italy](/nations/italy)'s youth teams but never won a senior cap, and retired in 2017.

## Coaching career

He learned under Manuel Pellegrini at West Ham and ran Manchester City's elite development squad in 2020-21, winning the Premier League 2 title with a side built on Guardiola's principles. A short, unhappy stint as Parma head coach in 2021 ended after 14 games. He returned to City as [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola)'s assistant for the 2022-23 treble, then took Leicester City to the Championship title with 97 points in 2023-24.

[Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) appointed him in June 2024. His first season brought fourth place, the Europa Conference League and, in July 2025, the FIFA [Club World Cup](/tournaments/club-world-cup), beating [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) 3-0 in the final with [Cole Palmer](/players/cole-palmer) scoring twice. His second season in London was less settled and he left in 2025-26, with Liam Rosenior stepping in before [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) arrived. When Guardiola announced in May 2026 that he would leave City after 10 years and 20 trophies, the club chose the man who knew its systems best. Maresca was appointed for the 2026-27 season and backed with a summer outlay of around £458m.

## Tactical identity

Maresca's football is pure [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), with the City template visible in every phase. The shape is a 4-3-3 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession: one full-back inverts into midfield next to the pivot as an [inverted full-back](/tactics/inverted-full-backs), creating a [box midfield](/tactics/box-midfield), the other full-back stays wide, and the wingers hold the touchline. The goalkeeper is a genuine eleventh outfield player, a feature he made Robert Sánchez perform at Chelsea.

Build-up is slow and deliberate, designed to pull the opposition out of shape before a sudden acceleration. The striker is often asked to play as a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine) in the first phase and a penalty-box finisher in the last, though with [Erling Haaland](/players/erling-haaland) at City the emphasis has shifted to feeding the striker earlier. Out of possession Maresca's teams press in a 4-4-2 and counter-press immediately, and the criticism he faced at Chelsea, that the control came at the expense of excitement, has followed him to Manchester. His response has always been the same: the control is the point.

## Storyline

Following Guardiola is the hardest job in football, and Maresca took it with his eyes open. City finished the 2025-26 season without the title, which went to [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), and the club's response was a summer rebuild of unprecedented scale. The £458m spend brought in a new generation around Haaland, Rodri, the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) Golden Ball winner, and Phil Foden, and the message from the hierarchy was that the dynasty would not be allowed to fade quietly.

Early reports in 2026-27 describe a City side that looks recognisably Guardiola's but with a harder, more direct final third, and a dressing room still adjusting to a younger, less celebrated voice. Maresca's relationship with Rodri, whose fitness and form define City's ceiling, and with Haaland, whose goals are the simplest route back to the title, are the two relationships that matter most.

The context is unforgiving. Arsenal are champions and Champions League finalists, [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola) has reshaped [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool), and Xabi Alonso has replaced him at Chelsea. Maresca will be measured against a man who won six league titles in 10 years. He knows it, the supporters know it, and the Etihad will decide quickly whether the apprentice can run the master's house.
