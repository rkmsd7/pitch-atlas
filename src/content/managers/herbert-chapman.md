## Biography

Herbert Chapman was born in Kiveton Park, Yorkshire, on 19 January 1878, the son of a miner. He was the first football manager in the modern sense: a man who picked the team, bought the players, designed the tactics and shaped the club, at a time when directors did all of those things. He won two titles with Huddersfield Town, a provincial club, then transformed [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), who had never won anything, into the dominant side of the 1930s, winning the FA Cup and two championships before dying of pneumonia on 6 January 1934, with Arsenal top of the table. They won that title and the next.

## Playing career

Chapman was an inside-forward of modest ability who played for a dozen clubs between 1897 and 1909, including Northampton, Sheffield United, Notts County and Tottenham Hotspur, often as an amateur while working as a mining engineer. His yellow boots were more memorable than his goals. He became Northampton's player-manager in 1907.

The Leeds City scandal earned him a life ban, lifted in 1921 after he argued he had been working at a munitions factory at the time. At Huddersfield, with Clem Stephenson and George Brown, he won the Cup and two of the three titles that made them the first club to win three in a row. Arsenal's chairman Sir Henry Norris hired him with a newspaper advertisement that warned candidates against expecting to spend money; Chapman spent it anyway, on Charlie Buchan, Alex James, David Jack and Cliff Bastin, and the 1930 Cup final against Huddersfield was followed by the 1931 title with a record 66 points and 127 goals.

## Tactical identity

Chapman's great invention was the WM. The 1925 change to the offside law, which reduced the number of defenders needed to play a man onside from three to two, flooded the game with goals; Chapman, prompted by Buchan, withdrew the centre-half to become a third defender, a "stopper", and pulled the inside-forwards back into midfield, producing a 3-2-2-3 that defended in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and attacked with speed through the wingers and the centre-forward. It was a [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) system, "the most successful sides are those that score on the break", with [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) from Alex James's passes to Bastin and Joe Hulme, and it dominated English football for 30 years. Off the pitch he introduced numbered shirts, floodlights at Highbury before the FA banned them, white sleeves, the clock, team meetings, physiotherapy and the renaming of Gillespie Road tube station to Arsenal. He also proposed a European club competition 20 years before it existed.

## Achievements

- Four First Division titles with two clubs; Huddersfield's and Arsenal's first
- FA Cups with Huddersfield (1922) and Arsenal (1930)
- Inventor of the WM formation and the "stopper" centre-half
- Pioneer of numbered shirts, floodlights, physiotherapy and the manager's authority over selection and transfers
- A bust at the Emirates Stadium and a statue outside it

## Storyline

Died 1934. Chapman's Arsenal won five titles in the 1930s, and the club's identity, the white sleeves, the marble halls of Highbury and the belief that Arsenal should be the game's innovators, is his. Every manager who picks his own team and signs his own players is his descendant, and the WM was the shape of English football until the Hungarians dismantled it at Wembley in 1953.

The torch is carried by the modern Arsenal: [Arsène Wenger](/managers/arsene-wenger), who matched Chapman's innovations with his own, and [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta), whose 2025-26 title, the club's first in 22 years, was won with a defensive organisation and a set-piece armoury that the inventor of the stopper would have understood. Chapman's statue stands outside the Emirates beside Thierry Henry's and Tony Adams's, the only one of the three in a suit.
