## Biography

Béla Guttmann was born in Budapest on 27 January 1899 to a Jewish family of dance instructors. He was a Hungarian international, a survivor of the Holocaust that killed his brother, and the most restless coach of the 20th century: he had around two dozen jobs in a dozen countries and said that a coach's third season at a club was always fatal. In two of them, at [Benfica](/clubs/benfica), he won consecutive European Cups in 1961 and 1962, ending Real Madrid's monopoly and launching Eusébio. Denied a pay rise, he left with a curse that Benfica would not win a European trophy for a hundred years. They have lost eight finals since. He died in Vienna on 28 August 1981.

## Playing career

Guttmann was an elegant centre-half for MTK Budapest, winning two Hungarian titles, and then for Hakoah Vienna, the all-Jewish club, with whom he won the Austrian title in 1925. He played in the United States for Hakoah's touring teams and New York clubs during the Depression, won four caps for Hungary and played at the 1924 Olympics. He retired in 1933.

In Brazil he absorbed the 4-2-4 that Brazil used to win the 1958 World Cup and brought it back to Europe. At Benfica, with Mário Coluna, José Águas and the teenage Eusébio, whom he signed from under Sporting's nose in 1961, he beat Barcelona 3-2 in Bern in 1961 and Real Madrid 5-3 in Amsterdam in 1962, Eusébio scoring twice. He asked for a raise, was refused, and left. He coached Peñarol, Austria, Benfica again, briefly, and ended with Porto in 1973.

## Tactical identity

Guttmann was an apostle of attack. His 4-2-4 used two wingers, two central strikers and a deep midfield pair, defended with a flat back four, and relied on quick, [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into the forwards and a fast [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) that caught the man-marking defences of the era unprepared. He pushed his defensive line up, an early [high line](/tactics/high-line), to keep the team compact, and was obsessive about fitness and diet, decades before it was normal. He was also a showman and a mercenary, by his own description, who believed that a coach should take the money and move on before the club tired of him. The curse was his sense of humour and his sense of justice combined.

## Achievements

- Two European Cups with Benfica (1961, 1962), ending Real Madrid's run of five
- Three Portuguese titles with Porto and Benfica
- Hungarian title and Mitropa Cup with Újpest in 1939
- Paulista title with São Paulo
- Brought the 4-2-4 to Europe and discovered Eusébio

## Storyline

Died 1981. Benfica have reached eight European finals since 1962, the last in the Europa League in 2014, and lost them all; before the 1990 European Cup final in Vienna, Eusébio prayed at Guttmann's grave. Whether or not one believes in curses, Guttmann's story, from Hakoah and the Holocaust to the Amsterdam final, is one of the great football lives, and David Bolchover's biography made him a figure of the game's conscience.

The torch is carried by every coach who believes in attacking football and by every nomad who takes the next job before the third season: [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho), a Portuguese who learned the Guttmann legend at Benfica and Porto, is the modern wanderer who most resembles him, down to the belief that the manager, not the club, is the star. Benfica's statue of Eusébio stands outside the Estádio da Luz; the curse stands inside it.
