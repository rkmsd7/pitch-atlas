## Biography

Francesco Farioli, born in Barberino di Mugello near Florence on 10 April 1989, is the rare top-level coach who never played professionally and who holds a degree in philosophy. He rose from goalkeeping coach to head coach in Turkey and France, reached [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) at 35 and arrived at [Porto](/clubs/porto) in July 2025 as the youngest coach to take charge of the club in the modern era. Porto have since extended his contract to 2028.

## Playing career

Farioli was an amateur goalkeeper in the Tuscan lower leagues who gave up any ambitions of a playing career in his early twenties. He studied philosophy at the University of Florence, wrote a thesis on goalkeeping, and moved into coaching as a specialist keeper coach, first at Lucchese and Fortis Juventus, then in Qatar at Al-Duhail's academy and the Aspire programme.

## Coaching career

The decisive relationship of his career was with [Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi). Farioli joined De Zerbi's staff at Benevento in 2017 and followed him to Sassuolo, where he was not only the goalkeeping coach but a methodological sounding board. In 2021 he struck out on his own in Turkey with Fatih Karagümrük and then Alanyaspor, where his bright, ball-dominant team drew attention from across Europe.

Nice appointed him in 2023 and his first season was a statistical marvel: the best defence in [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1), a long unbeaten run through the autumn and a fifth-place finish, even if the attacking output stalled in the spring. Ajax then hired him in May 2024 to steady a club in freefall. He took them from fifth to second in a season, led the title race for much of the spring, and resigned after a late collapse handed the title to PSV, citing differences over the club's direction.

Porto, having sacked Martín Anselmi six months into his contract after a poor 2024-25, turned to Farioli in July 2025. The first season brought a strong league challenge and a renewed European presence, and the board rewarded him with a new deal running to the summer of 2028.

## Tactical identity

Farioli is a De Zerbi disciple with a defensive coach's caution. His teams build patiently from the goalkeeper, with the centre-backs splitting and a pivot dropping to form a provocative first line in the spirit of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play); the full-backs are often [inverted](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) so that the wingers can hold maximum width. What separates him from De Zerbi is the willingness to sit in a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block) when the game demands it, which was the foundation of that remarkable Nice defence.

Out of possession he uses carefully rehearsed [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps), inviting a pass into a specific zone before closing it, rather than a relentless all-pitch press. Set-piece work is meticulous; he employed dedicated coaches at both Ajax and Porto. At Porto the shape has shifted between a 4-2-3-1 and a 4-3-3 depending on whether he uses a dedicated number ten, and the team's identity is calm circulation followed by sudden vertical runs from the wingers.

## Achievements

- Took Nice to fifth in Ligue 1 in 2023-24 with the division's best defensive record.
- Second place with Ajax in 2024-25 after inheriting a club in crisis.
- Earned a contract extension at Porto after one season, a sign of a settled, improving project.

## Storyline

Porto's rivalry with [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) and [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) has rarely been as tactically interesting. Farioli faces [Marco Silva](/managers/marco-silva), newly installed at Benfica after [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s exit for Madrid, and Sporting are in flux after opening talks to end Rui Borges' contract on 1 November 2026, with [Paulo Fonseca](/managers/paulo-fonseca) widely reported to be arriving to "catch Porto" according to Portuguese coverage, which tells you where the Dragons stand in the early-season table.

Farioli's challenge in 2026-27 is converting a good team into a championship team in the [Primeira Liga](/leagues/primeira-liga) while competing credibly in the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league). His squad has been refreshed with younger, saleable talent in the Porto tradition, and the club's financial model depends on both trophies and transfers. There is pressure, as always at the Dragão, but the extension to 2028 suggests the board sees him as the long-term answer rather than another short-term fix. As of November 2026 the early signs are encouraging, though the season is far from decided.
