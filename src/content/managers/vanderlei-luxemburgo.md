## Biography

Vanderlei Luxemburgo da Silva was born in Nova Iguaçu, Rio de Janeiro, on 10 May 1952. He is the most successful coach in the history of the [Brasileirão](/leagues/brasileirao), a five-time champion with four different clubs, the man who built the Cruzeiro treble of 2003 and the Santos "magic square" of 2004 around Robinho and Diego, and the only Brazilian to have coached [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid). His last job was a short spell at [Corinthians](/clubs/corinthians) in 2023; as of the 2025-26 season he was out of work and has spoken of retirement, so this page treats him as retired.

## Playing career

Luxemburgo was a left-back for Flamengo, Botafogo, Internacional, Fluminense and a dozen smaller clubs between 1970 and 1983, winning a Carioca title with Flamengo in 1974. He was more notable for his nickname, after a Luxembourg-born teammate's confusion, than for his play, and retired at 31 to coach.

The 1993-94 Palmeiras of Edmundo, Evair and Rivaldo ended a 17-year title drought with back-to-back championships. Corinthians won in 1998, and [Brazil](/nations/brazil) under him won the 1999 Copa América with Rivaldo and Ronaldo before the 2000 Olympic failure and a parliamentary inquiry into his finances cost him the job. Cruzeiro in 2003, with Alex and Deivid, won the first treble of the modern Brasileirão. Santos won again in 2004, after which Florentino Pérez hired him for Madrid; he lasted eleven months with the galácticos.

## Tactical identity

Luxemburgo was a modernising influence in Brazilian coaching, a disciplinarian who introduced video analysis, fitness science and a more organised defensive structure in the 1990s. His preferred base was a 3-5-2 with wing-backs, a [back three](/tactics/back-three) that his 1990s Palmeiras used to great effect, and his teams played a possession game built on short combinations, an instinctive version of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play). At Santos in 2004 he built the "quadrado mágico", the magic square, of Robinho, Diego, Elano and Ricardinho behind Deivid, a four-man creative unit that became the most admired attacking idea in Brazilian club football; at Madrid he tried the same with Zidane, Figo, Beckham and Ronaldo and was ridiculed. His sides could also [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) with real speed, and his man-management was famously blunt.

## Achievements

- Five Brasileirão titles with four clubs, a record
- Copa América 1999 with Brazil
- The 2003 treble with Cruzeiro, the first in the national league era
- Seven Paulista titles and state titles in Rio and Minas
- The 2004 Santos magic square, the model for a generation of Brazilian attacking play

## Storyline

Retired as of the 2025-26 season. Luxemburgo's record in Brazil is unmatched, and his reputation abroad is defined by the Madrid failure, a contrast he has always resented. His influence on Brazilian coaching, in its professionalism and its embrace of the three-man defence, is large; [Abel Ferreira](/managers/abel-ferreira)'s Palmeiras, the dominant Brazilian club of the 2020s, plays a back three in the tradition Luxemburgo helped establish.

The torch is carried by his former players turned coaches and by the Brazilian domestic game that he dominated for three decades; the magic square lives on every time a Brazilian side plays four creators behind one striker.
