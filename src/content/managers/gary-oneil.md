## Biography

Gary Paul O'Neil was born in Beckenham, south-east London, on 18 May 1983 and spent most of his life in football as a tidy, hard-running midfielder before becoming one of the most interesting young English coaches. He is calm, analytical and unusually candid in press conferences about tactical choices, and his career so far has been defined by taking jobs in difficult circumstances and stabilising them.

## Playing career

O'Neil came through Portsmouth's academy and made his debut at 17, becoming a mainstay in the side promoted to the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) in 2003 under Harry Redknapp. He moved to Middlesbrough in 2007 and played in the UEFA Cup, then spent three seasons at West Ham, before later spells with Queens Park Rangers, Norwich, Bristol City and Bolton. He played England U21 football and retired in 2019, immediately joining Liverpool's academy coaching staff and then [Bournemouth](/clubs/bournemouth) as a first-team coach.

## Coaching career

Bournemouth's sacking of Scott Parker after a 9-0 defeat at [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) in August 2022 thrust O'Neil into the caretaker role. He kept the club up comfortably, was given the job permanently and was then surprisingly dismissed in June 2023 to make way for [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola). Wolves hired him weeks before the 2023-24 season with the club in financial trouble and having sold key players; he took them to 14th and an FA Cup quarter-final playing bright, counter-attacking football. The following season began badly and he was sacked in December 2024.

[Ipswich Town](/clubs/ipswich-town) had been relegated in 2025 after one season back in the top flight, and when Kieran McKenna, the architect of back-to-back promotions, stepped down, the club chose O'Neil to lead the rebuild in 2025-26. Ipswich went up at the first attempt as one of the Championship's best sides, and he is now back in the Premier League for a third club.

## Tactical identity

O'Neil is a flexible coach whose teams are organised first. At Bournemouth he used a 4-4-2 and 5-4-1 [low block](/tactics/low-block) to survive; at Wolves he moved to a 3-4-2-1 [back three](/tactics/back-three) that gave Pedro Neto, Matheus Cunha and Hwang Hee-chan freedom to run at space on the [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking). Wolves' transitions in 2023-24 were among the most effective in the league, and O'Neil's in-game changes, often switching between a back four and five, were a feature.

At Ipswich he inherited a squad built for McKenna's possession-based 4-2-3-1 and blended it with his own emphasis on transitions and [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces). The promotion side pressed more selectively than McKenna's, kept more clean sheets, and relied on Liam Delap's replacement profiles and wide pace to score. He is clear-eyed about the limits of a promoted squad and prefers solidity to spectacle.

## Achievements

- Championship promotion with Ipswich Town in 2025-26
- Premier League survival with Bournemouth in 2022-23 from a caretaker start
- 14th place and FA Cup quarter-final with a financially stricken Wolves in 2023-24
- Promotion as a player with Portsmouth in 2003

## Storyline

Ipswich's third promotion in four seasons returned the club to the top flight with a different feel from the McKenna era. The 2024-25 relegation was painful, and the decision to appoint a coach known for survival rather than style was a deliberate statement: this time the club wanted to stay. The squad was reshaped with experienced Premier League players, and the training ground and stadium investment under the American ownership group continued.

The early months of 2026-27 placed Ipswich in the familiar cluster of promoted sides alongside [Coventry City](/clubs/coventry-city) and [Hull City](/clubs/hull-city), and the results against the division's middle class will decide the season. O'Neil's history with Bournemouth, where [Marco Rose](/managers/marco-rose) is now in charge, and with Wolves, relegated in 2026, gives his fixtures a personal edge.

Within the game O'Neil is widely regarded as the English coach most likely to make the step up to a top-six job, and the Ipswich appointment was in part a bet that he could deliver what McKenna could not: a second season. If he does, Portman Road will have a manager of a kind the club has not had since the days of Bobby Robson and George Burley, one whose best work is still ahead of him.
