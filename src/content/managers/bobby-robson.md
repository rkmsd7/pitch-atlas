## Biography

Robert William Robson was born in Sacriston, County Durham, on 18 February 1933, the son of a miner who missed one shift in 51 years. He was the most loved manager in English football: builder of a great Ipswich Town, coach of [England](/nations/england) through the Hand of God and the tears of Italia 90, title-winner in the Netherlands and Portugal, coach of Ronaldo's Barcelona, and finally, at 66, the manager who restored [Newcastle United](/clubs/newcastle-united) to the Champions League. He was knighted in 2002, fought cancer five times and died in Durham on 31 July 2009. His foundation has raised millions for cancer research.

## Playing career

Robson was an inside-forward and later a wing-half for Fulham and West Bromwich Albion, playing more than 500 league games between 1950 and 1967. He won 20 caps for England and played at the 1958 and 1962 World Cups. He finished as player-manager in Vancouver.

Ipswich, under Robson for 13 years with Kevin Beattie, John Wark, Arnold Mühren and Frans Thijssen, won the 1978 FA Cup against Arsenal and the 1981 UEFA Cup against AZ Alkmaar, and were title contenders for a decade on a small budget. England lost to Maradona's Argentina in 1986 and to West Germany on penalties in Turin in 1990, with Paul Gascoigne's tears and Gary Lineker's "have a word with him". He won two titles at [PSV](/clubs/psv-eindhoven) and two at [Porto](/clubs/porto), where his translator, José Mourinho, followed him to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), where Ronaldo scored 47 goals and Robson won three trophies before being moved upstairs for Louis van Gaal. Newcastle, bottom when he arrived, finished third in 2003.

## Tactical identity

Robson was an attacking coach of the English tradition who learned from the Dutch. Ipswich played a 4-4-2 with the Dutch midfielders Mühren and Thijssen passing through the middle, a sophisticated side by English standards, and [counter-attacked](/tactics/counter-attacking) through wingers and overlapping full-backs; with England he moved to a sweeper system, a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with Mark Wright at Italia 90, at the players' suggestion, and it took them to the semi-final. He was comfortable with [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) to a target man, Paul Mariner at Ipswich, Alan Shearer at Newcastle, and his teams were always brave. His gift was for people: Mourinho has said he learned from Robson "how to love the players", and Gascoigne, Ronaldo and Shearer all spoke of him as a father.

## Achievements

- UEFA Cup 1981 and FA Cup 1978 with Ipswich
- World Cup semi-final 1990 with England, their best result since 1966 until 2018
- League titles in the Netherlands (two) and Portugal (two)
- Cup Winners' Cup and Copa del Rey with Barcelona
- Knighted 2002; the Sir Bobby Robson Foundation; statue outside St James' Park and Portman Road

## Storyline

Died 2009. Robson's legacy runs through [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho), who began as his interpreter at Sporting and Porto and became his assistant at Barcelona, and who has always described him as his mentor, and through the whole tradition of English coaching abroad that he pioneered. Newcastle's Champions League seasons under [Eddie Howe](/managers/eddie-howe) in 2023 and 2025 were the first since Robson's.

The torch is carried by decency as much as tactics: by [Gareth Southgate](/managers/gareth-southgate), who modelled his England on Robson's humanity, by Howe, and by Mourinho, back at Real Madrid since July 2026 and still, in his gentler moments, quoting Sir Bobby. The statues at Portman Road and St James' Park, and the memorial garden at Newcastle, are visited every match day.
