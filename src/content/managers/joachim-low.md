## Biography

Joachim Löw was born in Schönau im Schwarzwald on 3 February 1960, the son of a stove-fitter. He coached [Germany](/nations/germany) for 15 years, longer than anyone but Sepp Herberger, and his teams reached the semi-finals of six consecutive tournaments between 2006 and 2016, winning the 2014 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in Brazil after the 7-1 in Belo Horizonte. The 2018 group-stage exit and the Euro 2020 defeat to England ended his reign in 2021. He has not coached since, and has turned down several offers, saying he is content.

## Playing career

Löw was a quick, technical forward for Freiburg, where he was a crowd favourite, Stuttgart, Eintracht Frankfurt, Karlsruhe and Schaffhausen and Winterthur in Switzerland. He scored more than 80 goals in the 2. Bundesliga and won no caps, despite a call-up to a provisional squad in the early 1980s. He retired in 1995.

His Stuttgart "magic triangle" of Krasimir Balakov, Fredi Bobic and Giovane Élber won the cup in 1997. Jürgen Klinsmann hired him as assistant for the 2006 home World Cup and left him the job. Löw's Germany beat Portugal and Turkey to reach the Euro 2008 final, demolished England 4-1 and Argentina 4-0 in 2010, and in 2014, with Manuel Neuer, Philipp Lahm, Toni Kroos, Thomas Müller and the 36-year-old Miroslav Klose, won the World Cup through Mario Götze's extra-time volley against Argentina in the Maracanã.

## Tactical identity

Löw changed Germany's identity from the counter-attacking, physical side of 2006 to a possession team modelled on Spain. The 4-2-3-1 built on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), with Kroos and Schweinsteiger as a double pivot, Mesut Özil as the ten, inverted wingers and Lahm as an inverting full-back or midfielder, was the mature expression; at Euro 2012 and in 2014 he often used Götze or Müller as a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine) rather than a conventional striker, and the [high line](/tactics/high-line) behind Neuer's sweeping was a hallmark. The 2014 side combined this with the transitional speed of Müller and André Schürrle. After 2016 the system calcified: a back three experiment at Euro 2020 and the stubbornness over Müller and Mats Hummels, dropped and then recalled, marked the decline.

## Achievements

- World Cup 2014, Germany's fourth
- Six consecutive tournament semi-finals (2006-2016), a record
- Confederations Cup 2017 with a reserve squad
- 198 matches as Germany coach, a national record
- FIFA World Coach of the Year 2014

## Storyline

Retired. Löw's reign ended with the sense that Germany had drifted, and the three coaches since, Hansi Flick, [Julian Nagelsmann](/managers/julian-nagelsmann) and, from July 2026, [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), have all been hired to recover the intensity that the 2014 team combined with its possession. Germany's round-of-32 exit at the 2026 World Cup was their third consecutive early exit, a run that began on his watch in 2018.

The torch is carried by Flick, his former assistant, who won a treble at Bayern and now coaches Barcelona; by Kroos, Lahm and Müller as the voices of the 2014 generation; and by the Spanish-influenced positional school that Löw brought into the DFB's coaching curriculum. He lives in Freiburg, appears occasionally on German television and has been linked, without result, to several national teams.
