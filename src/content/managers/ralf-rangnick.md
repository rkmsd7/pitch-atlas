## Biography

Ralf Rangnick was born in Backnang, near Stuttgart, on 29 June 1958. He is the intellectual godfather of German pressing football, the man who went on national television in 1998 with a tactics board to explain the back four and zonal marking, and who later built the Red Bull coaching empire that produced [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s inspiration, [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel)'s mentorship and [Julian Nagelsmann](/managers/julian-nagelsmann)'s first jobs. Since 2022 he has coached [Austria](/nations/austria), and in June 2026, days before the World Cup, the Austrian federation extended his contract to [Euro 2028](/tournaments/euro).

## Playing career

Rangnick was a modest midfielder in the German amateur and lower leagues with Ulm, Southwick in England while studying at Sussex University, and Viktoria Backnang, where in 1983 a friendly against Valeriy Lobanovskyi's Dynamo Kyiv changed his understanding of the game. He became a player-coach at 25.

At Hoffenheim he took a village club from the third division to the top of the Bundesliga table in three years. At Leipzig he built the entire club, from academy to first team, and his network spawned Red Bull Salzburg, which produced Erling Haaland, Sadio Mané and a generation of coaches. His interim spell at [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in 2021-22 was unhappy, but his first two years with Austria, who won a Euro 2024 group containing France and the Netherlands, were a triumph.

## Tactical identity

Rangnick is the theorist of [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing). His principles, written into the Red Bull manual, are to win the ball back within eight seconds, to shoot within ten seconds of winning it, to press in pre-planned [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) and to play vertically rather than sideways. He prefers a 4-2-2-2 with narrow attacking midfielders, a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and immediate [counter-attacks](/tactics/counter-attacking). Austria under him run more than almost any team in Europe, press the first pass and play with a front two of Marko Arnautović or Michael Gregoritsch and Marcel Sabitzer behind. He was decades ahead of German football on video analysis, sports science and the idea of a club-wide playing philosophy.

## Achievements

- DFB-Pokal 2010-11 with Schalke, plus a Champions League semi-final
- Three Bundesliga promotions (Hannover 2002, Hoffenheim 2008, Leipzig 2016)
- Built RB Leipzig from the fourth tier to the Champions League as sporting director
- Euro 2024: Austria topped a group with France and the Netherlands
- Qualified Austria for the 2026 World Cup, their first since 1998

## Storyline

Austria's 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) ended at the hands of eventual champions [Spain](/nations/spain) in the knockout rounds, a creditable finish for a nation that had not been to the tournament for 28 years. Rangnick was linked with a return to the Bundesliga and with [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) before the tournament; instead he signed a two-year extension on 13 June 2026 and said he wanted to finish the job with the group he had built.

What comes next is the 2026-27 [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league) and qualification for Euro 2028, with a squad built around Sabitzer, Christoph Baumgartner, Konrad Laimer, David Alaba in his final cycle and goalkeeper Patrick Pentz. At 68, Rangnick has said this will be his last coaching job. His legacy is already secure: he is the reason a generation of coaches, from Klopp to Marco Rose to Jesse Marsch, talk about football the way they do.
