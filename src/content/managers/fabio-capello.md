## Biography

Fabio Capello was born in Pieris, near Gorizia, on 18 June 1946. Severe, precise and relentlessly successful, he won the league in nine of his first 13 seasons as a head coach, at [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan), [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) and [Roma](/clubs/roma), plus two at Juventus that were later struck off. He coached [England](/nations/england) and Russia at World Cups, finished in China in 2018 and now works as a television analyst with the air of a headmaster who has read your report.

## Playing career

Capello was a cultured midfielder for SPAL, Roma, [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), where he won three Serie A titles, and Milan, where he won the 1979 title. He scored the goal that gave Italy their first win at Wembley in 1973 and won 32 caps, playing at the 1974 World Cup. He retired in 1980 and worked in Milan's youth system and as a television pundit before Silvio Berlusconi appointed him to replace Arrigo Sacchi in 1991.

His first Milan side went unbeaten through 1991-92, set a record of 58 league games without defeat, and won three titles in a row; the 4-0 against Barcelona in Athens, without Marco van Basten, Franco Baresi or Alessandro Costacurta, is still the most complete Champions League final performance. He won La Liga at Madrid with Roberto Carlos, Clarence Seedorf and Davor Šuker, gave Roma their third title with Francesco Totti and Gabriel Batistuta, and won two more at Juventus before Calciopoli. His second Madrid title, in 2007, was followed by a sacking for playing boring football. England won nine of ten qualifiers under him and then failed in South Africa.

## Tactical identity

Capello is the master of the organised 4-4-2: a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block), the back four and midfield four moving as two tight lines, pressure applied only when the ball enters certain zones, and then a fast, direct [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking). His Milan were more cautious than Sacchi's but harder to beat; the 1993-94 side scored 36 goals in 34 games and conceded 15. He placed enormous weight on [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), on individual discipline, diet, timekeeping and the elimination of mobile phones, and on the idea that the manager decides everything. Players found him cold; chairmen found him worth it.

## Achievements

- Champions League 1994 and three finals (1993, 1994, 1995)
- Five Serie A titles (four with Milan, one with Roma) plus two revoked with Juventus
- Two La Liga titles with Real Madrid in two separate single seasons
- Unbeaten Serie A season 1991-92
- Serie A Coach of the Year 1993, 1994

## Storyline

Retired. Capello represents the Italian idea that a coach's first duty is to make his team hard to beat, and his trophies are the argument that it works. His influence on English football was mixed: he brought discipline to a national team that lacked it and could not solve the 2010 World Cup, after which John Terry's captaincy saga ended his reign. In Spain he was twice a champion and twice unloved.

The torch is carried by the pragmatists who win without apology: [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho), back at Real Madrid since July 2026 and a disciple of the same defensive clarity, Diego Simeone, and [Antonio Conte](/managers/antonio-conte), whose Milan-born obsession with organisation echoes Capello's. Capello himself appears regularly on Sky Italia, still unimpressed by most of what he sees.
