## Biography

Brian Howard Clough was born in Middlesbrough on 21 March 1935, the sixth of nine children. He was the most charismatic manager in English football, the most quotable, the most difficult, and, with his partner Peter Taylor, one of the most brilliant. He took Derby County from the Second Division to the league title, took Nottingham Forest from the Second Division to the title and two European Cups in three years, and was never given the England job he wanted and the public wanted for him. He retired in 1993 as Forest were relegated and died in Derby on 20 September 2004.

## Playing career

Clough was a centre-forward of astonishing efficiency: 251 goals in 274 league games for Middlesbrough and Sunderland between 1955 and 1964, the fastest to 250 in English football. He won two England caps in 1959. A cruciate injury on Boxing Day 1962 effectively ended his career at 27, and he moved into coaching Sunderland's youth team.

At Derby, with Taylor finding the players, Dave Mackay, Roy McFarland, Colin Todd and Archie Gemmill, he won the 1972 title and reached the European Cup semi-final, then resigned in a row with the chairman. The 44 days at Leeds, where he told Don Revie's champions to throw their medals away, became a film. At Forest, with Taylor again, he won promotion, the title at the first attempt with Kenny Burns, John Robertson and Peter Shilton, then the European Cup against Malmö with Trevor Francis's header and retained it against Hamburg. Forest also went 42 league games unbeaten in 1977-78. He stayed 18 years, won four League Cups, and left in 1993 after relegation and years of heavy drinking.

## Tactical identity

Clough despised tactical jargon and said the game was simple: "If God had wanted us to play football in the clouds, he'd have put grass up there." His Forest kept the ball on the ground, passed it quickly through the midfield and relied on Robertson, the overweight winger he called the Picasso of the game, to create. It was [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in a 4-4-2 without the theory, with a disciplined, deep-ish defence that invited pressure and a lethal [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Tony Woodcock and Garry Birtles; in Europe Forest often had less of the ball and did not mind. Some [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) to a big striker was allowed, but hoofing was not. His real tools were psychological: fear, affection, surprise, days off before big matches, and a belief that players should be treated like adults and punished like children.

## Achievements

- Two European Cups (1979, 1980) with Nottingham Forest, a club that had never won the league before 1978
- League titles with two provincial clubs, Derby (1972) and Forest (1978)
- Four League Cups, a record at the time
- 42-match unbeaten league run 1977-78
- Named the greatest manager never to manage England in almost every poll on the subject

## Storyline

Died 2004. Clough's achievement, taking two clubs from the second tier to the title and winning Europe with one of them, has not been repeated and probably cannot be. The statues in Nottingham, Derby and Middlesbrough, the Brian Clough Trophy contested by Derby and Forest, and the films and books keep him in the culture. The FA's refusal to appoint him in 1977, choosing Ron Greenwood, is the great "what if" of English football.

The torch is carried by charisma and by provincial ambition: by [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), whose touchline presence and one-liners Clough would have recognised, and by the coaches who have taken unfashionable clubs to the top. Nottingham Forest's Europa League and Champions League qualifications in the 2020s under Nuno Espírito Santo and then Oliver Glasner were the first echoes of 1979 at the City Ground in 40 years.
