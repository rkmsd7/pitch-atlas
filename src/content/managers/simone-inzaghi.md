## Biography

Simone Inzaghi, born in Piacenza on 5 April 1976, spent most of his playing life being introduced as Filippo's younger brother and most of his coaching life proving he was the better manager. At [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) between 2021 and 2025 he built one of Europe's most coherent teams, won the 2023-24 Scudetto with a record margin and reached two [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history) finals. Days after the second, a 5-0 defeat to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) in Munich in May 2025, he left for [Al Hilal](/clubs/al-hilal) in Riyadh.

## Playing career

A striker with a sharp instinct in the box, Inzaghi came through at Piacenza and had his best years at [Lazio](/clubs/lazio) from 1999 to 2010, part of the Scudetto-winning squad in his first season and scorer of four goals in a Champions League match against Marseille in 2000. He won three caps for [Italy](/nations/italy). Injuries limited him in later years, and he retired at Lazio in 2010, moving straight into the club's youth set-up.

## Coaching career

Inzaghi coached Lazio's youth sides for six years, winning the Primavera title, before being promoted to the first team in April 2016 after Marcelo Bielsa's abrupt withdrawal. He stayed five seasons, won the 2019 Coppa Italia and two Supercoppe, and took Lazio to the brink of a title challenge in 2019-20 before the pandemic interruption. His 3-5-2 with Ciro Immobile, Luis Alberto and Sergej Milinković-Savić was a joy to watch.

Inter hired him in 2021 to replace [Antonio Conte](/managers/antonio-conte) after the Scudetto and the sale of Lukaku and Hakimi. He won the Coppa Italia in 2022, reached the 2023 Champions League final in Istanbul, lost narrowly to [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city), and then produced a masterpiece in 2023-24: 94 points, the title clinched in a derby against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan), and the second star on the badge. The 2024-25 season brought a second final after an epic semi-final against [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), but the loss of the league to Napoli on the final weekend and the Munich humiliation ended his Inter era. [Cristian Chivu](/managers/cristian-chivu) succeeded him and won the 2025-26 Scudetto.

Al Hilal, the richest club in the [Saudi Pro League](/leagues/saudi-pro-league), appointed him in June 2025 with the Club World Cup imminent. He has coached a squad containing Rúben Neves, Aleksandar Mitrović, Kalidou Koulibaly and Salem Al-Dawsari.

## Tactical identity

Inzaghi's 3-5-2 is one of the defining tactical systems of the 2020s. The [back three](/tactics/back-three) includes a ball-playing wide centre-back, Alessandro Bastoni at Inter, who steps into midfield; the wing-backs, Federico Dimarco and Denzel Dumfries, provide the width and much of the goal threat; the midfield three is anchored by a regista (Hakan Çalhanoğlu) with two mezzale (Nicolò Barella, Henrikh Mkhitaryan) who run beyond the strikers.

In possession the structure resembles [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) with constant rotations, but the heart of the approach is the relationship between the two forwards, Lautaro Martínez and Marcus Thuram, and the speed of the [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) once the ball is won. Inter were exceptional at [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), scoring heavily from corners with pre-planned blocks. Defensively the team press in a 5-3-2 mid-block and squeeze the pitch, conceding very few shots. At Al Hilal he has kept the shape, with Neves as regista, but the league's physical and tactical level has made games more open.

## Achievements

- Serie A 2023-24 with 94 points and Inter's 20th title; two Champions League finals in three seasons.
- Six domestic cups across Lazio and Inter, including a record run of Supercoppa wins.
- Developed Lazio and Inter into model clubs for coherent three-at-the-back football.

## Storyline

Inzaghi's second season in Riyadh is complicated. Al Hilal are the dominant force in Saudi football and expect to win everything, and after a surprise 2-0 defeat to NEOM in autumn 2026 reports emerged that the club was reviewing his future, with the suggestion that a decision on a new coach would be postponed until the summer if performances improved. He remains in post as of November 2026 and the situation should be treated as fluid rather than settled.

The league around him has become a coaching showcase. [Ange Postecoglou](/managers/ange-postecoglou) at champions [Al Nassr](/clubs/al-nassr) and [Jens Wissing](/managers/jens-wissing) at [Al Ittihad](/clubs/al-ittihad) are both new, while Al Hilal, having lost the title in 2025-26, carry the burden of favourites. Inzaghi has the AFC Champions League and a crowded fixture list to manage. A return to Europe is widely expected eventually; several Italian and English clubs have been linked. For now he is in Saudi Arabia with something to prove again.
