## Biography

Slaven Bilić was born in Split on 11 September 1968. Lawyer by training, rock guitarist by hobby and centre-back by trade, he was part of the Croatian side that finished third at the 1998 World Cup, and as a young coach he gave [Croatia](/nations/croatia) the most exciting years of the 2000s, including two wins over England that kept Steve McClaren's side out of Euro 2008. After a long club career abroad, in England, Russia, Turkey, Saudi Arabia and China, he was appointed Croatia head coach for a second time on 13 July 2026, succeeding Zlatko Dalić, the most successful coach in the country's history, who stepped down after the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup).

## Playing career

Bilić played for Hajduk Split, Karlsruhe, [West Ham](/clubs/west-ham-united) and Everton, where he was a hard, intelligent centre-back, before finishing at Hajduk. He won 44 caps, played at Euro 1996 and at the 1998 World Cup, where Croatia finished third and his tussle with Laurent Blanc in the semi-final got the Frenchman sent off for the final. He retired in 2001 with a hip problem.

His first Croatia reign, with [Luka Modrić](/players/luka-modric) emerging and Eduardo da Silva and Ivica Olić up front, produced the famous 2-0 and 3-2 wins over England and a Euro 2008 quarter-final lost on penalties to Turkey. He later took West Ham to seventh in the last season at Upton Park and West Brom into the Premier League. Dalić, who reached the 2018 World Cup final and finished third in 2022, resigned on 8 July 2026 after Croatia lost 2-1 to Portugal in the round of 32, and the HNS turned to Bilić five days later.

## Tactical identity

Bilić's teams are pragmatic, physical and direct. His Croatia of 2006-12 played a 4-2-3-1 that defended in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and broke at speed, [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Olić and Eduardo with Modrić and Niko Kranjčar the creators, and his club sides, notably West Ham in 2015-16 with Dimitri Payet, were at their best as transition teams. He uses [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) to a target striker willingly and has rarely been a possession coach. His assets are man-management, charisma and the ability to make a dressing room feel like a band; the 2026 appointment was as much about reconnecting the public with a team that had grown tired as about tactics.

## Storyline

Croatia's 2026 World Cup was their worst under Dalić, a round-of-32 exit to [Portugal](/nations/portugal), and it marked the end of an era: Modrić, 40, Ivan Perišić and Marcelo Brozović had carried the team through a final and two semi-finals. Dalić left with the federation's thanks, saying he left "with a full heart". Bilić inherits the transition to Joško Gvardiol, Luka Sučić, Martin Baturina and Josip Šutalo, and the question of whether Croatia, population four million, can keep producing teams that reach the last four.

The first tasks are the 2026-27 [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league), beginning in September 2026, and qualification for [Euro 2028](/tournaments/euro). Croatia reached the Nations League final in 2023 and have been to the knockout stages of every European Championship since 2008 bar one; the public expects that to continue even without Modrić. Results since July 2026 should be checked against the live data on this site.
