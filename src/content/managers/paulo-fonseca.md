## Biography

Paulo Fonseca was born in Nampula, Mozambique, on 5 March 1973 and grew up in Portugal, where he became a coach of the possession school with a gift for building attractive teams in difficult circumstances. He has won titles in Ukraine, taken Lille to the Champions League, had a brief and unhappy spell at [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan), and from January 2025 coached [Lyon](/clubs/lyon) through the most financially fraught period in the club's modern history. In November 2026 he left Lyon to take charge of [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp).

## Playing career

Fonseca was a centre-back for Barreirense, Leça, Belenenses, Marítimo and Estrela da Amadora in the Portuguese top flight and second tier between 1991 and 2005. He was a solid, intelligent defender rather than a star, and moved into coaching at Estrela immediately after retiring.

## Coaching career

He rose through Portugal's lower leagues and made his name at Paços de Ferreira, taking a small club to third in 2012-13 and into the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league) qualifiers. [Porto](/clubs/porto) hired him in 2013 but sacked him within a year; a return to Paços and a cup win at Braga in 2016 restored him. At Shakhtar Donetsk (2016-19) he won three straight doubles playing beautiful football and became famous for the Zorro costume he wore after qualifying for the Champions League knockouts.

[Roma](/clubs/roma) (2019-21) brought a Europa League semi-final; [Lille](/clubs/lille) (2022-24) brought fourth place and a Champions League return. AC Milan hired him in 2024 and dismissed him in December after a Supercoppa-less half season. Lyon, in turmoil over finances and threatened with relegation by the DNCG, appointed him in January 2025, and he kept the club in Europe through the crisis. He received a lengthy touchline ban in 2025 for confronting a referee, served it, and steered Lyon into 2026-27.

## Tactical identity

Fonseca is a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) coach whose teams play a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession. He likes a full-back to invert ([inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs)), two wingers high and wide, and a number ten who drifts to create overloads. His Shakhtar were among the most fluid passing teams in Europe; his Lille combined that with a counter-press, a controlled form of [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing), that made them hard to play through.

Without the ball he uses a mid-block with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the wide areas. The criticism, at Milan especially, was that his teams were open in transition; the response, at Lyon, has been a slightly more cautious rest defence and greater use of a double pivot. He is excellent at developing attacking players, with Jonathan David, Malick Fofana and Rayan Cherki all improving under him.

## Achievements

- Three consecutive Ukrainian league and cup doubles with Shakhtar Donetsk (2017-19).
- Champions League qualification with Lille in 2024 and Paços de Ferreira in 2013.
- Taça de Portugal 2016 with Braga and the 2013 Supertaça with Porto.
- Kept Lyon in European competition through the 2025 financial crisis.

## Storyline

Fonseca's 2026-27 began at Lyon, where the club sat among the chasing pack in [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1) behind [PSG](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) under [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique), with [Marseille](/clubs/marseille), [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) and Lille as rivals for second. Lyon were third in the live table in early November, which made what followed all the more striking.

Events in Lisbon intervened. [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) announced on 1 November 2026 that they had opened talks to end Rui Borges's contract, with reserve coach Tiago Fernandes taking interim charge. Within days reports, including a beIN Sports piece on 3 November and Belgian coverage on 4 November, said Fonseca had agreed to leave Lyon for Sporting, with Lyon receiving around €5m in compensation, and Lyon confirmed his departure. Club records now list Fonseca as Sporting head coach, with his long-time assistant Jorge Maciel ([profile](/managers/jorge-maciel)) stepping up at Lyon; the exact contract terms and formal presentation dates should be treated as early reports until the clubs publish them.

The brief at Sporting is clear: restore the standards of the Ruben Amorim era, chase [Porto](/clubs/porto) under [Francesco Farioli](/managers/francesco-farioli) and [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) under [Marco Silva](/managers/marco-silva) in the [Primeira Liga](/leagues/primeira-liga), and give a talented squad an identity again. For Fonseca it is a homecoming a decade in the making, and his eighteen months at Lyon will be remembered for keeping a great French club afloat when its own board could not.
