## Biography

Raffaele Palladino was born in Mugnano di Napoli on 17 April 1984, a Neapolitan who built his reputation in the north. Soft-spoken, meticulous and more modern in his methods than many Italian contemporaries, he is one of the leading figures of the generation of former players who have adopted the [back three](/tactics/back-three) as a default. His career has moved quickly, perhaps too quickly for his own good: four Serie A clubs in four years by the time [Bologna](/clubs/bologna) called in September 2026.

## Playing career

Palladino was a quick, versatile forward who came through at Benevento and [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), where he made his debut in 2004. He was loaned to Salernitana, Livorno and Genoa before returning to Juve in Serie B after Calciopoli, scoring in the 2006-07 promotion season. He spent five years at [Genoa](/clubs/genoa), where he played under [Gian Piero Gasperini](/managers/gian-piero-gasperini), then had spells at Parma, Crotone and Spezia before finishing at Monza in 2019. He won one cap for [Italy](/nations/italy) in 2007.

## Coaching career

He retired at Monza and coached the under-15s and then the Primavera. In September 2022, with Monza bottom of Serie A and Giovanni Stroppa sacked, Palladino was promoted to the first team for their debut top-flight season. He beat Juventus in his first match and took the newly promoted club to 11th, a result Silvio Berlusconi called a miracle. A 12th-placed finish in 2023-24 followed.

[Fiorentina](/clubs/fiorentina) hired him in 2024 and he took them to sixth and a Conference League semi-final, a season remembered for Moise Kean's revival and for Palladino's handling of Edoardo Bove's cardiac arrest on the pitch in December 2024. He left Florence in the summer of 2025 and, in November 2025, replaced Ivan Jurić at [Atalanta](/clubs/atalanta) after a seven-game winless run. He steadied the side to seventh and Conference League qualification, but Atalanta sacked him in June 2026 after six months and hired [Maurizio Sarri](/managers/maurizio-sarri).

Bologna's call came in mid-September 2026. Domenico Tedesco, appointed in June to replace Vincenzo Italiano, had collected one point from four games and was dismissed; Palladino was announced within a day on a contract to 2029.

## Tactical identity

Palladino's teams play a 3-4-2-1 with a sharp, structured press. The wide centre-backs are aggressive, the wing-backs provide width and the two players behind the striker drift into half-spaces to receive between lines. His Monza side was unusual for a promoted team in building patiently from the back and keeping possession, while his Fiorentina was more vertical, with Kean as a running target and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) bursts down the flanks.

He uses [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) rather than all-out man-marking, steering the opponent's build-up towards the touchline and then collapsing on the receiver, and he devotes significant training time to [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), both as a source of goals and as a defensive routine. The evolution of his work has been towards flexibility; at Atalanta he had to blend his methods with a Gasperini-trained squad, and he has learned to adjust the height of the block to the players available.

At Bologna he inherits a team built by Thiago Motta and Italiano on a 4-2-3-1 and intense pressing, and early reports suggested he would ease the squad into his three-at-the-back shape gradually.

## Achievements

- Kept Monza in Serie A in their first top-flight season after taking over with the club bottom.
- Sixth place and a Conference League semi-final with Fiorentina in 2024-25.
- Seventh place and European qualification with Atalanta in 2025-26 after a mid-season appointment.

## Storyline

Bologna are a club that reached the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history) in 2024 and won the 2025 Coppa Italia under Italiano, and the summer decision to replace him with Tedesco went wrong almost immediately. Palladino is the corrective: an Italian coach with a track record of quick stabilisation at three clubs, hired on a long contract to signal that the board wants an end to churn.

The task in 2026-27 is to lift Bologna out of the relegation zone they occupied in September and back towards the European places. [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a) is crowded above them: [Cristian Chivu](/managers/cristian-chivu)'s [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), [Massimiliano Allegri](/managers/massimiliano-allegri)'s [Napoli](/clubs/napoli), [Luciano Spalletti](/managers/luciano-spalletti)'s Juventus, [Ruben Amorim](/managers/ruben-amorim)'s [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan), and his own former clubs Atalanta and Fiorentina, where [Paolo Vanoli](/managers/paolo-vanoli) was rehired in the same week he arrived in Bologna.

The campaign's state in November 2026 cannot be confirmed here beyond his appointment. The personal stakes are high: after being sacked by Atalanta despite a respectable finish, Palladino needs Bologna to be the job where he stays long enough to build something.
