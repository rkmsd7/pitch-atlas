## Biography

Sébastien Pocognoli was born in Seraing, near Liège, on 1 August 1987. A left-back who played in Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany and England, he moved into coaching at Union Saint-Gilloise and led them to the Belgian title in 2025, the club's first since 1935. After a short, turbulent spell at [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) in 2025-26, he was appointed head coach of [Scotland](/nations/scotland), where as of November 2026 he is building towards a home [Euro 2028](/tournaments/euro).

## Playing career

Pocognoli came through Genk's academy, played for AZ Alkmaar and Standard Liège, then Hannover 96, West Bromwich Albion and Brighton in England before finishing at Standard and Union SG. He won 13 caps for [Belgium](/nations/belgium) between 2008 and 2014 and played at the 2008 Olympics. He retired in 2021, at 34, having spent his last season as a Union SG player while the club returned to the top flight.

Promoted to head coach in the summer of 2024 after Alexander Blessin left for St Pauli, Pocognoli kept Union's model intact, strengthened their pressing and guided them through the championship play-off to the title, nine decades after their last. The Belgian title earned him a move to Monaco in the autumn of 2025, a spell that was brief and difficult; he left before the 2026 World Cup and was named Scotland coach in 2026, succeeding Steve Clarke, the country's longest-serving coach of the modern era.

## Tactical identity

Pocognoli's teams play with a [back three](/tactics/back-three), two aggressive wing-backs and a front three that presses in a narrow 3-4-2-1. Union were a counter-pressing side, winning the ball high and attacking quickly through Promise David and Franjo Ivanović, which places him in the [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) family. He favours a man-oriented press with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the touchline, and his build-up asks the outside centre-backs to carry the ball forward. For Scotland the fit is natural: the 3-4-2-1 that Clarke used for years already suits Andy Robertson, Kieran Tierney, Scott McTominay and John McGinn, and Pocognoli has simply asked for more intensity in the press and more vertical passing out of defence.

## Storyline

Scotland's 2026 was a year of change. The campaign for the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) under Steve Clarke, with McTominay in the best form of his life after his Serie A title at Napoli, and the subsequent end of the Clarke era, left the Scottish FA looking for a coach with a modern pressing identity and an eye for development. Pocognoli, young, multilingual and fresh from a title in a league that produces more good coaches per head than almost any other, was the choice.

The road ahead is the 2026-27 [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league) and then qualification for Euro 2028, where Scotland are co-hosts and will play at Hampden. The goal is simple: become the first Scottish side to win a knockout match at a major tournament. A generation of Robertson, McGinn, McTominay, Billy Gilmour and the emerging [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) and [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) players is reaching its last peak, and Pocognoli's job is to get the most from it before it has to be rebuilt.
