## Biography

Luciano Spalletti was born in Certaldo, Tuscany, on 7 March 1959, the son of a farming family, and he still retreats to his vineyard and olive groves between jobs. He is one of Italian football's great eccentrics: a bald, intense figure who speaks in extended metaphors, cries at the right moments and has an unusual gift for making players feel they are part of something historic. He is also one of the most innovative coaches of his generation, the man who gave Europe the modern [false nine](/tactics/false-nine).

## Playing career

Spalletti was a modest midfielder who spent his whole career in the lower divisions: Virtus Entella, Spezia, Viareggio and Empoli, where he played from 1986 to 1993 and finished as a Serie C regular. He never played in Serie A, a fact he has turned into a motivational point, insisting coaches who were not stars have to study harder.

## Coaching career

He stepped straight from Empoli's dressing room to its bench in 1993 and took them from Serie C1 to [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a) in four years. Spells at Sampdoria, Venezia, Udinese and Ancona followed before a second, celebrated period at Udinese, whom he took to fourth and the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history) in 2005.

At [Roma](/clubs/roma) from 2005 to 2009 he built the side that defined the false nine, with Francesco Totti dropping from the front and runners flooding past him. Roma won two Coppa Italia titles and finished second twice behind [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan). He then spent five years at Zenit, winning two Russian titles, before returning to Roma and taking Inter back into the Champions League in 2018.

[Napoli](/clubs/napoli) was his masterpiece. Appointed in 2021, he won the 2022-23 Scudetto, Napoli's first since Diego Maradona in 1990, by 16 points, with Victor Osimhen and [Khvicha Kvaratskhelia](/players/khvicha-kvaratskhelia) as his stars. He walked away immediately, took a sabbatical, and then accepted the [Italy](/nations/italy) job in September 2023. Euro 2024 ended in a last-16 defeat to Switzerland, and he left the role in 2025 to be succeeded by [Gennaro Gattuso](/managers/gennaro-gattuso), whose side failed to qualify for the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup).

[Juventus](/clubs/juventus) turned to him in the 2025-26 season as the club searched for a coach capable of giving a talented but fragmented squad a clear idea.

## Tactical identity

Spalletti's football is possession-first and built on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), but with an Italian accent: the structures are rigorous, the pressing is organised in zones, and the [high line](/tactics/high-line) is protected by a goalkeeper and centre-backs who can defend space. His great Napoli side played a 4-3-3 with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) and the right winger staying wide while the left winger cut inside, a deliberate asymmetry that created overloads.

He is obsessive about the third-man run, the pass into a forward's feet followed by an immediate lay-off to a midfielder arriving. His teams are also remarkably direct for a possession side, taking shots early once the ball is in the final third.

At Juventus he has alternated between a 4-3-3 and a 3-4-2-1, partly to accommodate [Kenan Yıldız](/players/kenan-yildiz) in the half-space and partly because the squad's defenders are more comfortable in a three. The evolution of his career has been from the romantic chaos of early Roma to the controlled, almost industrial precision of Napoli 2023.

## Achievements

- Serie A champion 2022-23 with Napoli, the club's third title and first in 33 years.
- Two Russian Premier League titles and a Russian Cup with Zenit.
- Two Coppa Italia titles and a Supercoppa with Roma.
- Serie A Coach of the Year 2023 and four Panchina d'Oro awards.

## Storyline

Juventus hired Spalletti to stop the drift. The club has not won the Scudetto since 2020, and the sequence of Pirlo, Allegri, Motta and Tudor left the squad without a shared idea. Spalletti's presence is itself a statement: he is the only active Italian coach with a recent title to his name, and the board wanted someone who could speak to the fanbase in the club's own language.

The context of 2026-27 is demanding. Inter are champions under [Cristian Chivu](/managers/cristian-chivu), Napoli have hired [Massimiliano Allegri](/managers/massimiliano-allegri), Juve's old title-winner, and [Ruben Amorim](/managers/ruben-amorim) is at [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan). Spalletti's Juventus must also carry the weight of Italian football's wounded pride after the national side missed a third straight World Cup while [Spain](/nations/spain) lifted the trophy.

How the season is unfolding as of November 2026 cannot be confirmed here beyond the fact that he remains in charge. The narrative is clear enough: a coach in his late sixties, with nothing left to prove, trying to make Juventus play beautifully again before he returns to his vineyard for good.
