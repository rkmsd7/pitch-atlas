## Biography

Diego Martín Forlán Corazo was born in Montevideo on 19 May 1979, into a footballing family: his father Pablo played at the 1966 and 1974 World Cups and his grandfather Juan Carlos Corazo coached Independiente. As a player he was one of the best strikers of his generation, the best player of the 2010 World Cup and a Copa América winner in 2011. As a coach his career has been modest so far, but in August 2026 the Uruguayan federation named him interim head coach of [Uruguay](/nations/uruguay) after Marcelo Bielsa resigned, on a mandate running to March 2027, while he continues to coach the under-20s.

## Playing career

Forlán started at Independiente in Argentina, moved to [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in 2002 and won the Premier League in 2003, then became a star in Spain with Villarreal and [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid), winning two European Golden Shoes and scoring twice in the 2010 Europa League final. At the 2010 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) he scored five goals, including the volley against Germany in the third-place match, and won the Golden Ball as Uruguay finished fourth. He later played for Inter, Internacional, Cerezo Osaka, Peñarol and Mumbai City, and won 112 caps with 36 goals.

His first job at Peñarol lasted a few months in 2020 and ended during the pandemic; a short spell at second-division Atenas followed. The AUF appointed him to the under-20 programme, and when Bielsa left after the 2026 World Cup the federation chose continuity and national identity over a quick foreign hire, giving Forlán the senior team on an interim basis with the proviso that he would be evaluated before March 2027.

## Tactical identity

Forlán's coaching ideas are those of a modern Uruguayan striker: a compact 4-4-2 that defends in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and attacks with speed, direct balls into the front two and crosses from the full-backs. At Peñarol he used a 4-3-3 with wingers and a pressing trigger on the opponent's full-backs, but the DNA is [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) with a strong dose of [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) and garra, the traditional Uruguayan intensity. With the senior side he has simplified after the Bielsa years, which many players found exhausting, and leaned on Federico Valverde and Rodrigo Bentancur to control games from midfield.

## Storyline

Uruguay's 2026 World Cup was a disaster. Bielsa's side went out in the group stage, the worst Uruguayan performance at a tournament they had qualified for since 1974, and the coach, who had said in May that "our job ends with the World Cup", resigned within days, saying he had left nothing good for Uruguayan football. The AUF confirmed Forlán as interim coach on 6 August 2026, giving him the senior team until March 2027 and asking him to keep the under-20s for the 2027 South American championship in parallel.

What comes next is a rebuild around Valverde, Bentancur, Darwin Núñez, Ronald Araújo and the generation that won the 2023 Under-20 World Cup, with the 2027 [Copa América](/tournaments/copa-america) and the start of 2030 qualifying as the first tests. The 2030 World Cup will open in Montevideo, at the Centenario, as part of the centenary celebrations, which gives Uruguay a date they cannot miss. Whether Forlán is still the coach by then depends on the federation's review in early 2027. Results since August 2026 should be checked against the live data on this site.
