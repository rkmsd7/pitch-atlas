## Biography

Rodolfo Arruabarrena, known to everyone in Argentine football as "El Vasco", was born in Las Flores, Buenos Aires province, on 20 June 1975. A combative left-back who became a [Boca Juniors](/clubs/boca-juniors) idol in the great Carlos Bianchi sides of the late 1990s and early 2000s, he later won the 2015 league title as Boca's coach before a long spell in the Gulf. Club records for 2026-27 list him as Boca's head coach once again, the latest appointment in a managerial carousel that has defined Juan Román Riquelme's presidency; given how fast that carousel has turned, the details of his return should be read as reported rather than settled.

## Playing career

Arruabarrena came through Boca's academy and made his debut in 1994, becoming a fixture at left-back under Bianchi. He won four league titles, the [Copa Libertadores](/tournaments/copa-libertadores) in 2000 and 2001 and the Intercontinental Cup in 2000, when Boca beat the [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) of Raúl and Figo in Tokyo. He moved to Spain in 2001, spending seven seasons at [Villarreal](/clubs/villarreal), where he was part of the side that reached the 2006 [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league) semi-final under [Manuel Pellegrini](/managers/manuel-pellegrini). He finished his career at Tigre in 2010 after winning 14 caps for [Argentina](/nations/argentina).

## Coaching career

He stepped straight from playing into coaching at Tigre in 2010 and quickly made the club a force, taking them to the 2012 Copa Sudamericana final, where they refused to come out for the second half against São Paulo in the Morumbi after alleged confrontations in the tunnel. A short spell at Nacional of Montevideo followed before Boca came calling in August 2014.

His first Boca spell was successful on paper and unloved in the stands. He won the 2015 Primera División and the Copa Argentina in a side built around Carlos Tevez's return, but exited the Libertadores amid the pepper-spray scandal against [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate) and was sacked in March 2016 after a poor start. He then spent most of a decade in the Middle East: two spells at Al-Wasl in Dubai, a season at Pyramids in Egypt and a stint in charge of the United Arab Emirates national team from 2022 to 2023.

## Tactical identity

Arruabarrena is a pragmatist. His Tigre and Boca teams were organised in a 4-3-3 or 4-2-3-1 that defended in a mid-to-[low block](/tactics/low-block) and attacked quickly, with wingers running in behind and full-backs overlapping late. He favours [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) and [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into a strong centre-forward, and his sides are typically physical and set-piece conscious rather than possession heavy.

The 2015 title side did control games more, largely because Tevez and Pablo Pérez could keep the ball under pressure, and his Gulf teams played a more patient style suited to the heat. The criticism, from Boca's demanding supporters in particular, has always been aesthetic: effective, rarely thrilling. The counter-argument is that in a league decided by clásicos and knockout ties, organisation travels better than beauty.

## Achievements

- Primera División 2015 and Copa Argentina 2015 with Boca Juniors.
- Copa Sudamericana final 2012 with Tigre.
- As a player: four league titles, two Libertadores and the 2000 Intercontinental Cup with Boca.

## Storyline

Boca's recent history is a list of coaches. Fernando Gago's spell ended after 191 days in 2025; Miguel Ángel Russo's death in October 2025 left Claudio Úbeda in interim charge; Úbeda was confirmed for 2026 in December and then departed in June 2026. Riquelme's board has been criticised for a lack of planning, and the return of a club legend who already knows the dressing-room politics of the Bombonera is a familiar move. Club records for 2026-27 name Arruabarrena as head coach; the date of his appointment and the length of his contract were not independently verifiable at the time of writing, so treat the timeline as reported.

The job is the usual one. Boca have not won the [Copa Libertadores](/tournaments/copa-libertadores) since 2007 and the pressure to end that run grows every year, especially with River Plate in their own transition under interim coach [Leonardo Ponzio](/managers/leonardo-ponzio) after Eduardo Coudet's exit. In the [Argentine Primera](/leagues/argentine-primera), the Clausura decides the year, and the Superclásico decides the mood. Arruabarrena was never adored in his first spell, even while winning; the question now is whether a decade abroad has made him the calmer, more complete coach Boca need, or whether the carousel simply turns again.
