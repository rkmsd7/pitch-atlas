## Biography

Martin O'Neill, born in Kilrea, County Londonderry on 1 March 1952, is one of the most charismatic figures in British football management and, improbably, the oldest coach in charge of a major European club. He first led [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) between 2000 and 2005, winning three titles and taking the club to the 2003 UEFA Cup final in Seville. Two decades later, with the club in turmoil after Brendan Rodgers' departure in October 2025, he returned as caretaker, delivered a domestic double, watched his successor Wilfried Nancy fail inside 33 days, and came back a second time before the board made him permanent manager on 11 June 2026 on a one-year contract with a one-year option.

## Playing career

O'Neill was a clever, energetic midfielder who left Distillery for Nottingham Forest in 1971 and became a key figure in Brian Clough's miracle: promotion in 1977, the First Division title in 1978 and the European Cup in 1979 and 1980, though injury kept him out of the first final. He later played for Norwich, Manchester City and Notts County and captained [Northern Ireland](/nations/northern-ireland) at the 1982 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), including the famous 1-0 win over hosts Spain. He won 64 caps.

## Coaching career

He learned his trade in non-league with Grantham and Shepshed before building Wycombe Wanderers from the Conference into the Football League with two promotions. After a brief, unhappy spell at Norwich he transformed Leicester City, winning promotion in 1996 and two League Cups while establishing them as a Premier League fixture.

Celtic appointed him in 2000 and his first season was a clean sweep: the Treble, with Henrik Larsson scoring 53 goals. Two more titles followed in 2002 and 2004, plus the European run of 2002-03 that ended with a 3-2 extra-time defeat to Porto, coached by [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho), in Seville. He left in 2005 to care for his wife, then managed [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa) to three successive sixth-place finishes and a League Cup final, had an uneven spell at [Sunderland](/clubs/sunderland), and led the [Republic of Ireland](/nations/republic-of-ireland) to Euro 2016, where they beat Italy and reached the round of 16. A short return to Nottingham Forest in 2019 appeared to close the book.

Celtic reopened it in October 2025. Brought back as interim manager with Shaun Maloney alongside him, he steadied the dressing room and, as the SPFL's Manager of the Month for November, went on to win the 2025-26 [Scottish Premiership](/leagues/scottish-premiership) and Scottish Cup. The club then appointed Columbus Crew coach Wilfried Nancy, who lost six of eight matches and was sacked after 33 days, a sequence the Scottish press described as a horror show. O'Neill stepped in again, and in June 2026 the board, with Dermot Desmond's backing, confirmed him on a permanent deal.

## Tactical identity

O'Neill has never pretended to be a tactician in the modern analytical sense; his gift is motivation, selection and a ruthless clarity about what wins matches. His Celtic sides of the early 2000s played a bold 3-5-2 with Larsson and Chris Sutton (or John Hartson) as a twin strike force, Neil Lennon shielding the defence, and wing-backs who bombed forward. The approach was [direct](/tactics/direct-play): quick, early service into the forwards, aggressive [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces), and a willingness to defend deep and [counter](/tactics/counter-attacking) in Europe.

In his 2025-26 revival he leaned on the squad's existing structure, often a back four or a [back three](/tactics/back-three) depending on the opponent, but the themes were the same: simplicity, tempo, physicality and a demand that players front up. He delegates detailed session planning to his staff and concentrates on the human side, which, at a club that had lost its nerve, proved exactly the medicine required.

## Achievements

- Four Scottish titles with Celtic across two eras, including the 2025-26 championship won from a caretaker role.
- Celtic's 2001 Treble and the 2003 UEFA Cup final.
- Two League Cups with Leicester and a Euro 2016 round-of-16 run with Ireland.

## Storyline

The 2026-27 season at Celtic is a story about whether a 74-year-old can hold together a club whose structure has been questioned. The club's accounts for the year to June 2026 were released with chief executive Michael Nicholson signalling that O'Neill would remain in charge, and he has spoken with typical dry humour about retreating to a "darkened room" after difficult nights and about a run of injuries in the autumn.

Across the city, [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) under [Derek McInnes](/managers/derek-mcinnes) are chasing hard, and the Old Firm balance is more open than for several seasons. Celtic are in European competition and are expected to retain the title; O'Neill, for his part, has said he will manage as long as he feels he is helping. His second Celtic era is already a feel-good tale. Whether it becomes a lasting one depends on the club's succession plan as much as on results.
