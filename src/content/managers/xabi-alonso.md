## Biography

Xabier Alonso Olano was born in Tolosa on 25 November 1981 and raised in San Sebastián, the son of Periko Alonso, who won La Liga twice with Real Sociedad and once with [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona). He played beach football with [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) as a boy, and the two are now rivals in the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league). Alonso is calm, fluent in four languages, and carries the authority of a player who won everything with an economy of movement that coaches adored.

## Playing career

Alonso broke through at Real Sociedad, captaining the side to second place in 2002-03 at 21. Rafael Benítez signed him for [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) in 2004, and his equaliser in the 2005 Champions League final in Istanbul, scored on the rebound after a saved penalty, became part of the club's folklore. [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) paid £30m for him in 2009, and under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) and then Carlo Ancelotti he won La Liga in 2012 and La Décima in 2014, although suspension kept him out of the final. He finished at [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) and Ancelotti, winning three Bundesliga titles, and retired in 2017.

For [Spain](/nations/spain) he won 114 caps and the treble of Euro 2008, the 2010 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) and Euro 2012, as the deep-lying passer behind Xavi and Iniesta.

## Coaching career

Alonso began with Real Madrid's U14s, then took Real Sociedad B to promotion to the Segunda División in 2021, the reserve side's first appearance there in 60 years. [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) appointed him in October 2022 with the club 17th in the Bundesliga; he took them to sixth and a Europa League semi-final. The following season, 2023-24, Leverkusen won their first Bundesliga title without losing a game, a 34-match unbeaten league season, added the DFB-Pokal, and went 51 matches unbeaten in all competitions before losing the Europa League final to [Atalanta](/clubs/atalanta).

He turned down Liverpool and Bayern in 2024, stayed one more year, and took the Real Madrid job in summer 2025. The marriage was short: Alonso left mid-season in 2025-26, with the club in transition after Ancelotti and the dressing room restless, and Madrid eventually turned back to Mourinho in July 2026. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) moved fast. After Enzo Maresca's exit and a brief spell under Liam Rosenior, Alonso was appointed from July 2026 and the club immediately broke the British transfer record for [Morgan Rogers](/players/morgan-rogers).

## Tactical identity

Alonso is a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) coach who prefers a [back three](/tactics/back-three). At Leverkusen the shape was a 3-4-2-1 in which Granit Xhaka dictated from the base of midfield, wing-backs Alejandro Grimaldo and Jeremie Frimpong provided the width and goals, and Florian Wirtz roamed between the lines. In possession it became a 3-2-5, with one midfielder dropping beside the centre-backs to create a diamond for clean build-up. His teams are patient, circulate the ball to pull the press apart and then attack the half-spaces with runners.

Defensively Leverkusen used [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) rather than full-pitch pressing, inviting play wide before collapsing on the touchline. At Madrid he experimented with a back four and more direct play to suit [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe) and [Vinícius Júnior](/players/vinicius-junior), which was part of the friction. At Chelsea he has returned to the three-at-the-back model, with Rogers in the Wirtz role and [Cole Palmer](/players/cole-palmer) as the second number ten.

## Achievements

- Bundesliga 2023-24, the first unbeaten season in the league's history
- DFB-Pokal 2024 and DFL-Supercup 2024
- 51-match unbeaten run across all competitions
- Promotion with Real Sociedad B in 2021
- As a player: World Cup, two Euros, two Champions Leagues, league titles in Spain and Germany

## Storyline

Chelsea's owners have spent more than any club in history and hired and fired coaches at a rate to match. Alonso is the first appointment that reads as a settled, long-term bet on a manager rather than a squad. The £117m for Rogers from [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa), a British record, signalled that the recruitment would follow the coach's needs this time rather than the other way round.

The early picture in 2026-27 is of a team learning a demanding system with a very young squad. Palmer, Rogers, Enzo Fernández, who was sent off in the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) final for [Argentina](/nations/argentina) in July 2026, and the young centre-backs give Alonso the profile he wants. Whether Chelsea can mount a title challenge against Arteta's champions and the enriched [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) of [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca), the man he replaced, is the story of the season at Stamford Bridge.

There is a personal thread too. Alonso's departure from Madrid was framed in Spain as a failure, the first of his coaching career, and his contract at Chelsea gives him the time Madrid did not. His admirers point out that it took him one full season to turn Leverkusen from relegation candidates into immortals. Chelsea are hoping the same clock applies.
