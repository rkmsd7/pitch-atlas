## Biography

Sarina Petronella Wiegman was born in The Hague on 26 October 1969. She played as a midfielder at a time when the Dutch federation barely acknowledged women's football, became a PE teacher, and then the most decorated coach the women's game has known. She won [Euro 2017](/tournaments/womens-euro) with the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands-women), reached the 2019 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) final, and with [England](/nations/england-women) won Euro 2022 and Euro 2025 and reached the 2023 World Cup final. In October 2026 the FA extended her contract by two years, to the end of Euro 2029.

## Playing career

Wiegman played for KFC '71 and Ter Leede in the Netherlands, spent a year at North Carolina Tar Heels under Anson Dorrance in 1989, where she played alongside Mia Hamm and Kristine Lilly, and won 104 caps for the Netherlands between 1987 and 2001, the first Dutch woman to reach a century. She combined football with teaching for her whole playing career.

She was appointed Netherlands coach seven months before a home Euro 2017 and won it, beating Denmark in the final with a side built around Lieke Martens and Vivianne Miedema. Two years later the Dutch reached the World Cup final. England hired her in September 2021; she won Euro 2022 at Wembley in her first tournament, lost the 2023 World Cup final to [Spain](/nations/spain-women), and retained the European title in 2025, again beating Spain, this time on penalties.

## Tactical identity

Wiegman is a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) coach with an unusual gift for simplicity. Her preferred 4-3-3 asks for a holding midfielder, Keira Walsh, who dictates tempo, two number eights who arrive in the box, and wingers who stay wide and attack full-backs one-on-one. Her teams hold a [high line](/tactics/high-line), press in organised [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) and are among the best in the world at set pieces. She also uses a consistent starting XI and a small group of trusted substitutes, the "finishers", whose impact at Euro 2022 and 2025 was decisive. She is calm, direct and unsentimental in selection, as England's veterans have discovered.

## Achievements

- Three European Championships: 2017 (Netherlands), 2022 and 2025 (England)
- Two World Cup finals (2019, 2023)
- Finalissima 2023
- FIFA Best Women's Coach four times (2017, 2020, 2022, 2023)
- Dutch and English domestic titles as a club coach

## Storyline

England's 2026 was about qualification. With the 2027 World Cup in Brazil approaching, the Lionesses played their qualifying campaign through the spring and autumn of 2026 with a squad in transition: Lucy Bronze and Millie Bright moving towards the end, Lauren James, Alessia Russo, Lauren Hemp and Michelle Agyemang leading the next generation, and Walsh still the organising presence. The Women's Super League title went to [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city-women) in 2025-26, and the [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal-women) and [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women) cores supply the bulk of her squad.

The new contract, announced on 19 October 2026, means Wiegman will lead England at the 2027 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup), the 2028 [Olympics](/tournaments/olympics-women) in Los Angeles as part of Team GB if selected, and the 2029 Euros. The World Cup is the one trophy she has not won, and England have never won it either. After two finals, the next attempt in Brazil is the obvious target.
