## Biography

Sergej Jakirović was born in Mostar on 4 November 1976, in what was then Yugoslavia. His career has crossed the borders of the former republics constantly, and he holds both Bosnian and Croatian citizenship. A combative character on the touchline, he is known for emotional post-match interviews and a relentless work ethic, and his arrival at [Hull City](/clubs/hull-city) in 2025 made him one of the few Balkan coaches to manage in English football.

## Playing career

Jakirović was a midfielder who played for Zrinjski Mostar and then in Croatia for Osijek, before a long spell in Germany's lower leagues with Rot-Weiss Essen and Wuppertaler SV. He later played in Slovenia and Austria and won two caps for [Bosnia and Herzegovina](/nations/bosnia-and-herzegovina) in 2004. He retired in 2009.

## Coaching career

He began at Zrinjski Mostar, whom he led to two Bosnian titles in 2017 and 2018 and into European qualifiers. Spells at Sarajevo and Maribor followed, and in 2022 HNK Rijeka hired him, where his high-tempo side finished third. Dinamo Zagreb, the dominant force in Croatia, made him coach in 2023, and he won the league and cup double in 2023-24 while taking the club into the Conference League knockout rounds. He left early in the following season after a heavy Champions League defeat, had a brief spell at Kayserispor in Turkey, and then surprised many by accepting Hull City's offer in 2025.

Hull, a Championship club owned by Acun Ilıcalı, who had previously hired Liam Rosenior and Tim Walter, were looking for an energetic, pressing coach. Jakirović delivered promotion at the first attempt, finishing in the play-off places and winning the final at Wembley in May 2026. It is Hull's third promotion to the top flight and the first since 2016.

## Tactical identity

Jakirović's teams are built on [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) and vertical speed. His Dinamo side played a 4-2-3-1 with the wingers pressing the opposition full-backs aggressively and the striker screening the pivot, aiming to win the ball in the opposition half and attack within three passes. The [high line](/tactics/high-line) behind the press is a feature and a risk, and his teams concede chances on the break.

At Hull he has kept the shape and the intensity while recognising the Championship's physical demands. The attack leans on [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into a target striker and second-ball pressure, with full-backs overlapping early. Promotion was built on the division's most aggressive pressing numbers and a strong [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) record away from home.

## Achievements

- Croatian league and cup double with Dinamo Zagreb in 2023-24
- Two Bosnian Premier League titles with Zrinjski Mostar
- Championship play-off winner with Hull City in 2026
- Conference League knockout rounds with Dinamo

## Storyline

Hull's promotion was a surprise even to their owner. Ilıcalı, a Turkish television mogul, had spent heavily and churned through coaches without success, and Jakirović's appointment was seen in England as a curiosity. A season later the club is in the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) alongside fellow promoted sides [Coventry City](/clubs/coventry-city) and [Ipswich Town](/clubs/ipswich-town), and the Balkan coach with the big gestures has become a cult figure at the MKM Stadium.

The task now is survival. Hull's budget is among the smallest in the division, and their recruitment has drawn on the coach's knowledge of the Croatian, Bosnian and Turkish markets, where value is easier to find than in England. The high-pressing style is a gamble against sides with the ball-playing quality of [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) and [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), and Jakirović has had to accept more compact phases than he would like.

Early reports in 2026-27 suggest Hull are competitive but in the bottom third, which is where every realistic projection placed them. The emotional connection between coach, owner and supporters is the club's intangible advantage. Jakirović has already achieved something that Rosenior and Walter could not; staying up would be an achievement of a different order, and would make him a reference point for a whole region of coaches who rarely get a chance in England.
