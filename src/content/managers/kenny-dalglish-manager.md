## Biography

Kenneth Mathieson Dalglish was born in Glasgow on 4 March 1951 and grew up in a flat overlooking Rangers' training ground, before signing for Celtic. He is the greatest player in the history of [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) and of [Scotland](/nations/scotland), and as a manager he won three league titles and the double in his first season as player-manager, carried the club through the Hillsborough disaster with a dignity that made him a civic figure, and then won the Premier League with [Blackburn Rovers](/clubs/blackburn-rovers) in 1995. He was knighted in 2018. His player page is at [Kenny Dalglish](/players/kenny-dalglish).

## Playing career

Dalglish scored 167 goals for [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) under Jock Stein, winning four titles, and joined Liverpool in 1977 as Kevin Keegan's replacement. He scored the winner in the 1978 European Cup final, won three European Cups and six league titles, formed the most celebrated partnership in English football with Ian Rush, and won a record 102 caps and a record 30 goals for Scotland. He played in three World Cups.

Appointed in the aftermath of Heysel, he won the double in 1986, scoring the title-winning goal at Chelsea himself, and built the 1987-88 side of John Barnes, Peter Beardsley and John Aldridge that many consider Liverpool's finest, losing only two league games. After Hillsborough in April 1989 he attended funerals, visited the bereaved and, in February 1991, resigned from exhaustion. Jack Walker's money and Dalglish's judgement took Blackburn from the second tier to the title in four years with Alan Shearer and Chris Sutton. Newcastle and a short Liverpool return brought cup finals and a League Cup.

## Tactical identity

Dalglish inherited the Liverpool pass-and-move tradition, [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in its British form, and adapted it twice. The 1987-88 side was a fluid 4-4-2 with Barnes cutting in from the left, Beardsley dropping between the lines and Ray Houghton and Steve McMahon working the midfield, a more varied, more [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) team than Bob Paisley's. At Blackburn he built a classic, [direct](/tactics/direct-play) English 4-4-2: two wingers, Stuart Ripley and Jason Wilcox, crossing for the SAS strike pair of Shearer and Sutton, with Colin Hendry and Tim Sherwood as the spine. He was a shrewd buyer, a loyal and private manager, and a difficult interviewee.

## Achievements

- Three league titles and two FA Cups as Liverpool manager, including the 1986 double in his first season
- The Premier League with Blackburn in 1995, the last title by a club outside the modern "big six" until Leicester
- League Cups with Celtic and Liverpool
- Manager of the Year 1986, 1988, 1990, 1995
- Knighted 2018; the Kenny Dalglish Stand at Anfield; Freedom of Liverpool

## Storyline

Retired. Dalglish's place at Liverpool is unique, because it was earned as much by what he did in 1989 as by the trophies. The Kenny Dalglish Stand, opened in 2017, and his role as a non-executive director and club ambassador keep him at Anfield; the Hillsborough campaign for justice, which ended in the 2016 inquest verdicts, has his name on it.

The torch is carried by the tradition of the former player who manages his own club, and by Liverpool's current structure: [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) consulted him, Arne Slot won the 2024-25 title in the shadow of his stand, and Andoni Iraola, appointed in June 2026, inherited a club whose standards Dalglish helped set. In Scotland his 30 goals remain the record, and [Sébastien Pocognoli](/managers/sebastien-pocognoli)'s squad is still looking for a finisher of his class.
