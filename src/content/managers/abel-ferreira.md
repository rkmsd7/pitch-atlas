## Biography

Abel Ferreira, born in Penafiel on 22 December 1978, has become something close to a Brazilian institution despite being Portuguese. Appointed by [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) in October 2020, he won the [Copa Libertadores](/tournaments/copa-libertadores) within four months, retained it a year later, added two [Brasileirão](/leagues/brasileirao) titles and became, by every measure, the most decorated coach in the club's history. In December 2025, after a season that ended without a major trophy and with defeat in the Libertadores final, the club renewed his contract to the end of 2027, a statement of faith that remains in force as of November 2026.

## Playing career

Ferreira was a right-back of modest gifts and considerable intelligence. He came through at Penafiel, joined [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) in 2004, where he won a Portuguese Cup, and finished at Braga in 2011. He has described himself as a player who survived on reading the game, and he began coaching Sporting's youth sides while still registered as a player.

## Coaching career

He took charge of Sporting's B team in 2015 and was given the Braga first team in 2017, finishing fourth twice and establishing a reputation for organised, intense football. A year at PAOK in Greece followed in 2019-20, finishing second, before Palmeiras called in the middle of the pandemic-disrupted 2020 season.

The impact was immediate. Palmeiras beat Santos 1-0 in the January 2021 Libertadores final with a 99th-minute goal from Breno Lopes, then won the Copa do Brasil weeks later. In November 2021 they retained the Libertadores against [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) in Montevideo, Deyverson scoring the extra-time winner. The 2022 Brasileirão was won with a record points total; the 2023 title was retained in a dramatic final weekend. Players such as Endrick, who left for [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), Estêvão, sold to [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea), Raphael Veiga, Dudu, Gustavo Gómez and Weverton have defined his sides.

The 2025 season was the first without silverware, culminating in a Libertadores final defeat, and there was pressure for change. The board renewed his contract instead.

## Tactical identity

Ferreira's Palmeiras are defensively meticulous and lethal in transition. He switches between a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with attacking wing-backs, often Marcos Rocha and Piquerez, and a 4-2-3-1 with a double pivot, depending on the opponent. The team are comfortable in a [low block](/tactics/low-block), particularly away from home in continental competition, and spring forward on the [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) with Veiga arriving late and the wide forwards attacking the channels.

The signature features are compactness, discipline and [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces); Palmeiras have been among the best teams in South America from dead balls throughout his tenure, with Gustavo Gómez a constant threat. Critics in Brazil have called his football cautious, but he has adapted when needed, using a higher press and more possession in domestic games as the squad's quality has grown. His man-management, with a reputation for fierce loyalty and occasionally fierce press conferences, has been as important as any shape.

## Achievements

- Two Copa Libertadores (2020, 2021) and two Brazilian championships (2022, 2023).
- Copa do Brasil, Recopa Sudamericana, two Supercopas and three Paulista titles.
- Palmeiras' most successful coach by trophies, overtaking the club's historic names.

## Storyline

The 2026 season has been about reinvention. Palmeiras have sold their brightest talents to Europe, rebuilt the squad around a mix of experienced Brazilians and younger arrivals, and remained the benchmark for consistency in Brazil. As of November 2026 they are in the closing stretch of the Brasileirão, with reports in early November discussing a fixture against Remo in round 34, and the title race is tight. Ferreira's calm authority is a contrast with the turbulence at Flamengo, where [Leonardo Jardim](/managers/leonardo-jardim) faces scrutiny.

The questions are familiar: can Ferreira win a third Libertadores, and how long will he stay? He has been linked with jobs in Portugal and the Premier League for years and has repeatedly said he will decide his future with the club rather than through the press. A renewed contract to the end of 2027 suggests both parties want the era to continue. If Palmeiras end 2026 with a trophy, it will reinforce an already extraordinary record; if not, the Brazilian press will ask whether the cycle has run its course.
