## Biography

Jorge Fernando Pinheiro de Jesus was born in Amadora, on the edge of Lisbon, on 24 July 1954. Loud, blunt, superstitious and relentlessly competitive, he has spent more than thirty years as a head coach and won titles on three continents. He built the modern [Benfica](/clubs/benfica), reinvented [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) and then dominated the [Saudi Pro League](/leagues/saudi-pro-league) with [Al Hilal](/clubs/al-hilal). In the summer of 2026, at 72, he was finally given [Portugal](/nations/portugal), replacing Roberto Martínez after the 2026 World Cup.

## Playing career

Jesus was a midfielder who played for Sporting's youth teams and then had a long, nomadic career with Olhanense, Vitória de Setúbal, Belenenses, Estrela da Amadora and Farense between 1973 and 1989. He never became a star and never won a cap, which he says is why he coaches like a man with something to prove.

His Benfica side of Ángel Di María, David Luiz, Javier Saviola and later Nicolás Gaitán ended Porto's dominance and reached two Europa League finals. The 2019 Flamengo team, with Gabigol, Bruno Henrique, Éverton Ribeiro and Gerson, won the Libertadores against [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate) with two goals in the last ten minutes and took the Brasileirão with a record points total, the finest Brazilian club side in a generation. At Al Hilal he set a world record of 34 consecutive wins in 2023-24.

## Tactical identity

Jesus is a possession coach whose teams attack with width and an aggressive [high line](/tactics/high-line). His favourite shape has been a 4-4-2 that becomes a 4-2-3-1 or a 2-3-5 in possession, with full-backs pushed high, one deep playmaker and inside-forwards. The Flamengo side pressed with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the far touchline and a defensive line routinely 15 metres inside the opposition half, a radical idea in Brazil at the time. He belongs in the [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) family, although he is more pragmatic than Guardiola about direct passes to the strikers. He is also a notorious obsessive about training-ground detail and set pieces.

## Storyline

Portugal came into the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) as European champions and Nations League holders, and went out to [Spain](/nations/spain) in the knockout rounds after beating Croatia 2-1 in the round of 32. Martínez's cycle ended, and the federation turned to the coach the public had asked for during three previous searches. For Jesus it is the first national-team job of his life, and the last chapter he always said he wanted.

The immediate tasks are the 2026-27 [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league) and then qualification for [Euro 2028](/tournaments/euro), where Portugal will defend the title they won in 2016. The squad is in transition: [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo), whom Jesus coached at [Al Nassr](/clubs/al-nassr) in 2025-26, turned 41 in February 2026, and the future belongs to Vitinha, Nuno Mendes, Rafael Leão, Francisco Conceição and the [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) and [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) academies that Jesus knows better than anyone. Whether he can be diplomatic about Ronaldo's role is the subplot everyone in Lisbon is watching. Fixtures and results since his appointment should be checked against the live data on this site.
