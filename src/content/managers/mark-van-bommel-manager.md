## Biography

Mark Peter Gertruda Andreas van Bommel was born in Maasbracht, Limburg, on 22 April 1977. As a player he was the snarling, cynical, superb midfielder of PSV, Barcelona, Bayern and Milan, and the first foreign captain of [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich). As a coach he has been more measured than his playing reputation suggested, winning a double with Royal Antwerp in 2023. On 24 July 2026 the Royal Belgian FA announced him as head coach of [Belgium](/nations/belgium), effective 15 August, on a contract running through [Euro 2028](/tournaments/euro). He replaced Rudi Garcia after Belgium's quarter-final exit at the 2026 World Cup.

## Playing career

Van Bommel played for Fortuna Sittard and [PSV Eindhoven](/clubs/psv-eindhoven), where he won four Eredivisie titles, before joining [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in 2005 and winning the Champions League in 2006. At Bayern he won two Bundesliga titles and captained the club; he finished with Milan, winning Serie A in 2011, and a last season at PSV. For the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) he won 79 caps and played in the 2010 World Cup final. Bert van Marwijk, his father-in-law, was the coach of that team.

His first job at PSV began with 13 straight league wins and a Champions League group-stage place, but ended in December 2019 after a slump. Wolfsburg lasted four months. At Antwerp, with Toby Alderweireld and Vincent Janssen, he won the club's first title since 1957 with a 94th-minute goal on the final day, plus the cup, and took them into the Champions League. His assistants in Belgium are Boudewijn Zenden, Maarten Martens and Reinier Robbemond.

## Tactical identity

Van Bommel is a pragmatic positional coach. His Antwerp side played a 4-3-3 that was patient in build-up and compact without the ball, closer to [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) than to a pressing model, with midfielders instructed to find the forwards early rather than circulate for its own sake. Against stronger opponents he is comfortable sitting in a mid-block and attacking on the [counter](/tactics/counter-attacking), and his teams defend set pieces with the same physical edge he had as a player. With Belgium the plan is to use the 4-2-3-1 that suits [Kevin De Bruyne](/players/kevin-de-bruyne)'s successors, with Jérémy Doku and Leandro Trossard wide and Roméo Lavia and Amadou Onana as the double pivot. The federation's choice signals a preference for structure and discipline after years of debate about the golden generation's lack of it.

## Storyline

Belgium's 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) was, by the standards of recent tournaments, a success. Garcia's side beat [Senegal](/nations/senegal) 3-2 after extra time in the round of 32, having trailed 2-0 until the 86th minute, then thrashed the [United States](/nations/united-states) 4-1 in the round of 16 before losing to eventual champions [Spain](/nations/spain) in the quarter-finals. Garcia left anyway, and the federation moved within a week.

Van Bommel's immediate programme was the 2026-27 [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league), with group matches against [Italy](/nations/italy), [France](/nations/france) and Turkey in September and October 2026, and then the Euro 2028 qualifiers. The squad is in its post-golden-generation phase: Thibaut Courtois is 34, De Bruyne has retired from international football, and the new core is Doku, Lavia, Onana, Zeno Debast and the Club Brugge and Genk graduates. Belgium have never won a major tournament. The federation thinks a hard-nosed Dutchman who knows the Belgian league intimately is the right man to get them closer.
