## Biography

Xavier Hernández Creus was born in Terrassa on 25 January 1980 and spent 17 seasons as the brain of [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona)'s midfield. As a coach he has been a purist: Cruyffian to the core, obsessed with positional structure and the quality of the pass. After a successful apprenticeship at Al Sadd and a title-winning but draining spell as Barcelona coach, he spent two years out of the game before the KNVB stunned everyone on 12 August 2026 by naming him head coach of the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) on a contract to the 2030 World Cup. His player page is at [Xavi](/players/xavi).

## Playing career

Xavi made 767 appearances for Barcelona between 1998 and 2015, winning eight La Liga titles and four Champions Leagues, and became the pivot of Pep Guardiola's 2008-12 team alongside Andrés Iniesta and Sergio Busquets. With [Spain](/nations/spain) he won Euro 2008, the 2010 World Cup and Euro 2012, the player of the tournament in 2008. He finished with four seasons at Al Sadd in Qatar.

Xavi took over a demoralised, bankrupt Barcelona in November 2021 and within 18 months had won La Liga with the best defence in Europe, Marc-André ter Stegen conceding 20 goals in 38 games. He gave [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal), Gavi, Alejandro Balde and [Pau Cubarsí](/players/pau-cubarsi) their debuts and rebuilt the club's identity, but Champions League exits and a public spat over his future ended the spell in 2024, after which Hansi Flick inherited the group that went on to win two straight titles.

## Tactical identity

Xavi is a disciple of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) and the heir to [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka). His Barcelona played a 4-3-3 that became a 3-2-5 in possession, with one full-back inverting into midfield, a classic [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) structure, and genuine wingers hugging the touchline. He asks for a very [high line](/tactics/high-line) and an aggressive counter-press, and his sides are at their best when they can pin opponents in their own half. Critics felt his Barcelona lacked a plan B; admirers point to a 2022-23 defence that was the meanest in Europe. For the Netherlands the match is obvious: the KNVB wanted a return to the positional football that links [Rinus Michels](/managers/rinus-michels), [Johan Cruyff](/managers/johan-cruyff-manager) and [Louis van Gaal](/managers/louis-van-gaal), and no living coach embodies the Barcelona-Ajax axis more fully.

## Storyline

The Netherlands went into the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) under Ronald Koeman and went out in the round of 32, the worst result for a Dutch side at a tournament they had qualified for since 1990. Koeman stepped down, several Dutch candidates were sounded out, and the federation chose instead to reach outside the country for the first time in 48 years. The appointment is a statement about identity: the Dutch want to play the way Xavi was taught to play.

The first tasks are the 2026-27 [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league), with home fixtures against Greece on 13 November and away to [Jürgen Klopp's Germany](/managers/jurgen-klopp) on 16 November 2026, and then qualifying for [Euro 2028](/tournaments/euro). The squad is a good fit for his ideas: Frenkie de Jong is a Barcelona player who understands the structure, Tijjani Reijnders and Xavi Simons are modern interiors, and Virgil van Dijk, 35, anchors a high line for one more cycle. The sceptics will ask whether his Barcelona side's problems travel. The optimists point out that Xavi's teams have always been built around controlling the ball, and the Dutch, more than anyone, know what that looks like.
