## Biography

César Luis Menotti was born in Rosario on 5 November 1938. Tall, long-haired and never without a cigarette, "El Flaco" was the coach of [Argentina](/nations/argentina)'s first [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) win, on home soil in 1978 under the military dictatorship, and the author of the idea that football is a matter of conscience: that there is a left-wing football, of creativity and joy, and a right-wing football, of fear and results. He coached Barcelona, Atlético Madrid, both halves of Buenos Aires and Mexico, and spent his last years as Argentina's director of national teams, watching Lionel Scaloni's side win the 2022 World Cup. He died in Buenos Aires on 5 May 2024.

## Playing career

Menotti was an elegant centre-forward for Rosario Central, Racing, Boca Juniors, the New York Generals and Santos, where he played alongside Pelé in 1968, and Juventus of São Paulo. He won 11 caps for Argentina and retired in 1969, by his own account bored with running.

The Huracán of 1973, with René Houseman and Carlos Babington, was the most admired Argentine club side of its decade and his calling card. His Argentina beat the Netherlands 3-1 in the 1978 final with two goals from Mario Kempes, in a tournament stained by the junta's exploitation of it and by the 6-0 against Peru; Menotti left out the 17-year-old Diego Maradona, then won the 1979 under-20 World Cup with him. At [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) he coached Maradona again, won three cups and lost a famous brawl-ending Copa del Rey final to Athletic in 1984.

## Tactical identity

Menotti's football was a 4-3-3 built on possession, short passing, wingers and a [high line](/tactics/high-line), the Argentine ancestor of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play); he spoke of the ball as the team's property and the pitch as a space to be occupied rather than defended. His 1978 side had a creative midfield of Osvaldo Ardiles and Américo Gallego, Kempes playing from deep, and Daniel Bertoni and Leopoldo Luque in attack, and it pressed with the intensity the home crowd demanded; the [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) was a weapon rather than a philosophy. His real influence was in language: the moral vocabulary with which Argentines, and later Guardiola, Bielsa and Sampaoli, discuss the game is Menotti's.

## Achievements

- World Cup 1978, Argentina's first
- U20 World Cup 1979 with Maradona and Ramón Díaz
- Metropolitano 1973 with Huracán, Clausura 1994 with Independiente
- Three Spanish cups in a single season with Barcelona
- Director of Argentine national teams during the 2021 Copa América and 2022 World Cup wins

## Storyline

Died 2024. The debate between Menottismo and [Bilardismo](/managers/carlos-bilardo), beauty against results, the left against the right, is the organising argument of Argentine football, and the fact that Argentina's 2022 and 2024 titles under [Lionel Scaloni](/managers/lionel-scaloni) were won with a team that could do both was taken, in Argentina, as a reconciliation. Menotti, as director of national teams, was in the building for all of it.

The torch is carried by the Argentine coaching school that has conquered Europe: [Marcelo Bielsa](/managers/marcelo-bielsa), [Mauricio Pochettino](/managers/mauricio-pochettino), Jorge Sampaoli and, via Bielsa, [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), who said Menotti taught him that football could be a moral matter. His books and interviews remain required reading in Rosario, and his cigarettes remain in the photographs.
