## Biography

Giovanni Trapattoni was born in Cusano Milanino, outside Milan, on 17 March 1939. "Il Trap" is the most successful coach in the history of Italian club football, the winner of seven Scudetti and the only man to have won all three of the old UEFA club competitions. He won the league in Italy, Germany, Portugal and Austria, coached [Italy](/nations/italy) at a World Cup and a European Championship, and became a cult figure in Germany for a 1998 press conference in which he denounced his Bayern players in broken German. He retired in 2013 after five years with the Republic of Ireland.

## Playing career

Trapattoni was a defensive midfielder for [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) from 1959 to 1971, winning two Serie A titles and the European Cup in 1963 and 1969 under Nereo Rocco, and famously marked Pelé out of a game in 1963. He won 17 caps for Italy, played at the 1962 World Cup, and finished at Varese.

The Juventus decade, with Dino Zoff, Gaetano Scirea, Marco Tardelli, Paolo Rossi and then Michel Platini and Zbigniew Boniek, produced six titles and the 1985 European Cup won on the night of the Heysel disaster. His Inter of 1988-89, with Lothar Matthäus, Andreas Brehme and Jürgen Klinsmann, set a points record. His Italy went out of the 2002 World Cup to South Korea's golden goal amid refereeing controversy. He won Benfica their first title in 11 years and took Ireland to Euro 2012.

## Tactical identity

Trapattoni refined [catenaccio](/tactics/catenaccio) into the zona mista, the "mixed zone": a libero, Scirea, behind man-markers, with the rest of the team defending zonally, a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and a sharp [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking) led by a playmaker, Platini, and a striker who could hold the ball. He placed great weight on the midfield's work rate and on set pieces, and his teams were famous for winning tight matches 1-0. He was flexible enough to adapt the model in Germany, Portugal and Austria, and conservative enough that his Ireland side was criticised for never varying it. The press conferences, in Italian-German or Italian-English, were a performance in themselves.

## Storyline

Retired. Trapattoni is the direct link between [Nereo Rocco](/managers/nereo-rocco)'s catenaccio and the modern Italian school: his Juventus set the template of organisation that [Marcello Lippi](/managers/marcello-lippi) and then [Antonio Conte](/managers/antonio-conte) continued at the same club. His influence in Germany, where "Ich habe fertig" entered the language, and in Portugal and Austria, where he won titles with limited squads, shows a coach who could win anywhere with the same principles.

The torch is carried by the Italian pragmatists, [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) among their honorary members, and by Juventus itself, where Luciano Spalletti now works in the stadium whose record books still begin with Trapattoni. He remains, in his late eighties, a regular presence on Italian television and at the Bayern reunions where he is adored.
