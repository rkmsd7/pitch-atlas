## Biography

Valeriy Vasylyovych Lobanovskyi was born in Kyiv on 6 January 1939. Trained as a heating engineer, he brought a scientist's mind to football and, with the statistician Anatoliy Zelentsov, built at Dynamo Kyiv the first truly data-driven team in the game. His Dynamo won two Cup Winners' Cups, in 1975 and 1986, and 13 league titles across the Soviet and Ukrainian eras, and his USSR reached the Euro 1988 final. He coached in the Gulf in the 1990s, came home to take Dynamo to a Champions League semi-final in 1999, and died on 13 May 2002 after suffering a stroke on the bench in Zaporizhzhia. Dynamo's stadium bears his name.

## Playing career

Lobanovskyi was a tall, elegant left-winger famous for his curling corner kicks, the "dry leaf", and for a temperament that clashed with coaches. He won the Soviet title with Dynamo Kyiv in 1961, the first club outside Moscow to do so, and played for Chornomorets Odesa and Shakhtar Donetsk before retiring at 29 in 1968. He won two caps for the USSR.

The 1975 Dynamo side, with Oleg Blokhin, the Ballon d'Or winner that year, won the Cup Winners' Cup and beat Bayern Munich in the Super Cup, the first Soviet club to win in Europe. The 1986 team, with Igor Belanov, another Ballon d'Or winner, and Oleksandr Zavarov, thrashed Atlético Madrid 3-0 in Lyon and formed the core of the USSR side that lost the Euro 1988 final to Michels's Netherlands. His last Dynamo team, with Andriy Shevchenko and Serhiy Rebrov, beat Barcelona 4-0 and 3-0 in 1997 and Real Madrid in 1999 before losing the semi-final to Bayern.

## Tactical identity

Lobanovskyi saw football as a system of 22 elements in which the team's energy should be spent collectively. His Dynamo pressed in coordinated [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps), hunting the ball in packs with a [high line](/tactics/high-line) behind, then attacked at speed with universal players who could fill any role, a Soviet cousin of total football. Zelentsov's computer models rated every action and determined training loads; players were tested on reaction times and fitness. He was the first to measure pressing systematically, and the modern counter-pressing of [Ralf Rangnick](/managers/ralf-rangnick), who cites a 1983 friendly against Dynamo as his awakening, and [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) descends directly from Kyiv. The cost was a demanding, impersonal culture that some players found crushing.

## Achievements

- Two Cup Winners' Cups (1975, 1986) and the 1975 UEFA Super Cup
- Eight Soviet titles and five Ukrainian titles with Dynamo Kyiv
- Euro 1988 final and 1976 Olympic bronze with the USSR
- Champions League semi-final 1999
- UEFA Order of Merit; FIFA ranked him among the greatest coaches of the 20th century

## Storyline

Died 2002. The scientific approach that seemed eccentric in 1975 is now the industry: GPS vests, expected goals, pressing intensity, the whole analytics department. Lobanovskyi's Dynamo also founded a school of Ukrainian football that produced Shevchenko, Rebrov and Oleksandr Shovkovskyi as players and Shevchenko and Rebrov as coaches of [Ukraine](/nations/ukraine).

The torch is carried by the pressing tradition: Rangnick, now Austria's coach, Klopp, now Germany's, Marcelo Bielsa, and the Red Bull system, all of which treat the moment of losing the ball as the key moment of the game, exactly as the Colonel did. A statue of him sits outside the Lobanovskyi Dynamo Stadium in Kyiv, on a bench, watching.
