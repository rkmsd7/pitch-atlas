## Biography

Hossam Hassan Hussein was born in Cairo on 10 August 1966, the elder of the Hassan twins by minutes, and spent two decades as the most prolific striker in Egyptian history. As a coach he has been fiery, confrontational and, since taking over [Egypt](/nations/egypt) in February 2024, surprisingly successful: a fourth-place finish at AFCON 2025, qualification for the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) and a run to the round of 16 there. The Egyptian FA confirmed his continuation immediately after the tournament and formalised a contract to 2030 in September 2026. His twin brother Ibrahim serves as team director.

## Playing career

Hassan scored 69 goals in 169 internationals, a record that stood until Mohamed Salah approached it, and won the Africa Cup of Nations in 1986, 1998 and 2006, the last at 39. He played at the 1990 World Cup, Egypt's only appearance between 1934 and 2018, and spent most of his club career at Al Ahly before a controversial switch to Zamalek, with spells at PAOK, Neuchâtel Xamax and Al Ain in between. He won a record number of Egyptian league titles and finished playing in 2008.

He replaced Rui Vitória after the 2023 AFCON exit and was criticised as an emotional choice. Egypt then qualified for the World Cup with games to spare, reached the AFCON semi-finals in Morocco, and in the summer of 2026 won a World Cup match for the first time since 1934, beating New Zealand 3-1 in Group G, before losing to [Argentina](/nations/argentina) in the round of 16. He had been working without a signed contract since February 2026, a detail he made public himself.

## Tactical identity

Hassan's Egypt are built on a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block), the organisation of Al Ahly's defenders, and the transition quality of [Mohamed Salah](/players/mohamed-salah), Omar Marmoush and Mostafa Mohamed. The shape is a 4-2-3-1 that drops into a 4-4-2 without the ball, with a [back three](/tactics/back-three) used against stronger sides. It is [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) football with a strong [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) element: get the ball forward fast, let Salah and Marmoush run. He is also an aggressive, motivational coach who clashes with referees, journalists and occasionally his own federation, and his players have responded to it.

## Achievements

- World Cup 2026: Egypt's first World Cup win in 92 years and a first round-of-16 appearance
- AFCON 2025 fourth place
- Qualified Egypt for a second World Cup in the modern era
- As a player: three AFCON titles, Egypt's record scorer

## Storyline

For a country that had been to only three World Cups, the 2026 campaign was historic. Egypt beat New Zealand, progressed from a 48-team group for the first time, and were eventually beaten by the defending champions Argentina in the round of 16. The board met within days to confirm Hassan and his brother, and in September 2026 the contract to 2030 was signed, with a clause allowing the federation to terminate if Egypt fail to qualify for the Africa Cup of Nations.

Next is [AFCON 2027](/tournaments/afcon) in Kenya, Uganda and Tanzania. Egypt's seven titles are a record, but the last came in 2010, and the Salah generation, with the captain 34 at the time of the tournament, has one more real chance. The 2030 World Cup qualifiers begin in 2027, and the squad's future rests on Marmoush, Ibrahim Adel and the Al Ahly and Pyramids youngsters.
