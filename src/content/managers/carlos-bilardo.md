## Biography

Carlos Salvador Bilardo was born in Buenos Aires on 16 March 1939 and qualified as a gynaecologist while playing for Estudiantes, the club whose cynical, brilliant Libertadores-winning side of the late 1960s made him. As a coach he won the 1986 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) with [Argentina](/nations/argentina), built around [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona) in a 3-5-2 that he claimed to have invented, and reached the 1990 final with a side that most of the world hated. "Bilardismo", the doctrine that only the result matters, is the counterweight to [César Luis Menotti](/managers/cesar-luis-menotti)'s romanticism. He has lived with a neurological condition since 2018, and his family has said he does not know that Maradona died.

## Playing career

Bilardo was a central midfielder for San Lorenzo, Deportivo Español and Estudiantes de La Plata, where under Osvaldo Zubeldía he won the 1967 Metropolitano and three consecutive Copas Libertadores, plus the 1968 Intercontinental Cup against Manchester United in a notoriously violent tie. He was famous for needles, pins and the darker arts. He retired in 1970 to practise medicine and coach.

He took over Argentina after Menotti in 1983, endured years of public hostility and a poor qualifying campaign, and then in Mexico produced the tournament of Maradona's life: the two goals against England, the two against Belgium, and the final won 3-2 against West Germany with Jorge Burruchaga's late goal from Maradona's pass. The 1990 side lost the final 1-0 to the same opponents after a tournament of penalties and fouls. He later signed Maradona for Sevilla and served as his general manager at the 2010 World Cup.

## Tactical identity

Bilardo's 3-5-2 at the 1986 World Cup, with three centre-backs, wing-backs Julio Olarticoechea and Ricardo Giusti, a midfield built to feed Maradona and Jorge Valdano and Burruchaga ahead, is the origin of the modern [back three](/tactics/back-three) with wing-backs; he said he devised it to free Maradona from defensive duty and to cope with the heat. The system defended in a disciplined [low block](/tactics/low-block), marked tightly, and struck on the [counter](/tactics/counter-attacking) through Maradona's carrying. He was obsessive about preparation, video, opponents' set pieces and gamesmanship, and he believed that a team that did not do everything to win was betraying its supporters. Argentina have returned to the 3-5-2 at almost every World Cup since.

## Achievements

- World Cup 1986 and the 1990 final, the only Argentine coach to reach two
- Metropolitano 1982 and Nacional 1983 with Estudiantes
- Libertadores final 1978 with Deportivo Cali
- Credited with popularising the 3-5-2
- As a player: three Libertadores and an Intercontinental Cup

## Storyline

Retired. Bilardismo won. The Argentina that beat France in the 2022 final and lost to Spain in the 2026 final under [Lionel Scaloni](/managers/lionel-scaloni) defended like a Bilardo side and attacked like a Menotti one, and Scaloni, like most Argentine coaches of his generation, has said that the debate is over because both men were right. Argentina's Olympic gold in 2004 under Marcelo Bielsa and the 1986 victory are the two poles the country keeps returning to.

The torch is carried by the pragmatists: Diego Simeone, whose Atlético Madrid is Bilardismo with a Spanish passport, [Antonio Conte](/managers/antonio-conte)'s back-three systems, and the whole modern vogue for wing-backs. "El Doctor" is cared for in Buenos Aires; his players still visit.
