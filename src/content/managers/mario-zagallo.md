## Biography

Mário Jorge Lobo Zagallo was born in Atalaia, Alagoas, on 9 August 1931 and grew up in Rio de Janeiro. He was a World Cup winner as a left-winger in 1958 and 1962 and then, at 38, the coach of [Brazil](/nations/brazil)'s 1970 team, the side of Pelé, Jairzinho, Tostão, Gérson and Rivellino that won every match in Mexico and the Jules Rimet trophy outright. He was Carlos Alberto Parreira's assistant in 1994 and head coach again in 1998, when Brazil lost the final to France. "The Old Wolf", superstitious about the number 13, died in Rio on 5 January 2024, aged 92.

## Playing career

Zagallo was a hard-working, tactically intelligent left-winger for [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo), where he won three Carioca titles, and [Botafogo](/clubs/botafogo), where he played alongside Garrincha and Nílton Santos. He was a starter in the 1958 World Cup final, scoring the fourth goal against Sweden, and again in the 1962 final against Czechoslovakia, as the first winger expected to track back and help his midfield, the beginning of Brazil's 4-3-3. He won 33 caps and retired in 1965.

He was appointed to replace João Saldanha three months before the 1970 World Cup and made the decisive change: playing all five of his number tens together, with Tostão as a withdrawn centre-forward, Pelé and Rivellino as inside-forwards and Jairzinho as a right-sided striker. Brazil scored 19 goals in six games and beat Italy 4-1 in the final with Carlos Alberto's fourth goal. In 1998, with Ronaldo's convulsions before the final, Brazil lost 3-0 to France; Zagallo was sacked. He returned as Parreira's assistant to win in 2002.

## Tactical identity

Zagallo's 1970 Brazil was a 4-2-4 on paper and something much subtler on the pitch: Tostão dropped from the centre-forward position to create space for Pelé and Jairzinho, a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine) 40 years before the term was common; Gérson dictated from deep with his long passing; Rivellino drifted in from the left; and Carlos Alberto overlapped. The structure was positional in the sense that each player knew where the others would be, an early form of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), and the team's transitions were devastating [counter-attacks](/tactics/counter-attacking). Zagallo also brought the European discipline he had learned as a tracking winger: the 1970 side was fitter and more organised than its reputation for pure artistry suggests.

## Achievements

- World Cup 1970 as coach, 1958 and 1962 as a player, 1994 as assistant: four titles in all, a record
- Copa América and Confederations Cup 1997; World Cup final 1998
- The first person to win the World Cup as player and coach
- Carioca titles with Botafogo and Flamengo; a decade of success in the Gulf

## Storyline

Died 2024. Zagallo was Brazilian football's living memory for half a century, and his 1970 team is the measure against which every Brazil side is judged, including the one coached by [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) at the 2026 World Cup, who said before the tournament that 1970 was the standard for the way a team should attack together.

The torch is carried by the tradition of Brazilian coaches who combine flair with order: [Telê Santana](/managers/tele-santana), whose 1982 side was the romantic sequel, Carlos Alberto Parreira and [Luiz Felipe Scolari](/managers/luiz-felipe-scolari), who won the later titles with more pragmatism, and the Brazilian club game that still produces attackers for Europe. The 13 in his superstitions is on his tombstone, and the 1970 film "The Beautiful Team" is his monument.
