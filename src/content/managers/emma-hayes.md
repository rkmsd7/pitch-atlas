## Biography

Emma Carol Hayes was born in Camden, London, on 18 October 1976. She is the most successful club coach in the history of English women's football, the person who turned [Chelsea Women](/clubs/chelsea-women) from a mid-table side into a seven-time champion, and in 2024 became coach of the [United States](/nations/united-states-women), the most demanding job in the women's game. She won Olympic gold within ten weeks of her first training camp. Her contract, signed on appointment, runs through the 2028 [Olympics](/tournaments/olympics-women) in Los Angeles; this page reflects her position as of the 2025-26 season.

## Playing career

Hayes played as a youth for Arsenal but an ankle injury sustained on a skiing trip at 17 ended any hope of a playing career. She moved into coaching in her early twenties, first in England and then in the United States, where she coached college and semi-professional sides and absorbed the American emphasis on athleticism and mentality that she would later blend with European tactical ideas.

At Chelsea she won the first WSL title in 2015, built a squad of Fran Kirby, Sam Kerr, Millie Bright and Lauren James, reached the 2021 [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) final and won five consecutive league titles between 2020 and 2024, the last on goal difference in her final match. US Soccer hired her in November 2023 on a salary that made her the best-paid coach in women's football, and she took charge in May 2024.

## Tactical identity

Hayes is a flexible [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) coach. At Chelsea she moved between a 4-3-3, a 4-2-3-1 and a [back three](/tactics/back-three) depending on the opponent, and became known for in-game changes of shape. She likes a dominant number nine, Kerr at Chelsea and Sophia Wilson or Alyssa Thompson with the US, and fast wide forwards running in behind: her 2024 Olympic side, with Trinity Rodman, Mallory Swanson and Wilson, was a devastating transition team. She presses with [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) rather than all-out chasing, and places enormous weight on squad culture, rest and the psychology of players; she talks about football as a people business first.

## Storyline

The 2024 Olympic final against [Brazil](/nations/brazil-women) in Paris, won 1-0 with a Swanson goal, restored the US to the top of the women's game after a round-of-16 exit at the 2023 World Cup. Since then Hayes has been reshaping the squad: Alex Morgan retired, the goalkeeper and centre-back positions have been renewed, and a group of young attackers, with Rodman, Thompson and Wilson at the heart, has been given the keys. The 2025-26 period was spent in friendlies and the SheBelieves Cup, as CONCACAF's qualification for the 2027 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) in Brazil ran through the Gold Cup cycle.

What comes next is the biggest two-year stretch in the programme's history: a World Cup in Brazil in 2027, where the US have not won since 2019, and a home Olympics in Los Angeles in 2028. Hayes' contract was built around exactly that. The club pipeline has changed too: [NWSL](/leagues/nwsl) champions [Gotham FC](/clubs/gotham-fc) and the expanding league supply more of her squad than Europe does.
