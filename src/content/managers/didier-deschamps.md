## Biography

Didier Claude Deschamps was born in Bayonne on 15 October 1968. Eric Cantona once called him a water-carrier; he carried [France](/nations/france) to a World Cup as captain in 1998 and to another as coach in 2018, becoming the third man after Mário Zagallo and Franz Beckenbauer to win the tournament in both roles. His 14 years as national coach, the longest in French history, ended after the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), where France lost the semi-final to Spain and the third-place match 6-4 to England. He had announced in January 2025 that the tournament would be his last. As of November 2026 he has not taken another job.

## Playing career

Deschamps was a defensive midfielder for Nantes, [Marseille](/clubs/marseille), where he captained the 1993 Champions League winners, [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), where he won the 1996 Champions League and three Serie A titles, Chelsea, with the 2000 FA Cup, and Valencia. He won 103 caps, captained France to the 1998 World Cup and Euro 2000, and retired in 2001.

His [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) side, with Fernando Morientes and Ludovic Giuly, beat Real Madrid and Chelsea on the way to the 2004 Champions League final. He returned Juventus to Serie A at the first attempt and gave Marseille their first title in 18 years. Appointed France coach after the Euro 2012 mutiny, he restored discipline, reached a home final at Euro 2016, won the 2018 World Cup in Russia with [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe), Antoine Griezmann and Paul Pogba, and lost the greatest final ever played to Argentina in 2022 after Mbappé's hat-trick.

## Tactical identity

Deschamps was never a stylist and never pretended to be. His France sat in a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block) or mid-block, defended with two deep midfielders, N'Golo Kanté and Pogba in 2018, Aurélien Tchouaméni and Adrien Rabiot later, and attacked in transition at devastating speed: [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) football with the best counter-attackers in the world. He was comfortable with [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into Olivier Giroud and with long periods without the ball. The shape moved between 4-2-3-1, 4-3-3 and 3-4-1-2 depending on the opponent, and set pieces were a major source of goals. The criticism, that France were less than the sum of their parts, was answered by two finals and a title.

## Storyline

France's 2026 campaign was, by any ordinary measure, a success: a semi-final, Mbappé's Golden Boot with ten goals, a squad that had reached four semi-finals in a row. The exit to the eventual champions [Spain](/nations/spain) and the wild third-place match against [England](/nations/england) were a fitting, chaotic end for a coach whose teams were usually the opposite of chaos. Nine days after the final, [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager), his 1998 teammate, was named his successor.

What comes next is Deschamps's choice. At 58 he has said he does not intend to stop coaching, and his record, a title with Marseille and a Champions League final with Monaco before 14 years of near-constant success with France, makes him a candidate for any club in Europe that wants a winner. No appointment had been verified as of November 2026; his status should be checked against the live data on this site.
