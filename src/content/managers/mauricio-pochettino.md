## Biography

Mauricio Roberto Pochettino Trossero was born in Murphy, Santa Fe, on 2 March 1972, and was discovered as a teenager by [Marcelo Bielsa](/managers/marcelo-bielsa), who turned up at his family's farm at two in the morning. The Bielsa influence never left him. He built the most exciting [Tottenham](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) team in half a century, coached PSG and Chelsea, and in September 2024 became head coach of the [United States](/nations/united-states) for the home 2026 World Cup. After the tournament, US Soccer confirmed an extension through the 2030 World Cup.

## Playing career

Pochettino was a tough centre-back who won two titles with Bielsa's Newell's Old Boys, then spent most of his career at Espanyol, with spells at Paris Saint-Germain and Bordeaux. He won 20 caps for [Argentina](/nations/argentina) and played at the 2002 World Cup, where he conceded the penalty that gave England victory. He retired in 2006.

He saved Espanyol from relegation, made Southampton the Premier League's most intense pressing team, and at Tottenham turned Harry Kane, Dele Alli, Son Heung-min and Christian Eriksen into a side that finished second in 2017 and reached the 2019 Champions League final after the miracle in Amsterdam. He won the league at PSG with Messi, Neymar and Mbappé but was judged on Europe and sacked. At Chelsea he lasted a season. US Soccer hired him in 2024 on the biggest salary in national-team football.

## Tactical identity

Pochettino is a [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) coach with Bielsa's DNA: man-oriented pressing, a very [high line](/tactics/high-line), full-backs as the main source of width and relentless running. His Tottenham played a 4-2-3-1 that became a 3-4-3 for big matches; in possession the ideas are [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) principles with vertical passing to the front line. With the US he has built around Christian Pulisic as a free attacker, Weston McKennie and Tyler Adams in midfield, and Antonee Robinson's overlaps, and his insistence on fitness has been the main cultural change. He is also a demanding, emotional manager who asks for total commitment and has not always got it from club dressing rooms.

## Storyline

The 2026 World Cup was the moment American soccer had built towards for a decade. The US won their group, then beat their round-of-32 opponent for the programme's first knockout victory since 2002, a milestone softened by the fact that the round did not exist before the 48-team expansion. In the round of 16 they lost 4-1 to [Belgium](/nations/belgium), a result that exposed the gap between the US and Europe's top ten. Pochettino's record at that point was 17 wins, 2 draws and 12 defeats in 31 games.

US Soccer had offered him an extension before the tournament; he signed it after, through the 2030 World Cup. He will not coach the under-23 side at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, which goes to Steve Cherundolo. The next tests are the Nations League, the 2027 [Gold Cup](/tournaments/gold-cup) and the question of how to turn a generation of Pulisic, McKennie, Adams, Gio Reyna, Folarin Balogun and Ricardo Pepi into a side that beats, rather than competes with, Europe's best. Results since the World Cup should be checked against the live data on this site.
