## Biography

Marco Rose was born in Leipzig on 11 September 1976, in what was then East Germany, and his family history is unusually rich: his great-grandfather Walter Rose played for Germany in the 1920s. Rose grew up in the city whose football was later reshaped by Red Bull, and his coaching education was almost entirely inside that system. He is personable, direct with the media and known for running training sessions at match intensity.

## Playing career

Rose was a combative defender and defensive midfielder who began at Lokomotive Leipzig and had his best years at Mainz between 2002 and 2010, where his coach for most of that time was [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp). He was part of the Mainz side promoted to the Bundesliga for the first time in 2004. He retired in 2010 with more than 150 Bundesliga and 2. Bundesliga appearances and immediately went into coaching at Mainz's academy.

## Coaching career

After taking charge of Mainz's U23s, Rose moved to Red Bull Salzburg's youth set-up in 2013. His U19 side won the UEFA Youth League in 2017, beating Benfica in the final, and he was promoted to the first team that summer. Salzburg won two Austrian titles under him, playing a ferociously aggressive style, and reached the 2018 [Europa League](/tournaments/europa-league) semi-final, beating [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) and [Lazio](/clubs/lazio) on the way. Erling Haaland, Dominik Szoboszlai and Enock Mwepu all broke through under him.

Borussia Mönchengladbach took him to the Bundesliga in 2019, where he reached the Champions League knockout stage in 2020-21. The announcement that he would join Dortmund the following summer soured his final months, and his one season at Dortmund (2021-22) ended in a second-place finish and a surprising sacking. [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig) hired him in September 2022 and he won the DFB-Pokal that season, but he was dismissed in March 2025 with the club out of the European places. [Bournemouth](/clubs/bournemouth) appointed him in the summer of 2025 as [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola)'s successor-in-waiting took shape, and he is now in his second season on the south coast.

## Tactical identity

Rose is a [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) coach from the Rangnick and Klopp tradition. His Salzburg sides used a 4-4-2 diamond or 4-3-1-2 with no true wingers: width came from full-backs, and the compact central block allowed immediate counter-pressing when the ball was lost. The aim was vertical, [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into the strikers within seconds of winning possession.

At Gladbach and Leipzig he adapted to a 4-2-3-1 or 3-4-3, keeping the [high line](/tactics/high-line) and [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) while giving more structure to possession phases. At Bournemouth, inheriting the athletic, front-foot squad Iraola had built, he has kept the man-oriented pressing the club was known for and added more rehearsed patterns in the final third. The weakness, as with most pressing teams, is space behind the defence against sides that beat the first wave.

## Achievements

- Two Austrian Bundesliga titles and an Austrian Cup with Salzburg
- UEFA Youth League 2017
- Europa League semi-final 2018 with Salzburg
- DFB-Pokal and DFL-Supercup 2023 with Leipzig
- Champions League round of 16 with Gladbach in 2020-21

## Storyline

Rose arrived at Bournemouth with a very specific brief: keep the identity that made the club a top-half side under Iraola while coping with the annual sale of its best players. Dean Huijsen, Milos Kerkez and Illia Zabarnyi all left in 2025, and the pattern of selling defenders to the elite continued. His first season was solid rather than spectacular, but the club's recruitment model, scouting young, athletic players for a pressing system, suits a coach who built his reputation developing exactly those profiles at Salzburg.

As of November 2026 the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) has an unusually German flavour, with Rose joined by [Fabian Hürzeler](/managers/fabian-hurzeler) at [Brighton](/clubs/brighton), [Matthias Jaissle](/managers/matthias-jaissle) at [Newcastle United](/clubs/newcastle-united) and [Daniel Farke](/managers/daniel-farke) at [Leeds United](/clubs/leeds-united). Jaissle, like Rose, came through Salzburg, and their meetings carry a touch of Red Bull family rivalry.

The target is clear: stay comfortably clear of a relegation fight that includes three promoted sides, keep developing sellable talent, and perhaps mount another run at the European places. Rose knows the second season is where his previous jobs became complicated. Keeping the dressing room calm while the club continues to trade is the real test of his Bournemouth tenure.
