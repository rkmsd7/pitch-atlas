## Biography

Ole Werner was born in Preetz, Schleswig-Holstein, on 4 May 1988, and he became a Bundesliga head coach before his 34th birthday. He is understated to the point of anonymity in public, a coach who speaks in plain sentences and avoids the theatre of the touchline, but inside clubs he is known for clear structures and an unusual ability to make modest squads overperform. He belongs to the generation of German coaches who learned the game from the pressing school of the 2010s and then softened it with possession.

## Playing career

Werner was a youth player at Holstein Kiel and played amateur football in the Schleswig-Holstein regional leagues for Preetzer TSV and TSV Plön. He had no professional career and moved into coaching in his early twenties, joining Kiel's academy in 2013.

## Coaching career

He worked his way through Kiel's youth teams and under-23s before being promoted to the first team in September 2019. In 2020-21 he produced the most memorable season in the club's history: a DFB-Pokal run that included a penalty shoot-out win over [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) in January 2021 and a semi-final, and a third-placed finish in the 2. Bundesliga that took Kiel to the promotion play-off, lost to Köln. He resigned in September 2021 after a poor start.

Werder Bremen hired him in November 2021 with the club seventh in the second tier; he took them to second place and promotion in May 2022. Three seasons of comfortable mid-table Bundesliga football followed, with Werder playing a recognisable 3-5-2 and twice flirting with European qualification. He left Bremen in 2025, and [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig), who had sacked Marco Rose and then endured a disappointing 2024-25 season without European football, appointed him to rebuild.

His first season in Leipzig, 2025-26, was one of transition behind [Vincent Kompany](/managers/vincent-kompany)'s champions Bayern. The summer of 2026 brought the sale of [Yan Diomande](/players/yan-diomande) to [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) for around €125m, one of the summer's biggest transfers.

## Tactical identity

Werner's Werder side was built on a [back three](/tactics/back-three) with wing-backs and two strikers, pressing in a 5-3-2 and attacking through quick combinations between the forwards. At Leipzig he was hired to reconnect the club with its Red Bull DNA, the vertical [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) game of Ralf Rangnick and Julian Nagelsmann, and he has alternated between a 3-4-2-1 and the classic Leipzig 4-2-2-2 with narrow attacking midfielders.

His teams set [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) in midfield rather than chasing from the front at all costs, and they are dangerous in [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) transitions, with the strikers encouraged to run the channels early. In possession Werner asks for a patient first phase and a fast second one: build calmly, then play forward quickly once the midfield line is broken. The evolution from Kiel to Leipzig has been one of resources rather than ideas; the principles are consistent, the players are faster.

## Achievements

- Promotion to the Bundesliga with Werder Bremen in 2021-22 after a November appointment.
- DFB-Pokal semi-final and promotion play-off with Holstein Kiel in 2020-21, including a famous win over Bayern.
- Three consecutive Bundesliga survivals with a modest Werder budget.

## Storyline

Leipzig's project under Werner is a reset. The club's identity had blurred through the Rose years, its recruitment had drifted and its 2024-25 season was its worst since promotion. Werner was hired to make Leipzig a pressing team again and to develop the young players the Red Bull network produces. The Diomande sale to Real Madrid in the summer of 2026 was both proof that the model still works and a reminder that Leipzig sell their best talents.

The 2026-27 [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) is defined by Bayern's dominance, [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) having won the 2026 Ballon d'Or on 26 October after a 73-goal season, and by the challenge of [Niko Kovač](/managers/niko-kovac)'s [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) and [Kasper Hjulmand](/managers/kasper-hjulmand)'s [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen). [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), who left Red Bull's global football role to become [Germany](/nations/germany) coach on 24 July 2026, remains a point of reference for everything Leipzig claim to be.

Leipzig's standing as of November 2026 cannot be confirmed here. The storyline is about whether a coach who has never had money can do more with it than his predecessors did.
