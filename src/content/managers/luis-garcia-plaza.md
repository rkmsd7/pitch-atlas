## Biography

Luis García Plaza was born in Madrid on 1 December 1972 and has built a career on jobs nobody else wanted. He has taken three clubs up from the Segunda División and kept several more in La Liga on small budgets, and in March 2026 [Sevilla](/clubs/sevilla), a club that won seven Europa Leagues between 2006 and 2023 but had slid toward the relegation zone, asked him to save their season. He did, and his contract to June 2027 makes him the man charged with the first stage of a rebuild.

## Playing career

García Plaza was a modest midfielder in the Madrid regional leagues and the lower divisions, with clubs such as Alcalá and Fuenlabrada, and he stopped playing in his late twenties. He is one of a generation of Spanish coaches whose formation came through the coaching courses at the Federación rather than through a top-level career.

## Coaching career

He made his name at Levante, winning promotion in 2010 and then, with the smallest budget in the division, keeping them up in 2010-11. Getafe followed (2011-13), with two mid-table finishes, before spells in the Gulf and China that paid well and taught him patience. A short stint at [Villarreal](/clubs/villarreal) in 2018 did not work. Mallorca hired him in 2020 and he took them straight back up to La Liga in 2021 and kept them there. At Alavés he won promotion via the 2023 play-offs and achieved survival in 2023-24 before leaving in 2025.

Sevilla, who had finished 17th in 2024-25 and were 15th and three points above the drop in March 2026, sacked Matías Almeyda after 32 games and appointed García Plaza the next day, 24 March 2026, on a deal to June 2027. His immediate task was survival, which Sevilla achieved, and the summer was spent reshaping a squad that had been assembled piecemeal across several sporting directors.

## Tactical identity

García Plaza is a pragmatist in the Spanish tradition of Javier Clemente rather than Guardiola. His teams defend in a compact 4-4-2 or 4-2-3-1 [low block](/tactics/low-block), work relentlessly to close the half-spaces and rely on [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) speed and [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) for goals. At Mallorca and Alavés his sides were among the best in the division at defending crosses and among the most efficient from dead balls.

He is not dogmatic about possession and will use [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into a target striker when the opponent presses high. The emphasis is on clarity: every player knows his job without the ball, substitutions are made early when a game is drifting, and he communicates bluntly. At Sevilla he has tried to restore the intensity of the Ramón Sánchez-Pizjuán, demanding that the team at least match the crowd's energy, and has leaned on experienced players for stability while younger ones such as Akor Adams and Chidera Ejuke carry the transitions.

## Achievements

- Three promotions to La Liga: Levante (2010), Mallorca (2021) and Alavés (2023).
- Repeated survivals with low-budget squads at Levante, Mallorca and Alavés.
- Kept Sevilla in La Liga after taking over in March 2026 with the club three points above the drop.
- More than 400 matches as a head coach in Spanish professional football.

## Storyline

Sevilla's 2026-27 season is about proving that the decline has bottomed out. The club that was once the model for smart recruitment has spent three years in financial retrenchment, and García Plaza's brief is to make the team competitive with a reduced wage bill. Early-season reports suggested a more organised, harder-to-beat Sevilla, though with the campaign in progress it is too soon to call it a recovery.

The context sharpens the task. Across the city, [Real Betis](/clubs/real-betis) under [Manuel Pellegrini](/managers/manuel-pellegrini) have been in Europe for years, and the derby has become a measure of how far Sevilla have fallen. At the top of [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga), [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) are two-time champions and [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) is back at [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid); Sevilla's ambitions are a calm mid-table season and a cup run. García Plaza's contract to June 2027 means he must earn an extension by spring, and in a club with Sevilla's recent record of changing coaches, that is no formality. His track record suggests patience would be rewarded.
