## Biography

Marcelo Alberto Bielsa Caldera was born in Rosario on 21 July 1955 into a family of lawyers and politicians. He is the most influential coach never to have won a major international tournament, a man whose ideas about pressing, verticality and preparation have been absorbed by [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), [Mauricio Pochettino](/managers/mauricio-pochettino), Jorge Sampaoli and dozens of others. His last job, [Uruguay](/nations/uruguay), ended in June 2026 when he resigned after a group-stage exit at the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup); he said beforehand that "our job ends with the World Cup" and afterwards that he had left nothing good for Uruguayan football. As of November 2026 he is without a club.

## Playing career

Bielsa was a centre-back of limited ability who played a handful of games for Newell's Old Boys and then for Instituto and Argentino de Rosario, retiring at 25 in 1980 to coach. He is fond of saying he failed as a player, which is why he studied the game so hard.

Newell's named their stadium after him. His [Argentina](/nations/argentina) dominated qualifying for 2002 and went out in the group; his Chile were the most exciting team of the 2010 World Cup; his [Athletic Bilbao](/clubs/athletic-bilbao) destroyed Manchester United home and away in 2012. At Leeds he ended a 16-year Premier League exile and was voted the city's hero. Uruguay under him beat Brazil and Argentina in qualifying and reached the Copa América semi-finals in 2024, before a decline in form and a souring of relations with senior players ended in the 2026 group-stage exit.

## Tactical identity

Bielsa is a founding father of modern [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing): his teams press man-to-man across the whole pitch, hold a [high line](/tactics/high-line) and attack vertically with the ball moved forward within two or three passes. His favoured 3-3-1-3, which becomes a 4-1-4-1 against two strikers, uses an enganche, a classic number ten, behind three forwards and asks everyone to run constantly. He pioneered [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps), the rotation of wingers inside, and obsessive video analysis, famously watching dozens of opponent matches before each game. His approach exhausts squads, which is why his cycles are short, and inspires them, which is why his players remain devoted.

## Achievements

- Olympic gold 2004 with Argentina
- Three Argentine league titles (Newell's 1991, 1992; Vélez 1998)
- Championship 2019-20 with Leeds, plus the 2021 FIFA Fair Play award
- Finals of the Copa Libertadores (1992), Copa América (2004), Europa League and Copa del Rey (2012)
- Named by Guardiola as "the best coach in the world"

## Storyline

Uruguay's 2026 World Cup was the low point: a group-stage exit for a nation that had reached the quarter-finals in 2010 and 2018 and arrived with Federico Valverde, Darwin Núñez and Ronald Araújo in their prime. Bielsa took responsibility, said he had been a toxic influence, and left. The federation appointed [Diego Forlán](/managers/diego-forlan-manager) as interim coach in August 2026, until March 2027.

At 71 he may have coached his last team, or he may be two years from the next project; nobody, including Bielsa, seems to know. His legacy is not in his trophy cabinet but in the coaches who cite him, from Guardiola and Pochettino to Sampaoli, Eduardo Berizzo and Gabriel Heinze, and in the idea, now mainstream, that a team's identity should be visible in its first five seconds without the ball.
