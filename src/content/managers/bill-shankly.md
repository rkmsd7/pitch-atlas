## Biography

William Shankly was born in Glenbuck, Ayrshire, on 2 September 1913, one of ten children in a mining village that produced an astonishing number of professional footballers. He arrived at [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) in December 1959 with the club in the Second Division, the training ground a wasteland and the team, he said, "a mess". By the time he retired in 1974 he had won three league titles, two FA Cups and the UEFA Cup, built the Boot Room that would run the club for a quarter of a century, and created the relationship between manager and supporters that still defines Anfield. He died in Liverpool on 29 September 1981.

## Playing career

Shankly was a hard-running right-half for Carlisle United and then Preston North End, where he spent 16 years, won the 1938 FA Cup and played in the 1937 final. He won five caps for Scotland before the war, which took the best years of his career. He retired in 1949 and became Carlisle's manager within weeks.

Shankly's first act at Liverpool was to clear out 24 players and rebuild Melwood; his second was to sign Ron Yeats and Ian St John. Promotion came in 1962, the title in 1964, the first FA Cup in 1965, and the title again in 1966. He dismantled that team in the early 1970s and built a second, with Kevin Keegan, John Toshack, Emlyn Hughes and Ray Clemence, that won the 1973 league and UEFA Cup double and the 1974 FA Cup. He resigned that summer, to general astonishment, and handed over to [Bob Paisley](/managers/bob-paisley).

## Tactical identity

Shankly's football was simple by design: pass and move, keep the ball on the ground, support the man in possession, and work harder than the opponent. The five-a-side games at Melwood, the "sweat box" of boards for rapid passing, and the insistence that every player be comfortable in possession made Liverpool an early British version of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), long before the term existed. The shape was a 4-4-2 or a 4-3-3 that pushed the full-backs forward and held a fairly [high line](/tactics/high-line), with wingers Peter Thompson and Ian Callaghan supplying crosses, so there was [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) too. What mattered more was culture: the Boot Room, where Paisley, Joe Fagan, Reuben Bennett and Ronnie Moran discussed every detail, and the bond with the Kop that Shankly cultivated like a preacher.

## Achievements

- Three First Division titles, two FA Cups, the 1973 UEFA Cup
- Promotion from the Second Division in 1962 after eight years out of the top flight
- Founded the Boot Room, which produced Paisley, Fagan, Kenny Dalglish's staff and Roy Evans
- Manager of the Year 1973; OBE 1974; the Shankly Gates and statue at Anfield

## Storyline

Died 1981. "Some people believe football is a matter of life and death... I can assure you it is much, much more important than that." Shankly's quotations are the scripture of Liverpool Football Club, and his socialism, the idea of everybody working for each other and sharing the rewards, is the explanation the club gives for itself. Paisley, his assistant, won six titles and three European Cups with Shankly's structure; [Kenny Dalglish](/managers/kenny-dalglish-manager), whom Shankly tried to sign, won three more.

The torch today is carried by anyone who takes Anfield's atmosphere seriously. [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) was the most Shankly-like manager Liverpool have had since the man himself, a charismatic outsider who made the crowd part of the team, and he spoke of Shankly often. Andoni Iraola, appointed in June 2026, inherits the standards, the gates and the obligation to play fast, honest, attacking football.
