## Biography

Luiz Felipe Scolari was born in Passo Fundo, Rio Grande do Sul, on 9 November 1948, of Italian descent. "Felipão", Big Phil, is the coach of [Brazil](/nations/brazil)'s fifth [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in 2002, the winner of two Copas Libertadores and an AFC Champions League, and the man who took [Portugal](/nations/portugal) to the Euro 2004 final on home soil. He is also, inescapably, the coach of the 7-1 against Germany in 2014. He finished his career with a string of Brazilian clubs, the last Atlético Mineiro in 2024, and announced his retirement in early 2025; this page reflects his status as of the 2025-26 season.

## Playing career

Scolari was a rugged centre-back in the Rio Grande do Sul leagues for Aimoré, Caxias, Juventude, Novo Hamburgo and CSA between 1966 and 1982, famous for his hardness and his lack of refinement. He was never close to the national team and coached CSA within a year of retiring.

He inherited a Brazil side in danger of missing the 2002 World Cup, dropped Romário, built the "Scolari family" around Ronaldo, Rivaldo and Ronaldinho, and won all seven matches in Japan and South Korea. Portugal under him beat England at two tournaments on penalties, lost the Euro 2004 final to Greece, and finished fourth in 2006. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) sacked him after seven months in 2009. The second Brazil spell brought the 2013 Confederations Cup, a 3-0 over Spain, and then the Belo Horizonte semi-final against Germany in 2014. His late renaissance in China and a 2018 title with [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) restored his reputation at home.

## Tactical identity

Scolari was a pragmatist who favoured a [back three](/tactics/back-three) and wing-backs: the 2002 Brazil played a 3-4-2-1 with Cafu and Roberto Carlos as wing-backs, Gilberto Silva and Kléberson protecting the defence and the "three Rs" given freedom, a system that was defensively solid and lethal in transition, [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) with the best forwards in the world. At Portugal and Chelsea he used a 4-2-3-1 and asked for more possession, with mixed results; his Grêmio and Palmeiras sides were physical, set-piece-strong and comfortable with [direct play](/tactics/direct-play). His gift was for the group: the "família Scolari" of 2002 and the Portuguese squad of 2004 were built on loyalty, discipline and a coach who protected his players in public and terrified them in private.

## Achievements

- World Cup 2002 and Confederations Cup 2013 with Brazil
- Copa Libertadores with Grêmio (1995) and Palmeiras (1999); AFC Champions League 2015
- Brasileirão 1996 and 2018; four Copas do Brasil; three Chinese titles
- Euro 2004 final and 2006 World Cup fourth place with Portugal
- Only coach to win the World Cup and both the Libertadores and the AFC Champions League

## Storyline

Retired as of the 2025-26 season. Scolari's 2002 title is the last World Cup Brazil have won, and every coach since, including [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) at the 2026 tournament, has been measured against it; the 7-1 is the other half of his legacy, the night Brazilian football's self-image broke. He is beloved in Portugal, where the 2004 generation of Cristiano Ronaldo and Deco grew up under him.

The torch is carried by the Brazilian coaching tradition of the three-man defence and the strong dressing room, visible in [Abel Ferreira](/managers/abel-ferreira)'s Palmeiras and [Jorge Jesus](/managers/jorge-jesus)'s Portugal, and by Ronaldinho, Kaká and Cafu as the voices of 2002.
