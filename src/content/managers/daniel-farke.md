## Biography

Daniel Farke was born in Büren, North Rhine-Westphalia, on 30 October 1976. He is dry, methodical and unusually articulate in English, which he learned to a high level before arriving at Norwich in 2017, and he has built a reputation as the Championship's most reliable promotion coach. His stint at [Leeds United](/clubs/leeds-united) has been the longest and most demanding of his career.

## Playing career

Farke was a striker who played almost entirely in Germany's regional leagues, mostly for SV Lippstadt 08, where he was a prolific scorer in the fourth and fifth tiers for over a decade. He retired in 2009 and immediately took over as Lippstadt's manager, combining the job with running the club's commercial side.

## Coaching career

He won two promotions with Lippstadt and in 2015 took charge of [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund)'s reserve team, where his work developing young players caught the attention of Norwich sporting director Stuart Webber. Norwich appointed him in May 2017. After a first season in mid-table, his side won the Championship in 2018-19 playing expansive possession football with Teemu Pukki, Emi Buendía and Max Aarons. Relegation followed with a 21-point season, but Norwich bounced back immediately as champions again in 2020-21. A second relegation in 2021-22 and his sacking in November 2021 ended the relationship.

A move to Krasnodar in Russia was aborted after the invasion of Ukraine, and his one season at Borussia Mönchengladbach (2022-23) produced a tenth-place finish. Leeds, newly relegated and in turmoil, hired him in July 2023. He lost the 2024 play-off final to Southampton, then won the 2024-25 Championship with 100 points, his third title in the division. Leeds stayed up in 2025-26, the first time Farke had kept a promoted side in the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league), and he is now in his fourth season at Elland Road.

## Tactical identity

Farke is a possession coach in the German tradition of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play). The shape is a 4-2-3-1 or 4-3-3, with the full-backs pushing high and one often inverting into midfield as an [inverted full-back](/tactics/inverted-full-backs), and the goalkeeper involved in build-up from the first pass. His Championship sides dominated the ball, moved it quickly through the thirds and relied on wingers isolated against full-backs: Buendía at Norwich, Manor Solomon, Daniel James and Willy Gnonto at Leeds.

The [high line](/tactics/high-line) and insistence on building short have been the criticism in the Premier League, where Norwich twice conceded more than 70 goals. At Leeds he has adjusted: the team still holds the ball but presses with more structure, using [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) rather than chasing, and is far more willing to defend deep in blocks of five in the top flight. The 2025-26 survival season was the most pragmatic of his career.

## Achievements

- Three Championship titles (Norwich 2019, 2021; Leeds 2025), a record shared with few coaches
- 100-point season with Leeds in 2024-25
- Premier League survival with Leeds in 2025-26
- Two Championship Manager of the Season awards

## Storyline

Leeds' return to the Premier League under the 49ers Enterprises ownership was meant to be different from 2020-23. The club spent on experience in the summer of 2025, kept Farke despite doubts about his top-flight record, and were rewarded with a mid-table finish that broke the pattern of his Norwich years. The second season has brought the harder question: can Leeds, with their enormous support and stadium expansion plans, push towards the top half?

The 2026-27 division has three promoted sides, which gives Leeds a cushion, and early reports suggest the club is in a comfortable position. Farke's contract runs to 2027, and the board has been patient in a way his previous employers were not. The German coaching presence in England has become notable, with [Fabian Hürzeler](/managers/fabian-hurzeler), [Marco Rose](/managers/marco-rose) and [Matthias Jaissle](/managers/matthias-jaissle) all in the league, and Farke, the eldest of the group, has been the quietest and most durable.

His story is one of persistence. Twice relegated with Norwich, he was labelled a Championship specialist; Leeds have given him the chance to prove the label wrong. If Elland Road sees a third consecutive top-flight season under him, the question will shift from whether Farke can survive to whether he can build something lasting at a club that has waited 20 years for stability.
