## Biography

Fabian Hürzeler was born in Houston, Texas, on 26 February 1993 to German parents and grew up near Munich. He holds both German and American passports, which the [United States](/nations/united-states) federation has occasionally noted with interest. He took his coaching badges while still playing and became the youngest coach in Germany's fourth tier at 23. When [Brighton](/clubs/brighton) appointed him in June 2024 he was 31, the youngest permanent manager in [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) history.

## Playing career

Hürzeler was a central midfielder in [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich)'s academy, where he captained the U19s and played alongside future internationals, but he never made the first team. He moved to 1860 Munich's reserves and then into the regional leagues with Pipinsried, a village club in Bavaria, where he was still playing when he became player-manager in 2016. He retired as a player in 2020 to concentrate on coaching.

## Coaching career

At Pipinsried he won promotion to the Regionalliga Bayern in 2017, and his reputation brought him to St. Pauli as assistant to Timo Schultz in 2020. He also worked as an assistant with Germany's U18 and U20 sides. When Schultz was sacked in December 2022 with St. Pauli 15th in the 2. Bundesliga, Hürzeler took over and won his first 10 league games, a record for the division. The following season, 2023-24, St. Pauli went up as champions, unbeaten at home, and he was named the league's Coach of the Season.

Brighton, which had already taken a chance on Graham Potter and Roberto De Zerbi, made him their most radical appointment yet. His first season brought an eighth-place finish and the club's best Premier League points total after a record-breaking January window that included Matt O'Riley and Georginio Rutter. In 2025-26 Brighton were again in the top half, and the club's ability to sell at a premium (João Pedro, Simon Adingra) and reinvest continued around him. He is now in his third season at the Amex.

## Tactical identity

Hürzeler is a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) coach with a German pressing education. At St. Pauli he used a 3-4-3 with wing-backs very high and a pivot of two midfielders feeding half-space players. At Brighton, inheriting [De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi)'s squad, he has mostly used a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a [back three](/tactics/back-three) in build-up, with one full-back inverting into midfield and the other pushing high. Brighton attract pressure with short goalkeeper distribution and look for third-man runs through the lines, a direct inheritance from De Zerbi.

The pressing is where he differs: Brighton [gegenpress](/tactics/gegenpressing) in waves after losing the ball and jump to a man-to-man structure in the opposition half. The downside is exposure in transition, and Brighton's goals-against total under him has been high for a top-half side. He is also a frequent and sometimes early substitute-maker, and his touchline intensity has earned several dismissals.

## Achievements

- 2. Bundesliga champion 2023-24, unbeaten at home
- 2. Bundesliga Coach of the Season 2024
- Record 10 consecutive wins on taking over at St. Pauli
- Brighton's best Premier League points total in 2024-25
- Youngest permanent Premier League manager

## Storyline

Brighton's project is often described as the most intelligent in English football, and Hürzeler is its latest proof of concept: hire young, hire brave, sell high. The pattern repeated in 2026, with the club recouping large fees for players they had bought cheaply while promoting academy graduates. The question that follows every Brighton coach, and that followed Potter to [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) and De Zerbi to [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) and now [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur), is when he leaves and for whom.

As of November 2026 Hürzeler is in his third season, which already makes him one of the division's longer-serving coaches given the churn at the top: [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) gone from [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city), [Arne Slot](/managers/arne-slot) gone from [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool), Thomas Frank replaced at Tottenham. He is routinely named as a candidate for the biggest German jobs, and the arrival of [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) as [Germany](/nations/germany) coach has only increased the interest in the next generation of German coaches.

Brighton's ambition remains a return to Europe, which they last reached under De Zerbi in 2023-24. Early reports in 2026-27 suggest the side are once again among the most watchable in the league and once again conceding more than a coach would like. Hürzeler's ability to tighten the back without losing the front is the thing that will decide whether he is still here next November.
