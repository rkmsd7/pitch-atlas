## Biography

Leonardo Jardim, born in Barcelona, Venezuela on 1 August 1974 and raised in Madeira, is the Portuguese coach who built the thrilling [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) team that broke [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain)'s grip on [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1) in 2017 and reached the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history) semi-finals with [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe), Bernardo Silva and Fabinho. After years in the Gulf and a return to Brazil with Cruzeiro, he was appointed by [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo) in March 2026 after the dismissal of Filipe Luís, on a contract to the end of 2027.

## Playing career

Jardim never played professionally. He grew up in Madeira, studied physical education at the University of Madeira and began coaching youth teams in his early twenties, in the same self-taught tradition as [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) and André Villas-Boas. He has often said that not having played gave him an outsider's willingness to question orthodoxy.

## Coaching career

After years in Madeiran football, Jardim led Chaves and then Beira-Mar, winning promotion in 2010 and keeping them up. Braga hired him in 2011 and he took them to third place and the Champions League play-offs. A single season at Olympiacos in 2012-13 delivered the Greek title and a cup final; a single season at [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) in 2013-14 took the Lisbon club from seventh to second.

Monaco, in the summer of 2014, were cutting costs after their first lavish spending phase, and Jardim's genius was to build a young, saleable, winning team. He reached the Champions League quarter-finals in 2015, and in 2016-17 produced one of the great modern French sides: 107 league goals, the title ahead of PSG by eight points, and a Champions League run past [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) and Dortmund before losing to Juventus in the semi-final. The squad was sold piece by piece; he was sacked in October 2018, recalled in January 2019 and sacked again that December.

Spells at [Al Hilal](/clubs/al-hilal), where he won the 2022 Saudi title, Al Rayyan and Al Ain followed before he returned to club football in Brazil with Cruzeiro in 2025. Flamengo, having parted with Filipe Luís 64 days after a contract renewal saga, saw in him a coach with continental pedigree.

## Tactical identity

Jardim's Monaco played a 4-4-2 that was, in truth, a 4-2-2-2 with Bernardo Silva and Thomas Lemar drifting inside from the flanks, Fabinho and Tiémoué Bakayoko as the engine, and Radamel Falcao and Mbappé running the channels. The team were devastating on the [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking), comfortable in a [low block](/tactics/low-block) when necessary, and happy to go [direct](/tactics/direct-play) into Falcao when pressed. Full-backs Benjamin Mendy and Djibril Sidibé provided the width that the inverted wingers vacated.

He is adaptable rather than dogmatic, which has served him in Greece, Saudi Arabia and Brazil. At Flamengo he has used a 4-2-3-1 more often, with the full-backs more conservative and the attack built around quick combinations between the wide forwards and a central striker. Pressing is organised in a mid-block with [traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the touchline rather than a full-pitch press, and the team's set-piece organisation has drawn praise. Brazilian critics have questioned whether the approach is attacking enough for a club of Flamengo's resources.

## Achievements

- Ligue 1 champion with Monaco in 2016-17 and a Champions League semi-final the same season.
- Championships in Greece (2013) and Saudi Arabia (2022).
- Developed Mbappé, Bernardo Silva, Fabinho, Lemar and Bakayoko into elite players.

## Storyline

Flamengo's 2026 has been turbulent even by Rio standards. Filipe Luís, who had renewed his contract in December 2025, was dismissed in early March after a public dispute over the club's direction, and Jardim inherited a squad expected to win the [Brasileirão](/leagues/brasileirao) and the [Copa Libertadores](/tournaments/copa-libertadores) every year. Eliminations from the Libertadores and the Copa do Brasil in the autumn triggered rumours that both he and football director José Boto were on their way out; on 3 November 2026 the club issued a denial, calling reports of a search for a new coach false.

The contract to the end of 2027 offers security on paper, but Brazilian reporting on 31 October was blunt that his permanence "is not guaranteed" and that Boto's situation will also be discussed. The rest of the Brasileirão season, with [Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras) under [Abel Ferreira](/managers/abel-ferreira) the perennial benchmark, will decide whether Jardim gets a full 2027 or becomes another short chapter in Flamengo's coaching history.
