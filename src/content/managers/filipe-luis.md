## Biography

Filipe Luís Kasmirski was born in Jaraguá do Sul, in the south of Brazil, on 9 August 1985, and was for a decade one of the finest left-backs in Europe under [Diego Simeone](/managers/diego-simeone). His second career has been even faster: within two years of retiring he had won the Copa Libertadores and the Brasileirão with [Flamengo](/clubs/flamengo), and in June 2026 [AS Monaco](/clubs/monaco) made him their head coach, the club's third in eight months.

## Playing career

He came through Figueirense, moved to Europe with Ajax and then Deportivo La Coruña, and joined [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) in 2010, where he became a pillar of Simeone's 2014 title side and played in two [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league) finals. A season at [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in 2014-15 brought a [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) title before he returned to Atlético, won the 2018 Europa League and went home to Flamengo in 2019, winning the Libertadores and two Brasileirão titles. He won 44 caps for [Brazil](/nations/brazil), including the 2018 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), and retired in 2023.

## Coaching career

Flamengo put him in charge of the under-17s in 2023 and the under-20s in 2024, where he won the Copa Intercontinental Sub-20. In September 2024 he was promoted to the first team after Tite's exit and won the Copa do Brasil within two months. In 2025 his Flamengo won the Carioca, the Supercopa, the Brasileirão and the Copa Libertadores, a remarkable haul that made him the most sought-after coach in South America. He renewed his contract to December 2027 on 29 December 2025.

The renewal lasted 64 days. After a run of poor results and tension with the football department, Flamengo dismissed him in early March 2026 and later appointed Leonardo Jardim. Monaco, who had sacked Sébastien Pocognoli on 1 June 2026 after eight months, announced Filipe Luís on 7 June 2026, describing it as the beginning of a new era. He is the first Brazilian to manage the club.

## Tactical identity

Filipe Luís plays the game he learned at Ajax and refined under Simeone and Guardiola-influenced coaches: a 4-2-3-1 or 4-3-3 built on [positional play](/tactics/positional-play), with [inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) stepping into midfield, a high defensive line and sustained possession in the opponent's half. His Flamengo averaged the most possession in Brazil and pressed immediately after losing the ball, a South American take on [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing).

He is demanding about structure, insisting that the number ten and the wingers hold specific positions rather than roam, and he is a coach of the [high line](/tactics/high-line), which cost Flamengo goals on the break and was part of the critique that preceded his sacking. At Monaco he inherited a young, athletic squad built by the club's recruitment model and has worked on a slightly more conservative rest defence while keeping the possession principles. His intensity on the touchline is a legacy of Simeone; his ideas owe more to the Dutch school.

## Achievements

- Copa Libertadores 2025 and Brasileirão 2025 with Flamengo, in his first full season as a senior coach.
- Copa do Brasil 2024 within two months of his first-team appointment.
- As a player: La Liga 2014, Premier League 2015, two Europa Leagues, two Libertadores and three Brasileirão titles.
- Appointed AS Monaco head coach in June 2026 at 40.

## Storyline

Monaco's 2026-27 season began with a coach whose stock had risen and fallen within a single year. The principality club had gone from Adi Hütter (sacked November 2025) to Pocognoli (sacked after under eight months) and wanted a long-term identity; Filipe Luís, with his Libertadores pedigree and European playing background, was the ambitious choice. The early months have been about installing a possession game in a league where [PSG](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), five-time consecutive champions under [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique), set an impossibly high bar.

The competition for second place in [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1) is open: [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) under Bruno Génésio, [Lille](/clubs/lille) under Davide Ancelotti, [Lyon](/clubs/lyon) under Paulo Fonseca. Monaco's model depends on developing and selling young players, which suits a coach who built his reputation with Flamengo's academy. As of November 2026 the project is still early, and the Flamengo experience is a reminder of how quickly things can turn; the Monaco board has been patient with coaches historically, and his credibility with players, as a man who did it all on the pitch, is his greatest asset.
