## Biography

Zinedine Zidane was born in Marseille on 23 June 1972 to Algerian Kabyle parents and grew up in the La Castellane estate. As a player he was the most elegant midfielder of his generation; as a coach he has become something rarer, a man who wins the biggest matches without ever appearing to force the issue. On 28 July 2026 the French federation named him head coach of [France](/nations/france), the job he had quietly waited for since leaving [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) in 2021. He replaced [Didier Deschamps](/managers/didier-deschamps), his captain in 1998, who had announced a year earlier that the 2026 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) would be his last tournament.

## Playing career

Zidane rose through Cannes and Bordeaux before Juventus signed him in 1996. He won two Serie A titles in Turin, then moved to Real Madrid in 2001 for a world-record fee and scored the volley that won the 2002 Champions League final at Hampden. For France he scored twice in the 1998 World Cup final, was the best player at Euro 2000, and dragged an ageing side to the 2006 final, which ended with his red card for the head-butt on Marco Materazzi. He won the 1998 Ballon d'Or and three FIFA World Player awards. His own player page is at [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane).

Zidane took over Real Madrid in January 2016 after Rafael Benítez was sacked and won the Champions League in Milan five months later. He then became the first coach to win three European Cups in a row in the modern era, beating Juventus in Cardiff and Liverpool in Kyiv, and squeezed the 2016-17 La Liga title in between. He walked away in May 2018, returned in March 2019 to clean up after Julen Lopetegui and Santiago Solari, and won the 2019-20 title during the pandemic restart with ten wins from eleven. He left again in 2021 and turned down club offers for five years, including, by most accounts, a conversation with Manchester United, because he wanted the France job and did not want to be the man who pushed Deschamps out.

## Tactical identity

Zidane has never been a tactical ideologue, and that is the point. At Madrid he mostly played a 4-3-3 that became a 4-4-2 diamond when [Luka Modrić](/players/luka-modric), Toni Kroos and Casemiro needed Isco as a fourth midfielder, and his teams defended in a compact mid-block before breaking at speed through Cristiano Ronaldo and Gareth Bale. That is why [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) is the closest label, although there was plenty of [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) in the way Kroos and Modrić controlled tempo. His real skills are man-management and game management: rotating a squad without losing stars, substituting early, and keeping a dressing room of egos pulling in one direction. Expect France to keep a four-man defence, a pivot built on Aurélien Tchouaméni, and freedom for [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe), who scored ten goals at the 2026 World Cup to win the Golden Boot.

## Storyline

France arrived at the 2026 World Cup as one of the favourites and left with a strange mixture of pride and regret. Deschamps' side reached the semi-finals, lost to eventual champions [Spain](/nations/spain), then played out a 6-4 third-place match against [England](/nations/england) that will be replayed for years. Mbappé's ten goals were a tournament record for a non-winner. Deschamps stepped down as planned, and Zidane was unveiled nine days after the final.

The brief is clear: win a first European Championship since 2000 at [Euro 2028](/tournaments/euro) in the UK and Ireland. The 2026-27 [Nations League](/tournaments/nations-league) campaign, which started in September 2026, is his testing ground, and the Euro 2028 qualifiers begin in 2027. The questions are generational: how long Antoine Griezmann and Olivier Giroud's successors need, whether Mbappé is handed the captaincy for a full cycle, and how to integrate the midfielders coming out of [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) and the Bundesliga. Zidane has never coached a national team before, but he has never needed long to make a group believe in him. As of November 2026, his France are unbeaten in his first matches in charge, though results after June 2026 should be checked against the live data on this site.
