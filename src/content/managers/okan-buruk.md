## Biography

Okan Buruk, born in Istanbul on 19 October 1973, is a [Galatasaray](/clubs/galatasaray) legend twice over: first as the elegant midfielder in the 2000 UEFA Cup-winning side, then as the coach who returned in 2022 and restored the club to the summit of Turkish football. Under him Galatasaray have won the [Süper Lig](/leagues/super-lig) in 2023, 2024 and 2025, and the club's 2026-27 season records list a further title in 2025-26 alongside a [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) league-phase campaign.

## Playing career

Buruk came through Galatasaray's youth system and was a creative central or right-sided midfielder with a fine passing range. He was part of Fatih Terim's great side that won four straight titles between 1997 and 2000 and beat Arsenal on penalties in the 2000 UEFA Cup final in Copenhagen, then the UEFA Super Cup against Real Madrid. A move to [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) in 2001 brought three seasons in Serie A, after which he played for Beşiktaş and returned to Galatasaray for a final spell. He won 56 caps for [Turkey](/nations/turkey) and was in the squad that finished third at the 2002 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup).

## Coaching career

He began at Elazığspor in 2010 and spent a decade learning in Anatolia: Gaziantepspor, Sivasspor and Akhisarspor, where he won the Turkish Cup in 2018 against Fenerbahçe. His first great achievement came at İstanbul Başakşehir, whom he led to the 2019-20 Süper Lig title, the first in the club's history, with a side built around Edin Višća and Demba Ba.

Galatasaray appointed him in June 2022 after a seventh-place finish that was the club's worst in years. He rebuilt immediately, winning the title in his first season with Mauro Icardi scoring freely, retaining it in 2024 after a ferocious race with Fenerbahçe under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s predecessors, and winning again in 2025, when [Victor Osimhen](/players/victor-osimhen) arrived on loan and the club added the Turkish Cup. The 2025-26 campaign, per the club's season records, ended with another championship, a cup exit in the fifth round and a Champions League league-phase run.

## Tactical identity

Buruk's Galatasaray play a 4-2-3-1 that is pragmatic in structure and lavish in talent. The double pivot, often Lucas Torreira and a ball-carrier, allows the full-backs to push high while the wingers (Barış Alper Yılmaz, Dries Mertens in his twilight, later Leroy Sané) attack the half-spaces. The centre-forward is the focal point: Icardi's movement and finishing, then Osimhen's power, have been the engines of three title wins.

Out of possession the team press in a mid-block and spring [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the flanks; in Europe Buruk is more conservative, inviting pressure and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) through the wingers. He is excellent at managing a star-laden dressing room and at tailoring game plans to opponents, and his sides are dangerous from [set pieces](/tactics/set-pieces) and in [direct](/tactics/direct-play) transitions after regaining the ball deep.

## Achievements

- Four Süper Lig titles with Galatasaray in his first four seasons (the fourth as reported for 2025-26), making him the club's most successful coach since Fatih Terim.
- Başakşehir's first-ever championship in 2020.
- Two Turkish Cups and a Super Cup, plus consistent Champions League qualification.

## Storyline

The 2026-27 season finds Galatasaray at the top of Turkish football and determined to prove it in Europe. The club's investment in established stars has produced domestic dominance but Champions League progress has been harder; the league phase is the benchmark fans want cleared.

Across the Bosphorus, [Fenerbahçe](/clubs/fenerbahce) have lurched from İsmail Kartal's resignation to Dirk Kuyt's interim spell and, on 3 November 2026, the appointment of [Emre Belözoğlu](/managers/emre-belozoglu), a chaotic sequence that included a derby defeat to Galatasaray. Buruk's calm authority, by contrast, has become part of his club's identity. Early-season coverage suggests Galatasaray are again leading the Süper Lig, but with the season only a third gone the usual caution applies. A fifth consecutive title would be unprecedented in the modern Turkish game.
