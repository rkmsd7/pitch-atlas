## Biography

Jorge Maciel is a Portuguese coach who spent a decade as the right-hand man of [Paulo Fonseca](/managers/paulo-fonseca) before stepping into the spotlight at [Lyon](/clubs/lyon) in November 2026. He belongs to the generation of Portuguese coaches who learned the game through sports science and analysis rather than a long playing career, and his reputation inside dressing rooms has always been as the man who turns Fonseca's ideas into detailed, repeatable training sessions. When Fonseca left Lyon for [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) in the first week of November 2026, club records moved Maciel into the head coach's seat, a promotion that early reports describe as continuity rather than revolution.

## Playing career

Maciel did not have a notable professional playing career. Like many of the modern Portuguese coaching school, including [Jose Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) and [Ruben Amorim](/managers/ruben-amorim)'s assistants, he moved into coaching and analysis early, building his craft through academy work and staff roles rather than on the pitch. That background shapes how he works: his sessions are built around video, positional detail and repetition.

## Coaching career

Maciel joined Fonseca's staff at Shakhtar Donetsk in 2016 and was part of three consecutive Ukrainian league and cup doubles, a period in which Shakhtar played some of the most fluent football in eastern Europe and reached the [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league) knockouts. He followed Fonseca to [Roma](/clubs/roma) in 2019, where the team reached a Europa League semi-final, then to [Lille](/clubs/lille) in 2022, where a fourth-place finish in 2023-24 returned the club to the Champions League.

The short, difficult spell at [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) in 2024 ended in December, and in January 2025 the pair arrived at Lyon in the middle of a financial crisis that threatened the club's place in [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1). Maciel ran much of the day-to-day coaching during Fonseca's lengthy touchline ban in 2025, and it was that experience, with players already used to his voice, that made him the natural choice when Lyon needed a coach in a hurry in November 2026.

## Tactical identity

Expect the Lyon of November 2026 to look very much like the Lyon of October 2026. The model is [positional play](/tactics/positional-play): a 4-2-3-1 on paper that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, with one full-back inverting into midfield ([inverted full-backs](/tactics/inverted-full-backs)), wingers held wide to stretch the pitch and a number ten drifting between the lines. Build-up starts short from the goalkeeper and looks to find the free man in midfield before accelerating into the final third.

Without the ball, the team sits in a compact mid-block and uses [pressing traps](/tactics/pressing-traps) on the touchline, stepping up aggressively when the ball goes wide. Set pieces under Fonseca's staff have been organised rather than spectacular, with Maciel credited internally for the detail on defensive corners. The interesting question is whether he adds anything of his own; early reports suggest a slightly more pragmatic rest defence, but it is far too soon to say.

## Achievements

- Part of the staff that won three straight league and cup doubles with Shakhtar Donetsk (2017-19).
- Assistant for Lille's Champions League qualification in 2024.
- Helped keep Lyon in European competition through the 2025 financial crisis.
- Promoted to Lyon head coach in November 2026, as reported.

## Storyline

Maciel inherits a good position and a nervous club. Lyon were third in the live Ligue 1 table in early November 2026, behind [PSG](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) under [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique) and Lille under [Davide Ancelotti](/managers/davide-ancelotti), with [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) under [Bruno Genesio](/managers/bruno-genesio) and [Monaco](/clubs/monaco) under [Filipe Luis](/managers/filipe-luis) close behind. The squad has lost its coach but not its structure, and the board's calculation in promoting from within was that a European campaign and a title race for second are better served by continuity than by a new voice in mid-season.

The pressure is real nonetheless. Lyon's finances remain fragile after the DNCG saga of 2025, and the supporters who watched Fonseca steady the club will judge his successor quickly. Whether Maciel is a caretaker who holds the line until the summer or the start of something longer depends on results between now and the winter break; as of this writing the club had not published the length or status of his contract, so his position should be read as reported rather than settled. What is certain is that few coaches have been better prepared to take over a particular team: he has spent ten years teaching exactly this football to exactly these ideas, and now gets to find out whether he can lead it.
