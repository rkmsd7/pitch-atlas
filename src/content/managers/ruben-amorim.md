## Biography

Rúben Filipe Marques Amorim was born in Lisbon on 27 January 1985 and raised in the city's Benfica-loving suburbs, which makes his later status as a [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) icon one of Portuguese football's richer ironies. Articulate, stubborn and prone to a blunt honesty that can land him in trouble, Amorim is a coach of conviction: he has one system, he believes in it completely, and he has repeatedly said he would rather lose with it than win with somebody else's.

## Playing career

Amorim was a combative central midfielder, a runner and organiser rather than a creator. He came through at Belenenses and joined [Benfica](/clubs/benfica) in 2008, where he spent nine years and won three Primeira Liga titles under Jorge Jesus, often as a utility player capable of covering right-back and the wide midfield roles. He had loan spells at Braga and Al-Wakrah in Qatar, played 14 times for [Portugal](/nations/portugal), and went to the 2010 and 2014 [World Cups](/tournaments/world-cup). Injuries hastened his retirement in 2017 at 32.

## Coaching career

He began at third-tier Casa Pia in 2018, where a dispute over his coaching badges led to a suspension, then moved to Braga's reserves and was promoted to the first team in December 2019. Within six weeks he had beaten Porto, Sporting and Benfica and lifted the Taça da Liga. Sporting paid a Portuguese-record €10m release clause in March 2020 for a coach with 13 senior games.

Lisbon's green half got its money's worth. Amorim's Sporting won the 2020-21 title, their first since 2002, and added a second in 2023-24 with a side built on Viktor Gyökeres's goals and a back three that every club in Europe tried to copy. He left for [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in November 2024.

Old Trafford was a bruising experience. United finished 15th in 2024-25 and lost the Europa League final to Tottenham, and Amorim's insistence on his 3-4-3 with a squad assembled for other shapes became the central argument of the season. Despite summer signings and a better autumn, he was sacked on 13 January 2026 and replaced by [Michael Carrick](/managers/michael-carrick).

[AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) gave him a route back on 16 June 2026. After [Massimiliano Allegri](/managers/massimiliano-allegri) was dismissed in late May for missing [Champions League](/tournaments/champions-league-history) qualification, the Rossoneri cleared out their executive floor too, and Amorim was announced on a long-term deal with Zlatan Ibrahimović reportedly taking a larger role above him.

## Tactical identity

Amorim's football begins with the [back three](/tactics/back-three). The two wide centre-backs are expected to be comfortable carrying the ball into midfield and defending wide spaces one-against-one; the wing-backs supply all the width; the two central midfielders form a double pivot that rotates with the number tens. In possession the shape becomes a 3-2-5 that sits close to [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) orthodoxy, with the two inside forwards operating between the lines rather than hugging touchlines.

Out of possession his teams press high in a 5-2-3, the front three closing passing lanes to the opposition pivot and the wing-backs jumping aggressively, a method with clear [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) roots. The problem at United was less the system than the players' decision-making inside it, and the speed at which he expected them to learn it.

At Milan he has had a pre-season to teach rather than a mid-season emergency, and the squad contains players such as [Rafael Leão](/players/rafael-leao) who are suited to the half-space role on the left. Early reports from Milanello suggest he has been more flexible than in Manchester, occasionally using a 3-5-2 to protect the centre.

## Achievements

- Two Primeira Liga titles with Sporting CP (2020-21, 2023-24), ending a 19-year drought.
- Two Taça da Liga wins with Sporting and one with Braga.
- Portuguese Coach of the Year honours in 2021 and 2024.

## Storyline

Amorim's Milan job is a referendum on the man as much as the method. Serie A has long been a comfortable home for the back three, and the Italian press was far more receptive to his ideas than England's. His task is to return Milan to the Champions League, which they missed in 2025-26, and to close the gap on [Cristian Chivu](/managers/cristian-chivu)'s champions [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) across the city.

The summer brought changes above and around him, with a new CEO structure and Ibrahimović's influence growing, and the pressure from a demanding fanbase is constant. The season's state as of November 2026 cannot be verified here, so the fair description is this: a coach who lost his aura in England trying to prove, in the most tactically literate league in Europe, that the Sporting version of Amorim was the real one.
