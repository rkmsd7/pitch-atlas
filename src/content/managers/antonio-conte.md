## Biography

Antonio Conte was born in Lecce on 31 July 1969. Player and coach, he has been the most intense figure in Italian football for three decades: a captain of Juventus who won everything, then a manager who has won the league title with four different clubs in two countries. His cycles are short and his departures are famous, usually after a public dispute over the transfer budget. He left [Napoli](/clubs/napoli) by mutual agreement in May 2026, a year after winning the Scudetto there, and as of November 2026 is without a club. His own public statement was that he had failed to bring unity to the Napoli environment.

## Playing career

Conte came through Lecce and joined [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) in 1991, playing 419 games in 13 seasons as a box-to-box midfielder and later captain. He won five Serie A titles, the Champions League in 1996, the UEFA Cup in 1993 and reached three more Champions League finals. He won 20 caps for [Italy](/nations/italy), played at the 1994 World Cup and Euro 2000, and retired in 2004.

His Juventus went unbeaten in 2011-12 and won three straight titles with Andrea Pirlo and Arturo Vidal. Italy beat Spain at Euro 2016 with a team he described as having no stars. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) won the 2017 Premier League with a record 30 wins after he switched to a back three in September. [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) ended Juventus's nine-year run in 2021. [Tottenham](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) got Champions League qualification and a bitter exit. Napoli, rebuilt around Scott McTominay and Romelu Lukaku after Kvaratskhelia's sale, won the 2025 title on the final day; the second season brought Champions League football, tension with Aurelio De Laurentiis and an early end.

## Tactical identity

Conte is the modern master of the [back three](/tactics/back-three). His 3-5-2 at Juventus and Inter, and the 3-4-3 at Chelsea, use wing-backs as the primary source of width, two strikers or inside forwards who combine quickly, and a midfield that is drilled to reach pre-rehearsed positions. He is not a possession coach in the Guardiola sense; his teams are vertical, [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) when the opponent commits numbers, and comfortable with [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) into a target striker such as Lukaku. His training is legendarily hard, with "the Conte run" a rite of passage, and his man-management is total commitment or nothing. Every one of his teams has had the best defence in its league within a year.

## Storyline

Conte's departure from Napoli in May 2026 followed the familiar script: a title in year one, friction over recruitment in year two, a March statement that he intended to see out his three-year contract, and a mutual separation within weeks. Reports immediately linked him with the Italy job after the Azzurri's play-off defeat to Bosnia and Herzegovina had cost them a third straight World Cup, but the federation chose [Roberto Mancini](/managers/roberto-mancini) in July 2026, and Conte remains free.

The next chapter is the interesting question. He is 57, has won in Italy and England, and has never taken a job in Spain or Germany. He is one of a handful of coaches guaranteed to make a mid-table club into a title contender inside a season, and one of a handful guaranteed to leave within three. For an Italian federation looking for a winner before Euro 2028, or a Premier League club in crisis in January, he is the obvious phone call. His status after November 2026 should be checked against the live data on this site.
