## The idea

A high line is the defence's contribution to the press. If the forwards press the opponent's centre-backs but the defenders stay deep, the gap between the lines is 50 metres and the press is pointless. If the defenders push up to 35 or 40 metres from their own goal, the whole team plays inside a 30-metre band, the opponent has no room between the lines, and every pass forward risks offside. The pitch shrinks to the size the pressing team wants.

The cost is the space behind. A high line is a bet that the team can press well enough to stop the long ball being played, and that its centre-backs and goalkeeper can deal with it when it is.

## How it works on the pitch

**Positioning.** The back four holds a straight line, often on or above the halfway line when the team has the ball, and 35-40 metres out when defending. The line steps up on the trigger of a backwards pass by the opponent and holds when a forward pass is possible.

**The offside trap.** With the line straight and stepping together, any attacker who runs early is offside. Flick's Barcelona caught opponents offside more than 180 times in 2024-25, by far the most in Europe. The semi-automated offside technology introduced in 2024-25 made the trap more reliable, since marginal calls go the defence's way consistently.

**Cover.** The goalkeeper is a sweeper (Neuer, Alisson, Ederson, Ter Stegen), ready to come 30 metres off his line to clear a through-ball. Centre-backs must be quick in recovery runs (Van Dijk, Cubarsi, Saliba).

**Pressing coordination.** The forwards press to force the long ball early, before the opponent can pick the pass; the midfield screens the passes between the lines; the defenders step up as the ball is played backwards.

**In possession.** The high line is also an attacking tool. With the defence at halfway, the team is already in the opponent's half, and a turnover becomes an instant [counter-press](/tactics/gegenpressing).

```
   Opponent half |     9  11  7        (press)
   -------------- halfway -----------------
                 |   8   6   10        (screen)
                 | 3   5   4   2       (line at ~40 m)
                 |        1            (sweeper, ~20 m off line)
```

## Origins and evolution

[Rinus Michels](/managers/rinus-michels)' Ajax and Netherlands of the early 1970s invented the compact high line as part of [total football](/tactics/total-football), with the offside trap as a weapon. [Arrigo Sacchi](/managers/arrigo-sacchi)'s [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) (1987-1991) made it a science: Baresi, Costacurta, Maldini and Tassotti played a line so high and so synchronised that Sacchi's training sessions used ropes to keep the players connected. Milan won two European Cups and were the model for everything that followed.

The 2010s pressing revival brought it back: [Marcelo Bielsa](/managers/marcelo-bielsa)'s Athletic and Leeds, [Jurgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s Dortmund and Liverpool, where Van Dijk and Alisson allowed a line that would have been reckless with lesser players. [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick)'s Bayern won the 2020 Champions League with the highest line in Europe (and conceded a famous 8-2 win over Barcelona with it), then took the idea to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in 2024 and won two straight La Liga titles with Inigo Martinez and [Pau Cubarsi](/players/pau-cubarsi) stepping up on cue.

## Who does it best

- **[Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick)'s [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona)**: the highest, most aggressive line in world football, which has now won two La Liga titles and caught opponents offside at record rates. Cubarsi, the 2026 World Cup's Best Young Player, is the pivot of it.
- **[Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal)**: Saliba and Gabriel defend a high, well-covered line; the 2025-26 champions conceded fewest.
- **[Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola)'s [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool)**: Iraola's Bournemouth played man-to-man with a line at halfway; he brought it to Anfield in 2026.
- **[Jurgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s [Germany](/nations/germany)**, since July 2026, with Rudiger and Tah.
- **[Marcelo Bielsa](/managers/marcelo-bielsa)'s [Uruguay](/nations/uruguay)**.
- **[Spain](/nations/spain)**: the 2026 world champions pressed from a high line with Cubarsi and Le Normand.

## Weaknesses and counters

The ball over the top. One long pass from a calm centre-back or goalkeeper to a quick striker running from an onside position, and the goalkeeper is in a 50-50. Real Madrid's Vinicius beat Liverpool's line repeatedly; Barcelona's line was torn apart by Inter in the 2025 Champions League semi-final and by Mourinho's Real Madrid in October 2026. Teams that hold a striker on the shoulder of the last defender and play early, diagonal passes are the natural counter. A press that gets beaten with one pass turns the high line into a 3-v-3 race; without quick centre-backs it is suicidal. Referees and VAR decisions on millimetres matter more than for any other system. And it is exhausting: a line that steps up and drops 200 times a game needs concentration from the first minute to the last.
