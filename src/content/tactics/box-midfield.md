## The idea

A box midfield is four central players arranged as a square: two deeper (the "double pivot") and two higher (the tens), roughly ten to fifteen metres apart. It can come from a 4-2-2-2, from a 3-2-2-3 or, most famously, from a 4-3-3 in which a full-back inverts and a centre-back steps up. The point is to dominate the centre of the pitch. With four players in the middle against the opponent's two or three, the team can always find a free man, play through the press, and have a solid base of two when the ball is lost.

The box also simplifies decisions. The two deep players build and screen; the two advanced players receive between the lines and create. The wingers and striker, the front three, provide width and depth. Everyone knows their zone.

## How it works on the pitch

**Build-up.** The two deep box players (at Guardiola's City in 2022-23, Rodri and John Stones, with Stones stepping up from centre-back) offer passing options either side of the opponent's first pressing line. The centre-backs (or a back three) play into them.

**Progression.** The two tens (De Bruyne and Gundogan in that season) sit in the half-spaces between the opponent's midfield and defence. The deep players look for the vertical pass into their feet; the ten who receives turns, and the other makes a run beyond.

**Attacking.** The box is the 2-3-5's engine room: the two deep players stay, the two tens join the front three to make five. Wingers stretch the pitch; the striker (Haaland) pins the centre-backs; the tens arrive late into the box.

**Rest-defence.** On turnover, the two deep box players plus the two centre-backs form a 2-2 or 3-2 base, screening the counter-attack. This is why the box is so stable in transition.

**Pressing.** The tens press the opponent's pivots, the deep players step up into the gaps, and the front three go to the centre-backs. The box is a natural press-trap.

## Origins and evolution

The 4-2-2-2 "magic square" (quadrado magico) was Brazil's shape under Tele Santana in 1982: Falcao and Cerezo deep, Socrates and Zico ahead, with full-backs overlapping. It became the default Brazilian club formation for decades. In Europe, Ralf Rangnick's Red Bull teams (Salzburg, Leipzig) used a 4-2-2-2 with narrow tens as a [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing) machine, and [Julian Nagelsmann](/managers/julian-nagelsmann) added positional subtlety at Hoffenheim and Leipzig. Marcelo Gallardo's River Plate won two Copa Libertadores (2015, 2018) with the shape.

The modern revival belongs to [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola). In early 2023, chasing a treble with [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city), he moved Stones from centre-back into midfield alongside Rodri, behind De Bruyne and Gundogan, with Grealish and Bernardo wide and Haaland central. The "3-box-3" won the Premier League, FA Cup and Champions League. Within a year [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) ([Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal)), [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca) (Leicester, Chelsea) and [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) ([Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen)) were all using versions.

## Who does it best

- **[Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca)'s [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city)**: Maresca learned the box from Guardiola as his assistant in 2022-23 and built Leicester's promotion and Chelsea's 2025 Club World Cup on it. He inherited City in the summer of 2026 and the shape remains.
- **[Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal)**: the 2025-26 champions build with Rice and a stepped-up full-back deep, Odegaard and a second ten ahead.
- **[Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso)'s [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea)**: a 3-2-2-3 with Caicedo and a partner deep and Morgan Rogers as one of the tens.
- **[Julian Nagelsmann](/managers/julian-nagelsmann)** carried the 4-2-2-2 into the Germany job before Jurgen Klopp replaced him after the 2026 World Cup.
- **[Palmeiras](/clubs/palmeiras)** and much of the [Brasileirao](/leagues/brasileirao) still play the Brazilian 4-2-2-2.

## Key numbers

The box gives a team four central passing options against the usual two or three central defenders, which is why City's pass completion in the middle third rose above 92 percent in the spring of 2023 and why their opponents' counter-attacks fell to the lowest in the league. The structure asks the two deep players to cover a lot of lateral ground; Rodri routinely ran further than any City midfielder that season. The 2-2 rest-defence it creates is the main reason modern box teams concede so few goals from transitions.

## Weaknesses and counters

The box concentrates players centrally, so the wings can be isolated: a quick switch to an opponent winger finds only one defender wide. If the opponent plays with three central midfielders and a compact [low block](/tactics/low-block), the central overload disappears and the tens receive with their backs to goal under pressure. The stepped-up centre-back or inverted full-back must be excellent in possession; a weak link in the box is a turnover in the most dangerous zone. And when the box is broken through the middle by a sharp [counter-attack](/tactics/counter-attacking), the two centre-backs are exposed in a huge space, which is what beat City in the 2024 Champions League quarter-final against Real Madrid.
