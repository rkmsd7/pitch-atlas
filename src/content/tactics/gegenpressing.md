## The idea

Gegenpressing, literally "counter-pressing", is built on one observation: the moment a team wins the ball is the moment it is most vulnerable to losing it again. Its players have just sprinted into defensive positions, its shape is stretched, and the player with the ball is usually facing his own goal. [Jurgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) called it the best playmaker in the world. Instead of retreating into shape after losing possession, the team attacks the ball immediately, collectively and with intent, aiming to win it back high up the pitch and shoot within ten seconds.

This is more than a pressing scheme; it is an attacking philosophy. The pass that creates the chance is the opponent's clearance. A team that gegenpresses well does not need patient build-up, because its best chances come from chaos it has created on purpose.

## How it works on the pitch

**The trigger.** The ball is lost. Every player within reach moves towards the ball carrier at once. The nearest closes him down at an angle that blocks the easiest forward pass; the next two cut off the passing lanes to his nearest team-mates; the others squeeze the space behind. The rule at Klopp's Dortmund was five seconds: if the ball is not won in five seconds, drop into the mid-block and reset.

**Variants.** Rangnick's school distinguishes four types. Ball-oriented (Klopp): swarm the ball. Passing-lane oriented (Guardiola): cut the options and let the ball carrier walk into a dead end. Man-oriented (Bielsa): each player takes the nearest opponent. Ball-carrier oriented (Tuchel): isolate the receiver, make his first touch his last.

**The transition.** Winning the ball is only half the job. The first pass after recovery must go forward, usually into the striker or a winger already sprinting into the space the opponent's full-back has vacated. Klopp's Liverpool front three of Mane, Firmino and Salah scored dozens of goals within eight seconds of a turnover.

**The rest.** Gegenpressing is physically brutal, so the team plays in waves. High press for the first twenty minutes, mid-block for stretches, then another surge after half-time. Substitutions are tactical fuel.

```
   Opponent wins ball here:        Our response (5 s):
            x                          x <- 7  <- 11
        o       o                   9 ->  o      o
      o    o   o                   8 -> o   6 -> o <- 10
```

Roles: a tireless striker who starts the press; wingers who press inside-out to keep the ball central; two or three box-to-box midfielders (the "pressing monsters", Milner, Henderson, Kampl, Sabitzer); a [high line](/tactics/high-line) with quick centre-backs; and a sweeper-keeper.

## Origins and evolution

The pressing lineage runs through [Rinus Michels](/managers/rinus-michels)' Ajax and [Arrigo Sacchi](/managers/arrigo-sacchi)'s Milan of 1987-1991, but gegenpressing as a named doctrine belongs to the German school. [Ralf Rangnick](/managers/ralf-rangnick) explained his ideas on German television in 1998 and was mocked as "the football professor"; a decade later he had built Hoffenheim and then the Red Bull network (Salzburg, [RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig)) on exactly those principles: win the ball within eight seconds, shoot within ten.

Klopp made it famous. His [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) won the Bundesliga in 2011 and 2012 and reached the 2013 Champions League final, then his [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) won the 2019 Champions League and the 2019-20 Premier League with a more controlled, possession-aware version. [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel) (Mainz, Dortmund, Chelsea's 2021 Champions League) and [Julian Nagelsmann](/managers/julian-nagelsmann) (Hoffenheim, Leipzig, Bayern, Germany) refined the positional detail; Nagelsmann's 2026 World Cup campaign ended early, and Klopp himself took the Germany job in July 2026.

The idea spread everywhere. By the mid-2020s almost every elite team counter-pressed; the difference was intensity and what happened when it failed.

## Who does it best

- **[Jurgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)**, now with Germany, remains the reference point; his 2018-2020 Liverpool is the textbook.
- **[Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola)'s [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool)**: appointed in June 2026 to replace Arne Slot, Iraola brought the man-oriented, high-risk press that made Bournemouth the most aggressive team in the Premier League, now with Isak and Barcola to run in behind.
- **[RB Leipzig](/clubs/rb-leipzig)** under Ole Werner and the wider Red Bull group keep the Rangnick template alive.
- **[Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi)'s [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur)**: De Zerbi's baiting build-up is designed to provoke a press so that his team can counter-press the counter, a sort of gegenpressing in reverse.
- **[Marcelo Bielsa](/managers/marcelo-bielsa)** with Uruguay still runs the purest man-to-man version.

## Weaknesses and counters

It is exhausting; Klopp's own teams faded in the second half of seasons (Dortmund 2014-15, Liverpool 2020-21, 2022-23). It depends on a high line, so one accurate long ball over the top from a calm goalkeeper or centre-back can break it, the way Real Madrid's Courtois-to-Vinicius route hurt Liverpool in 2022. Teams that are comfortable going long on the first pass, such as [direct-play](/tactics/direct-play) sides, simply refuse to engage. And a possession team that passes faster than the press can shift, Guardiola's City at its best, turns the press into a series of wasted sprints. The counter-counter is positional discipline: a [low block](/tactics/low-block) that does not try to play out gives the gegenpress nothing to feed on.
