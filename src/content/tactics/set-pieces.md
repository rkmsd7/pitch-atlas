## The idea

A set piece is the one moment in football when the attacking team controls everything: where the ball is, who stands where, when it is delivered. Around a quarter of goals come from them, and in tight matches between well-organised teams the proportion rises. For most of football history they were an afterthought, five minutes at the end of training. The modern game treats them as a department, with specialist coaches, video libraries of opponents' zonal schemes, and rehearsed routines for every situation.

The economics are simple. A team that scores 15 set-piece goals a season instead of eight has bought itself a top striker for the price of one coach. [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) made this calculation first and won the 2025-26 Premier League in large part because of it.

## How it works on the pitch

**Corners.** The attacking team chooses between an inswinger (towards goal, for near-post flicks and goalkeeper pressure) and an outswinger (away from goal, for runners attacking from deep). Blockers screen defenders; runners start late and arrive at full speed. Arsenal's signature is the inswinging corner with a cluster of players blocking the goalkeeper's path while Gabriel or Saliba attacks the six-yard line. The defending team chooses zonal (every player guards a space), man-marking (every player tracks a runner) or a hybrid, with a player on the posts or not depending on the coach's philosophy.

**Free-kicks.** Direct shots within 25 metres (the specialists: Messi, Ward-Prowse, Trippier); indirect routines from wide, which are effectively corners from a different angle; and the short, worked free-kick to disrupt a wall.

**Throw-ins.** Long throws (Rory Delap at Stoke, now revived in the Premier League) act as corners. Short throws are a possession tool; Thomas Gronnemark, Liverpool's throw-in coach from 2018, turned a retained-possession rate of 45 percent into 68 percent.

**Penalties.** Preparation includes goalkeeper research, the taker's routine and the rebound structure.

**Defending.** The modern approach is a hybrid: four zonal players on the six-yard line, three or four markers on the opponent's best headers, one at the near post, the rest on the edge for counters.

## Origins and evolution

Set-piece innovation is old: the 1960s Brazilian banana free-kick, Wimbledon's long throws in the 1980s, Pulis's Stoke in 2008-10. Greece won Euro 2004 and Portugal won Euro 2016 with corners. But the professionalisation began around 2016, when clubs like Brentford and Midtjylland, driven by data, hired dedicated coaches. Gianni Vio, the Italian set-piece specialist with 4,830 routines in his notebook, worked for Fiorentina, Tottenham and Italy. Arsenal hired Nicolas Jover from Manchester City in 2021 and became the Premier League's best set-piece team, scoring 20-plus set-piece goals a season by 2024-25 and building their first title in 22 years on them in 2025-26.

By 2026 every elite club had a specialist. Brentford's Keith Andrews, now Brentford's head coach, came from that department. Liverpool, Chelsea and PSG all hired or promoted set-piece coaches, and the Champions League final of 2026, PSG against Arsenal, turned on a Havertz header from a set play before PSG won on penalties.

## Who does it best

- **[Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal)**: the reference. Jover's corners, Saliba and Gabriel's headers, and a defensive record at set pieces to match.
- **[Unai Emery](/managers/unai-emery)'s [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa)**: Emery prepares every dead ball personally and Villa's routines are among the most varied in England.
- **[Antonio Conte](/managers/antonio-conte)'s [Napoli](/clubs/napoli)**: tall back three, rehearsed corners.
- **[Diego Simeone](/managers/diego-simeone)'s [Atletico Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid)**: set pieces as the main attacking source for a defensive team.
- **[Jose Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid)**: Mourinho has always demanded dead-ball excellence and Madrid's aerial power (Rudiger, Tchouameni) suits it.
- **[Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel)'s [England](/nations/england)** scored several set-piece goals at the 2026 World Cup.
- Brentford under Keith Andrews, and newly promoted Hull City, lean on set pieces to survive.

## Weaknesses and counters

A set-piece-dependent team can be neutralised by conceding fewer dead balls: keep the ball, do not foul near the box, play the ball out rather than clearing for corners. Zonal defending with tall players and a goalkeeper who claims crosses removes most of the threat. The attacking team commits six or seven players forward at corners, so a fast counter-attack from a cleared corner (two sprinters stationed at halfway) is the standard punishment; Mourinho's teams have scored many goals this way. Blocking routines invite penalties under tighter VAR interpretation. And a team that relies on set pieces often lacks the open-play creativity to break down a deep block when the dead balls do not come.
