## The idea

For a century the full-back's job was the touchline: defend the winger, then overlap him. The inverted full-back does the opposite. In possession he moves inside, into the space beside the defensive midfielder, and becomes a central player. The team's shape goes from 4-3-3 to 2-3-5 (two centre-backs, a line of three in midfield including the inverted full-back, five attackers) or 3-2-5 if one full-back tucks in beside the centre-backs instead.

Why? Three reasons. First, central overloads: an extra body in midfield helps the team play through the opponent's press. Second, rest-defence: with the full-back already central, there are three players in front of the two centre-backs when the ball is lost, which kills the counter-attack down the channels. Third, it frees the wingers to be the only width, holding the touchline and isolating their marker.

## How it works on the pitch

**Build-up.** The full-back starts in his normal position, then, as the ball goes to the centre-back on his side, jogs inside to sit level with the pivot. The opponent's winger now has a choice: follow him in and open the wing, or stay wide and let the overload happen.

**Progression.** The 2-3-5 gives the team a midfield "W": pivot, two inverted full-backs (or one plus an eight), and two advanced eights in the half-spaces. The ball moves through this W and out to a winger isolated one against one, or into an eight receiving between the lines.

**Attacking phase.** The inverted full-back rarely enters the box. He holds the central position, recycles possession and screens. Occasionally (Zinchenko at Arsenal, Cancelo at City) he pushes up as an extra playmaker.

**Transition.** When the ball is lost, the inverted full-back is already in the middle, so the team can [counter-press](/tactics/gegenpressing) with a solid base behind the ball.

```
   4-3-3 on paper:          2-3-5 in possession:
   11     9     7            11   10   9    8    7
     10   6   8                 3    6    2
   3   5    4   2                 5     4
        1                            1
```

Roles: the inverted full-back needs the passing range and press-resistance of a midfielder (Lahm, Alaba, Cancelo, Zinchenko, Rico Lewis, Trent Alexander-Arnold's hybrid at Liverpool). The pivot must be comfortable sharing space. The wingers must be true one-against-one players.

## Origins and evolution

[Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) arrived at [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) in 2013 and found Philipp Lahm, the best full-back in the world, who was also a superb midfielder. Rather than choose, he did both: Lahm and David Alaba stepped inside in possession, forming a 2-3-5 that let Robben and Ribery stay wide. Bayern won three Bundesligas and the idea came with Guardiola to [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) in 2016, where Fabian Delph and then Joao Cancelo made it central to the 100-point 2017-18 season and after.

Guardiola's disciples adopted it. [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) rebuilt [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) around Zinchenko stepping into midfield from 2022, and later Timber and Calafiori. Jurgen Klopp borrowed it for Trent Alexander-Arnold in 2023. [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca) made it the core of his Leicester and Chelsea teams. By 2024 almost every elite side had a version, and the debate had moved on: full-back into midfield (2-3-5), or full-back into the back three (3-2-5), with John Stones' centre-back-to-midfield move at City being the mirror image.

## Who does it best

- **[Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal)**: the 2025-26 champions build in a 3-2-5 with the left-back inverting next to the pivot and [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka) kept wide on the right.
- **[Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca)'s [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city)**: Guardiola's successor, who has used inverted full-backs since his Parma days.
- **[Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique)'s [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain)**: Nuno Mendes and Hakimi alternate between inverting and overlapping; Hakimi often ends up as a fifth forward.
- **[Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick)'s [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona)**: a full-back steps into midfield to free Pedri higher up.
- **[Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola)** himself, inventor and finest practitioner, now without a club after leaving City in the summer of 2026.

## Weaknesses and counters

The wide spaces behind the inverted full-back are open: a quick switch of play to a winger who has stayed high leaves a centre-back isolated 30 metres from goal. Teams have countered by pinning the full-back with their winger so that he cannot invert without conceding a free man. The role asks a full-back to defend one-against-one on the touchline and then play like a midfielder, and few can do both; City's problems in 2024-25 came partly from inverted full-backs who could no longer recover. And the 2-3-5 is only as good as the two centre-backs behind it, who must defend huge spaces in transition. Teams built on [wing-play](/tactics/wing-play) with overlapping full-backs, and [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) sides who target the channels, are the natural antidote.
