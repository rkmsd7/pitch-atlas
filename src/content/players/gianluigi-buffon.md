## Overview

Gianluigi Buffon played professional football from 1995 to 2023, kept goal for [Italy](/nations/italy) at five [World Cups](/tournaments/world-cup), and won ten [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a) titles, including nine with [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) after following them down to Serie B in 2006. He was runner-up in the 2006 Ballon d'Or, the closest a goalkeeper has come to winning it since Lev Yashin, and holds the record for the longest run without conceding in Serie A, 974 minutes in 2015-16. The one trophy he never won was the Champions League, losing three finals.

## Early life and breakthrough

Buffon was born in Carrara, Tuscany, into a family of athletes: his mother was a discus thrower, his father a weightlifter, his sisters volleyball internationals. He joined Parma's academy at 13 as a midfielder and moved into goal at 14, modelling himself on Cameroon's Thomas N'Kono, after whom he later named his son.

His Serie A debut came at 17 in November 1995 against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan), chosen by Nevio Scala ahead of senior keepers, and he kept a clean sheet against George Weah and Roberto Baggio. By 1999 he had won the UEFA Cup with a Parma side containing [Fabio Cannavaro](/players/fabio-cannavaro), Lilian Thuram and Hernán Crespo, and made his Italy debut in a snowstorm in Moscow in 1997, in a World Cup play-off.

## Peak years

Juventus paid Parma around €52 million in 2001, which remained the world record fee for a goalkeeper for 17 years. He won Serie A in 2002 and 2003 and reached the 2003 Champions League final against Milan, saving two penalties in the shoot-out defeat.

The 2006 World Cup was his summit. Italy conceded twice in seven games, one an own goal and one a penalty, and Buffon's save from [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane)'s extra-time header in the final was decisive. He won the tournament's best goalkeeper award and finished second to Cannavaro in the Ballon d'Or.

Weeks later Juventus were relegated over the Calciopoli scandal. Buffon, Cannavaro's successor as the dressing-room leader, stayed, played a season in Serie B, and was back in Serie A in 2007. From 2012 to 2018 under Antonio Conte and Massimiliano Allegri he captained Juventus to seven straight titles, two Champions League finals (2015 against [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), 2017 against [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid)) and the record unbeaten run. He was in the Euro 2012 and 2016 teams of the tournament.

## How he played

Buffon was a reflex goalkeeper of the old school. His set position was low and wide, his push off the line powerful, and he could reach shots to either side with equal strength, which coaches attributed to the gym work his family background gave him. He was fearless at close range, spreading himself for one-on-ones and taking the ball at the striker's feet.

He was less of a sweeper-keeper than contemporaries such as Manuel Neuer and preferred to stay on his line; his distribution was functional rather than a weapon, and he was not asked to build play even in Juventus's possession years. Crosses were a weakness in his twenties and a strength in his thirties, once he learned to command the box and his centre-backs, Cannavaro, then Giorgio Chiellini and Leonardo Bonucci, were coached to protect him.

His great gift was psychological. He talked constantly, bullied his defence into shape, and was a presence in shoot-outs, saving in the 2003 and 2006 semi-final wins. He also had a short fuse, with a famous rant at the referee after the 2018 Champions League quarter-final at the Bernabéu.

## Evolution

Scala's Parma played a flat back four; Marcello Lippi and Fabio Capello at Juventus asked him for little more than saves. Conte's 3-5-2 pushed the defence higher and required him to sweep more, and in the 2015-17 Allegri sides he was often in a 4-2-3-1 behind a high line, making more interventions outside the box than in his youth.

He left for [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) in 2018 at 40, won [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1), then returned to Juventus as Wojciech Szczęsny's understudy, breaking the Serie A appearance record in July 2020. His last two seasons were in Serie B at Parma, where he had started, and he retired in August 2023 at 45. He is now head of delegation for the Italian national team.

## Legacy

Buffon is the yardstick for Italian goalkeeping, and probably for goalkeeping longevity anywhere. He played 1,100-plus senior matches, held Italy's caps record at 176 until the 2020s, and his Serie A record of 657 appearances still stands. His decision to stay with Juventus in Serie B is told as the definitive act of loyalty in the modern Italian game.

He is a member of the Italian Football Hall of Fame and was named the best goalkeeper of the 21st century by IFFHS. With [Iker Casillas](/players/iker-casillas), [Petr Čech](/players/petr-cech) and [Oliver Kahn](/players/oliver-kahn) he defined a generation of keepers before the sweeper-keeper era, and it is the absence of a Champions League medal, after finals in 2003, 2015 and 2017, that gives his career its one unfinished chapter.
