## Overview

Ousmane Dembélé was, for six years at [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), the most expensive disappointment in football: a €105m signing who could do things nobody else could and did them once a month. At [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), under [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique), he became the best player in the world. The 2024-25 season brought 35 goals, the Champions League and the Ballon d'Or; 2025-26 brought a second Champions League, won on penalties against Arsenal in Budapest, where Dembélé scored PSG's equaliser from the spot. He is 29, captain in spirit if not in name, and the model for what a modern false nine looks like.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Vernon, Normandy, to a Malian mother and a Mauritanian-Senegalese father, Dembélé came through at Évreux and Rennes, where he scored 12 goals in his only Ligue 1 season at 18. [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) paid €15m in 2016 and he was Bundesliga Rookie of the Season under Thomas Tuchel, winning the DFB-Pokal with a stunning semi-final goal against Bayern. Barcelona, needing to replace Neymar, paid €105m plus add-ons in August 2017.

## Peak years

The Barcelona years were a cycle of brilliance and injury. He won three league titles and two cups, scored a famous goal against Chelsea in 2018 and was part of the 2022-23 title side under Xavi, but hamstring injuries kept him to around 30 games a season. He won the 2018 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) with [France](/nations/france) as a squad player.

PSG triggered his €50m release clause in 2023. The first season was good; the second, after Mbappé left, was historic. Luis Enrique moved him from the right wing to a central false-nine role in December 2024 and he scored 33 goals in the next five months, including the goals that beat Liverpool, Aston Villa and Arsenal en route to the 2025 Champions League final, a 5-0 win over Inter. He won the 2025 Ballon d'Or in September, then led PSG to the 2025-26 Ligue 1 title and a retained Champions League.

## How he plays

Dembélé is the rarest thing in football, a player who is genuinely two-footed. He shoots with either foot from either side, which makes him impossible to show one way, and his dribbling is at full sprint, with a bounce in his stride that changes direction without slowing. As a false nine he drops into midfield to receive, turns and drives, or spins in behind when the ball goes wide.

The most important change has been off the ball. Under Luis Enrique he presses relentlessly, leading PSG's counter-press from the front, and his decision-making, once his great flaw, has become sharp: fewer dribbles for their own sake, more early shots and quick passes. The weaknesses are durability (hamstrings again in 2025-26) and aerial presence; he is not a target.

## Evolution

A left-footed-looking right winger at Rennes and Dortmund, a right winger at Barcelona who was constantly injured, a right winger in his first PSG season, and then a false nine from late 2024. For France, Didier Deschamps used him as a right winger alongside Mbappé; [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager), since July 2026, has used him both wide and central.

## Best plays and moments

- 2017, DFB-Pokal semi-final, v Bayern Munich: a curled winner at 19.
- 2018, Champions League, v Chelsea: a goal at Camp Nou in a 3-0 win.
- 2018, World Cup final: a winner's medal in Moscow.
- 2025, Champions League, v Liverpool, Aston Villa and Arsenal: the goals that carried PSG to Munich.
- 2025, Champions League final, PSG 5-0 Inter: the first European Cup in the club's history.
- 2025, Ballon d'Or: named the world's best player ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Dembele+2024+25+season+all+goals+PSG)).
- 2026, Champions League final, v Arsenal: the equalising penalty before the shoot-out win in Budapest ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Dembele+Champions+League+final+2026+Arsenal)).

## Current situation

Dembélé is the leader of Luis Enrique's 2026-27 PSG, now with [Ferran Torres](/players/ferran-torres) as the central striker and Dembélé free to play through the middle or from the right, as the club chases a third straight Champions League. His contract runs to 2028. For France, he is a starter under Zidane in the first season of the post-Deschamps era.
