## Overview

Ryan Gravenberch is one of the best reclamation stories in recent Premier League history. A prodigy at [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) who stalled at [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) and then spent a frustrating first season at [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) under [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), he was handed the No.6 role by Arne Slot in the summer of 2024 and became the foundation of a title-winning midfield. He was named the Premier League's Young Player of the Season for 2024-25, and in 2026-27 he remains the pivot of [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola)'s side and a regular for the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Amsterdam to Surinamese parents, Gravenberch joined Ajax at eight and moved through the academy in the same cohort as several future internationals. He became Ajax's youngest ever Eredivisie player in September 2018, aged 16 years and 130 days, breaking Clarence Seedorf's record. By 2020-21 he was a first-team regular under Erik ten Hag, playing as a No.8 in a 4-3-3, winning two league titles and earning a Netherlands debut in March 2021. He went to Euro 2020 at 19 and was regarded, with Frenkie de Jong, as the future of the Dutch midfield.

## Peak years

Bayern Munich signed him for around €18.5m in 2022, but he started only three Bundesliga games in a season that saw Julian Nagelsmann sacked and Thomas Tuchel arrive. Liverpool paid £34m on deadline day in September 2023; Klopp used him sparingly as a No.8. Slot's decision to play him as the single pivot, after Liverpool failed to sign Martín Zubimendi, was the turning point: he started almost every league game in 2024-25, Liverpool won the title with four games to spare, and his press resistance became the most-discussed detail of the campaign. He kept the role in 2025-26, when Liverpool finished fifth, and was a starter for the Netherlands at the 2026 World Cup.

## How he plays

Gravenberch's game is unusual for a No.6. Where most holding midfielders play one touch and move the ball on, he invites pressure and beats it. He takes the ball on the half-turn, feels the presser on his back and rolls away with a drop of the shoulder, then carries 15 or 20 metres into the space the press has vacated. Opponents who press him often make the game easier for Liverpool.

Defensively he relies on reading and reach. He steps in front of passes rather than tackling from behind, and his long legs let him nick the ball in situations where a smaller player would have to foul. He is good at screening the back four in a deep block and at covering for full-backs who push on. His passing is tidy and progressive rather than spectacular; he rarely plays the 50-yard switch, but he rarely gives the ball away either.

The weaknesses are a modest goal and assist output for a player with his technique, some lapses in concentration when the game is slow, and a tendency to be a fraction late to second balls in frantic games. He is also better with a mobile partner next to him than as the sole protector of a tiring defence.

## Evolution

Ten Hag's Ajax played him as an advanced No.8 who arrived in the box. Bayern tried him in several roles without settling on one. Klopp saw him as a No.8 in a 4-3-3 and a stand-in when Mac Allister or Szoboszlai were absent. Slot reinvented him as the single pivot in a 4-2-3-1, trusting his press resistance to let Liverpool build through the middle, and pairing him with Mac Allister as the more creative partner. Iraola's 2026-27 Liverpool press higher and defend more man-to-man, and Gravenberch now plays in a double pivot with [Dominik Szoboszlai](/players/dominik-szoboszlai), which gives him more licence to carry and relieves him of some of the covering work.

## Current situation

Gravenberch is a first-choice midfielder for Iraola in 2026-27, operating in the double pivot of a 4-2-3-1 alongside Szoboszlai, with [Florian Wirtz](/players/florian-wirtz) ahead of them. His contract was extended during 2025 and runs well beyond the current season. Liverpool have begun the campaign behind champions [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) and [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city), and the balance of the midfield under a new manager is one of the themes of their autumn. For the Netherlands he is now a fixture alongside Frenkie de Jong and Tijjani Reijnders, having played at the 2026 World Cup.
