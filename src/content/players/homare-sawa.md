## Overview

Homare Sawa is the greatest Asian footballer in the women's game and the leader of the [Japan](/nations/japan-women) side that won the 2011 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup), the first senior world title for an Asian nation. She captained the team, scored five goals to win the Golden Boot, took the Golden Ball and became FIFA World Player of the Year, the first Asian to do so. She played six World Cups between 1995 and 2015, a record shared with few, and retired in December 2015 after winning the Empress's Cup in her final match.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Tokyo, Sawa joined NTV Beleza's youth system at 12 and debuted in the L-League at 15, scoring four goals in her first game. She made her Japan debut at 15 in 1993 and played her first World Cup at 16 in 1995. Spells in the American WUSA with Atlanta Beat and later Washington Freedom made her a fixture in the world's best league.

## Peak years

Sawa's prime came remarkably late. At 32, under Norio Sasaki, she drove Japan through the 2011 tournament in Germany, months after the Tōhoku earthquake: a hat-trick against Mexico, a quarter-final win over the hosts, a semi-final win over Sweden, and then, in the final in Frankfurt, a corner-kick flick with the outside of her boot in the 117th minute to equalise at 2-2 against the United States before Japan won on penalties. She also won Asian Games gold in 2010 and Olympic silver in London 2012, and at 36 she was part of the 2015 World Cup final squad, losing to the USA. She won the Asian Cup in 2014.

## How she plays

Sawa was the complete central midfielder. She played deep enough to dictate the tempo with short, quick passes, the [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) that made Nadeshiko Japan famous, and had the stamina to arrive in the box at the end of moves. Her late runs and first-time finishes made her a goalscorer, and her positional sense on set pieces produced the 2011 equaliser. She was neither tall nor fast; she was simply always in the right place, and she led by calm example.

## Evolution

A prodigy forward at 15, a box-to-box midfielder through her twenties in Japan and America, and the deep-lying captain and conscience of the 2011 and 2012 teams. She retired from international football after the 2015 World Cup and from clubs at the end of that year.

## Best plays and moments

- Four goals on her L-League debut at 15 for NTV Beleza, 1993.
- Hat-trick against Mexico, 2011 World Cup group stage.
- 117th-minute equaliser in the 2011 World Cup final against the USA, Frankfurt.
- Golden Ball, Golden Boot and FIFA World Player of the Year, 2011.
- Olympic silver, London 2012, and a sixth World Cup in 2015.

## Legacy / Current situation

Sawa retired in 2015, married and has worked in Japanese television and as an ambassador for the game. Yui Hasegawa and the current Nadeshiko generation grew up watching her, and the 2026 Asian Cup triumph was built on the passing style she embodied.
