## Overview

Robin van Persie's career divides neatly in two: eight years at [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) in which he grew from a gifted, injury-prone winger into the best striker in England, and three at [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in which he won the title in his first season and gave [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) the perfect send-off. In between he produced one of the most replayed goals in [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) history, the diving header against [Spain](/nations/spain) in 2014.

He finished as the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands)' record goalscorer with 50, and his volleying technique is still used in coaching sessions.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Rotterdam to artist parents, Van Persie came through Excelsior's youth ranks before joining Feyenoord. Bert van Marwijk gave him his debut at 18 and he was part of the 2002 UEFA Cup-winning squad. He was precocious, left-footed and difficult: he clashed with Van Marwijk, was dropped to the reserves and left for Arsenal in 2004 for just 2.75 million pounds.

[Arsène Wenger](/managers/arsene-wenger) saw him as the heir to [Dennis Bergkamp](/players/dennis-bergkamp), another Dutchman with impossible technique. Early on he was used wide left or as a second striker, and he scored in the 2005 FA Cup final shootout. The problem was his body: between 2005 and 2010 he never managed a full season, with ankle and knee injuries repeatedly interrupting his development.

## Peak years

The turning point came in 2011. Wenger moved him permanently to centre-forward, [Cesc Fàbregas](/players/cesc-fabregas) left, and Van Persie became the focal point. In 2011-12 he scored 30 [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) goals, 37 in all competitions, and won the Golden Boot and PFA Player of the Year in a side that finished third. A hat-trick at Chelsea and a hooked volley against Everton became the season's signature images.

With his contract running down he refused a new deal and moved to Manchester United for 24 million pounds in August 2012, a transfer that still divides Arsenal supporters. He scored 26 league goals, won a second successive Golden Boot and sealed the title with a hat-trick against [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa), the second goal a volley from Wayne Rooney's long pass that Ferguson called the goal of the season.

Then came Brazil 2014. In the opening game against reigning champions Spain he launched himself at Daley Blind's cross for a header that looped over Iker Casillas, the first of two goals in a 5-1 win. He captained the Dutch to third place.

## How he played

Van Persie's first touch was so soft that the ball seemed to stop on command, and his striking technique on the volley was immaculate: head still, foot over the ball, body shape turned to generate power without lift. He favoured the left half-space, drifting off the centre-back to receive on the half-turn, where he could either slide a pass to a runner or curl a shot into the far corner.

He was an intelligent link player, forming a double act with [Wayne Rooney](/players/wayne-rooney) at United and with Fàbregas and Samir Nasri at Arsenal. In the box he moved late and across the front post, which produced many of his tap-ins.

The weaknesses were durability and, until his late twenties, consistency of temperament. He did not press with great energy and in his United decline he could look static. But at full fitness, from 2011 to 2014, there was no more complete finisher in Europe.

## Evolution

Under Wenger he began as a left winger in a 4-4-2, cutting in to shoot. He became a second striker behind [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry) and Emmanuel Adebayor, then a lone striker in a 4-3-3. Ferguson used him as a pure centre-forward with Rooney dropping off. Under David Moyes and Louis van Gaal he was less mobile and more injured, and in 2015 he moved to [Fenerbahçe](/clubs/fenerbahce), where he won the Turkish Cup, before a farewell season at Feyenoord that ended in a KNVB Cup final in 2018 and retirement in 2019.

## Best plays and moments

- 2014, World Cup group stage v Spain: the flying header, then a second goal in a 5-1 demolition of the champions ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Van+Persie+header+Spain+2014+World+Cup)).
- 2013, Premier League v Aston Villa: the title-clinching hat-trick, including the volley from Rooney's pass.
- 2011, Premier League v Everton: the hooked left-foot volley from Alex Song's lofted pass.
- 2011, Premier League v Chelsea: a hat-trick in a 5-3 win at Stamford Bridge.
- 2006, Premier League v Charlton: the volley from an acute angle, the goal that first showed his technique.
- 2012, Premier League v Southampton: a United hat-trick in a 3-2 win.
- 2013, Champions League v Olympiacos: a hat-trick that turned a 2-0 first-leg deficit.
- 2012, Euro 2012 v Germany: a curled finish from distance.
- 2014, World Cup third-place play-off v Brazil: the opening penalty in a 3-0 win.
- 2018, KNVB Cup final v AZ: a goal in Feyenoord's 3-0 win in his final season.

## Legacy

Van Persie is remembered as the purest striker of the ball in a generation, and as a player whose Arsenal career asked what might have been had he stayed fit sooner. His United season is a rare case of a signing delivering exactly what he was bought for. He has since coached Feyenoord's youth and under-21 sides and, from 2024, Heerenveen as head coach, before returning to Feyenoord as manager in 2025. His son Shaqueel is in the Feyenoord academy.
