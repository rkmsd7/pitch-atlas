## Overview

Andrés Iniesta scored the most important goal in [Spain](/nations/spain)'s history, the 116th-minute winner against the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) in the 2010 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) final, and one of the most important in [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona)'s, the stoppage-time equaliser at [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in 2009 that sent them to the Champions League final. He was the partner to [Xavi](/players/xavi) in the midfield that won everything between 2008 and 2012, a player so admired that opposition crowds, including at the Bernabéu, applauded him. He won 35 trophies with Barcelona and was player of the tournament at Euro 2012.

## Early life and breakthrough

Iniesta grew up in Fuentealbilla, a village of 2,000 in Albacete, and was spotted by Barcelona scouts at a youth tournament in 1996. He moved to La Masia at 12, homesick and crying for weeks, and later said the loneliness shaped his quietness. He captained Barcelona's under-19s to the Nike Cup in 1999, where [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), then the first-team captain, told Xavi: "You will retire me, and this kid will retire us all."

Louis van Gaal gave him his debut in the Champions League against Club Brugge in October 2002, aged 18. He played across midfield and even on the wing under Frank Rijkaard, became a regular in 2005-06 and started the 2006 Champions League final against [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) after coming on at half-time to change the game.

## Peak years

Euro 2008 established him in the Spain team; the 2008-09 treble under Guardiola made him a superstar. His goal at Stamford Bridge in May 2009, a 93rd-minute strike with his weaker foot from the edge of the box, remains the defining moment of that era, and he had a hand in the Rome final win over [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united).

The 2010 World Cup was played through a difficult year: his close friend Daniel Jarque, the Espanyol captain, had died suddenly in August 2009, and Iniesta suffered a depression he later described openly. In the final he controlled Fàbregas's pass and volleyed low past Maarten Stekelenburg, then pulled off his shirt to reveal the message "Dani Jarque, always with us". He was man of the match in the final and later assisted David Villa's winner against Portugal in the last 16.

In 2011 he was part of the Wembley final masterclass against United; in 2012 he was named player of the tournament at the Euro without scoring, a recognition of how completely he controlled games. He was Champions League winner again in 2015 under Luis Enrique and captained Barcelona for his final three seasons before leaving in 2018 after a Clásico farewell at the Camp Nou.

## How he played

Iniesta's first touch was his escape mechanism. Where Xavi took the ball on the half-turn to pass, Iniesta took it to move: a touch with the outside of the right foot that carried him past the first presser, often followed by la croqueta, the rapid shift of the ball from right foot to left in one stride that Michael Laudrup had used before him. He played with his head up and his centre of gravity low, and his balance meant he was fouled constantly but rarely dispossessed.

He was not a scorer, averaging a goal every 12 games, but his goals arrived in finals and semi-finals. His passing was short and precise, with a gift for the pass into the half-space for [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) or a full-back overlapping. He had more pace than Xavi and drifted to the left touchline to create overloads with Dani Alves on the other side.

Weaknesses: he was slight and picked up muscle injuries throughout his twenties, missing large parts of 2009-10 and 2010-11, and he was not a defender in any traditional sense. Some critics argued his understated style meant he was never seriously in Ballon d'Or contention; he finished second in 2010, behind Messi, which many felt was unjust.

## Evolution

Rijkaard used him on the right wing, as a number 8 and even as a left winger, which gave him unusual versatility. Guardiola fixed him as the left-sided interior in a 4-3-3 with Xavi on the right and [Sergio Busquets](/players/sergio-busquets) behind, occasionally pushing him into a false winger role when Messi dropped deep.

Under Luis Enrique he became more of a controller as the team's attack moved to Messi, [Luis Suárez](/players/luis-suarez) and [Neymar](/players/neymar), and his minutes were managed. For Spain under Vicente del Bosque he moved higher, often playing as a left-sided attacking midfielder or even a false nine in 2012. At Vissel Kobe in the [J1 League](/leagues/j1-league) from 2018 he was the full creative hub, winning the Emperor's Cup in 2019, before a final season in the UAE and retirement in October 2024.

## Legacy

Iniesta is the proof that a midfielder can be universally loved. Real Madrid supporters gave him an ovation at the Bernabéu in 2018; Spanish rivals named stands and awards after him. He was runner-up in the 2010 Ballon d'Or and Barcelona's second-most capped player after Messi, and the Iniesta-Xavi-Busquets midfield is the template every possession team since has tried to reproduce.

He retired in October 2024 at 40 with 131 caps and 35 Barcelona trophies. Players like [Pedri](/players/pedri), who inherited his profile in the Barcelona and Spain midfield, are routinely compared with him, and Spain's 2026 World Cup winners built their midfield on the same ideas of press resistance and control. His legacy is the croqueta, the Stamford Bridge volley and the goal that made Spain world champions.
