## Overview

Zlatan Ibrahimović won 13 league titles with seven clubs across the Netherlands, Italy, Spain and France, scored 62 goals for [Sweden](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Zlatan+Sweden+goals) and was still a Serie A champion with [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) at 40. The one thing missing from his record, a Champions League, is the one thing he is asked about most. He is also the most quotable footballer of his era, a self-mythologiser whose "I am Zlatan" persona was built on a genuinely strange and brilliant technical gift.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Malmö in October 1981 to a Bosnian father and Croatian mother, Ibrahimović grew up in the Rosengård estate, took taekwondo as a child and played for Malmö FF's youth sides, where his individualism exasperated coaches. He made his first-team debut in 1999, scored 12 goals in his first full season, and in 2001 [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) paid a Swedish record fee. Under Ronald Koeman he won two Eredivisie titles and produced the goal that first made him famous: a slaloming run past six NAC Breda players in August 2004.

## Peak years

[Juventus](/clubs/juventus) signed him days later. Fabio Capello made him work, play with his back to goal and stop showboating, and he was Serie A's Foreign Footballer of the Year in 2005. After the Calciopoli scandal he moved to [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan), where he won three straight titles under Roberto Mancini and [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) and was top scorer in 2008-09 with 25.

The move to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in 2009, with [Samuel Eto'o](/players/samuel-etoo) going the other way, brought a La Liga title but ended in a famous feud with [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), who moved [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) into the centre and left Ibrahimović stranded on the wing. A year at Milan brought another Scudetto in 2011, then four seasons at [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) made him the club's record scorer at the time with 156 goals, including 38 league goals in 2015-16. At [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united), aged 35, he scored 28 in his first season and both winners in the League Cup final.

## How he played

Ibrahimović had the touch of a small player in a very large body. He would receive long passes on his chest or thigh, kill them dead, and then either turn and strike or play a one-touch combination with a runner. His striking technique was unorthodox: he hit volleys and half-volleys from impossible body positions, drawing on taekwondo flexibility, and his 30-yard overhead kick against [England](/nations/england) in 2012 is a goal few others could even attempt.

He was also a genuine creator. At Inter and PSG he dropped into the number ten space to play through balls, and his assist numbers at PSG were as striking as his goals. His weaknesses were pace, which he never had, and a reluctance to press that made him difficult to fit into high-intensity systems, as Guardiola found. His temper produced long suspensions, and he could be a divisive presence in dressing rooms, though team-mates at PSG, United and Milan describe him as a demanding, protective leader.

## Evolution

He began as a dribbling, individualist forward at Malmö and Ajax. Capello turned him into a penalty-box striker, Mancini and Mourinho into a target man and leader, and at PSG under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) and Laurent Blanc he became a complete number nine who also created. A serious knee injury at United in April 2017 at 35 looked like the end, but after two seasons in MLS with [LA Galaxy](/clubs/la-galaxy), where he scored 53 goals in 58 games, he returned to Milan in January 2020 at 38 as a mentor-striker and dragged a young side to the 2022 Scudetto. He retired in June 2023 and returned to Milan in a senior advisory role.

## Legacy

Ibrahimović's longevity, 24 seasons across five major leagues, and his range of acrobatic goals make him one of the most watched strikers in football history. He never won the Champions League, and that gap is the counterpoint to his claims of greatness, but the trophies in every other competition and the Swedish records, 62 international goals and a record 12 Guldbollen awards, are not in dispute. He was a prototype for the modern complete striker who creates as much as finishes, an influence visible in [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) and [Erling Haaland](/players/erling-haaland), and a reminder that charisma can be a footballing weapon.
