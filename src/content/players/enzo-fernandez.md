## Overview

Enzo Fernández has lived several careers in five years. He was River Plate's rising midfielder in 2021, Benfica's star in the autumn of 2022, the Young Player of the Tournament as [Argentina](/nations/argentina) won the [2022 World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), a British-record signing for [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in January 2023 and their captain by 2025. On 1 September 2026 he became a joint British-record signing again, joining [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) for £125m to reunite with [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca). In between came the lowest point: a red card in the 2026 World Cup final as Argentina lost 1-0 to Spain.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in San Martín, Buenos Aires, Fernández came through River Plate's academy under Marcelo Gallardo. A loan to Defensa y Justicia in 2020-21 made him: he won the Copa Sudamericana and Recopa there, and returned to River as a starter, scoring freely from midfield in 2021 and winning the league. Benfica paid around €10m in July 2022 and within three months he had become one of the most talked-about midfielders in Europe, running Benfica's Champions League campaign against PSG and Juventus. Argentina called him up in September 2022; by December he was a World Cup winner, having scored a curling goal against Mexico in the group stage and started every knockout game.

## Peak years

Chelsea paid £106.8m on deadline day in January 2023. The first eighteen months were turbulent at a club changing managers every season, but under Maresca in 2024-25 he found a settled role on the left of a double pivot with [Moisés Caicedo](/players/moises-caicedo), was given the captaincy, and helped Chelsea win the Conference League and the 2025 Club World Cup in the United States, where they beat PSG in the final. In 2025-26 he was again Chelsea's creative hub as the club went through Maresca's departure and Liam Rosenior's interim spell. With Argentina he won the 2024 Copa América and reached the 2026 World Cup final, where his dismissal left Lionel Scaloni's side a man short in extra time.

## How he plays

Fernández wants the ball from the first pass. He drops to the left of the centre-backs or into the left half-space, takes the ball on the half-turn and immediately looks for the far side: his cross-field diagonals to the right winger are a signature, as are the driven, clipped passes between the lines for runners. He sees the pass before the ball reaches him, and his range with the right foot is as good as any midfielder in the league.

He is also a runner. He covers ground relentlessly, presses the opposition's first pass and arrives at the edge of the box to score, often with a half-volley from a cleared corner. He takes set pieces. Defensively he is competitive and sometimes rash: his tackling is committed rather than precise, and he can be pulled out of position when he chases the ball. His decision-making under pressure, playing a riskier pass when a simple one would do, has occasionally cost his teams, and the red card in New Jersey in July 2026 will follow him.

## Evolution

At River he was a box-to-box No.8 who scored goals. Benfica and Argentina moved him into a deeper role, a No.6 or left-sided pivot, where his passing could set the tempo. Mauricio Pochettino at Chelsea tried him as a No.10 and as the deepest midfielder; Maresca settled him on the left of a 4-2-3-1 double pivot that becomes a 3-2 box in possession, with Caicedo doing more of the destroying. At City in 2026-27 he plays the same role in a very similar structure, effectively inheriting the tempo-setting job that [Rodri](/players/rodri) performed before his August 2026 move to Barcelona, which is exactly why Maresca wanted him.

## Best plays and moments

- 2021, Argentine Primera with River Plate: six goals in the title-winning campaign from midfield.
- 2022, Champions League v Juventus: runs the game in Benfica's 4-3 win in Turin.
- 2022, World Cup group stage v Mexico: a curling second goal that settled Argentina's nerves ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Enzo+Fernandez+goal+Mexico+World+Cup+2022)).
- 2022, World Cup final v France: starts and plays 120 minutes as Argentina win on penalties.
- 2024, Copa América final v Colombia: a second international title in the United States.
- 2025, Club World Cup final v PSG: part of the Chelsea side that beat the European champions ([Chelsea highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Enzo+Fernandez+Chelsea+goals+assists)).
- 2025, Conference League final v Real Betis: captain as Chelsea come from behind to win 4-1.
- 2026, World Cup final v Spain, MetLife Stadium: sent off as Argentina lose 1-0 after extra time.
- 2026, deadline day: a £125m move to Manchester City, matching the Isak fee as the joint British record ([City debut](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Enzo+Fernandez+Manchester+City+debut+2026)).

## Current situation

Fernández arrived at the Etihad on 1 September 2026 as the headline act of a summer in which City spent around £458m to rebuild after [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola)'s exit. Maresca knows exactly how to use him, and City sat second after nine games with the league's most productive attack. He wears No.17 at City. For Argentina, whose manager Scaloni is contracted only to December 2026, he remains a starter despite the final in July.
