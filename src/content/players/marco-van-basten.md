## Overview

Marco van Basten scored 276 goals in 373 club games, won three Ballons d'Or and two European Cups, and was the centre-forward of the two greatest teams of the late 1980s: the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) of Euro 88 and Arrigo Sacchi's [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan). He played his last match at 28, in the 1993 European Cup final, before ankle surgery failed and he retired at 30. His volley against the USSR in the Euro 88 final is still the most replayed goal in the competition's history. Many who saw him, including [Johan Cruyff](/players/johan-cruyff), considered him the best striker ever.

## Early life and breakthrough

Van Basten was born in Utrecht and joined [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) at 16 in 1981. He made his debut in April 1982 as a substitute for Cruyff himself and scored within minutes. When Cruyff left for Feyenoord in 1983, Van Basten became the focal point: he was Eredivisie top scorer four years running from 1984 to 1987, with 37 goals in 1985-86 winning the European Golden Boot, and 128 league goals in 133 games overall. In 1986 he scored a bicycle kick against Den Bosch that is still shown to Dutch children as the model for the technique. Under Cruyff as coach he won the 1987 Cup Winners' Cup, scoring the only goal of the final against Lokomotive Leipzig.

Silvio Berlusconi's Milan signed him in 1987 alongside [Ruud Gullit](/players/ruud-gullit). His first Serie A season was wrecked by the ankle injury that would eventually end his career; he played 11 league games but came back for the run-in as Milan won the Scudetto.

## Peak years

Euro 88 was the summer he became a global figure. Rinus Michels started him on the bench for the opening defeat to the USSR, then unleashed him: a goal against [England](/nations/england) became a hat-trick, an 88th-minute winner against West [Germany](/nations/germany) in Hamburg avenged 1974, and in the final against the USSR he met Arnold Mühren's cross with a volley from a near-impossible angle on the right of the box, which flew over Rinat Dasayev. The Netherlands won their first major trophy. Van Basten won his first Ballon d'Or.

At Milan he was the spearhead of Sacchi's pressing 4-4-2 with Gullit, [Frank Rijkaard](/players/frank-rijkaard), [Franco Baresi](/players/franco-baresi) and [Paolo Maldini](/players/paolo-maldini). He scored twice in the 4-0 European Cup final win over Steaua Bucharest in 1989, won the trophy again in 1990 against [Benfica](/clubs/benfica), and claimed two Intercontinental Cups. Under Fabio Capello from 1991 he was even more prolific: 25 Serie A goals in 1991-92 as Milan went unbeaten, four in one game against Göteborg in the 1992-93 Champions League, and a third Ballon d'Or plus the inaugural FIFA World Player of the Year.

## How he played

Van Basten was the complete number 9 before the phrase became a cliché. He was 1.88 m with a long stride and a ballerina's balance; his first touch, even from awkward heights, took the ball into his path. He could score with his head, with volleys, with placed finishes from the edge of the box and with outrageous improvisations like the bicycle kick. His signature was the volley on the move: hips open, eyes on the ball, contact timed so cleanly that the shot looked effortless.

Tactically he was ideal for Sacchi's [high line](/tactics/high-line) because he timed runs behind the last defender with precision and could hold the ball when Milan played direct. He pressed from the front, which was mandatory in that system. Weaknesses: the ankle, from 1987 onwards, limited his explosiveness, and he was not a natural dribbler in tight spaces, preferring to move the ball on. In today's game he would be the perfect striker for a team that mixes possession and transition, the sort of forward [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) is for Bayern but with more goals from open play and less deep involvement.

## Evolution

At Ajax he was a goal-hungry finisher who scored almost a goal a game in a possession-based Dutch system where the ball arrived constantly. At Milan he adapted to a far more physical league and to Sacchi's demands, pressing, running the channels and playing for the team while still topping the scoring charts. Under Capello he became more clinical still, dropping less and finishing more.

The game around him was changing from the libero-based man-marking of early 1980s Italy to the zonal pressing that Sacchi's Milan made standard, a revolution he embodied as the striker who ran defences ragged. His career ended abruptly. After the 1992-93 season, in which he played the Champions League final defeat to [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) half-fit, a third ankle operation failed. He never played again and announced his retirement in August 1995 after two seasons of rehabilitation. He later coached Ajax, the Netherlands at the 2006 World Cup and Euro 2008, Heerenveen and AZ, and worked for FIFA on the rules of the game.

## Legacy

Van Basten set the template for the complete modern centre-forward. [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry), [Zlatan Ibrahimović](/players/zlatan-ibrahimovic), [Ruud van Nistelrooy](/players/ruud-van-nistelrooy) and [Robin van Persie](/players/robin-van-persie) all cited him; Van Persie's volley against Spain at the 2014 World Cup was immediately compared to 1988. Dutch strikers are still judged against him.

Tactically his Milan was the proof of concept for [gegenpressing](/tactics/gegenpressing)'s ancestors: Sacchi's zonal press and high line needed a striker who could both start the press and punish the space it created, and Van Basten was that player. [Arrigo Sacchi](/managers/arrigo-sacchi) called him the best he coached; [Fabio Capello](/managers/fabio-capello) built the 1992 Invincibles around him. The brevity of his career is why he sits below [Pelé](/players/pele), [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona) and Cruyff in most all-time lists, and why nobody who watched him in 1988-92 accepts that ranking without an argument.
