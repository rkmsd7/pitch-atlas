## Overview

Erling Haaland is the purest goalscorer in world football. Since arriving at [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) in 2022 he has scored at a rate the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) had not seen before, setting the single-season record of 36 goals in 2022-23 as City won the treble. Tall, fast and almost unnervingly single-minded, he has made a career out of doing one thing better than anyone else, and in 2026-27 he is the point of the attack in [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca)'s first City side after [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola)'s departure.

## Early life and breakthrough

Haaland was born in Leeds while his father Alfie was playing for Leeds United, and grew up in Bryne on Norway's south-west coast. He made his senior debut for Bryne at 15, moved to Molde in 2017 under Ole Gunnar Solskjær, and scored four goals in 21 minutes against Brann in July 2018. Red Bull Salzburg signed him in January 2019; that autumn he scored a hat-trick on his Champions League debut against Genk and scored in each of his first five games in the competition.

Borussia Dortmund paid his release clause in January 2020 and he scored a 23-minute hat-trick off the bench on debut at Augsburg. In two and a half seasons in Germany he scored 86 goals in 89 games, won the 2021 DFB-Pokal and became the fastest player to 20 Champions League goals.

## Peak years

City activated his release clause in June 2022 and he was immediately the best striker in the league: 36 league goals, 52 in all competitions, the Premier League, FA Cup and Champions League treble, and a five-goal game against RB Leipzig in the Champions League last 16. He retained the Golden Boot in 2023-24 as City won a fourth straight title, and signed a contract to 2034 in January 2025. The 2024-25 and 2025-26 seasons were less decorated for the club, with [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) and then [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) winning the league, but his own output stayed in the high twenties in the league each year.

## How he plays

Everything starts with the run. Haaland reads the moment a team-mate's head comes up and sprints into the gap between the centre-backs, usually from the blind side of the player marking him. Because he is so fast over the first ten metres and so strong on the shoulder, defenders have to retreat early, which creates room for City's midfielders in front of them.

Inside the box his first touch is usually the finish: a side-footed first-time strike, a sliding far-post conversion or a stooping header. He rarely needs more than one touch and is clinical with his weaker right foot. He is also a reliable penalty taker.

The limits are well known. He does not drop deep to link play the way [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) does, his touch under pressure with his back to goal is ordinary, and when a side sits in a deep block and denies him runs he can go a full match with barely a touch. His pressing is energetic but not refined. City's problem in his quieter seasons was less about him than about the players asked to supply him.

## Evolution

At Salzburg and Dortmund he was a counter-attacking striker running into enormous spaces. Guardiola asked him to stay central and still, to occupy both centre-backs, and to feed off cut-backs and Kevin De Bruyne's crosses, which produced the record 2022-23 season. As City's midfield aged and De Bruyne left in 2025, Haaland was asked to do more with less, dropping to combine occasionally and taking on a greater share of the pressing. Under Maresca, whose teams build through a 3-2 box in midfield, he is again mostly a finisher but is expected to hold the ball up for runners more than before.

- 2019, Champions League group stage v Genk: hat-trick on his Champions League debut for Salzburg.
- 2020, Bundesliga v Augsburg: three goals in 23 minutes on his Dortmund debut, from the bench.
- 2021, DFB-Pokal final v RB Leipzig: two goals in a 4-1 win, his first major trophy ([Dortmund years](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Erling+Haaland+Borussia+Dortmund+goals)).

- 2024, Premier League v Wolves: four goals in a 5-1 win as City chased a fourth straight title.
- 2026, World Cup qualifying and finals with Norway: led the line as Norway reached a first World Cup since 1998 ([Norway goals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Erling+Haaland+Norway+goals)).

## Current situation

City rebuilt heavily in the summer of 2026, spending around £458m and completing the £125m signing of [Enzo Fernández](/players/enzo-fernandez) on deadline day. Haaland is the constant in a new-look team: after nine games City sat second in the table behind Arsenal with 20 goals scored, and he remains the first name on Maresca's team sheet. His contract runs to 2034, which makes him the long-term foundation of the post-Guardiola era. For [Norway](/nations/norway) he is captain and all-time record scorer.
