## Overview

William Saliba is the defender who made [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) believable. Signed from Saint-Étienne as an 18-year-old in 2019 and then loaned out for three seasons, he walked into [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s side in August 2022 and has barely been dropped since. His partnership with [Gabriel Magalhães](/players/gabriel-magalhaes) underpinned three second-place finishes and then the 2025-26 [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) title, and he is a regular for [France](/nations/france), with whom he reached the 2026 World Cup semi-finals.

## Early life and breakthrough

Saliba grew up in Bondy, the Paris suburb that also produced [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe), and joined Saint-Étienne's academy at 15. He made his Ligue 1 debut in September 2018 and was a regular by the spring. Arsenal paid around £27m in July 2019 but loaned him straight back to Saint-Étienne, where injury and the pandemic limited him to 17 games.

What followed was an unusual apprenticeship. Arteta left him out of the 2020-21 squad, and loans to Nice and then [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) did the development work: at Marseille in 2021-22 he played 52 games, was named Ligue 1's Young Player of the Year and won his first France caps.

## Peak years

Saliba returned to London in the summer of 2022 and was immediately first choice. Arsenal led the 2022-23 table for most of the season until a back injury in March removed him; the title challenge collapsed without him, which told everyone how important he had become. Over 2023-24 and 2024-25 he and Gabriel formed the league's best central pairing, with Arsenal conceding fewer goals than any side in both seasons.

He signed a new contract to 2030 on 30 September 2025, rejecting the interest of [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), and 2025-26 was his reward: the title, a Champions League final in Budapest lost 4-3 on penalties to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), and a reputation as the most complete centre-back in England.

## How he plays

Saliba's defining quality is that he makes a high line safe. Arsenal defend 40 yards from their own goal, and when a pass is played in behind he is the one who gets there first, long strides eating the ground before a clean tackle or a shepherding of the ball out of play. He almost never dives in, which is why his foul and card counts are so low for a defender who makes so many interventions.

In possession he is central to Arsenal's build-up. He receives from [David Raya](/players/david-raya) under pressure, carries into midfield when there is space and plays the line-breaking pass into [Martin Ødegaard](/players/martin-odegaard) or [Declan Rice](/players/declan-rice). His right-footed diagonals to [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka) are a regular route forward. In the air he is dominant at both ends, though his goal return is modest.

Weaknesses are hard to find. He can occasionally be too relaxed in possession near his own box, and when he is absent Arsenal's whole defensive structure looks different, which is as much a compliment as a criticism.

## Evolution

Saint-Étienne played him in a deep block where his pace was used for recoveries. At Marseille, under Jorge Sampaoli, he learned to defend a high line and to initiate play, which is the game Arteta wanted. Early in his Arsenal career he was the right-sided centre-back with Ben White tucking in beside him; as Arsenal moved to a back four that became a three in possession, Saliba's role expanded to include more carrying and more responsibility for the first pass.

For France, [Didier Deschamps](/managers/didier-deschamps) initially preferred established names, but Saliba became a starter by the 2026 World Cup, where France lost to eventual winners [Spain](/nations/spain) in the semi-final and then a 6-4 third-place game to England.

## Current situation

Saliba is contracted to 2030 and remains the first name in Arteta's defence in 2026-27. Arsenal started their title defence with a 2-0 win over Coventry City and sat top of the table in early November with the best defensive record in the division, a familiar story with Saliba and Gabriel in front of Raya. For [France](/nations/france), now coached by [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager) since July 2026, he is expected to be the cornerstone of the next cycle.
