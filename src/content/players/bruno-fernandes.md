## Overview

Bruno Fernandes arrived at [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in January 2020 and has been the club's best player in almost every season since. Six managers, two cup wins, a Europa League final and a great deal of chaos have passed around him while he has played nearly every minute, taken nearly every penalty and attempted nearly every difficult pass. He is the captain, a Nations League winner with [Portugal](/nations/portugal), and in the autumn of 2026 he is again at the centre of a United crisis, this time under [Michael Carrick](/managers/michael-carrick).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Maia, near Porto, Fernandes came through Boavista's academy and left for Italy at 17, joining Novara in Serie B. Udinese signed him in 2013 and he spent three seasons there before a year at Sampdoria. Sporting CP brought him home in 2017 for around €8.5m and he became the most productive midfielder in Europe outside the big five leagues: 63 goals in 137 games, including 32 in 2018-19, two Portuguese player of the year awards and the captaincy. He made his Portugal debut in 2017 and won the inaugural Nations League in 2019.

## Peak years

United paid an initial €55m in January 2020 and he transformed a drifting side, driving them from fifth to a Champions League place and then to second in 2020-21, when he scored 28 goals in all competitions and reached the Europa League final. He was voted into the PFA Team of the Year three times. The trophies came later: the League Cup in 2023 and the FA Cup in 2024, the latter under Erik ten Hag against Manchester City. He became captain in 2023, reached a second Europa League final in 2025 under Ruben Amorim, losing to Tottenham, and in June 2025 won a second Nations League with Portugal, beating Spain on penalties in the final.

The 2025-26 season was United's lowest ebb: Amorim was sacked in January 2026 and Carrick, the former midfielder, was appointed head coach on 13 January. Fernandes responded with his best form in two years, and United finished strongly enough to qualify for the Champions League.

## How he plays

Fernandes is a vertical playmaker. He wants the ball on the half-turn between the lines and his first thought is always forward: a clipped ball over the defence for a runner, a driven pass into the striker's feet, a switch to the far winger. He hits these passes early and often, and the price is a high number of giveaways, which has always divided opinion. He shoots from distance with a whipped right-footed strike, scores from the edge of the box and is one of the best penalty takers of his era.

He also runs. His pressing numbers and distance covered are those of a box-to-box midfielder, which is why managers have used him deeper. His weaknesses are the turnovers, a tendency to argue with referees and team-mates, and a lack of pace. But his availability is remarkable: for six years he has barely missed a league game.

## Evolution

At Sporting he was a goalscoring No.8 or No.10 in a 4-3-3. Ole Gunnar Solskjær used him as a pure No.10 behind a striker. Ten Hag pushed him to the right wing and then back inside; Amorim made him one of two No.8s in a 3-4-2-1 and, at times, the deepest midfielder, where his passing range started attacks but his defending was exposed. Carrick restored him to a more advanced role in a 4-2-3-1 and gave him freedom, but in the autumn of 2026, with [Andrey Santos](/players/andrey-santos) and [Kobbie Mainoo](/players/kobbie-mainoo) behind him, he has been in and out of the side.

## Best plays and moments

- 2019, Nations League final v Netherlands: starts as Portugal win the first edition on home soil.
- 2019, Primeira Liga with Sporting: 32 goals from midfield, a record for a European midfielder in a season at the time ([Sporting years](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Bruno+Fernandes+Sporting+CP+goals)).
- 2020, Premier League v Leicester City: scores in the 2-0 final-day win that secures Champions League football.
- 2021, Premier League v Leeds United: a hat-trick in a 6-2 win on the opening day of 2020-21.
- 2021, Premier League v Liverpool: a goal in a 4-2 win at Old Trafford.
- 2023, League Cup final v Newcastle: captain as United end a six-year trophy wait.
- 2024, FA Cup final v Manchester City: lifts the trophy after a 2-1 win ([best goals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Bruno+Fernandes+best+goals+Manchester+United)).
- 2025, Nations League final v Spain: a second international title, won on penalties in Munich.
- 2026, Premier League: his form under Carrick drives United back into the Champions League places in the spring.

## Current situation

United's 2026-27 season began with their worst start in 40 years, six points from the first six league games and a League Cup exit to Brighton after leading 2-0. Fernandes was dropped in October, with Harry Maguire wearing the armband against Bournemouth, and then publicly criticised Carrick's tactics; on 29 October Carrick confirmed that Fernandes, "fully committed to this football club", would remain captain and return to the side for the trip to Chelsea. His contract has around a year to run and Saudi clubs have been linked, but United hope that Champions League football and Carrick keep him. For Portugal, now coached by Jorge Jesus, he remains a starter after a World Cup in which Portugal lost to eventual champions Spain in the quarter-finals.
