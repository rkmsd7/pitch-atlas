## Overview

Evanilson was the signing that announced [Bournemouth](/clubs/bournemouth)'s ambition. In August 2024 the club paid [Porto](/clubs/porto) a record fee of around £31m plus add-ons for a striker who had won a league title and three Portuguese cups, and he became the point of Andoni Iraola's high-pressing, high-intensity side that finished ninth in 2024-25 and attracted buyers for half its squad. He is not a 25-goal striker, and has never pretended to be; he is the striker who makes a pressing team work. In 2026-27, under new manager Marco Rose, he has been given a deeper, freer role and, in late October, found a scoring run.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Fortaleza in Brazil's north-east, Evanilson came through the youth ranks at Fluminense and had a loan at STK Samorin in Slovakia as a teenager, part of a partnership that sent several Brazilian youngsters to Europe. He scored seven goals in 18 games for Fluminense in 2020 and Porto paid around €5m that summer. Sérgio Conceição used him sparingly at first, but by 2021-22 he was a regular, scoring in the Champions League and helping Porto win the league with a record points total.

## Peak years

His best Porto season was 2022-23, with 23 goals in all competitions and a cup double, followed by another Taça de Portugal in 2024. Bournemouth signed him in August 2024 as Dominic Solanke's replacement. His first season was a slow burn, with an injury in the autumn, but he finished with nine league goals and the kind of all-round performances that explained why Iraola never dropped him when fit. In 2025-26 he scored six in the league as Bournemouth consolidated in the top half under Iraola, who left for [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) in June 2026.

## How he plays

Evanilson's game is built on movement in the ten yards either side of the opposition's defensive line. He drops short to receive with his back to goal, lays the ball off and then spins behind the centre-back who followed him. Inside the box he is sharp at the near post and times runs onto cut-backs well, finishing first time and low. He is a good header of the ball for his size and presses the centre-backs with real intelligence, angling his runs to force play one way.

What he lacks is volume. He does not shoot much from distance, does not dribble past defenders and is not quick enough to be a pure channel-runner, which is why his seasonal goal totals have sat in single figures or the low teens. He also has a knack for offside. He is, in short, a striker who is valued for what he does for the team rather than for the number next to his name.

## Evolution

At Porto he was a second striker beside Mehdi Taremi in a 4-4-2, free to drift. Iraola used him as the lone No.9 in a 4-2-3-1 whose primary job was to trigger the press, with the goals shared among the wingers and midfielders. Rose, appointed in the summer of 2026, has said Evanilson suits a "9.5" role in a two-striker system, linking play beneath a partner and drifting from the centre, and the early-season evidence has supported him: two assists without a goal in the opening three games, then four consecutive games with a goal in October.

## Current situation

Bournemouth's 2026-27 season under Rose, the former Leipzig and Dortmund coach, has been built around a 4-4-2 shape in which Evanilson plays beneath a partner. His slow start, six shots and no goals in the first three games, gave way to a run of four scoring games in October, and reports at the start of November described him as the side's form player. His exact 2026-27 goal tally is not confirmed on this page. He wears No.9. He has not yet been capped by [Brazil](/nations/brazil) at senior level; the competition under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) is fierce.
