## Overview

For about three years, from 2004 to 2006, Ronaldinho was the best and most entertaining footballer on the planet. He arrived at a [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) that had not won a trophy in four years and turned it into the team everyone wanted to watch, winning two [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) titles, the 2006 [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) and the 2005 Ballon d'Or. He had already won the 2002 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) with [Brazil](/nations/brazil) and would later add the Copa Libertadores with Atlético Mineiro, making him one of the few to complete the full set of world, European and South American honours.

## Early life and breakthrough

Ronaldo de Assis Moreira was born in Porto Alegre. His father, a shipyard worker who also played for Esporte Clube Cruzeiro, died when Ronaldinho was eight, and his older brother Roberto, a Grêmio player, became his guardian and later his agent. The family's futsal background shaped his touch: the sole-of-the-foot control, the tight-space tricks and the elastico all came from the indoor game.

He scored 23 goals in one under-17 match as a 13-year-old, and starred at the 1997 Under-17 World Championship. His Grêmio debut came in 1998, and a goal in the 1999 Gaúcho final against Internacional, nutmegging and dancing past Dunga, announced him nationally. He was in Brazil's 1999 Copa América winning squad at 19.

[Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) signed him in 2001 after a messy contract exit. In [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1) he was brilliant and inconsistent, often clashing with coach Luis Fernández about nightlife, but the 2002 World Cup made him a star: the free-kick that sailed over David Seaman against [England](/nations/england) in the quarter-final, and a tournament-long partnership with [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) and [Rivaldo](/players/rivaldo).

## Peak years

[Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) nearly signed him in 2003; Barcelona's new president Joan Laporta got him instead for around €30 million. Under Frank Rijkaard he was given the left side of a 4-3-3 with licence to roam, [Deco](/players/deco) and [Xavi](/players/xavi) behind him and [Samuel Eto'o](/players/samuel-etoo) ahead of him. He won FIFA World Player of the Year in 2004 and 2005 and the Ballon d'Or in 2005.

The symbolic night came in November 2005 at the Santiago Bernabéu, where he scored two solo goals in a 3-0 win and the [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) fans stood to applaud him, something only [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona) had experienced there. In 2006 Barcelona beat [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) 2-1 in Paris to win the Champions League, with Ronaldinho the tournament's dominant creative force.

The decline was swift. Nightlife, weight and a loss of hunger meant 2007-08 was a write-off, and new coach [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) sold him to [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) in 2008, handing the number 10 to a teenage [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi).

## How he played

Ronaldinho's game was built on deception. The no-look pass, which he made famous, worked because his eyes and shoulders sold a fake before his foot did the real work. The elastico, where the outside of the boot pushes the ball one way and the inside snaps it back, was performed at speed and in tight areas. He used the sole of his foot constantly to stop, roll and change direction, a futsal habit.

He was genuinely two-footed, strong in the upper body, and had the knack of scoring from distance with a whipped shot that dipped late, often with little backlift. His free-kicks were struck with the instep over or around the wall, and he regularly tried the ball under the wall when he saw it jump.

Tactically he started wide left and came inside to overload the half-space, which gave the full-back room and gave Eto'o one-on-ones. His weaknesses were well known: he did little defensive work, his effectiveness depended on his fitness, which fluctuated, and after 2006 he increasingly chose the trick over the simple pass. Through 2004-06 his creative output was unmatched, producing goals and assists in roughly equal measure.

## Evolution

At Grêmio he was a second striker. At PSG, a free number 10. Rijkaard's great insight was to move him wide, which prevented him from being surrounded and gave him a running start. For Brazil he played as a number 10 behind Ronaldo and Adriano in the 2005 Confederations Cup, but Carlos Alberto Parreira's star-heavy "magic square" flopped at the 2006 World Cup.

At Milan, Carlo Ancelotti and then Leonardo rebuilt him as a deeper left-sided playmaker in a 4-3-3 or a 4-2-1-3, and he had a genuinely good 2009-10 season, scoring 12 Serie A goals. Back in Brazil, at Flamengo and especially Atlético Mineiro under Cuca, he played a free, walking role as a classic enganche, conserving energy and picking passes, and was named South American Footballer of the Year in 2013 at 33.

## Legacy

Ronaldinho's influence is less about tactics than about permission. He made it acceptable again for the best player in the world to play with a smile, to try things, and to treat football as entertainment. Messi has said he learned more from watching Ronaldinho in training than from any coach, and Ronaldinho assisted Messi's first Barcelona goal in 2005. [Neymar](/players/neymar) modelled his whole approach on him.

His later years were turbulent, including a prison spell in Paraguay in 2020 over a passport issue, but his standing among players has never slipped. Any list of the greatest dribblers in history has him near the top with Maradona, [Garrincha](/players/garrincha) and Messi, and his 2005 Ballon d'Or remains the last won by a player whose primary gift was pure joy.
