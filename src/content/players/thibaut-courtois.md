## Overview

Thibaut Courtois has been a top-level goalkeeper for 15 years and the best in the world for several of them. He won titles with [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid), Chelsea and [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), the Golden Glove at the 2018 World Cup and the Yashin Trophy in 2022, the year his nine-save performance against Liverpool in Paris decided a Champions League final almost by himself. At 34, after a serious knee injury in 2023, he remains Madrid's number one under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Bree, Courtois came through at Genk and was their first-choice goalkeeper at 18 as they won the 2010-11 Belgian title. Chelsea signed him in summer 2011 and immediately loaned him to Atlético Madrid, where he spent three seasons under Diego Simeone. He won the Europa League, the Copa del Rey and, in 2013-14, La Liga, and conceded only 26 league goals that year as Atlético also reached the Champions League final.

Chelsea recalled him in 2014 and he won two Premier League titles under José Mourinho and Antonio Conte. His 2018 World Cup, where [Belgium](/nations/belgium) finished third and he won the Golden Glove, prompted a €35m move to Real Madrid.

## Peak years

His first Madrid season was difficult, but from 2019-20 onwards he was the league's best goalkeeper: the 2020 title under Zidane, then the 2021-22 double under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti), when his saves against PSG, Chelsea, Manchester City and finally Liverpool in the final made him the first goalkeeper to be the Champions League final's man of the match in a generation. In August 2023 he tore his ACL in training, missed almost the entire season and then tore the meniscus in his other knee. He returned for the final weeks of 2023-24 and kept a clean sheet in the Champions League final against Dortmund.

He was back to his best in 2024-25 and 2025-26, often the reason Madrid stayed in games during a turbulent period for the club.

## How he plays

Courtois's size is his first weapon, but his positioning is what makes it work. He is rarely out of place, and because his reach covers so much of the goal he does not need to dive early. In one-on-ones he stays upright until the last moment, making the goal look tiny. He commands his area on crosses and is a reliable, if unspectacular, distributor with both feet.

The limitations are the usual ones for a very tall keeper: low shots to the corners and quick changes of direction can catch him, and his kicking under pressure is not at the level of the best modern sweeper-keepers. His outspokenness has occasionally made headlines, including a long absence from the Belgium squad after a dispute in 2023.

## Evolution

Simeone's Atlético had him as a last line behind a deep block. Chelsea asked more of his distribution. At Madrid he has become the archetypal big-club keeper: fewer shots, higher stakes, decisive saves in the games that matter. Mourinho, who signed him for Chelsea in 2014, has made him one of his senior figures again.

## Best plays and moments

- 2014, La Liga, with Atlético: the title-winning draw at Camp Nou.
- 2017, Premier League: a second title at Chelsea under Conte.
- 2018, World Cup: Golden Glove as Belgium finished third ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Courtois+World+Cup+2018+Golden+Glove)).
- 2022, Champions League final, v Liverpool: nine saves, man of the match ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Courtois+Champions+League+final+2022+saves)).
- 2022, Yashin Trophy: named the world's best goalkeeper.
- 2024, Champions League final, v Dortmund: a clean sheet on his return from a year out.
- 2025, Champions League, v Manchester City: key saves in the play-off.

## Current situation

Courtois is Madrid's starting goalkeeper and one of Mourinho's vice-captains in 2026-27, with his contract running to 2027. He returned to the Belgium squad and played at the 2026 World Cup. At 34, with his injury history, this may be one of his final seasons at the very top, but his performances have not suggested decline.
