## Overview

Kai Havertz has scored the winning goal in a Champions League final and the opening goal in another, and has been doubted in between. The German arrived at [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) from [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) for £65m in 2023 amid scepticism about what he was for, and [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) turned him into the focal point of a title-winning attack. By 2026-27 he is Arsenal's first-choice No.9 ahead of [Viktor Gyökeres](/players/viktor-gyokeres), and a senior figure for [Germany](/nations/germany).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Aachen, Havertz joined [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen)'s academy at 11 and became the club's youngest Bundesliga player at 17 in October 2016. He was a regular within months, playing as a number ten or a wide creator, and in 2018-19 and 2019-20 scored 17 and 18 goals in all competitions, numbers that made him the most sought-after young player in Germany. Chelsea paid around £71m in September 2020.

## Peak years

His first Chelsea season was uneven until May 2021, when he scored the only goal of the Champions League final against [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) in Porto. He added the winner in the 2021 Club World Cup final, but [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel)'s departure and Chelsea's upheaval left him drifting between positions, and he left for Arsenal in 2023.

At Arsenal he began as a left-sided number eight and struggled; from December 2023 Arteta moved him to centre-forward and everything changed. He scored 13 league goals in 2023-24 and was on course to beat that in 2024-25 before a hamstring injury in February ended his season. The 2025-26 campaign was his best: a strong return from injury, a central role in the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) title, and the sixth-minute opener in the Champions League final in Budapest, where Arsenal lost 4-3 on penalties to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) after a 1-1 draw.

## How he plays

Havertz is not a conventional No.9 and does not try to be. His first instinct is to drop towards the ball, drag a centre-back with him and open the space behind for [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka) or [Gabriel Martinelli](/players/gabriel-martinelli) to attack. Once the ball is wide he reverses direction and attacks the box, where his 1.90 m frame and timing make him an outstanding header of the ball and a reliable finisher of cut-backs with his left foot.

His pressing is subtle: he angles his runs to cut passing lanes rather than sprinting at the ball, and he is the trigger for the rest of the team. He is far stronger in hold-up play than his build suggests and his first touch under pressure is excellent. Shooting from range is not his game, and his confidence can dip visibly during goalless runs; when the supply from wide areas dries up he can go long spells without touching the ball in the box.

## Evolution

At Leverkusen he was a creator who scored; at Chelsea a forward who created; at Arsenal, after a false start in midfield, a striker who does both. Arteta's decision to move him to the front in late 2023 is one of the defining tactical choices of the title era. The arrival of Gyökeres in 2025 was expected to push him back into midfield, but it was Havertz who won the central role on form and kept it into 2026-27.

For Germany he has been used across the front line by Julian Nagelsmann and was part of the squad for the 2026 World Cup. Since July 2026 the national side has been led by [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp).

## Current situation

Havertz has started every Premier League game he has been fit for in 2026-27, with two goals in his first six starts, and holds the No.9 role ahead of Gyökeres. A hamstring injury picked up on Germany duty in late September 2026 was a reminder of his fragility, and Arsenal opened talks over a new contract in January 2026 without, as of early November, announcing an extension. He is central to the champions' title defence under [Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) and to [Germany](/nations/germany)'s rebuild under Klopp.
