## Overview

Alex Morgan was the face of the [United States](/nations/united-states-women) for over a decade: a World Cup winner in 2015 and 2019, Olympic champion in 2012, co-captain of the national team and one of the leaders of the players' equal-pay case. She scored 123 international goals, the fifth most in American history, and retired on 8 September 2024 after a final game for San Diego Wave, announcing she was pregnant with her second child.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in San Dimas, California, Morgan played college football at Berkeley and was the first pick in the 2011 WPS draft. She scored in the 2011 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) final defeat to Japan at 22 and the next year became a star: 28 goals and 21 assists in 2012, including the 123rd-minute header against Canada in the London Olympic semi-final, and a gold medal.

## Peak years

Injuries disrupted her mid-twenties, but she was a starter in the 2015 World Cup win and the best American player at the 2019 World Cup, scoring five against Thailand, six in total, and the semi-final winner against England, celebrated with a tea-sipping gesture that became a meme. She won the 2017 [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) on loan at [Lyon](/clubs/lyon-women), had a brief 2020 stint at Tottenham during the pandemic, and after the birth of her daughter in 2020 returned to win the 2022 NWSL Golden Boot and the 2023 Shield with San Diego. She was left out of the Paris 2024 Olympic squad by Emma Hayes and retired weeks later.

## How she plays

Morgan was a pace striker who timed runs behind the defence and finished across the keeper. Her heading at the far post produced many of her biggest goals, and she could hold the ball up better than her slight frame suggested. She drifted left to combine with [Megan Rapinoe](/players/megan-rapinoe) and later became a more rounded number nine who created chances for others. She was never a dribbler; her gift was anticipation.

## Evolution

An explosive youngster at the 2011 World Cup, the complete striker by 2019, and a wiser, slower link forward after motherhood. Her activism, on equal pay and on the [NWSL](/leagues/nwsl)'s player safety reforms, defined her final years as much as her goals.

## Best plays and moments

- Goal in the 2011 World Cup final against Japan at 22.
- 123rd-minute header against Canada, London 2012 Olympic semi-final.
- World Cup winner in Canada, 2015.
- Five goals against Thailand and the semi-final winner against England, 2019 World Cup.
- NWSL Golden Boot 2022 and the 2023 Shield with San Diego, after becoming a mother.

## Legacy / Current situation

Morgan retired in 2024 as a two-time world champion, an Olympic gold medallist and a business figure with a stake in San Diego Wave and her own media company. Her legacy includes the 2022 equal-pay agreement and a generation of American girls who grew up in her number 13.
