## Overview

Petr Čech kept 202 clean sheets in the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league), a record that still stands. He was the goalkeeper for every one of [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea)'s title-winning sides between 2005 and 2015, the man who saved Arjen Robben's extra-time penalty and two more in the shootout in the 2012 [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) final, and the one constant through eleven managers at Stamford Bridge. The protective headguard he wore after a fractured skull in 2006 made him instantly recognisable, but it was the stillness and anticipation beneath it that made him great.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Plzeň, Čech was a triplet and originally a striker in street football before going in goal. He turned professional with Chmel Blšany, moved to Sparta Prague in 2001 and went 903 league minutes without conceding, a Czech record. After a season and a half at Rennes in [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1), Chelsea signed him for 7 million pounds in 2004 as a long-term successor to Carlo Cudicini.

He was supposed to be the deputy. [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) arrived the same summer and made him first choice, and in 2004-05 Čech set a Premier League record with 24 clean sheets and conceded just 15 goals as Chelsea won their first league title in 50 years. He was 22.

## Peak years

From 2004 to 2012 he was in the top two or three goalkeepers in the world. He won back-to-back titles with Mourinho and anchored a defence of [John Terry](/players/john-terry), Ricardo Carvalho and Claude Makélélé that was the meanest in Europe. In October 2006 a collision with Reading's Stephen Hunt fractured his skull; he returned three months later in the headguard and his form barely wavered.

Internationally he was the Czech Republic's number one for 14 years, part of the Euro 2004 semi-final side with [Pavel Nedvěd](/players/pavel-nedved) and Tomáš Rosický.

The summit was Munich, 19 May 2012. Chelsea were outplayed by [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) in their own stadium, and Čech saved a Robben penalty in extra time before stopping Ivica Olić and Bastian Schweinsteiger in the shootout. He later revealed he had studied Bayern's takers and guessed the right way for every kick. A year later he added the Europa League.

## How he played

Čech was not a flashy goalkeeper. His strength was that shots which beat other keepers never seemed to be a problem for him, because he had read the shooter's body shape and shuffled across early. His set position was low for a man of 1.96 m, which let him push off either way, and his long arms turned half-saves into full ones. He caught crosses cleanly in an era when many keepers punched.

He was outstanding at penalties, keeping a dossier on takers long before analysts did it for him. His distribution was accurate if unambitious, mostly long throws and clipped balls to full-backs rather than the sweeper-keeper passing that became fashionable later.

Weaknesses were marginal. He was less dominant on the ground in one-on-ones than in the air, and as a passer he was never a build-up asset for a possession team, something that showed late in his [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) career when [Arsène Wenger](/managers/arsene-wenger) wanted the ball played short.

## Evolution

Čech was the beneficiary and then the victim of Chelsea's constant turnover. He adapted to Mourinho's [low-block](/tactics/low-block) teams, to Carlo Ancelotti's 4-3-3 and to Roberto Di Matteo's siege mentality of 2012. In 2014 Mourinho returned, signed Thibaut Courtois and made him first choice, and Čech moved across London to Arsenal in 2015 for 10 million pounds. He won two FA Cups there, kept a 2016 Golden Glove and played his final game in the 2019 Europa League final against Chelsea. He then returned to Chelsea as technical and performance adviser until 2022.

## Best plays and moments

- 2012, Champions League final v Bayern Munich: the Robben penalty save and two shootout stops ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Petr+Cech+Bayern+2012+penalty+saves)).
- 2005, Premier League: 24 clean sheets and 15 goals conceded, both records.
- 2012, Champions League semi-final v Barcelona: a string of saves at the Camp Nou as ten men held on.
- 2008, Champions League final v Manchester United: a save from Cristiano Ronaldo's penalty in the shootout.
- 2007, FA Cup final v Manchester United: a clean sheet in the first Wembley final, won by Drogba's late goal.
- 2004, Euro 2004 semi-final v Greece: an outstanding tournament ended in a silver-goal defeat.
- 2010, Premier League v Manchester United: a clean sheet in a 2-1 win at Old Trafford that swung the title to Chelsea.
- 2015, Premier League for Arsenal v Everton: his 170th clean sheet, breaking David James's record.
- 2016, Premier League: Golden Glove with Arsenal, aged 34.
- 2017, FA Cup final v Chelsea: part of the 2-1 win over his old club.

## Legacy

Čech is the measuring stick for goalkeeping consistency in England. He was a quiet, multilingual professional whose coolness after a career-threatening injury is still cited by sports psychologists. Since retiring from football he has played ice hockey in goal for Guildford Phoenix and Chelmsford Chieftains, and he remains a regular presence as an ambassador for Chelsea and the Czech game.
