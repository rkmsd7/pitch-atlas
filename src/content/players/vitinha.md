## Overview

Vitinha was a Wolves loan flop who became the midfielder every elite coach in Europe wishes they had. He is 1.72 m, plays in the most congested area of the pitch, and nobody can get the ball off him. Under [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique) at [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) he has won four [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1) titles and two Champions Leagues, and in 2025 he finished third in the Ballon d'Or, the highest a pure central midfielder had placed in years. He also scored in the 2025 Nations League final as [Portugal](/nations/portugal) beat Spain on penalties.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Santo Tirso, Vitinha came through at [Porto](/clubs/porto) and debuted at 19 in 2019. A season-long loan to Wolves in 2020-21 produced 22 appearances, no goals and little impact. He returned to Porto and, under Sérgio Conceição, became the best midfielder in Portugal in 2021-22, winning the league and cup double. PSG paid his €41.5m release clause in summer 2022.

## Peak years

His first PSG season under Christophe Galtier was a struggle in a team built around Messi, Neymar and Mbappé. Luis Enrique's arrival in 2023 changed everything: a possession structure in which Vitinha was the central reference, with everything flowing through him. In 2023-24 PSG won the domestic double and reached the Champions League semi-final; in 2024-25 Vitinha was the best midfielder in the competition as PSG beat Liverpool, Aston Villa, Arsenal and finally Inter 5-0 in Munich. He was Ligue 1's Player of the Season and third in the Ballon d'Or behind Dembélé and Lamine Yamal.

In 2025-26 PSG won the league again and retained the Champions League against Arsenal in Budapest, with Vitinha dictating the final. At the 2026 World Cup, Portugal lost to Spain in the quarter-final; Roberto Martínez was replaced by Jorge Jesus afterwards.

## How he plays

Vitinha receives the ball with a defender on his back and simply turns. His first touch is always into space, his body shape is always open, and he plays forward with one or two touches at a tempo that few can match. He is not a long-range passer in the Kroos mould; his game is the short, forward pass that breaks a line, repeated 80 times a match. He also arrives late at the edge of the box and scores with a side-footed finish.

Defensively he is better than his size suggests, pressing with aggression and timing. The weaknesses are physical: aerial duels, holding off big midfielders, and a certain vulnerability in transition when PSG's press is beaten. He has occasionally been caught in possession trying one dribble too many.

## Evolution

A number 8 at Porto, a peripheral figure in Galtier's PSG, and then the single pivot or the deeper of two in Luis Enrique's 4-3-3, where he partners [João Neves](/players/joao-neves) and Fabián Ruiz. For Portugal, Roberto Martínez used him as a deep playmaker; Jorge Jesus's plans are still forming.

## Best plays and moments

- 2022, Primeira Liga: a double with Porto that earned the PSG move.
- 2024, Champions League quarter-final, v Barcelona: a goal in the 4-1 win at Montjuïc.
- 2025, Champions League, v Liverpool and Arsenal: masterclasses in both ties.
- 2025, Champions League final, PSG 5-0 Inter: the conductor of the biggest final win in the competition's history ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Vitinha+Champions+League+final+2025+Inter)).
- 2025, Nations League final, v Spain: a goal in the 2-2 draw before the shoot-out win ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Vitinha+goal+Nations+League+final+2025+Spain)).
- 2025, Ballon d'Or: third place.
- 2026, Champions League final, v Arsenal: a second European Cup in Budapest.

## Current situation

Vitinha is the first name on Luis Enrique's team sheet in 2026-27, with a contract to 2029, and the midfield axis of a PSG side chasing a third straight Champions League. For Portugal he is a starter under Jorge Jesus.
