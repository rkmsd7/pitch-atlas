## Overview

Bradley Barcola is the player [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) chose to replace [Mohamed Salah](/players/mohamed-salah)'s goals, even if he plays on the other wing. Signed from [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) for around €125m in summer 2026, he arrived as a Champions League winner at 23, a French international, and one of the fastest attackers in Europe. At PSG he was the quiet third member of a front line led by Ousmane Dembélé and Khvicha Kvaratskhelia; at Anfield, under [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola), he is expected to be a headliner.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Villeurbanne to a family of Togolese heritage, Barcola joined [Lyon](/clubs/lyon)'s academy at seven, and Lyon's reserve side before making his Ligue 1 debut in November 2021. Peter Bosz and then Laurent Blanc used him as a left winger and occasional false nine, and in 2022-23 he produced five goals and eight assists in the league, the kind of productivity that marked him as the next Lyon export. PSG paid around €45m in August 2023, an eye-catching fee for a player with one full season, and he spent his first months in Paris as an understudy to [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe).

## Peak years

Everything changed when Mbappé left for Real Madrid in 2024. Luis Enrique rebuilt the attack as a collective, and Barcola started 2024-25 as the team's leading scorer, with ten goals before Christmas. Dembélé's move to centre-forward in January 2025 took the limelight, but Barcola remained a starter through the Champions League run, playing in the quarter-final against Aston Villa, the semi-final against Arsenal and the 5-0 final win over Inter in Munich. He added a third straight Ligue 1 title in 2025-26 as PSG retained the Champions League against [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) in Budapest, and he was part of the [France](/nations/france) squad that reached the 2026 World Cup semi-finals before losing to eventual champions Spain.

## How he plays

Barcola's running is the first thing you notice: long strides, an upright carriage and the ability to hit top speed in three or four touches. He stays on the touchline to stretch the pitch, which gives his team width on the left and opens the half-space for a midfielder to run into. When he receives, his first touch is almost always forward and outside, inviting the full-back to commit before he cuts in onto his right foot.

His finishing was once the weakness; at PSG he learned to shoot early and low across the goalkeeper and to arrive at the far post for cut-backs when play goes down the right. He is a willing but not natural crosser, better at square balls into the six-yard box than floated deliveries. In possession he keeps it simple, playing one-touch combinations with the left-back and the No.10 rather than taking players on in tight spaces. Out of possession he is a tireless presser, sprinting at the right-back and screening passing lanes, which is exactly what Iraola's system requires.

The weaknesses are a left foot he rarely uses, a tendency to run into traffic when the game is slow and congested, and streaky confidence: when the first few chances of a season go wrong, his output dips.

## Evolution

At Lyon he was an all-purpose forward who drifted centrally. Luis Enrique fixed him on the left, taught him to hold width and gave him licence to attack in transition while the rest of the team pressed in a compact block. Playing with Dembélé and Désiré Doué in a fluid front three forced him to improve his combination play and his decision-making at the end of counter-attacks. At Liverpool the picture is different: he is the main source of pace in a team whose other attackers, [Alexander Isak](/players/alexander-isak) and [Florian Wirtz](/players/florian-wirtz), prefer to receive to feet, and the balance between his running and their craft is the question Iraola is working to answer.

## Current situation

Barcola's Liverpool move was completed in July 2026, and he chose the No.29 shirt he wore in Paris. He has slotted in on the left of Iraola's 4-2-3-1 as the direct outlet alongside Isak, with Wirtz behind them and Salah's old right-wing berth now shared by rotation. Early returns have been promising rather than spectacular; Liverpool sat outside the top five as of early November 2026, behind champions [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) and [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city). For France he is a regular under new head coach [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager), who took over from Didier Deschamps after the World Cup.
