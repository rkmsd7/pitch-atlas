## Overview

Abby Wambach scored more international goals than any player in history when she retired in December 2015: 184 in 256 games for the [United States](/nations/united-states-women), a record that stood until Christine Sinclair passed it in 2020. She won Olympic gold in 2004 and 2012, the 2015 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup), the 2012 FIFA World Player of the Year award and produced the most famous goal in American women's football, the 122nd-minute header against Brazil in the 2011 World Cup quarter-final.

## Early life and breakthrough

The youngest of seven children from Rochester, New York, Wambach scored 27 goals in her first youth season and was moved to play with boys. At the University of Florida she won the 1998 NCAA title and was drafted by Washington Freedom in 2002, where she played alongside [Mia Hamm](/players/mia-hamm) and won the 2003 WUSA title. She made her US debut in 2001 and scored at her first World Cup in 2003.

## Peak years

Her extra-time header won the Athens 2004 Olympic final against Brazil. A broken leg days before the Beijing 2008 Olympics cost her a tournament, but she returned stronger: the Silver Ball at the 2011 World Cup, where her header from Megan Rapinoe's cross against Brazil and another in the final against Japan took the USA to a penalty shootout they lost, then London 2012 gold with five goals in the tournament. She passed Hamm's record with four goals against South Korea in June 2013 and finally won the World Cup in 2015 as a substitute, lifting the trophy in Vancouver before retiring after a farewell match in December 2015.

## How she plays

Wambach was the ultimate aerial striker. At 1.80 m, with exceptional timing and fearlessness, she attacked crosses at the near and far post and scored 77 of her international goals with her head, a figure no other player approaches. She held the ball up, brought wingers like Rapinoe and [Alex Morgan](/players/alex-morgan) into play, and finished with both feet from close range. She was a vocal, demanding leader and co-captain. Her limitations were pace and mobility, which meant the USA's style under Pia Sundhage was built around getting the ball wide and into the box, a brand of [direct play](/tactics/direct-play) that frustrated purists and won medals.

## Evolution

A target striker throughout, she adapted from a starter in her twenties to a game-changing substitute under Jill Ellis in 2015, a transition she accepted with public grace. She also became a leading advocate for equal pay and LGBTQ rights in sport.

## Best plays and moments

- Extra-time winner in the 2004 Olympic final, USA 2-1 Brazil, Athens.

- Lifting the 2015 World Cup in Vancouver.

## Legacy / Current situation

Wambach retired in 2015, wrote the best-selling memoir Forward and the leadership book Wolfpack, and is married to the author Glennon Doyle. She remains the greatest header of a ball the women's game has seen and one of its most important voices.
