## Overview

Hannah Hampton went from understudy to the world's best goalkeeper in a single year. Named [England](/nations/england-women)'s number one after Mary Earps's abrupt international retirement in June 2025, she was player of the match in the Euro 2025 final, saving two Spanish penalties in the shootout, and then swept the 2025 individual awards: the Yashin Trophy at the Ballon d'Or, The Best FIFA goalkeeper, the BBC Women's Footballer of the Year. For [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea-women) she has won two WSL titles and consecutive Golden Gloves.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Birmingham, Hampton grew up partly in Spain, where she trained in Villarreal's academy, and was born with strabismus, an eye condition that affects depth perception; doctors told her she should not play sport. She returned to England, came through Stoke and Birmingham City, made her WSL debut at 17 and moved to Aston Villa in 2021. A 2022-23 season in which she was briefly dropped by Villa amid reports of attitude issues looked like a setback until Chelsea signed her in summer 2023 as Emma Hayes's long-term number one.

## Peak years

At Chelsea she displaced Zećira Mušović, won the 2023-24 title in a final-day decider, shared the 2024-25 Golden Glove in Sonia Bompastor's unbeaten season and took it outright in 2025-26 with eight clean sheets in 18 games. For England she made her debut in 2022 and was a squad member at Euro 2022 and the 2023 World Cup before Sarina Wiegman made her first choice in 2025. The Euro final in Basel, with saves from Mariona Caldentey and Aitana Bonmatí in the shootout, was the moment of her career. She was made an MBE in June 2026 and signed a two-year Chelsea extension in July.

## How she plays

Hampton is comfortable playing as an eleventh outfielder. Chelsea build short through her, and she regularly breaks lines with driven passes into midfield or clipped balls to the full-backs. Her starting position is high, which suits teams defending a [high line](/tactics/high-line), and she is quick off her line in one-v-ones. In goal she is an explosive shot-stopper with strong hands, and her penalty record, for Chelsea and England, is exceptional. She is also unusually vocal, organising the back line. Critics occasionally point to high crosses, where she is good rather than dominant.

## Evolution

Her Spanish upbringing gave her the footwork; Chelsea and England have added decision-making and consistency. Under Bompastor's possession game she has become even more involved in build-up, and the 2025-26 Golden Glove showed a keeper who now produces big saves in big matches rather than only in the shootouts that made her name.

## Best plays and moments

- Two penalty saves in the Euro 2025 final shootout against Spain, Basel, July 2025.
- Final-day clean sheet as Chelsea won the 2023-24 title on goal difference at Old Trafford.
- Key saves in England's 1-0 World Cup qualifying win over Iceland in the Lionesses' 500th match, April 2026.
- The 2025 Yashin Trophy, the first by an English woman.
- Golden Glove in 2025-26 and the League Cup final clean sheet against Manchester United, March 2026.

## Legacy / Current situation

As of November 2026 Hampton is Chelsea's and England's undisputed number one at 25, with the [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) play-offs against Ukraine in December the next assignment. Her rise rewrote the perception of what a goalkeeper with a visual impairment could do.
