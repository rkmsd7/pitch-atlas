## Overview

Virgil van Dijk is the best centre-back of his generation and the single signing that changed [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool)'s modern history. When he arrived from Southampton in January 2018 for £75m, a world record for a defender, Liverpool had conceded 13 goals in their last five away games. Eighteen months later they were European champions; a year after that, champions of England for the first time in 30 years. He captained the club to a second title in 2024-25, and in 2026-27, at 35, he is still the organiser of the defence under [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola).

## Early life and breakthrough

Van Dijk grew up in Breda, the son of a Dutch father and Surinamese mother, and was released by Willem II for being too slow and too small, a verdict that a late growth spurt made absurd. Groningen gave him a professional debut in 2011, and [Celtic](/clubs/celtic) signed him for around £2.6m in 2013. Two Scottish titles and a Champions League education under Neil Lennon and Ronny Deila followed. Southampton paid £13m in 2015 and he was quickly the best defender outside the top six, captaining the club before a long-running pursuit by Liverpool ended with the record transfer in the 2018 winter window.

## Peak years

His effect at Liverpool was immediate: a header against Everton on his debut, a run to the 2018 Champions League final, and then the 2018-19 season in which Liverpool conceded 22 league goals, won 97 points and beat Tottenham in the Champions League final in Madrid. He was PFA Player of the Year, UEFA Player of the Year and second to Lionel Messi in the Ballon d'Or. The 2019-20 title was the culmination; an ACL injury in October 2020, after a challenge by Jordan Pickford, cost him most of a season. He returned to captain the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) at Euro 2024 and, after inheriting the Liverpool armband from Jordan Henderson in 2023, led Arne Slot's side to the 2024-25 Premier League title with a clean-sheet record that recalled his first full season.

## How he plays

Van Dijk's defending is built on anticipation and positioning rather than tackling. He rarely needs to slide because he is already where the ball is going: he sees the forward's run developing, adjusts his body shape early and arrives first. When he has to engage, he uses his frame to guide attackers wide rather than lunging, and he is almost impossible to dribble past in open play, a statistic that became a running joke during 2018-19.

In the air he wins everything, which is why Liverpool defend so well against crosses and why he scores so many headers from corners. In possession he is the team's first playmaker: the diagonal to the right winger, the vertical pass through the first line of pressure, and the carry into midfield when nobody closes him. His passing range allows Liverpool's full-backs to push high.

The weaknesses are the ones age brings. His recovery speed is no longer elite, which matters in a high line, and he has learned to drop a yard deeper and rely on timing. He is occasionally caught by a quick player running at him with the ball in space, and his leadership style, calm to the point of appearing detached, is sometimes criticised when the team is losing.

## Evolution

At Celtic and Southampton he was an aggressive, ball-carrying centre-back who scored regularly. [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) made him the anchor of a high line, trusting him to cover the space behind pressing full-backs, and paired him first with Joël Matip and later Joe Gomez and Ibrahima Konaté. After the knee injury he became more positional. Under Slot, Liverpool defended slightly deeper and held the ball more, and Van Dijk's role as the deepest distributor grew. Iraola, who likes a high press and man-to-man defending in the opposition half, has again asked him to defend a high line, with the compromise being a more compact block once the first press is beaten.

## Best plays and moments

- 2015, Scottish Premiership v St Johnstone: a 60-yard run and finish for Celtic, still shown in every highlight reel ([best goals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Virgil+van+Dijk+best+goals+headers)).
- 2018, FA Cup v Everton: a late header on his Liverpool debut to win the Merseyside derby.
- 2018, Premier League v Tottenham: the lunging block and recovery that showed what Liverpool had bought.
- 2019, Champions League semi-final v Barcelona: the defensive display that underpinned the 4-0 comeback at Anfield.
- 2019, Champions League final v Tottenham: player of the match in the 2-0 win in Madrid ([final highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Liverpool+Tottenham+Champions+League+final+2019+highlights)).
- 2019, Nations League semi-final v England: captain as the Netherlands win 3-1 in Guimarães.
- 2022, League Cup final v Chelsea: scores in the 11-10 penalty shoot-out at Wembley.
- 2024, League Cup final v Chelsea: the extra-time header that wins the trophy with a team full of academy players.
- 2025, Premier League v Tottenham: lifts the Premier League trophy as captain after the 5-1 win at Anfield.

## Current situation

Van Dijk signed a two-year extension in April 2025 that runs to the summer of 2027, and as of October 2026 there had been no talks about a further deal. He remains Liverpool's captain and a near ever-present at the heart of Iraola's defence, partnering Konaté, with [Alisson Becker](/players/alisson-becker) behind him. Liverpool finished fifth in 2025-26 and have started 2026-27 outside the leading pack of [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) and [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city), so the question of how long the captain can keep playing at this level is one of the themes of Iraola's first season. For the Netherlands he captained the side at the 2026 World Cup and has not yet announced his international retirement.
