## Overview

Rodri is the player coaches mean when they talk about control. The [Spain](/nations/spain) holding midfielder won the 2024 Ballon d'Or, scored the goal that won [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city)'s first Champions League in 2023, and captained Spain to the [2026 World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in the United States, where he was named the tournament's best player. Weeks later, with a year left on his City contract and [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) gone, he joined [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) on a deal to 2030. He is not quick, not flashy and not a prolific scorer. He simply makes every team he plays in better organised, and his teams lose remarkably few matches when he is on the pitch.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Madrid, Rodri joined Atlético Madrid's academy as a boy but was released at 17 for being too slow and too slight. He went to Villarreal, combined football with a university degree in business, and broke into the first team in 2015-16 under Marcelino. Three composed seasons at El Madrigal made him one of La Liga's most coveted young midfielders, and Atlético bought him back in 2018 for €20m. One year under Diego Simeone, playing as the deepest midfielder in a 4-4-2, taught him defensive discipline; in June 2019 City paid his €70m release clause to make him Fernandinho's successor.

## Peak years

Under Guardiola Rodri became the hub of the most dominant side in English football. He started almost every match of City's four straight titles between 2021 and 2024, scored the only goal of the 2023 Champions League final against [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) and was the heartbeat of the treble. With Spain he won the 2023 Nations League and was player of the tournament at [Euro 2024](/tournaments/euro), then in October 2024 became the first Spanish male winner of the Ballon d'Or since Luis Suárez in 1960.

A ruptured anterior cruciate ligament in September 2024 cost him almost all of that season and City fell away badly without him, which only underlined his importance. He returned in 2025, managed 21 league appearances in a stop-start 2025-26, and arrived at the 2026 World Cup as Spain's captain. Spain beat Austria, [Portugal](/nations/portugal), [France](/nations/france) and [Argentina](/nations/argentina) in the knockout rounds; Rodri's Golden Ball recognised a tournament of complete midfield authority. He finished his City career with 298 appearances, 28 goals and 14 major trophies.

## How he plays

Rodri's game is built on receiving. He drops into the pocket just in front of the centre-backs, shows for the ball on the half-turn and scans before it arrives, so that his first touch is already the start of the next pass. He completes an enormous volume of passes without being a safe passer: his diagonals to the wingers and his vertical balls into the No.8s break lines because they are played a beat early.

Defensively he is a positional midfielder rather than a tackler. He reads where a counter-attack will go and is already there, using his height and long legs to block, intercept and win headers. He commits few fouls and even fewer dangerous ones. In the final third he arrives late to the edge of the box, where his low, driven right-footed strike has produced a steady supply of important goals.

His weaknesses are straightforward: he lacks acceleration, so a quick runner coming from deep can get past him, and when a side presses him man-to-man and denies the first pass his team can look stuck. His body has also become a factor: the ACL injury and a minor back operation in July 2026 have made his availability something to be managed rather than assumed.

## Evolution

At Villarreal he was an all-action No.6 in a 4-4-2 who covered ground. Simeone made him a screener. Guardiola rebuilt him as the single pivot in a 4-3-3 and later as half of a double pivot alongside an inverted full-back such as John Stones, which gave City a 3-2 build-up shape that other teams copied. After the ACL injury he returned a little less mobile but even more economical, choosing moments to step in rather than chasing. With Spain, [Luis de la Fuente](/managers/luis-de-la-fuente) uses him as the fixed point around which [Pedri](/players/pedri) and [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal) are free to roam, and at Barcelona under [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick) he has slotted into the same role at the base of a 4-2-3-1, with Pedri and Frenkie de Jong ahead of or beside him.

## Best plays and moments

- 2018, La Liga with Villarreal: a breakthrough season that persuaded Atlético to buy him back.
- 2021, Premier League: ever-present in City's title run-in as the team won 12 league games in a row.
- 2022, Premier League final day v Aston Villa: part of the comeback from 2-0 down to win 3-2 and the title.
- 2023, Champions League final v Inter: the 68th-minute winner in Istanbul, City's first European Cup ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Rodri+goal+Champions+League+final+2023+Inter)).
- 2023, Nations League final v Croatia: man of the match as Spain win on penalties.
- 2024, Euro 2024 final v England: an injury at half-time, but player of the tournament as Spain win 2-1.
- 2024, Ballon d'Or ceremony, Paris: first City player to win the award.
- 2026, World Cup final v Argentina, MetLife Stadium: captains Spain to a 1-0 extra-time win and collects the Golden Ball ([Spain highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Rodri+Spain+World+Cup+2026+highlights)).
- 2026, 18 August: unveiled at Barcelona after a move reported at around £65m, taking the No.16 shirt vacated by Fermín López ([Barcelona debut](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Rodri+Barcelona+debut+2026)).

## Current situation

Rodri's transfer was the defining move of Barcelona's summer: City accepted a bid in mid-August, the player called leaving "a tough decision" and said Barcelona had always been his preferred destination, and he signed to June 2030. The reigning [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) champions began 2026-27 with six wins from six, with Rodri anchoring a midfield that also absorbed [Anthony Gordon](/players/anthony-gordon)'s arrival on the wing. Back surgery in late July delayed his start, and his minutes are being handled carefully after a long World Cup summer. For City, who replaced him with the £125m signing of [Enzo Fernández](/players/enzo-fernandez), his departure closed the Guardiola chapter for good. He remains captain of the world champions.
