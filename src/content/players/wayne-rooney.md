## Overview

Wayne Rooney arrived in the Premier League at 16 as a goalscoring hurricane and left [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) 13 years later as the club's record scorer with 253 goals. He won five league titles and the 2008 Champions League, and for seven years he was [England](/nations/england)'s record scorer too, with 53 goals in 120 caps, before [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) overtook him. His career is a study in sacrifice: he was good enough to be a pure striker and generous enough to spend much of his prime as something else.

## Early life and breakthrough

Rooney grew up in Croxteth, Liverpool, an Everton fanatic who joined the club's academy at nine. He scored eight goals in eight games as Everton reached the 2002 FA Youth Cup final and was promoted at 16. On 19 October 2002, five days before his 17th birthday, he came off the bench against Arsenal, took the ball on the turn 30 yards out and curled it in off the bar past David Seaman, ending Arsenal's 30-match unbeaten run. "Remember the name," said the commentator.

He became England's youngest scorer at 17 in September 2003 and then exploded at Euro 2004, scoring four goals in four games against Switzerland and [Croatia](/nations/croatia) before breaking a metatarsal against [Portugal](/nations/portugal). Two months later United paid Everton a reported £27 million, then a record for a teenager.

## Peak years

Rooney's United debut, in September 2004 against Fenerbahçe in the Champions League, produced a hat-trick. Alongside [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) and Carlos Tevez he formed the interchanging front three of the 2006-07 to 2008-09 sides that won three straight titles and the 2008 Champions League, often playing wide left to let Ronaldo thrive.

After Ronaldo left in 2009, [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) made Rooney the central striker and he scored 34 goals in 2009-10, winning the PFA Player of the Year award. A contract stand-off in October 2010 was resolved with a new deal, and in February 2011 he scored the overhead kick against [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) that defines his highlight reel. The 2011-12 season brought 34 more goals, and in 2012-13 he played deeper while [Robin van Persie](/players/robin-van-persie) led the line to Ferguson's 13th title.

## How he played

Rooney received the ball with his back to goal and spun in one movement, carrying it at defenders with low centre of gravity and a strong upper body that made him hard to knock off. He loved to shoot early from 20 to 25 yards and his striking of a dropping ball, volleys and half-volleys, was technically superb. He was two-footed enough to finish on either side and a fine crosser when used wide.

His greatest attribute was football intelligence allied to work-rate. He saw passes other strikers did not and had a genuine playmaker's range, which is why managers kept dropping him into midfield. He pressed from the front before pressing was fashionable and tracked back without complaint.

Weaknesses: temperament in his younger years, with red cards against Portugal at the 2006 World Cup and Montenegro in 2011. His explosiveness faded early, from around 28, partly because of the mileage of playing every position, and his finishing was streaky rather than clinical compared with Ronaldo or [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry).

## Evolution

At Everton he was a second striker with licence to roam. Under Ferguson he was a left-sided forward, then a lone striker, then a number ten behind van Persie. David Moyes and Louis van Gaal used him as a striker and, increasingly, a central midfielder, and under van Gaal and [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) he was captain and a deep-lying passer. In January 2017 his free-kick at Stoke took him past Bobby Charlton as United's record scorer. He returned to Everton in 2017, scored a hat-trick against West Ham that included a goal from his own half, then played in MLS for D.C. United, where a famous sprint-tackle-and-assist against Orlando in 2018 showed the engine was still running. He finished as player-coach at Derby County.

## Legacy

Rooney is the most complete English forward of the Premier League era and, with [Steven Gerrard](/players/steven-gerrard) and [Frank Lampard](/players/frank-lampard-player), the face of the golden generation that never delivered a tournament. He was inducted into the Premier League Hall of Fame in 2022. He moved into management at Derby County, where he kept the club competitive through administration, then had short spells at D.C. United, Birmingham City and Plymouth Argyle, leaving Plymouth at the end of 2024. As of November 2026 he works as a pundit on the BBC's Match of the Day, and he has said he would be open to returning to the dugout for the right job.
