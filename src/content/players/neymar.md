## Overview

Neymar was supposed to be the successor to [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) and [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) as the world's best player, and for a few years in Barcelona, between 2013 and 2017, he looked like it. Then came the world-record €222m move to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), six seasons of brilliance interrupted by injury, a short and unhappy spell at Al Hilal, and in January 2025 a return to [Santos](/clubs/santos), the club where it all began. He is [Brazil](/nations/brazil)'s all-time record scorer, an Olympic champion and a Champions League winner, and the question of what he might have been is as much a part of his story as what he did.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Mogi das Cruzes, São Paulo, Neymar joined Santos at 11 and debuted at 17 in March 2009. By 2010 he was the best player in Brazil; by 2011 he had won the Copa Libertadores, the Puskás Award for a goal against Flamengo and a place in the Brazil side. He scored 136 goals for Santos and turned down Real Madrid and Chelsea before joining [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in 2013.

## Peak years

The 2014-15 season was his peak. Alongside Messi and Luis Suárez he was part of the "MSN" front three that scored 122 goals, won the treble and beat Juventus 3-1 in the Champions League final in Berlin, where Neymar scored the third. He scored 39 goals that season and finished third in the Ballon d'Or. In 2016 he captained Brazil to Olympic gold in the Maracanã, scoring in the final and the winning penalty against Germany; in March 2017 he scored twice and set up the winner in the 6-1 "remontada" against PSG.

PSG paid his €222m release clause in August 2017. He scored 118 goals in 173 games, won five Ligue 1 titles and reached the 2020 Champions League final, but a metatarsal fracture, repeated ankle injuries and a torn ACL playing for Brazil in October 2023 defined the second half of his twenties. Al Hilal paid €90m in 2023; he played seven games. He returned to Santos in January 2025, had knee surgery late that year and in January 2026 extended his contract to 31 December 2026 with the World Cup as the stated goal.

## How he plays

Neymar's dribbling at his peak was the most inventive in the world: elastico, rainbow flick, sombrero, used not for show but because they beat the defender. He received on the left, cut inside and either shot with his right foot or played the pass through the gap his run had opened. He was a genuine playmaker, with 100-plus assists in Europe, and a free-kick specialist with a bending right foot.

The physical decline has been steep. At 34, after the ACL and the knee surgery, his acceleration is gone, and he plays as a walking 10 who relies on vision and set-pieces. He has never pressed, his durability has been poor since 2018, and his theatrical reactions to fouls made him a target. In 2026 he played only a fraction of Santos's matches.

## Evolution

A second striker at Santos, a left winger at Barcelona under Tata Martino and Luis Enrique, a free forward at PSG under Emery, Tuchel, Pochettino and Galtier, a 10 at Al Hilal, and back at Santos a central playmaker protected by the team. For Brazil, he played left and central under Dunga, Tite and Dorival, and his role, if any, under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) at the 2026 World Cup should be checked against current reports.

- 2011, Copa Libertadores: Santos's first continental title since Pelé.
- 2011, Brasileirão, v Flamengo: the Puskás Award-winning solo goal.
- 2013, Confederations Cup final, v Spain: a goal and Player of the Tournament.
- 2015, Champions League final, v Juventus: the third goal in Berlin to complete the treble ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Neymar+Barcelona+MSN+highlights)).

## Current situation

Neymar is at Santos on a contract that expires on 31 December 2026, having played a limited number of games in 2026 after knee surgery. In July 2026 AFP described his future as open, and no new club had been confirmed in the sources available for this profile. Whether the 2026 World Cup was his last tournament for Brazil, and whether he plays on in 2027, are open questions as of November 2026.
