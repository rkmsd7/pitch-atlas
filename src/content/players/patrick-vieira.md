## Overview

Patrick Vieira was [Arsène Wenger](/managers/arsene-wenger)'s first signing for [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), arriving before Wenger himself in August 1996 for 3.5 million pounds. Within two years he had won the Double and the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup). Within eight he had captained the Invincibles. His duels with [Roy Keane](/players/roy-keane) defined the Arsenal-[Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) rivalry, and for a decade he was the model of what a central midfielder could be: 1.93 m of tackling, striding and passing.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Dakar, Senegal, Vieira moved to France at eight and came through at Cannes, where he was captain at 19. [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) signed him in 1995 but played him twice in the league; Wenger, then at Nagoya Grampus and preparing to join Arsenal, told the club to sign him. He was immediately a starter at Highbury and won the FA Cup and league in 1997-98.

In the same summer he came on in the World Cup final against [Brazil](/nations/brazil) and set up Emmanuel Petit for the third goal of a 3-0 win. He was 22.

## Peak years

From 1998 to 2004 Vieira was the dominant midfielder in England. He partnered Petit, then Gilberto Silva, in a 4-4-2 that gave him licence to go forward, and he won titles in 2002 and 2004 and FA Cups in 2002, 2003 and 2005. He was made captain in 2002 after Tony Adams retired. With [France](/nations/france) he added Euro 2000 and the 2001 Confederations Cup, playing in a midfield with [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane) ahead of him.

The 2003-04 unbeaten season was his monument. He scored the goal against Leicester that secured it and lifted the trophy at Highbury. His last Arsenal act, in May 2005, was to score the winning penalty in the FA Cup final shootout against United. Wenger sold him to [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) that summer.

## How he played

Vieira was unique for the way he combined scale with elegance. His tackle reach meant he could win the ball from positions other midfielders could not, and his timing was good enough that he was not simply a fouler, despite nine Arsenal red cards. Once he had the ball he carried it with long strides that ate the ground, and he had the awareness to release it at the right moment, usually to Henry, Pires or Ljungberg.

He was not a prolific passer of the killer ball, nor a regular scorer, but he was the engine that converted defence into attack. He organised the pressing and set the physical tone of every match, and in big games he was often the best player on the pitch.

Weaknesses were discipline, a tendency to drift out of games when the team was not dominant, and, late in his career, mobility as his knees and groin gave way.

## Evolution

At Arsenal he was a box-to-box force in a two-man midfield. At Juventus under Fabio Capello he won [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a) in 2006, a title later revoked by the Calciopoli scandal, and at [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan) under [Roberto Mancini](/managers/roberto-mancini) and [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) he was a more conservative, deeper midfielder who won four straight titles but played less each year. He finished at [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) under Mancini, winning the 2011 FA Cup, the club's first trophy in 35 years, before retiring. He captained France at the 2006 World Cup final defeat to Italy.

## Legacy

Vieira is the midfielder every Arsenal signing since has been measured against, and the half of the Keane-Vieira axis that fans of both clubs still argue about. He has managed New York City FC, Nice, Crystal Palace, Strasbourg and Genoa, with mixed results and a reputation for building attractive, youthful teams. For France he is one of the last links to the 1998 side, and for Arsenal he is the captain of the only unbeaten season in modern English football.
