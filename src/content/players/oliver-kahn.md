## Overview

Oliver Kahn is the most intimidating goalkeeper of the modern era. For 14 years at [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) he was captain, conscience and last line, winning eight [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) titles and the 2001 [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league). For [Germany](/nations/germany) he carried a modest side to the 2002 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) final and won the tournament's Golden Ball, still the only goalkeeper to receive it. His nickname, Der Titan, was earned.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Karlsruhe to a footballing father, Kahn joined Karlsruher SC at six and made his Bundesliga debut in 1987. Under Winfried Schäfer he became one of the league's best keepers, famously helping KSC thrash Valencia 7-0 in the 1993-94 UEFA Cup. Bayern paid a then German record fee for a goalkeeper, 4.6 million marks, in 1994.

He won the UEFA Cup in 1996 and the first of his league titles in 1997, and was Germany's deputy to Andreas Köpke at Euro 96 before taking over the national jersey.

## Peak years

Between 1999 and 2002 Kahn was the best goalkeeper in the world, winning the IFFHS award three years running. The 1999 Champions League final defeat to [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) was a wound; the 2001 final against Valencia was the reply. Kahn saved three penalties in the shootout and Bayern won the trophy for the first time in 25 years. He also won four Bundesliga titles in five years under Ottmar Hitzfeld, who built his defence-first team on Kahn's authority.

In 2002 Germany went to the World Cup with low expectations. Kahn conceded one goal in six games on the way to the final, with heroic performances against Paraguay, the [United States](/nations/united-states) and [South Korea](/nations/south-korea). In the final against [Brazil](/nations/brazil) he spilled a Rivaldo shot and [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) scored. He sat alone against the post afterwards; the image became one of the tournament's defining pictures.

## How he played

Kahn was a goalkeeper of extremes. He was exceptionally quick off his line and in his reflexes, with a vertical leap that let him tip over shots other keepers could only watch. He attacked crosses rather than waiting, and in one-on-ones he made himself huge and came out fast. He was also a screamer who physically shoved defenders into position and once grabbed Borussia Dortmund's Heiko Herrlich by the neck. The aggression was part of the method: opponents felt watched.

Technically he was better on his feet than his wild reputation suggests, with good footwork across the goal and solid kicking. His distribution was functional, rarely a weapon.

Weaknesses were in the mind as much as the hands. His intensity occasionally tipped into errors under pressure, most notably in the 2002 final, and his confrontational style made for a volatile dressing room. He was not a modern sweeper keeper; his starting position was deep.

## Evolution

As a young man Kahn was all reflexes and rage. Under Hitzfeld he learned to channel it into organisation and became a captain in the German tradition. The rivalry with Jens Lehmann dominated his final national-team years; coach [Jürgen Klinsmann](/managers/jurgen-klinsmann) chose Lehmann for the 2006 World Cup on home soil, and Kahn's handshake with his rival before the Argentina quarter-final shootout became a symbol of sportsmanship. He played his last Germany game in the third-place play-off. At Bayern he retired in 2008, aged 38, after a final title.

## Best plays and moments

- 2001, Champions League final v Valencia: three shootout saves to win the trophy in Milan ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Oliver+Kahn+Valencia+2001+penalty+saves)).
- 2002, World Cup group stage v Cameroon: a string of saves as Germany went through.
- 2002, World Cup round of 16 v Paraguay: an outstanding display in a 1-0 win.
- 2002, World Cup semi-final v South Korea: a clean sheet in Seoul in front of a hostile crowd.
- 2001, Bundesliga final day v Hamburg: Kahn's late goal-line organisation as Patrik Andersson's injury-time free kick won the title.
- 1994, UEFA Cup v Valencia: the 7-0 for Karlsruhe that made his name.
- 1999, Champions League final v Manchester United: the night of two stoppage-time goals, remembered for his restraint afterwards.
- 2006, World Cup quarter-final v Argentina: the handshake with Lehmann before the shootout.
- 2006, World Cup third-place play-off v Portugal: his farewell game for Germany.
- 2008, Bundesliga: an eighth title in his final season.

## Legacy

Kahn is the template for the German goalkeeping tradition between Sepp Maier and Manuel Neuer: a captain in gloves, a man who won games by presence alone. He later took a business degree, worked as a television pundit and served as Bayern's chief executive from 2021 until a messy departure in 2023. Among players and supporters he remains the goalkeeper against whom intensity is measured.
