## Overview

Florian Wirtz is the most gifted German attacking midfielder since Mesut Özil and the most expensive signing in [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool)'s history, at around £116m from [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) in June 2025. At Leverkusen he was the creative centre of [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso)'s unbeaten 2023-24 Bundesliga champions. At Anfield his first 18 months have been a story of adaptation: flashes of the player who dismantled defences in Germany, interrupted by a system still being built around him and, in 2026-27, by a new manager in [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola).

## Early life and breakthrough

Wirtz grew up in Pulheim, near Cologne, and spent a decade in Köln's academy before a contentious switch to Leverkusen in January 2020. Four months later, aged 17 years and 15 days, he became the youngest goalscorer in Bundesliga history at the time with a goal against Bayern Munich. He was a regular by the autumn of 2020, and in 2021-22 produced ten goals and 14 assists in all competitions before an ACL tear in March 2022 cost him ten months. Germany gave him a debut in 2021 at 18, and he was a starter at Euro 2024 on home soil, scoring the tournament's opening goal against Scotland.

## Peak years

The arrival of Xabi Alonso in October 2022 transformed Leverkusen and made Wirtz the fulcrum. In 2023-24 they went unbeaten through 34 league games, the first club to do so, won the DFB-Pokal, and reached the Europa League final. Wirtz scored 18 goals and set up 20 in all competitions, including the hat-trick against Werder Bremen that sealed the title in April 2024. He was voted Bundesliga Player of the Season and German Footballer of the Year. A second strong season followed in 2024-25, and that summer Liverpool beat Bayern Munich and Manchester City to his signature, agreeing a contract to 2030.

## How he plays

Wirtz's great gift is finding space where there seems to be none. He hovers in the half-spaces just in front of the opposition midfield, shows for the ball at the moment a centre-back steps out, and takes his first touch across his body so that the pressing midfielder is already behind him. From there he drives forward, keeping the ball close with short touches, and his eyes are up throughout.

His decision-making in the final third is unusually mature. He will slow a counter-attack to let runners arrive, play a disguised reverse pass through a gap he saw two seconds earlier, or slip a ball into a channel for a striker. His own finishing is varied, with a preference for low side-footed shots and clipped finishes over the goalkeeper. He presses hard, which is rare for a creator, and his counter-pressing was a feature of Alonso's Leverkusen.

The weaknesses are physical. He is slight, can be bullied out of games by big, aggressive midfields, and has a history of muscle injuries. He is also a player who needs the team built around him; when he is asked to hug a touchline, or when the striker does not run in behind, his influence shrinks noticeably.

## Evolution

At Leverkusen under Peter Bosz and Gerardo Seoane he was a free-roaming No.10 in a 4-2-3-1. Alonso moved to a 3-4-2-1 and played him as one of two inside forwards behind a striker, which gave him more licence to drift and combine with Jeremie Frimpong on the right and Alejandro Grimaldo on the left. Arne Slot's Liverpool used him as a No.10 in a 4-2-3-1, with [Mohamed Salah](/players/mohamed-salah) and Cody Gakpo wide; his debut season brought a respectable but not spectacular return, and the discussion through 2025-26 was whether Liverpool were getting the best out of him. Iraola has kept him as the No.10 in 2026-27 but in a more vertical, pressing team, with [Alexander Isak](/players/alexander-isak) ahead of him and [Bradley Barcola](/players/bradley-barcola) running off him.

## Best plays and moments

- 2020, Bundesliga v Bayern Munich: the goal aged 17 that made him the youngest scorer in league history at the time ([best goals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Florian+Wirtz+best+goals+assists)).
- 2021, Bundesliga v Borussia Dortmund: two goals in a 4-3 defeat that announced his first full season.
- 2023, Europa League quarter-final v Union SG: a goal and assist as Leverkusen reach the semi-finals.
- 2024, Bundesliga v Werder Bremen: a hat-trick in the 5-0 win that clinched Leverkusen's first ever Bundesliga title ([title match](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Florian+Wirtz+Leverkusen+Werder+Bremen+5-0+title+hat-trick)).
- 2024, DFB-Pokal final v Kaiserslautern: part of the 1-0 win that completed the domestic double.
- 2024, Euro 2024 v Scotland: the opening goal of the tournament in Munich, a 5-1 win.
- 2025, Premier League: his first Liverpool goal, the moment the Anfield crowd had waited for ([Liverpool highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Florian+Wirtz+Liverpool+highlights+2026)).
- 2026, World Cup v Spain: part of the Germany side eliminated early in the tournament, a result that led to Jürgen Klopp's appointment as national coach.

## Current situation

Wirtz's 2026-27 season has begun slowly: no goals and one assist from his first six Premier League appearances, starting with the 2-2 draw at Newcastle on the opening day, where he played as the No.10 in Iraola's 4-2-3-1 and was substituted in the second half. Iraola challenged him and Isak publicly in pre-season, and former Liverpool players have debated whether the issue is form or role. His contract runs to 2030, and the club's position is that he is central to the project. For [Germany](/nations/germany), he is now working under [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), who took over in July 2026 after the World Cup, and who knows better than most how to build a team around a pressing No.10.
