## Overview

Diego Forlán is the striker who rewrote his own story. Written off in England after 17 goals in two seasons at [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united), he went to Spain and became one of the best finishers in Europe, winning the Pichichi and European Golden Shoe with [Villarreal](/clubs/villarreal) in 2005 and again with [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) in 2009. He scored twice in the 2010 Europa League final and that summer won the Golden Ball at the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) as [Uruguay](/nations/uruguay) reached the semi-finals, then added the 2011 [Copa América](/tournaments/copa-america). He is Uruguay's joint top scorer at World Cup finals.

## Early life and breakthrough

Forlán was born into football: his father Pablo played for Uruguay at the 1966 and 1974 World Cups and his grandfather Juan Carlos Corazzo coached the national team. He trained as a tennis player until his teens, played for Peñarol's youth teams and Nancy's academy in France, then broke through at Independiente in Argentina, where 37 goals in 80 games alerted Europe. Middlesbrough agreed a fee in January 2002 but [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) hijacked the deal and took him to Old Trafford for £7.5m.

## Peak years

The United years produced famous moments (two goals at Anfield in December 2002, a winner against Chelsea, the shirt-off celebration) but only ten league goals, and he joined Villarreal in 2004. In his first Spanish season he scored 25 league goals, outscoring Ronaldinho and Samuel Eto'o to win the Pichichi and the Golden Shoe. He reached the Champions League semi-final with Villarreal in 2006 and moved to Atlético for €21m in 2007 to partner [Sergio Agüero](/players/sergio-aguero), scoring 32 league goals in 2008-09 for a second Golden Shoe. In 2010 he carried Atlético to their first European trophy in 48 years, scoring both goals in the 2-1 Europa League final win over Fulham in Hamburg, then went to South Africa and scored five goals, including two against Germany in the third-place match, to win the Golden Ball.

## How he played

Forlán's shooting was his gift. He was comfortable with both feet to an unusual degree, and he struck the ball with a locked ankle that gave it a knuckling, dipping flight; the 2010 World Cup ball, the Jabulani, which most players hated, suited him perfectly. He scored from 25 and 30 yards regularly and his free kicks were flat and fast. In the box he finished early, rarely taking an extra touch.

He was also a complete team player. He dropped deep to link, worked the channels, pressed and tracked back, which made him ideal alongside a second striker such as Agüero or [Luis Suárez](/players/luis-suarez) in Uruguay's 4-4-2 and 4-3-1-2 under Óscar Tabárez. Weaknesses: he was not quick, not a great header for his height and could drift out of games when the service did not come, which is partly why United, with their emphasis on wing play and [Ruud van Nistelrooy](/players/ruud-van-nistelrooy) in the box, never got the best from him. His confidence was fragile early, and his form could swing with it.

## Evolution

At United he was a second striker; at Villarreal under Manuel Pellegrini he became a complete centre-forward in a possession side; at Atlético under Javier Aguirre and Quique Sánchez Flores he was the senior partner to Agüero and the team's set-piece taker. For Uruguay, Tabárez used him behind Suárez and Edinson Cavani, a playmaker-striker role he adapted to in his thirties. A disappointing spell at [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), a cult period at Internacional in Brazil and seasons in Japan, back home with Peñarol, India with [Mumbai City](/clubs/mumbai-city) and Hong Kong followed before he retired in 2019. He has coached Peñarol and Atenas and worked in media since.

## Best plays and moments

- 2002, Premier League, Liverpool 1-2 Manchester United: two goals at Anfield, including one from Jerzy Dudek's fumble ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Diego+Forlan+Liverpool+Anfield+2002+two+goals)).
- 2005, La Liga, Villarreal: 25 goals, the Pichichi and the European Golden Shoe.
- 2006, Champions League semi-final v Arsenal: the Villarreal side that pushed Arsenal to a last-minute penalty save.
- 2009, La Liga, Atlético Madrid: 32 goals and a second Golden Shoe, including a hat-trick against Athletic.
- 2010, Europa League final, Atlético 2-1 Fulham: both goals, the winner in extra time ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Diego+Forlan+Europa+League+final+2010+Fulham)).
- 2010, World Cup, Uruguay 3-0 South Africa: a stunning long-range opener in Pretoria.
- 2010, World Cup quarter-final v Ghana: a swerving free kick in the 1-1 draw before the famous shoot-out.
- 2010, World Cup semi-final v Netherlands: a 40-yard dipping strike past Maarten Stekelenburg ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Diego+Forlan+2010+World+Cup+goals)).
- 2010, World Cup third-place match v Germany: a volley and a late free kick off the bar, then the Golden Ball.
- 2011, Copa América final v Paraguay: two goals in the 3-0 win, Uruguay's record 15th title.

## Legacy

Forlán is the model of the late bloomer, the player who found the right league for his skills and became a continent's best striker at 26. Atlético fans regard his 2010 Europa League as the start of the modern club, the first trophy before [Diego Simeone](/managers/diego-simeone)'s arrival. In Uruguay he bridged the Enzo Francescoli generation and the Suárez-Cavani era, and the 2010 World Cup, with Forlán leading a nation of three million to the semi-finals, remains one of the tournament's great romantic stories. His 112 caps are the second most in Uruguay's history, and his five World Cup goals in 2010 made him only the second player in history to win the Golden Ball with a team that did not reach the final.
