## Overview

Sergio "Kun" Agüero is [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city)'s all-time top scorer with 260 goals in 390 games, the fourth-highest scorer in [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) history with 184 and the record holder for Premier League hat-tricks with 12. On 13 May 2012 he scored the 93rd-minute winner against QPR that gave City their first league title in 44 years, a goal referred to in Manchester simply by its clock time, 93:20. He won five Premier League titles, the Europa League with [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) and, in his final year, the 2021 [Copa América](/tournaments/copa-america) with [Argentina](/nations/argentina).

## Early life and breakthrough

Agüero grew up in Los Eucaliptos, a poor district of Quilmes outside Buenos Aires, and made his Independiente debut in July 2003 aged 15 years and 35 days, breaking Diego Maradona's record as the youngest player in the Argentine Primera. He won the Under-20 World Cup with Argentina in 2005 and again in 2007, where he was player of the tournament, and Atlético Madrid signed him in 2006 for around €20m. The following year [Diego Forlán](/players/diego-forlan) arrived and the two formed a partnership that produced the 2010 Europa League and UEFA Super Cup. In 2008 Agüero won Olympic gold in Beijing alongside [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi), scoring twice against [Brazil](/nations/brazil) in the semi-final.

## Peak years

City paid £35m in July 2011, and Agüero scored twice on his debut against Swansea. The season ended with the QPR goal. He was City's top scorer in each of his first nine full seasons, won the Golden Boot in 2014-15 with 26 goals, scored five in a game against Newcastle in October 2015 in a 20-minute spell, and became City's record scorer in November 2017 with a goal against Napoli. Under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) from 2016 he adapted to a more demanding pressing game and scored 20 or more league goals in four of five seasons, taking City to the 2018 centurions title (100 points) and the 2019 domestic treble. His last touch of the ball for City was a header in his final home game against Everton in May 2021, part of a late brace that took him past the record. Four weeks later he was a Copa América winner in Rio; a cardiac arrhythmia diagnosed at [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in October 2021 forced his retirement that December at 33.

## How he played

Agüero was the purest finisher of his era in England. His centre of gravity let him turn in a phone box, his first touch was so good that he could receive a pass with his back to goal, roll the defender and shoot before the centre-back had recovered. He shot early, often from the first touch, and hit the ball with both feet with the same technique: low, hard, across the keeper. He was an excellent header for his size.

Movement was his quieter skill: he lurked between the centre-backs, waited for the ball to be played wide and then made a short diagonal run to the front post or pulled off to the back. Weaknesses were obvious. He did not press consistently, which caused friction with Guardiola early on; his link play was functional rather than creative, and his fitness record was patchy, with hamstring, knee and calf injuries almost every season. His international record for Argentina, 41 goals in 101 games, was respectable but he never dominated a tournament; he was never quite the centre-forward Argentina wanted to pair with Messi, partly because they occupied similar spaces.

## Evolution

He arrived in Spain as a second striker and dribbler, more Tévez than Batistuta, and Atlético used him off Forlán. Under Roberto Mancini and Manuel Pellegrini at City he became a conventional number 9 in a 4-4-2 and 4-2-3-1, scoring from service by David Silva and Yaya Touré. Guardiola asked for more pressing and less dropping deep, and after a difficult first year, when Gabriel Jesus briefly took his place, Agüero became a more disciplined striker who stayed high and worked the half-spaces, with Kevin De Bruyne as his main supplier. The late-career version was a box poacher who could be devastating in 20 minutes. After retiring he became a streamer, a Kings League team owner and an occasional Argentina ambassador.

## Best plays and moments

- 2008, Olympic semi-final v Brazil: two goals in a 3-0 win on the way to gold.
- 2010, Europa League final v Fulham: the cross for Forlán's extra-time winner in Hamburg.
- 2011, Premier League, Manchester City 4-0 Swansea: two goals and an assist on debut as a substitute.
- 2012, Premier League, Manchester City 3-2 QPR: 93:20, the title-winning goal in stoppage time ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Sergio+Aguero+93+20+QPR+goal+2012)).
- 2014, Champions League group v Bayern Munich: a hat-trick in a 3-2 comeback win.
- 2015, Premier League, Manchester City 6-1 Newcastle: five goals in 20 minutes ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Sergio+Aguero+Premier+League+hat+tricks)).
- 2017, Champions League, Manchester City v Napoli: the goal that made him City's record scorer.
- 2018, League Cup final v Arsenal: the opener in a 3-0 win at Wembley.
- 2019, Premier League, Manchester City 1-0 Burnley: the goal that crossed the line by 29 mm in the title run-in.
- 2021, Premier League, Manchester City 5-0 Everton: two goals in his final home game to pass Wayne Rooney's record for most goals at one Premier League club.

## Legacy

A statue of Agüero, frozen in the QPR celebration, stands outside the Etihad Stadium. He is the striker who made City's project credible, the first global star to win trophies with the club, and the bridge between the Mancini era and Guardiola's. His finishing is routinely compared to that of [Erling Haaland](/players/erling-haaland), who inherited his number of expected goals and surpassed his single-season tallies but has not matched his longevity at the club. In Argentina he was Messi's closest friend in the squad and a figure of enormous public affection, and his early retirement, announced tearfully at the Camp Nou, is one of the sadder exits of the era. His place in the Premier League's top four scorers is secure, and 93:20 is forever.
