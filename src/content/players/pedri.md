## Overview

Pedri is the quiet heart of the two best teams in the world. At [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) he is the midfielder [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick) builds every possession around; for [Spain](/nations/spain) he was the connective tissue of the sides that won [Euro 2024](/tournaments/euro) and the [2026 World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup). He is not a goalscorer and he is not a flashy dribbler. He is simply the player opponents cannot get the ball off, and the one whose absence Barcelona feel most.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Tegueste on Tenerife, Pedri was rejected by Real Madrid after a trial as a teenager and instead joined Las Palmas, where he made his Segunda División debut at 16 in August 2019. Barcelona had already agreed to sign him for a modest initial fee before he had played a senior game. He arrived in the summer of 2020 expecting a loan and instead became a first-team regular under Ronald Koeman, playing 52 matches in his debut season at 18.

The summer of 2021 made him famous. He was the Young Player of the Tournament at Euro 2020 as a 18-year-old, famously completing every pass in the semi-final against Italy, then went straight to the Tokyo Olympics with Spain and won silver. That year he played 73 competitive matches, won the Golden Boy and the Kopa Trophy, and promptly suffered the first of a series of muscle injuries that would shape the next two years.

## Peak years

Pedri's best football has come since 2024. Having missed large chunks of 2022 and 2023 with hamstring problems, he returned fully fit for Euro 2024, where he started the group stage before a Toni Kroos tackle in the quarter-final against [Germany](/nations/germany) ended his tournament. Under Flick in 2024-25 he was the outstanding midfielder in [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga): a full season of 50-plus games, a league title, the Copa del Rey and the Supercopa, and a run to the Champions League semi-final.

He repeated it in 2025-26 as Barcelona retained the title, and in summer 2026 he finally got the full tournament he had been denied. Alongside Rodri and [Gavi](/players/gavi), he was the midfielder who gave Spain control in the knockout wins over Austria, [Portugal](/nations/portugal), [France](/nations/france) and, in the final, [Argentina](/nations/argentina).

## How he plays

Pedri's game is about body shape before he receives. He checks his shoulder constantly, takes the ball side-on and plays his first touch into the space the pressing opponent has just vacated. He is comfortable receiving with his back to goal under pressure, where a quick turn and a short pass are usually enough to break a line. His passing is more about timing than range: the ball arrives at the moment a teammate is ready, not before.

He presses intelligently, closing passing lanes rather than chasing, and his recovery runs are better than his slight frame suggests. The weaknesses are goal output (he has rarely reached double figures in a season) and durability. His hamstrings have been his career's greatest opponent, and Barcelona have learned to rotate him in cup competitions.

## Evolution

Koeman used him as a left-sided number 8 in a 4-3-3, almost a second playmaker next to Frenkie de Jong. Xavi moved him higher at times, closer to a number 10, which produced more goals but more injuries. Flick has settled him as the deeper of two 8s in a 4-2-3-1 that becomes a 3-2-5 in possession, where he partners de Jong or Marc Casadó and is responsible for the first pass out of pressure. For Spain, [Luis de la Fuente](/managers/luis-de-la-fuente) uses him as the left-sided interior ahead of Rodri, closer to the touchline so that [Nico Williams](/players/nico-williams) always has a passing option inside.

## Best plays and moments

- 2020, La Liga, v Real Madrid: his first Clásico at 17, a signal of what was coming.
- 2021, Euro 2020 semi-final, v Italy: 65 of 66 passes completed, a performance that defined his reputation.
- 2021, Olympics final, v Brazil: silver medal in Tokyo after a 73-game season.
- 2021, Champions League, v Bayern Munich: the goal in Munich that Xavi called "a Pedri goal".
- 2023, La Liga, v Sevilla: the goal that helped seal Barcelona's first title in four years.
- 2025, Copa del Rey final, v Real Madrid: the engine of the 3-2 extra-time win in Seville.
- 2026, World Cup final, v Argentina: ran the midfield in the 1-0 extra-time win ([highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Pedri+Spain+World+Cup+2026)).

## Current situation

Pedri is in his seventh season at Barcelona, now wearing the number 8 that belonged to [Andrés Iniesta](/players/andres-iniesta), and is in Flick's starting eleven every time he is fit. With Frenkie de Jong sidelined by a knee injury early in 2026-27 and [Raphinha](/players/raphinha) the new captain, he carries even more of the possession load. His contract runs to 2030. For Spain he is a certain starter under de la Fuente, who signed an extension after the World Cup, and the obvious successor to Rodri as the team's organiser.
