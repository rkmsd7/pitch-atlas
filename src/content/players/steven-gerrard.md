## Overview

Steven Gerrard was [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool)'s captain from 2003 to 2015 and the player who defined the club between its great eras. He scored 186 goals from midfield, led the comeback from 3-0 down against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) to win the 2005 [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) in Istanbul, scored one of the great FA Cup final goals a year later, and won 114 caps for [England](/nations/england). The one honour he never won was the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league), and the slip against [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in April 2014 that helped cost Liverpool the title is the cruel counterweight to everything else.

## Early life and breakthrough

Gerrard was born in Whiston and grew up on the Bluebell estate in Huyton, joining Liverpool's academy at nine. A childhood accident with a garden fork nearly cost him a toe. Gérard Houllier gave him his debut in November 1998 against Blackburn, and he was a first-team regular by the 2000-01 treble season, when Liverpool won the League Cup, FA Cup and UEFA Cup and he scored in the UEFA Cup final against Alavés. He made his England debut in 2000 and scored in the 5-1 win in Munich in 2001.

Houllier made him captain in October 2003, aged 23, and when Liverpool struggled he was repeatedly the man who kept them in the Champions League places on his own, including the 86th-minute goal against Olympiacos in December 2004 that kept them in the competition they would win.

## Peak years

Rafael Benítez's Liverpool reached the 2005 final as outsiders and were 3-0 down to Milan at half-time. Gerrard scored the header that began the comeback in the 54th minute, won the penalty for the equaliser, played the last half hour at right-back, and lifted the trophy after the shoot-out. He was UEFA Club Footballer of the Year and third in the Ballon d'Or. He was within hours of joining Chelsea that summer and changed his mind.

In 2006 he scored twice in the FA Cup final against West Ham, including a 35-yard volley in the 91st minute to force extra time, and was PFA Player of the Year. He reached a second Champions League final in 2007, scored 24 goals in 2008-09 as a second striker behind Fernando Torres, when Liverpool finished second with 86 points, and was Footballer of the Year.

His last great season was 2013-14 under Brendan Rodgers, when he was reinvented as a deep-lying playmaker behind [Luis Suárez](/players/luis-suarez) and Daniel Sturridge. Liverpool came within two points of the title; the slip against Chelsea and the collapse at Crystal Palace ended it. He left for LA Galaxy in 2015 after 710 appearances.

## How he played

Gerrard's defining quality was impact. He struck the ball harder and more cleanly than almost any midfielder of his generation, with 20 and 30-yard goals every season, and he had the athleticism to make 30 sprints a game. His long diagonal passing, from right to left with the outside of the foot or the instep, was a weapon that [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) and [Andrea Pirlo](/players/andrea-pirlo) admired.

He was a late-arriving goalscorer rather than a pure number 8: 186 goals from midfield includes a lot of penalties, but also the headers and volleys that made him Liverpool's talisman. Defensively he was a strong tackler, occasionally reckless, and he was sent off seven times.

The weakness was discipline of a different kind. He played on adrenaline and chose the Hollywood pass or the shot when a simple ball was on, which is why he and [Frank Lampard](/players/frank-lampard-player) never worked together for England and why Benítez tried to channel him by moving him to the right in 2005-06. He struggled when asked to play within strict positional limits, and a run of groin and hip injuries affected him from 2011.

## Evolution

Houllier played him as a box-to-box number 8 in a 4-4-2; Benítez used him as a right winger, a second striker behind Torres and finally a central midfielder in a 4-2-3-1. Kenny Dalglish restored him centrally. Rodgers's masterstroke in 2013 was to drop him to the number 6, where his passing range and leadership were preserved and his declining legs less exposed.

For England he was played out of position for years: left midfield at the 2006 World Cup, deep-lying under Roy Hodgson in 2012 and 2014. He captained England from 2012 and retired after the 2014 World Cup. In MLS he had a modest 18 months before retiring in November 2016.

## Legacy

Gerrard is the most complete English midfielder of the Premier League era, a player whose game included the goals of a striker, the range of a playmaker and the tackling of a destroyer. He is Liverpool's third-highest scorer, and the Istanbul final is told as his story. The absence of a league title, and the slip, are part of that story rather than diminishing it.

As a manager he won the Scottish Premiership with [Rangers](/clubs/rangers) in 2020-21, ending [Celtic](/clubs/celtic)'s run of nine, had an unsuccessful spell at [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa) in 2021-22 and coached Al-Ettifaq in the [Saudi Pro League](/leagues/saudi-pro-league) from 2023 until leaving by mutual consent in January 2025. In spring 2026 he was in talks with Burnley and was offered the Bristol City job; as of the 2025-26 season he remained without a club, and any appointment since could not be verified at the time of writing.
