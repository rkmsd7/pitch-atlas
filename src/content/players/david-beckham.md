## Overview

David Beckham was a footballer first and a phenomenon second, though the second tended to obscure the first. Over 21 seasons he won league titles in four countries, the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) with [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in 1999, and 115 caps for [England](/nations/england), 59 as captain. He was never the fastest or most skilful player, but he had the best right foot of his era for crossing and dead balls, and a stamina that made him a worker as much as a star.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Leytonstone to a United-supporting family, Beckham signed schoolboy forms with the club at 14 and was part of the 1992 FA Youth Cup-winning side, the "Class of 92" with [Ryan Giggs](/players/ryan-giggs), [Paul Scholes](/players/paul-scholes), Nicky Butt and the Nevilles. A loan at Preston North End in 1995 toughened him, and [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) made him a regular in 1995-96, the season Alan Hansen said you could not win anything with kids. United won the Double.

On the opening day of 1996-97 he scored from the halfway line against Wimbledon, the goal that made him a national name.

## Peak years

From 1996 to 2003 Beckham was the right-sided engine of Ferguson's greatest teams. He was a key figure in the 1999 treble, delivering both corners that produced the stoppage-time goals against [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) in the Champions League final, and finished second in the Ballon d'Or that year behind Rivaldo. He led the league in assists repeatedly and scored 85 goals for the club, many from free kicks.

Internationally his story was more dramatic. Sent off against [Argentina](/nations/argentina) at the 1998 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), he was vilified, then rehabilitated by a performance of extraordinary will against Greece in October 2001, when his stoppage-time free kick sent England to the 2002 World Cup. There he scored the penalty that beat Argentina.

His relationship with Ferguson soured and he moved to [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) in 2003 as the last of the Galácticos, playing alongside [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane), [Luís Figo](/players/luis-figo), [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario) and [Raúl](/players/raul). The team underachieved for three seasons; in his fourth, under Fabio Capello, he fought back from exile to win [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) in 2007.

## How he played

Beckham's technique was built on a long, straight run-up, a planted left foot and a wrapping motion of the right instep that imparted both pace and curl. From open play he crossed from deep rather than going to the byline, which suited strikers who attacked the ball like Andy Cole and Dwight Yorke, and later Ruud van Nistelrooy. His diagonals from right to left, often to Giggs, were a weapon in themselves.

He was not a dribbler, and he lacked the burst to beat a full-back, so he relied on angles and early delivery. Off the ball he was tireless, tracking back, covering his full-back and pressing with a work rate that explains why coaches as different as Ferguson, Capello and Carlo Ancelotti trusted him.

His free kicks, struck with the instep and curling over the wall and down, were among the most reliable of the era. Weaknesses were the lack of pace, a thin left foot and, occasionally, a temper that led to the 1998 red card and a dismissal against Austria in 2005.

## Evolution

At United he was a winger in a 4-4-2. At Madrid Capello moved him into central midfield, where his passing range flourished and his legs could not be exposed, and he was excellent in the 2007 title run. At [LA Galaxy](/clubs/la-galaxy) he was a central playmaker and the face of a league, winning two [MLS](/leagues/mls) Cups. Loans to [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) under Ancelotti kept him in England contention until an Achilles rupture in 2010 ended his international career. He finished with a short spell at [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), won [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1) and retired in May 2013.

## Best plays and moments

- 1996, Premier League v Wimbledon: the halfway-line lob over Neil Sullivan ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Beckham+halfway+line+goal+Wimbledon)).
- 1999, Champions League final v Bayern Munich: two corners, two goals, the treble.
- 2001, World Cup qualifier v Greece: the 93rd-minute free kick at Old Trafford ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Beckham+free+kick+Greece+2001)).
- 2002, World Cup group stage v Argentina: the penalty that avenged 1998.
- 1999, FA Cup semi-final replay v Arsenal: a curling opener in the match of Giggs's famous goal.
- 2000, Premier League v Chelsea: a free kick that typified United's dominance.
- 2007, La Liga v Mallorca: the final-day title win in his last Madrid game.
- 2011, MLS Cup final v Houston Dynamo: a first MLS title with LA Galaxy.
- 2006, World Cup round of 16 v Ecuador: a free-kick winner, making him the first Englishman to score at three World Cups.
- 2013, Ligue 1 v Brest: a farewell appearance as PSG captain.

## Legacy

Beckham's influence stretches beyond the pitch into fashion, celebrity and business, but the football case is strong on its own: six Premier League titles, the Champions League, 115 caps, the first Englishman to win titles in four countries. He co-owns [Inter Miami](/clubs/inter-miami), the club that brought [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) to MLS, and remains the most recognisable English footballer in the world.
