## Overview

Kaká won the 2007 Ballon d'Or in the season he dragged [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) to the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) with 10 goals, including both in the semi-final first leg against [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united). He was the bridge between the classic number 10 and the modern ball-carrying attacking midfielder, a player who ran the length of the pitch rather than waiting in pockets. He was part of [Brazil](/nations/brazil)'s 2002 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) squad at 20 and later a world-record signing for [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), where injuries blunted him.

## Early life and breakthrough

Ricardo Izecson dos Santos Leite was born in Gama, near Brasília, into a middle-class, devoutly evangelical family, which made him an unusual figure among Brazilian stars. His younger brother Digão could not pronounce "Ricardo" and called him Kaká. At 18 he fractured a vertebra in a swimming-pool accident and was told he might never walk normally again; his full recovery deepened the faith that defined his public image.

He broke into the São Paulo first team in 2001, scored 12 goals in his first full season and won the Rio-São Paulo tournament, scoring twice in the final. Luiz Felipe Scolari took him to the 2002 World Cup, where he played 25 minutes against Costa Rica, enough to earn a winner's medal.

## Peak years

Milan paid €8.5 million for him in 2003, a fee Silvio Berlusconi later called "peanuts". Carlo Ancelotti put him at the tip of his midfield diamond, behind [Andriy Shevchenko](/players/andriy-shevchenko) and in front of [Andrea Pirlo](/players/andrea-pirlo), Gennaro Gattuso and [Clarence Seedorf](/players/clarence-seedorf). Milan won [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a) in his first season and reached the 2005 Champions League final, where Kaká produced the assist for Hernán Crespo's second goal in Istanbul before the collapse against [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool).

The 2006-07 season was his monument. With Shevchenko gone to [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea), Kaká became the primary scorer: a hat-trick against Anderlecht, a run through Celtic's defence in the last 16, the double at Old Trafford, and the winning run of form that beat Liverpool 2-1 in the Athens final, with Kaká's touch setting up Filippo Inzaghi's second. He was top scorer in the competition, won the Ballon d'Or, FIFA World Player of the Year and the Club World Cup. He was 25.

Real Madrid paid €67 million in June 2009, a world record for a few days until [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) arrived. A knee injury carried from the 2010 World Cup required surgery, and he never regained his acceleration. He still won La Liga in 2012 under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho), mostly as a rotation player.

## How he played

Kaká's defining attribute was speed with the ball. His strides were long and his touch, usually with the outside or top of the right foot, pushed the ball just far enough ahead that a defender who committed was beaten, but never so far that he lost it. Milan's counter-attacks ran through him: Pirlo or Gattuso won it, Kaká took it on the half-turn in the centre circle, and within four seconds he was shooting.

He finished with his instep low and across the keeper, and could shoot from 25 yards with minimal backlift. He was tall and strong, difficult to muscle off the ball, and a good passer in behind, though not a complex one; his decision-making was about timing the release rather than disguising it. He was not a dribbler in tight spaces like [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho) and did not thrive when asked to play with his back to goal in a crowded final third. He was also a willing but not natural defender.

## Evolution

At São Paulo he was a second striker. Ancelotti's diamond gave him a free role as trequartista, and as Shevchenko and Inzaghi aged, Ancelotti increasingly pushed him higher, almost as a forward in a 4-3-2-1 "Christmas tree" with Seedorf also behind the striker. At Madrid, Manuel Pellegrini used him on the left of a 4-2-3-1, then Mourinho asked him to play as a number 10 behind Karim Benzema, where he competed with Mesut Özil and often lost.

A return to Milan in 2013-14 as captain produced nine goals for a poor side. He finished in MLS with Orlando City as a franchise player and captain, where he played a deeper, roaming creative role and was an All-Star three times before retiring in 2017.

## Legacy

Kaká was the last player to win the Ballon d'Or before Messi and Ronaldo shared the next ten, and his 2007 season is routinely listed among the best individual Champions League campaigns. He showed that a number 10 could be a vertical, transition-based weapon rather than a slow conductor, a template later followed by [Jude Bellingham](/players/jude-bellingham) and others.

His clean image, "I belong to Jesus" shirt and the Madrid disappointment have sometimes led to him being underrated. Among those who saw the 2006-07 Milan side, there is no such doubt. He retired in December 2017 and now works in media and as an ambassador, with occasional reports linking him to a sporting-director role at São Paulo.
