## Overview

Mohamed Salah is the greatest African player of the Premier League era and, by most measures, the most productive forward [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) have ever had. Signed from Roma in 2017 for a fee that looked modest within months, he scored at a rate that no winger had managed in English football, won every major trophy available to the club under [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp), and then drove Arne Slot's side to the 2024-25 title in his ninth season. His Anfield story closed in summer 2026, after a hamstring injury cut short a farewell campaign.

## Early life and breakthrough

Salah grew up in Nagrig, a village in Egypt's Nile Delta, and made a daily round trip of several hours to train with El Mokawloon in Cairo. Basel took him to Switzerland in 2012, where his pace and directness caught the eye of Chelsea, who signed him in January 2014. He was a bit-part figure under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) and was loaned to Fiorentina and then Roma, where he found his best position: a wide forward cutting in from the right. Two prolific seasons in Serie A, including 15 league goals in 2016-17, convinced Klopp that Salah could lead a front three built on pressing and transitions.

## Peak years

The first season at Liverpool was a record: 32 Premier League goals, the most in a 38-game campaign at the time, and 44 in all competitions. He was injured early in the 2018 Champions League final against Real Madrid, but a year later he scored the penalty that opened a 2-0 win over Tottenham in Madrid as Liverpool won their sixth European Cup. The 2019-20 title followed, ending a 30-year wait. Between 2017 and 2025 he was top scorer in the league four times and consistently among the leaders for assists too. In 2024-25, aged 32, he produced his best all-round season, with 29 goals and 18 assists as Slot's Liverpool won the league with games to spare and he collected a second PFA award.

## How he plays

Salah starts high and wide on the right but his most dangerous moments come without the ball. He waits on the shoulder of the left-back, then bends his run inside between the full-back and the centre-back as the ball travels, so that he is arriving in the box at pace at the exact moment a cut-back or through-ball is played. His first touch when receiving to feet is taken infield, opening the body to his left foot, and he shoots early and low with a very short backlift, which is why goalkeepers often cannot set themselves.

He is comfortable in one-on-one duels but is not a trick dribbler; he beats defenders with a change of direction and acceleration over the first five yards, and he is strong enough to ride challenges. His decision-making in the final third is ruthless: he shoots when the angle is poor more often than purists would like, but his conversion rate justifies it. He took Liverpool's penalties for years and is a willing presser, especially at Anfield, triggering the counter-press that defined Klopp's football.

Weaknesses are a right foot used mostly for standing on, a tendency to drift out of games when the service down his side dries up, and, from 2024 onwards, a gradual decline in top-end sprint speed, which he offset with smarter positioning.

## Evolution

At Basel and Chelsea he was a straight-line sprinter, a counter-attacking winger. Roma taught him to play from the touchline in a settled possession side. Klopp's Liverpool turned him into a goalscorer first and winger second, with Roberto Firmino dropping deep to open the space he ran into and [Trent Alexander-Arnold](/players/trent-alexander-arnold) providing the supply from behind. Under Slot in 2024-25 the structure changed: Liverpool held the ball more, Salah touched it more, and his assist numbers climbed as he became a creator in the right half-space as much as a finisher. By his final season he was the senior statesman of a squad being rebuilt around [Alexander Isak](/players/alexander-isak) and [Florian Wirtz](/players/florian-wirtz).

## Best plays and moments

- 2017, Premier League v Watford: scores on his debut in a 3-3 draw, the first of 44 goals that season ([best goals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mohamed+Salah+best+goals+Liverpool)).
- 2018, Premier League v Roma: two goals and two assists in the 5-2 Champions League semi-final first leg against his former club, refusing to celebrate.
- 2018, World Cup qualifier v Congo: a stoppage-time penalty that sends Egypt to their first World Cup since 1990.
- 2019, Champions League final v Tottenham: the second-minute penalty in Madrid as Liverpool win their sixth European Cup.
- 2019, Champions League semi-final v Barcelona: absent injured but his "Never Give Up" shirt becomes the image of the 4-0 comeback ([match](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Liverpool+4-0+Barcelona+2019+highlights)).
- 2021, Premier League v Manchester City: the solo goal at Anfield, slaloming past four defenders, widely voted Premier League goal of the season.
- 2021, Premier League v Watford: a flick-and-spin finish that even Klopp called one of the best goals he had seen.
- 2025, Premier League v Tottenham: a goal and assist in the 5-1 win that clinched Liverpool's 20th title, followed by a selfie with the Kop.

## Current situation

Salah's Liverpool career ended in summer 2026. In March 2026 the club confirmed he would leave when the season finished, despite the two-year contract he had signed the previous spring, and a hamstring injury in April meant his final Anfield outings came earlier than anyone wanted. He left as a free agent with 442 appearances and 257 goals, third on the club's all-time scoring list behind Ian Rush and Roger Hunt. On 6 August 2026 he signed a two-year contract with Trabzonspor in Turkey's [Süper Lig](/leagues/super-lig), reportedly worth around €17m a season, was unveiled in a symbolic No.61 shirt and then took the No.10 he wears for Egypt. He came off the bench for his debut in a 1-1 draw with Kasımpaşa on 15 August and by mid-October had scored seven goals in seven league games. At Liverpool, now managed by [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola), [Bradley Barcola](/players/bradley-barcola) was signed to fill the right-sided role. For [Egypt](/nations/egypt), he remains captain and the country's record goalscorer, with the 2027 Africa Cup of Nations the next target.
