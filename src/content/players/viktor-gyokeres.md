## Overview

Viktor Gyökeres took the long road to the top. Released by [Brighton](/clubs/brighton-and-hove-albion) without a Premier League start, he scored his way through the Championship with Coventry City and then produced one of the great modern goalscoring spells at [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp), 97 goals in 102 games across two seasons. [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) paid around £64m in the summer of 2025 and he won the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) in his first season. His second has been harder, with [Kai Havertz](/players/kai-havertz) holding the No.9 shirt on form.

## Early life and breakthrough

Gyökeres grew up in Stockholm and came through at Brommapojkarna, a club with a famous youth system, scoring 13 goals in Sweden's second tier as a teenager. Brighton signed him in January 2018 but never found a place for him: loans to St. Pauli in Germany and then Swansea City and Coventry in the Championship followed, and it was Coventry who bought him in 2021. Two full seasons there, including 21 league goals and a play-off final in 2022-23, made him one of the division's most feared forwards.

## Peak years

Sporting paid around €20m in 2023 and Rúben Amorim built his attack around him. The numbers were absurd: 43 goals in 2023-24 as Sporting won the Primeira Liga, then 54 in 2024-25 to win the European Golden Shoe and a second title, with a Taça de Portugal added. The hat-trick against [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) in the Champions League in November 2024, in Amorim's last home game before leaving for Manchester United, made him a global name.

A drawn-out transfer saga ended with Arsenal in July 2025. He started well in England, with a brace against Leeds in his first home league game, but a mid-season dip and a strong run of form from Havertz meant he was more rotation option than talisman by the spring. He still contributed to the title, the club's first in 22 years, and came on in the Champions League final in Budapest that Arsenal lost on penalties to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain).

## How he plays

Gyökeres is a striker of momentum. His best work starts 30 yards from goal: he reads when a centre-back is a yard too high, sprints into the channel and takes the ball on the run, using a powerful frame to hold off defenders before shooting early and low. He rarely needs more than two touches in the box and he hits the ball hard with minimal backlift, which is why so many of his goals come before the goalkeeper is set.

He presses with real aggression, is a reliable penalty taker and is good in the air from crosses, less so from set pieces. The limitations show against teams that sit deep: he does not have the close control to wriggle through crowded areas, his link play is functional rather than creative, and in a possession-heavy side that does not play in behind he can be left standing between the centre-backs with nothing to run on to. That, more than any lack of finishing, is why Havertz's more fluid game has suited Arsenal.

## Evolution

In Sweden and the Championship he was a wide forward or second striker who used his pace in transition. Amorim's 3-4-3 at Sporting turned him into a lone, direct No.9 with two number tens feeding him, and his game became purely about running and finishing. At Arsenal, [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) asked for more link play and more patience against low blocks. He has worked on his hold-up play and movement across the line, but the tension between his instincts and Arsenal's control-first approach has not fully resolved.

For [Sweden](/nations/sweden) he has been the first-choice striker since 2023, partnering [Alexander Isak](/players/alexander-isak).

## Current situation

Gyökeres said in August 2026 that he felt in far better physical shape than when he arrived, but the early weeks of 2026-27 have been frustrating: two starts in Arsenal's first six matches and no goal from 154 league minutes by early October, with Havertz firmly in possession of the central role. Arsenal closed the door on a sale in the summer of 2026, though reports suggest a move in 2027 is possible if a top-class replacement is found. He remains a valuable option for the champions, who sat top of the table in early November, and the main striker for [Sweden](/nations/sweden).
