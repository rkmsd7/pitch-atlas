## Overview

Gabriel Magalhães is the enforcer in [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal)'s defence and, from set pieces, one of its most reliable goalscorers. Signed from [Lille](/clubs/lille) in 2020 for around £27m, he was a fixture through the whole of [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s rebuild, and his partnership with [William Saliba](/players/william-saliba) gave the club the platform for the 2025-26 [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) title, the first in 22 years. He is a regular for [Brazil](/nations/brazil) under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti).

## Early life and breakthrough

Gabriel grew up in São Paulo and came through at Avaí in Santa Catarina, making his senior debut in 2016. Lille signed him in January 2017, but his early years in France were stop-start: loans to Troyes and Dinamo Zagreb went nowhere, and he did not become a Lille regular until 2019-20, when he was outstanding in a side that finished fourth in Ligue 1 and played in the Champions League. Napoli, Everton and Manchester United were all interested before Arsenal won the race in September 2020.

## Peak years

He scored on his Premier League debut at Fulham and was a starter from day one, though Arsenal's defence in 2020-21 and 2021-22 was still a work in progress. Saliba's return in 2022 changed everything. With a quick, composed partner on his right, Gabriel's aggression became an asset rather than a risk, and from 2022-23 the pair were the league's best central partnership, with Arsenal conceding fewer goals than anyone across 2023-24 and 2024-25.

The set-piece goals became a trademark in those seasons, with Gabriel attacking the near post from in-swinging corners. In 2025-26 he played the bulk of the title-winning season and the Champions League final in Budapest, lost 4-3 on penalties to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) after a 1-1 draw.

## How he plays

Where Saliba waits, Gabriel attacks. His game is about engaging the forward early, winning the first contact and clearing danger with authority. He is strong in the air at both ends and times his jumps well, and his left foot gives Arsenal's build-up natural balance: he plays out to the left-back, clips passes into the left half-space and occasionally drives into midfield when space opens.

At attacking corners he is Arsenal's most dangerous weapon, flicking on at the near post or finishing direct from [Declan Rice](/players/declan-rice) and [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka) deliveries. He scores between four and six goals a season from set plays, an exceptional return for a centre-back.

His weaknesses are the flip side of his strengths. He can be too keen to step out, leaving a gap that a quick forward exploits, and his physicality sometimes earns needless fouls and yellow cards. Opponents have tried to provoke him, with mixed success.

## Evolution

Lille's Christophe Galtier used him as the left centre-back in a compact 4-4-2 that defended deep and countered. Arteta initially paired him with Rob Holding or Ben White in a back four that defended in a mid block; the tactical shift to a high line in 2022, built around Saliba's recovery pace, let Gabriel push up and win the ball higher. As Arsenal's left-back became an inverted midfielder in possession, he also took on more responsibility for covering the left flank. His discipline has improved markedly since his first two seasons.

## Current situation

Gabriel remains Arsenal's first-choice left centre-back in 2026-27, with the champions top of the table in early November and once again the division's meanest defence. His partnership with Saliba, now contracted to 2030, remains the foundation of Arteta's side, and his set-piece threat is as valuable as ever in a team that scores so many from dead balls. For [Brazil](/nations/brazil) he is part of the core that Ancelotti took to the 2026 World Cup.
