## Overview

Gareth Bale won five [Champions Leagues](/leagues/champions-league) with [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), scored in two of the finals, 2014 and 2018, and produced the 2018 overhead kick against [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) that many consider the greatest goal in a European Cup final. He is [Wales](/nations/wales)'s record scorer with 41 goals, captained them to the [Euro](/tournaments/euro) 2016 semi-final and the 2022 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), their first since 1958, and when he moved from [Tottenham](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) to Madrid in 2013 for around €100m he was the most expensive footballer in the world. He retired in January 2023 at 33.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Cardiff, Bale was spotted by Southampton at nine and made his debut at 16 as a left-back with a free kick that marked him out. Tottenham signed him in 2007 for £5m rising to £10m; famously he did not win a Premier League game in his first 24 appearances. Harry Redknapp moved him forward to left wing in 2009-10 and the transformation was immediate: in October 2010 he scored a hat-trick at San Siro against [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), running Maicon ragged, and destroyed the same full-back again at White Hart Lane two weeks later. He was PFA Player of the Year in 2011 and again in 2013, when he scored 21 league goals from a free central role under André Villas-Boas.

## Peak years

Madrid paid a world record fee in September 2013 and Bale repaid it immediately: 22 goals in his first season including the 2014 Copa del Rey final winner against [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), a 60-metre run off the pitch and back past Marc Bartra, and the extra-time header in the Champions League final against [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) that put Madrid ahead on the way to La Décima. He scored in the 2016 shoot-out win over Atlético, started the 2017 final in Cardiff and, as a substitute, scored twice in the 2018 final in Kyiv, the bicycle kick and a 40-yard shot through Loris Karius. With [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) and [Karim Benzema](/players/karim-benzema) he formed the "BBC" front line that won four European Cups in five years.

For Wales he was the star of Euro 2016, scoring three on the way to the semi-final against [Portugal](/nations/portugal), and his goals in the 2022 play-off against Ukraine took the country to Qatar.

## How he played

Bale's game was built on explosive straight-line pace, measured at over 36 km/h in La Liga, and the power of his left foot. He struck the ball with a locked ankle and a high follow-through that gave it lift and dip from 30 yards; his free kicks used the same knuckleball technique as Ronaldo's but with more pace. In the air he was a genuine threat, scoring from headers in two European finals. He was best when running onto the ball into space, either wide on the right cutting inside or through the middle.

He was not a tight-space dribbler and preferred to knock the ball past a defender and run. His link play was simple, his pressing adequate when motivated. Weaknesses: his injury record was dreadful, with recurring calf and hamstring problems that cost him more than 100 Madrid games; he was a peripheral figure under [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager) in the later seasons, who clearly preferred other options, and the relationship with Madrid's fans soured over perceived indifference, golf and the "Wales. Golf. Madrid. In that order" flag.

## Evolution

He moved from left-back to left wing to right wing to free forward across his Tottenham years, and at Madrid was used on the right by [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti), as a central striker when Benzema was absent under Zidane, and as an impact substitute late on. His 2020-21 loan back to Tottenham under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) produced 16 goals and showed he could still finish. He ended his Madrid contract in 2022 with a fifth Champions League and a third La Liga, played a half-season at LAFC, scored a 128th-minute equaliser in the MLS Cup final, played the 2022 World Cup and retired.

## Best plays and moments

- 2010, Champions League group, Inter 4-3 Tottenham: a second-half hat-trick at San Siro ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Gareth+Bale+hat+trick+Inter+San+Siro+2010)).
- 2010, Champions League group, Tottenham 3-1 Inter: two assists, Maicon "taxi for" chants.
- 2013, Premier League, West Ham 2-3 Tottenham: a 30-yard stoppage-time winner, PFA Player of the Year season.
- 2014, Copa del Rey final, Real Madrid 2-1 Barcelona: the sprint around Bartra in the 85th minute ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Gareth+Bale+Copa+del+Rey+final+2014+goal+Barcelona)).
- 2014, Champions League final v Atlético Madrid: the extra-time header for 2-1 in Lisbon, La Décima.
- 2016, Euro 2016 v Slovakia, England and Russia: free kicks and a goal in each group game ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Gareth+Bale+Wales+Euro+2016)).
- 2018, Champions League final, Real Madrid 3-1 Liverpool: the overhead kick, then a 40-yarder, as a substitute ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Gareth+Bale+bicycle+kick+Champions+League+final+2018)).
- 2021, Premier League, Tottenham 4-0 Sheffield United: a hat-trick in the loan season.
- 2022, World Cup play-off, Wales 1-0 Ukraine: his free kick, deflected in by Andriy Yarmolenko, sent Wales to a first World Cup since 1958.
- 2022, MLS Cup final, LAFC v Philadelphia Union: a 128th-minute header to force penalties, then the title.

## Legacy

Bale's Madrid career is one of the strangest in modern football: more decisive goals in European finals than almost any player alive, yet he was whistled at the Bernabéu and left with barely a farewell. The cleanest verdict is that his trophy haul, 16 at Madrid, settles the argument. For Wales he is the greatest player in the nation's history, the man who took a small country to a Euro semi-final and a World Cup; his retirement left [Aaron Ramsey](/players/aaron-ramsey)'s generation without its leader. He now plays golf semi-seriously and works in media, and his 2018 bicycle kick is in the permanent highlight reel of the Champions League.
