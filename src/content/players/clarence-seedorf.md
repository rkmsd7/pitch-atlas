## Overview

Clarence Seedorf won the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) with [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) in 1995, [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) in 1998 and [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) in 2003 and 2007, a record that nobody else holds. He made 432 appearances for Milan over a decade, playing until he was 36 in one of the best midfields in European history, and he did it all with a serenity that made him look like a man who had decided the result in advance. His [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) career, 87 caps, was long but strangely unfulfilled.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Paramaribo, Suriname, Seedorf moved to Amsterdam as a toddler and joined Ajax's academy at ten. Louis van Gaal gave him a debut at 16 in 1992, the youngest in club history at the time, and by 1995 he was a starter in the extraordinary Ajax side, with [Dennis Bergkamp](/players/dennis-bergkamp)'s successors Patrick Kluivert, Edgar Davids, Frank and Ronald de Boer and Marc Overmars, that beat Milan in the Champions League final in Vienna. He was 19. A year at Sampdoria under Sven-Göran Eriksson taught him Italian football and attracted Real Madrid, who signed him in 1996.

## Peak years

At Madrid he won La Liga in 1997 under Fabio Capello and the 1998 Champions League against [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) as the club ended a 32-year wait. His 1997 goal from the halfway line against Atlético Madrid remains a Bernabéu favourite. He left for [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) in 2000 and crossed the city to Milan in 2002 in a swap for Francesco Coco, one of the great pieces of transfer business.

Under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) Seedorf became the left-sided attacking midfielder of the 4-3-1-2 and later 4-3-2-1 "Christmas tree", playing beside [Andrea Pirlo](/players/andrea-pirlo), Gennaro Gattuso and [Kaká](/players/kaka). He won the Champions League in 2003 against Juventus, the Scudetto in 2004 and, after the trauma of Istanbul in 2005, the 2007 Champions League, where his semi-final performance against [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in the 3-0 second leg was one of his finest. A second Serie A title came in 2011 under Massimiliano Allegri.

## How he played

Seedorf's body was his weapon. He received the ball on the half-turn, used his thighs and backside to keep defenders off, and took three or four touches before deciding, which could frustrate team-mates but which almost never resulted in a turnover. He was genuinely two-footed and shot from 25 to 30 yards with both; a dozen of his best goals are long-range strikes. His passing was more about weight and timing than invention, and he excelled at the diagonal switch and the disguised pass into the striker.

He was not fast, and critics in the Netherlands never forgave him for a series of penalty misses, including in shoot-outs at Euro 1996, the 1998 World Cup and Euro 2000. He was also a divisive dressing-room figure, articulate and confident to the point that coaches like Marco van Basten left him out entirely. He pressed well for a player of his build but could be bypassed by quick passing in central areas, which is why Ancelotti positioned him wide-left of the midfield.

## Evolution

He was a central midfielder at Ajax, a box-to-box midfielder at Madrid, a trequartista at Inter and a left-sided midfielder-playmaker at Milan who moved into the centre when Kaká left in 2009 and Pirlo in 2011. His final Milan seasons saw him used as a creative substitute, still scoring. At Botafogo in Brazil, aged 36 to 38, he was the team's best player and a cult figure. He retired in January 2014 to become Milan's head coach, a brief and unhappy spell, followed by Shenzhen, Deportivo La Coruña and Cameroon, with whom he reached the 2019 [AFCON](/tournaments/afcon) round of 16. He has since worked as a pundit and in UEFA and FIFA advisory roles.

## Best plays and moments

- 1995, Champions League final, Ajax 1-0 AC Milan: a teenage starter in Vienna.
- 1997, La Liga, Real Madrid v Atlético Madrid: a 50-yard strike that bounced over the goalkeeper, one of the Bernabéu's most-replayed goals ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Clarence+Seedorf+goal+Atletico+Madrid+1997+long+range)).
- 1998, Champions League final v Juventus: a second European Cup at 22, the full 90 minutes.
- 2003, Champions League final v Juventus: a third, with a converted spot-kick in the shoot-out at Old Trafford.
- 2004, Serie A, Milan v Roma: a goal and a performance that sealed the Scudetto race.
- 2007, Champions League semi-final, Milan 3-0 Manchester United: a goal and total control of midfield after the 3-2 first-leg defeat ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Seedorf+Milan+Manchester+United+2007+semi+final)).
- 2007, Champions League final v Liverpool: fourth winner's medal, the record for three clubs ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Clarence+Seedorf+Champions+League+three+clubs)).
- 2007, Club World Cup final v Boca Juniors: a 4-2 win in Yokohama.
- 2011, Serie A, Milan: an 18th title and a goal against Lecce in a 4-3 comeback win.
- 2013, Brasileirão, Botafogo v Flamengo: a derby winner at 37.

## Legacy

Seedorf's record of Champions Leagues with three clubs is one of the sport's unmatched achievements, and his ability to be a key player in winning sides across four decades of football, 1995 to 2013, speaks to his adaptability. He was never the glamour name of any side he played in, but the Dutch, Spanish and Italian championships he collected and the respect of coaches as different as Van Gaal, Capello and Ancelotti say what he was. He remains one of the most prominent Black voices in European football administration and a frequent commentator on racism in the game.
