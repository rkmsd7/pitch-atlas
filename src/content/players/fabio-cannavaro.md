## Overview

Fabio Cannavaro captained [Italy](/nations/italy) to the 2006 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), conceded only two goals in seven matches (an own goal and a penalty) and became the first defender since Matthias Sammer in 1996, and the last as of 2026, to win the Ballon d'Or. He also won two La Liga titles with [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) and is Italy's second most-capped player with 136 appearances. For a centre-back who stood 1.76 m, he spent his career proving that height is a detail.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Naples, Cannavaro was a ball boy at the San Paolo and famously, as a Napoli youth player, tackled Diego Maradona in training so hard that coaches apologised. Maradona told them to leave him alone. He made his Serie A debut in 1993 and moved to Parma in 1995, where under Carlo Ancelotti, Alberto Malesani and others he grew into one of Europe's best defenders alongside [Gianluigi Buffon](/players/gianluigi-buffon) and Lilian Thuram. Parma won the Coppa Italia and UEFA Cup in 1999, and Cannavaro made his Italy debut in 1997 against Northern Ireland.

## Peak years

Cannavaro was a starter at the 1998 and 2002 World Cups and at Euro 2000, where Italy reached the final, but his peak came late. After two frustrating years at [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), where injuries and an unsettled defence held him back, he joined [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) in 2004 under Fabio Capello. Paired with Thuram again, he was the best defender in Serie A for two seasons, though both titles were later revoked over Calciopoli. In Germany in 2006 he was immaculate: the semi-final against the hosts, the 100th cap, is often called the greatest game a defender has played at a World Cup. He lifted the trophy in Berlin after the shoot-out win over [France](/nations/france) and won the Ballon d'Or that December ahead of [Buffon](/players/gianluigi-buffon) and [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry).

With Juventus relegated, he and Capello went to Madrid, where he won La Liga in 2007 and 2008.

## How he played

Cannavaro's defining skill was the interception. He positioned himself a yard off his striker, read the pass as it was played and stepped in front; his tackles were usually standing blocks rather than slides. In the air he timed his jump from a short run-up and used his arms to create space, winning headers against [Didier Drogba](/players/didier-drogba) and Miroslav Klose despite giving away 10 to 15 cm.

His recovery pace was good, his one-on-one defending superb because he refused to commit, and he organised the line with constant talking. Weaknesses: his passing was functional, and when teams got him turned, as happened in the chaotic Madrid side of 2008-09 under Bernd Schuster and Juande Ramos, he looked ordinary. Critics in Spain felt he was never the player he had been in 2006, and he was open about struggling with the quicker, more open games La Liga produced. He was also suspended for an anti-doping offence in 2009 after a cortisone treatment, though the ban was lifted on appeal.

## Evolution

He started as a right-back and defensive midfielder at Napoli and became a centre-back at Parma, where Italian coaching trained him in zonal marking and the offside line. At Juventus under Capello he played in a high, compact four. At Madrid he had to cover more ground in a more open side, and he adapted by dropping deeper. He returned to Juventus for a final Serie A season in 2009-10, then played a year at Al-Ahli in Dubai before retiring in 2011. He became a manager in Asia, winning the Chinese Super League with Guangzhou Evergrande in 2019, then coached China, Benevento, Udinese and Dinamo Zagreb, where he was in charge in the 2024-25 season.

## Best plays and moments

- 1999, UEFA Cup final, Parma 3-0 Marseille: a Parma defence that barely gave the French side a shot.
- 2000, Euro 2000 semi-final v Netherlands: 120 minutes of resistance with ten men before the shoot-out win.
- 2002, World Cup, Italy v Mexico and South Korea: an uncomfortable tournament defended well despite the controversial exit.
- 2005, Serie A, Juventus v Milan: the win in the title run-in, with Cannavaro marshalling the line.
- 2006, World Cup quarter-final v Ukraine: a 3-0 shutout, Cannavaro's clearance off the line a highlight.
- 2006, World Cup semi-final, Germany 0-2 Italy: his 100th cap, universally considered his masterpiece ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Cannavaro+Italy+Germany+2006+semi+final)).
- 2006, World Cup final v France: a trophy lift after the 5-3 shoot-out in Berlin ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Fabio+Cannavaro+2006+World+Cup+defending)).
- 2006, Ballon d'Or: the first defender winner in a decade ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Fabio+Cannavaro+Ballon+d%27Or+2006)).
- 2007, La Liga, Real Madrid: the title won on the last day against Mallorca.
- 2008, La Liga, Real Madrid 4-1 Barcelona: a Clásico win in a title season.

## Legacy

Cannavaro is the standard-bearer for the intelligent, undersized centre-back, cited by [Sergio Ramos](/players/sergio-ramos), who inherited his place at Madrid, and by countless Italian defenders. His 2006 tournament is the reference performance for defending at a World Cup; the Germany semi-final is still used in coaching courses to illustrate anticipation and body shape. Together with [Alessandro Nesta](/players/alessandro-nesta), [Paolo Maldini](/players/paolo-maldini) and Buffon he represents the last great wave of the Italian defensive school, and his Ballon d'Or stands as a monument to a kind of football that has not been rewarded since.
