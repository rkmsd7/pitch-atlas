## Overview

Dennis Bergkamp came to [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) in 1995 for 7.5 million pounds, when the club were drab and the league was still suspicious of foreign imports. He left in 2006 with three [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) titles, four FA Cups and a statue on the way. In between he scored goals that are still discussed as works of art, and he helped [Arsène Wenger](/managers/arsene-wenger) turn Arsenal into a club defined by technique.

He refused to fly after a frightening flight at the 1994 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), which cost him European games and gave him his nickname.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Amsterdam and named after Denis Law, Bergkamp joined [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) at 11 and debuted under [Johan Cruyff](/managers/johan-cruyff) in December 1986. Cruyff saw a player who could do everything with one touch and built him into the side that won the 1987 Cup Winners' Cup. By 1990 he was a regular in the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) team, and he was Eredivisie top scorer three years running from 1991 to 1993, winning the UEFA Cup in 1992 and finishing second in the 1993 Ballon d'Or.

[Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan) bought him in 1993 for 7 million pounds. He won another UEFA Cup in 1994 but struggled in a defensive, counter-attacking team that did not know how to use him, and the Italian press was brutal.

## Peak years

Bruce Rioch signed him for Arsenal in 1995 and Wenger arrived a year later. Bergkamp became the fulcrum of a 4-4-2 that paired him with Ian Wright and then Nicolas Anelka, and in 1997-98 Arsenal won the Double. He was PFA and FWA Player of the Year, and his hat-trick at Leicester in August 1997 took the top three places in the Goal of the Month vote.

At the 1998 World Cup he scored the winner against [Argentina](/nations/argentina) in the quarter-final, a goal of three touches from a 60-yard Frank de Boer pass: control, turn past Ayala, finish. It is a candidate for the best World Cup goal ever scored.

With [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry) and [Patrick Vieira](/players/patrick-vieira) he won further titles in 2002 and 2004, the latter unbeaten, and played until 2006, his last season at Highbury.

## How he played

Bergkamp's game was about the first touch and the next idea. He took the ball on the half-turn so that his second touch was a pass or a shot, and he could control any ball, at any height, into the space he wanted. He played as a second striker who dropped into the number 10 position, linking midfield to attack and timing his own runs into the box.

His finishing was precise rather than powerful: side-footed into corners, often first time. He also scored outside-of-the-foot goals and chips that defied the physics of the moment. His passing was imaginative, particularly the lofted ball over the defence for Henry or Anelka.

Weaknesses: he was not fast and did not dribble past players with pace. He had a temper, with several red cards for elbows and stamping, and the flying issue limited him in Europe, where Arsenal's record was poor in his era.

## Evolution

Under Cruyff he was an inside-right in a 3-4-3 who scored prolifically. In Italy he was a frustrated number 10. Wenger used him as a second striker, then, as Henry grew, as the creator behind a lone striker in what was effectively a 4-4-1-1. After 2003 he started fewer games, a mentor to [Robin van Persie](/players/robin-van-persie) and Cesc Fàbregas, and retired after the 2006 Champions League final, the last game before Arsenal moved to the Emirates.

- 2000, Euro 2000 semi-final v Italy: a frustrated tournament ended in a shootout.

## Legacy

Bergkamp is the player most often cited when Arsenal supporters explain what they expect their club to look like. He spent nine years coaching in the Ajax academy and as assistant to Frank de Boer, and later turned down several management offers. Wenger said that the Dutchman's arrival changed the standards of the club; the statue outside the Emirates, mid-control, says the same thing in bronze.
