## Overview

Raúl González Blanco was [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid)'s all-time leading scorer and appearance maker when he left in 2010, with 323 goals in 741 games, and he was the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league)'s top scorer with 71 goals until [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) and [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) overtook him. He won three European Cups and six La Liga titles, captained the club for seven years and was [Spain](/nations/spain)'s record scorer with 44 goals until David Villa passed him. His kiss of the ring, the celebration that followed each goal, became the club's image for a decade.

## Early life and breakthrough

Raúl was born in Madrid and grew up supporting Atlético, whose academy he joined; when Jesús Gil closed the youth system in 1992 to save money, Real Madrid picked him up, one of the great free transfers. Jorge Valdano gave him a debut in October 1994 at 17 years and 124 days, the youngest in club history. He scored his first goal in a derby against Atlético a week later. In 1994-95 he won La Liga, and in 1996 he was a Spain international.

## Peak years

From 1997 to 2003 Raúl was the leading striker in Europe. He scored in the 2000 Champions League final against [Valencia](/clubs/valencia), running 50 metres to finish a counter, and in the 2002 final against [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) at Hampden, a game remembered for [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane)'s volley but in which Raúl's opener was the classic Raúl goal, first to a long throw-in and poking home. He won the Pichichi twice, in 1999 and 2001, and the 1998 Champions League against [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) as a 20-year-old. He finished second in the 2001 Ballon d'Or to Michael Owen.

Raúl played in three [World Cups](/tournaments/world-cup) and two [Euros](/tournaments/euro) for Spain, scoring in each tournament, but the national team's golden generation arrived just after him; Luis Aragonés dropped him in 2006 and he never returned. He left Madrid in 2010 for Schalke 04, where he scored 40 goals in two seasons and reached the Champions League semi-final in 2011.

## How he played

Raúl's genius was positional. He drifted off the striker he was paired with, usually [Fernando Morientes](/players/fernando-morientes) or [Ronaldo](/players/ronaldo-nazario), dropped into the left half-space to receive and then timed his run to arrive in the box unmarked. His finishing was economical: side-footed first-time shots, dinks over goalkeepers and low headers. He scored a lot of goals from one touch inside the six-yard box because his first movement had taken him away from the defender and his second brought him back.

He pressed from the front before it was fashionable, tracked back on the left when he was pushed wide in Vicente del Bosque's 4-4-2 and was an on-pitch leader as captain. Weaknesses: he lacked pace and strength, which left him unable to dominate centre-backs physically, and his touch and dribbling were modest for a number 7 at Madrid. In the later Galácticos era, without a creative midfield supplying him, his numbers fell and some supporters turned on him, especially in 2004-06 under a succession of coaches.

## Evolution

Raúl was a second striker beside Iván Zamorano and Davor Šuker, became the main man under Guus Hiddink and John Toshack, and under Del Bosque played as a left-sided forward in a 4-4-2 that created room for Ronaldo through the middle. Under Fabio Capello in 2006-07 he was rejuvenated, scoring vital goals in a title run-in, and he kept the number 7 through the arrival of Cristiano Ronaldo in 2009 before finally leaving. At Schalke under Felix Magath and Ralf Rangnick he was a deeper-lying forward and a cup winner; at Al Sadd and the New York Cosmos he played until 38. He retired in 2015, returned to Madrid's youth set-up as a coach in 2018 and led Real Madrid Castilla for six seasons until 2025.

## Best plays and moments

- 1994, La Liga, Real Madrid v Atlético Madrid: a first goal, aged 17, in a derby.
- 1998, Champions League final v Juventus: 90 minutes at 20 in the win that ended the 32-year wait.
- 1999, La Liga, Barcelona 2-2 Real Madrid: the "silencing" of the Camp Nou, finger to lips after equalising ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Raul+Camp+Nou+1999+silence+celebration)).
- 2000, Champions League final v Valencia: a 50-metre run and dink for the third goal in Paris ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Raul+goal+Champions+League+final+2000+Valencia)).
- 2000, Champions League quarter-final v Manchester United: a brace at Old Trafford in a 3-2 win.
- 2002, Champions League final v Bayer Leverkusen: the opening goal from a long throw at Hampden.
- 2002, World Cup, Spain v Slovenia and Paraguay: goals in Spain's run to the quarter-final.
- 2007, La Liga, Real Madrid v Barcelona and v Mallorca: late-season goals in the title race won on the last day.
- 2011, Champions League quarter-final, Schalke v Inter: a goal in a 5-2 win at San Siro.
- 2011, DFB-Pokal final v Duisburg: 5-0 and a German trophy at 33 ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Raul+Schalke+goals)).

## Legacy

Raúl is the embodiment of Real Madrid between the Quinta del Buitre and Cristiano Ronaldo: the home-grown captain who never changed, never lost his hunger and never left the club in doubt about who he played for. The number 7 passed from him to Cristiano to Vinícius Júnior and carries a weight because of him. His Spain career is the odd note; the team won Euro 2008 immediately after his exclusion and Aragonés's decision is usually judged right in hindsight. His transition to coaching at Castilla, with repeated links to the first-team job, has kept him at the centre of Madrid's future, and the fans' chant of "Raúl, Raúl" still echoes at the Bernabéu.
