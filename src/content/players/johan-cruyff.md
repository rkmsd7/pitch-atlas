## Overview

Johan Cruyff is the only footballer whose ideas matter as much as his goals. As a player he was the centre of Rinus Michels' [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) and the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) sides that invented [total football](/tactics/total-football), winning three European Cups in a row and three Ballons d'Or, and reaching the 1974 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) final as the tournament's best player. As a coach he built the Barcelona Dream Team and La Masia, and his pupil [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) turned his principles into the dominant style of the 21st century. The Cruyff turn is a move every child learns. The number 14 is his. Even his aphorisms, "every disadvantage has its advantage", are quoted at coaching courses. Few people have shaped a sport so completely from both sides of the touchline.

## Early life and breakthrough

Hendrik Johannes Cruijff was born on 25 April 1947 in Betondorp, a working-class district next to Ajax's old De Meer stadium, where his mother cleaned the dressing rooms after his father, a greengrocer, died when Johan was 12. He joined Ajax's youth system at ten, debuted at 17 in November 1964 and scored, and by 1966 was the Netherlands' best player and a first-team regular under Michels, who had arrived in 1965 with the club at the bottom of the Eredivisie.

Michels and Cruyff together built something new. Ajax won the league in 1966, 1967 and 1968, reached the 1969 European Cup final, losing 4-1 to [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan), and then won it three times running in 1971 against Panathinaikos, 1972 against [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan) with two Cruyff goals, and 1973 against [Juventus](/clubs/juventus). The team pressed as a unit, pushed its defence up, and swapped positions constantly; Cruyff, nominally the number 9, might be at left-back one minute and in the centre circle the next.

## Peak years

Between 1971 and 1974 he was unarguably the best player in the world. He won his first Ballon d'Or in 1971 and the second in 1973, the year he moved to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) for a world-record fee of around £922,000, insisting he could not play for [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) because of the club's association with Franco. Barcelona were near the bottom when he arrived in October 1973; they won their first La Liga in 14 years by eight points, beating Madrid 5-0 at the Bernabéu in February 1974 along the way. He was named European Footballer of the Year a third time in 1974.

That summer the Netherlands, with Michels back in charge, played the football of their lives. They beat Uruguay, Bulgaria, [Argentina](/nations/argentina) 4-0, East Germany and [Brazil](/nations/brazil) 2-0, Cruyff scoring the second with a volley on the run, and reached the final in Munich against West Germany. He won a penalty in the second minute before a German had touched the ball, and then was man-marked by Berti Vogts into relative silence as [Franz Beckenbauer](/players/franz-beckenbauer)'s side won 2-1. It is the most celebrated losing performance in football history. The Cruyff turn, against Sweden's Jan Olsson in the group stage, is its most replayed image.

## How he played

Cruyff's position was wherever the ball needed to go next. He started as a striker and finished most Ajax matches with a goal, but his real value was as a roving organiser: he dropped into midfield to receive, pulled centre-backs out of position, and used the gaps that his movement created for runners like Johan Neeskens and Piet Keizer. His first touch was precise and his passing, often with the outside of the right foot, cut lines that others did not see. He was fast in a lean, spindly way, with a very quick change of direction, which the turn against Olsson shows: a feint to cross, a drag of the ball behind the standing leg, and he was gone the other way.

He also thought aloud. Team-mates recall him pointing and shouting throughout matches, telling defenders where to stand, and that constant coaching is the thread into his second career. Weaknesses: he was not a physical player and did not enjoy being kicked, he smoked heavily, and his temperament could tip into argument with referees and officials. In today's game he would be a [false nine](/tactics/false-nine) in the most literal sense, the position he effectively invented, or a number 10 in a [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) side built exactly as Guardiola built his; the whole structure of modern elite football is arranged to give a player like him the ball between the lines.

## Evolution

The young Cruyff was a goalscorer, 33 in 1966-67 as the Eredivisie's top scorer, who happened to be brilliant elsewhere. As Ajax matured he scored less and created more, and the 1972 European Cup final, where he scored twice, was perhaps the last time his output as a finisher matched his influence. At Barcelona he was a playmaker who occasionally finished, and his famous "phantom goal" against Atlético in December 1973, a flying volleyed flick at head height, was a creator's improvisation rather than a striker's.

He retired from the Netherlands in 1977, declining to play at the 1978 World Cup after an attempted kidnapping at his Barcelona home. A failed business investment took him to the North American Soccer League, where he was outstanding for the Los Angeles Aztecs and Washington Diplomats, and he returned to Ajax in 1981 to win two more titles at 35 and 36, including a penalty passed to Jesper Olsen and returned for a tap-in. When Ajax declined to renew his contract he joined Feyenoord and won the double with them in 1984, out of spite, and then retired. The game had meanwhile absorbed total football's pressing and positional rotation, and the next step, codifying it at Barcelona from 1988 with a 3-4-3 and a youth academy that taught the same ideas at every age, was his.

## Legacy

No single person has had more influence on how elite football is played. The Ajax and Netherlands sides he led made [total football](/tactics/total-football) the ideal against which attacking teams are measured. His Barcelona of 1988 to 1996, the Dream Team that won four straight La Ligas and the club's first European Cup in 1992, established the 3-4-3, the insistence on possession and a high defensive line, and the principle that a youth academy should teach one style, which produced Xavi, Andrés Iniesta and [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi). [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), his midfielder in that team, built [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) and [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) on his foundations, and the modern [Spain](/nations/spain) national team, including the 2026 World Cup winners under Luis de la Fuente, is a Cruyffian project at one remove.

As a person he was blunt, contrarian and funny, feuding with Ajax's board well into his sixties and leaving behind a foundation that builds pitches for children. He died of lung cancer on 24 March 2016 at 68. The Johan Cruijff ArenA, the Cruyff turn, the number 14 and a thousand quotes carry his name. Of the great players, [Pelé](/players/pele) and [Diego Maradona](/players/diego-maradona) won more; nobody has changed more.
