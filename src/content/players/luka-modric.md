## Overview

Luka Modrić is the midfielder who broke the Messi-Ronaldo Ballon d'Or duopoly in 2018, the heartbeat of [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid)'s six Champions Leagues between 2014 and 2024, and the captain who took [Croatia](/nations/croatia) to a World Cup final. In 2025, at 39, he left Madrid after 13 seasons and joined [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) on a one-year deal. In July 2026 Milan extended it to June 2027. He will turn 41 during this season and still plays the number 14 role in the middle of the pitch as if the game were slower for him.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Zadar, Modrić grew up as a refugee during the Croatian war of independence; his grandfather was killed in 1991. He came through at Dinamo Zagreb after being considered too small by Hajduk Split, developed on loans in Bosnia and the Croatian second tier, and won three titles with Dinamo. [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) paid £16.5m in 2008, and under Harry Redknapp he became the Premier League's most elegant playmaker, taking Spurs to the Champions League quarter-final in 2011.

## Peak years

Real Madrid paid around €30m in 2012. After a difficult first half-season he became, under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) and then Zidane, the midfield engine of the most successful period in the club's modern history: four Champions Leagues in five seasons between 2014 and 2018, with Toni Kroos and Casemiro alongside him. In 2018 he was the Golden Ball winner at the World Cup as Croatia reached the final, and he won the Ballon d'Or and FIFA's The Best.

He kept going. He was decisive in the 2022 Champions League run at 36, with an outside-of-the-foot assist against Chelsea that is still studied, won a fourth league title in 2024 and a sixth Champions League that year. He captained Madrid in 2024-25 before leaving for Milan.

## How he plays

Modrić receives the ball on the half-turn, always with a picture of the pitch already in his head, and plays the pass that moves the opponent's block. His outside-of-the-right-foot pass, the trivela, is the most famous in the game, and he uses it for through-balls, switches and crosses alike. He dribbles out of pressure with a drop of the shoulder and never looks hurried. His long-range shooting is underrated.

At 41 the running is reduced; he no longer covers the ground he did under Zidane, and he must be protected by the midfielders around him. Milan under Ruben Amorim use him in short spells and as a tempo-setter. His defensive contribution is now about positioning rather than tackling.

## Evolution

An attacking midfielder at Dinamo and Spurs, an 8 in Ancelotti's 4-3-3 and Zidane's diamond, a deep-lying regista in his later Madrid years, and at Milan a central midfielder under Massimiliano Allegri in 2025-26 and now Amorim's 3-4-2-1 in 2026-27. For Croatia, Zlatko Dalić built three tournament runs around him; his international status after the 2026 World Cup should be checked against current reports.

## Best plays and moments

- 2011, Champions League, with Tottenham: the run to the quarter-final.
- 2014, Champions League final, v Atlético Madrid: the corner for Ramos's equaliser in Lisbon.
- 2017, Champions League final, v Juventus: the assist for Ronaldo in Cardiff.
- 2018, World Cup: Golden Ball as Croatia reached the final ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Modric+World+Cup+2018+Golden+Ball+highlights)).
- 2018, Ballon d'Or: the first winner other than Messi or Ronaldo since 2007.
- 2022, Champions League quarter-final, v Chelsea: the trivela assist for Rodrygo ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Modric+outside+foot+assist+Chelsea+Rodrygo)).
- 2024, Champions League final, v Dortmund: a sixth European Cup at 38.
- 2026, Serie A: a Milan extension to 2027 at 40.

## Current situation

Modrić is in his second Milan season, under Ruben Amorim, who replaced Allegri in summer 2026 after Milan finished fifth and missed the Champions League by a point. He has said he wants to help the club return to Europe's top competition. His contract runs to 30 June 2027.
