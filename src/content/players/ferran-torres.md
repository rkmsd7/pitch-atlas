## Overview

For most of his career Ferran Torres was the useful one: the forward who could play anywhere across the front, score a dozen goals a season and never quite be the star. Then, on 19 July 2026 at MetLife Stadium, he scored the only goal of the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) final against [Argentina](/nations/argentina) in the 106th minute, and became the most important Spanish goalscorer since Andrés Iniesta in 2010. Weeks later he left [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) for [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), where he wears the number 9 for the European champions.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Foios, outside Valencia, on a leap day, Torres joined Valencia's academy at six and debuted in the first team at 17 in 2017. He was part of the side that won the 2019 Copa del Rey against Barcelona and was Spain's best player at the 2019 under-19 Euros. In August 2020 Manchester City signed him for around £21m, and under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) he scored a hat-trick against Germany in a 6-0 Nations League win on his fifth cap.

Barcelona paid around €55m for him in January 2022, one of Xavi's first signings, and he scored his first Clásico goal that March in a 4-0 win at the Bernabéu.

## Peak years

His Barcelona career was one of steady improvement rather than fireworks. He scored in the 2023 title season, scored the winner against Real Sociedad that effectively sealed the 2024-25 title, and under [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick) became the squad's most reliable finisher off the bench, with a goal roughly every 90 minutes in 2024-25. He won a third league title in 2025-26. For [Spain](/nations/spain), he scored at Euro 2024 against Albania and started several games at the 2026 World Cup, where his movement off [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal) and [Nico Williams](/players/nico-williams) was crucial, before the goal in the final that made him a national hero.

The World Cup transformed his market. PSG, looking for a mobile striker to complement [Ousmane Dembélé](/players/ousmane-dembele) after Bradley Barcola's sale to Liverpool, signed him in the summer 2026 window.

## How he plays

Torres is a runner. He plays on the shoulder of the last defender, times his movement off the back of the centre-back and finishes first time, often with a side-footed or sliding effort from a cutback. He is two-footed enough to play on either wing and physical enough to lead the line. His headed goals, especially at the far post, are a feature.

He is not a dribbler and not a creator; his passing in the final third is tidy but unambitious. His first touch under pressure can let him down, and his form has historically come in streaks. The best coaches have used him as a finisher rather than a builder.

## Evolution

At Valencia he was a right winger. Guardiola turned him into a false nine, then a goalscoring striker. Xavi used him on both wings. Flick made him a genuine centre-forward option alongside [Robert Lewandowski](/players/robert-lewandowski). At PSG, [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique) uses him as the central striker in a fluid front three with Dembélé and [Khvicha Kvaratskhelia](/players/khvicha-kvaratskhelia), with [Désiré Doué](/players/desire-doue) rotating in. For Spain, [Luis de la Fuente](/managers/luis-de-la-fuente) has used him as a starting striker when he wants pace in behind.

## Current situation

Torres joined PSG in summer 2026 and is Luis Enrique's first-choice striker in 2026-27 as the club chases a third consecutive Champions League and a sixth straight [Ligue 1](/leagues/ligue-1) title. The transfer fee and contract length were not confirmed in the sources available for this profile. For Spain, the World Cup winner is secure in de la Fuente's squad.
