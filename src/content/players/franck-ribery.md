## Overview

Franck Ribéry made 425 appearances for [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) between 2007 and 2019 and won nine Bundesliga titles, six German Cups and the 2013 [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) as the creative half of the Robben-Ribéry partnership. In 2013 he was named UEFA's Best Player in Europe after a treble in which he was Bayern's most important attacker, and he finished third in a Ballon d'Or vote behind [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) and [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) that he, and many Germans, still consider a robbery of the only kind he did not enjoy. For [France](/nations/france) he played 81 times and reached the 2006 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) final at 23.

## Early life and breakthrough

Ribéry was born in Boulogne-sur-Mer and bears the facial scars of a car accident when he was two. He was released by Lille's academy at 13 for being too small, worked as a labourer and played in the lower divisions for Boulogne, Alès and Brest before a season at Metz in Ligue 1 in 2004-05. A six-month spell at [Galatasaray](/clubs/galatasaray), where he won the Turkish Cup, ended in a contract dispute and he joined [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) in 2005. Two seasons at the Vélodrome made him the most exciting player in France: Raymond Domenech picked him for the 2006 World Cup and he scored against [Spain](/nations/spain) in the round of 16, breaking through after a pass from [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane), and started the final against [Italy](/nations/italy).

## Peak years

Bayern paid a then-club-record €25m in 2007 and Ribéry won the Bundesliga and DFB-Pokal double in his first season under Ottmar Hitzfeld, named German Footballer of the Year. The arrival of [Arjen Robben](/players/arjen-robben) in 2009 created the partnership; the two reached the 2010 Champions League final (Ribéry suspended) and lost the 2012 final at home to [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea). The 2012-13 season was the summit: 11 goals and 23 assists in all competitions, a Bundesliga won by 25 points, the 7-0 aggregate win over [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in the semi-final in which he tormented Dani Alves, and the backheel that set up Robben's winner against [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) at Wembley. Under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) he won three more titles, though injuries reduced his minutes, and he signed off in 2019 with a goal in his final home game against Eintracht Frankfurt.

## How he played

Ribéry was a dribbler who could go either way. From the left with the ball on his right foot he could cut inside like a conventional inverted winger or, more unusually, take the full-back on the outside with a flick and a burst, then deliver a low cross or cut-back. His feet were quick and his tricks, the stepover and the drag-back especially, were functional rather than showy; he used them to change the defender's balance. He was an excellent crosser from the byline and a precise passer into the box.

Off the ball he was unusually hard-working for a star winger, tracking his full-back and pressing in Guardiola's system. His finishing was his least reliable skill; he scored 124 Bayern goals but missed chances that Robben would have taken, and his shooting from distance was ordinary. Temperament was a recurring problem: red cards, a feud with Robben in 2012 over free kicks, a bust-up with a French TV pundit, and a role in the 2010 World Cup mutiny at Knysna that got him a three-match international ban. Injuries, especially ankle and knee problems, cost him large parts of 2014-15 and 2015-16.

## Evolution

He was a number 10 or right winger at Marseille, became a left winger at Bayern under Hitzfeld and Jürgen Klinsmann and then Louis van Gaal's 4-2-3-1 fixed him there permanently. Guardiola used him narrower and occasionally as a false nine and asked him to combine more with [Philipp Lahm](/players/philipp-lahm) and David Alaba at full-back. Under Jupp Heynckes in 2017-18 and Niko Kovač in 2018-19 he was a rotation player who could still decide games. Two seasons at Fiorentina showed his dribbling intact at 36, and he retired at Salernitana in October 2022 after a knee injury, staying as a coach there and later at Fiorentina's youth set-up.

## Best plays and moments

- 2006, World Cup round of 16, France 3-1 Spain: the equaliser, running from the halfway line after Zidane's pass ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Ribery+goal+Spain+2006+World+Cup)).
- 2006, World Cup final v Italy: 100 minutes as the youngest starter on the pitch.
- 2008, Bundesliga, Bayern: the double in his first season and German Footballer of the Year.
- 2008, Champions League, Bayern v Sporting: a goal in a 12-1 aggregate win.
- 2012, Bundesliga, Borussia Dortmund 1-0 Bayern and the 2012 title race: the duel with Klopp's Dortmund that defined the era.
- 2013, Champions League semi-final, Bayern 4-0 Barcelona: an assist for Thomas Müller and total control of the left wing.
- 2013, Champions League final, Bayern 2-1 Borussia Dortmund: the backheel that set up Robben's winner ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Franck+Ribery+2013+treble+Bayern+highlights)).
- 2013, UEFA Super Cup v Chelsea: the equaliser in Prague before a shoot-out win.
- 2013, Club World Cup final v Raja Casablanca: a 2-0 win and player of the tournament.
- 2019, Bundesliga, Bayern 5-1 Eintracht Frankfurt: a farewell goal in his final Allianz Arena appearance.

## Legacy

Ribéry is in Bayern's Hall of Fame and his number 7 shirt is woven into the club's most successful decade; the 2013 treble side is routinely rated among the best European club teams of the century, and he was its most creative player. In France the picture is more complicated: the Knysna affair and the 2014 injury that denied him a third World Cup mean he is remembered more warmly in Munich than in Paris. He is the model of the two-way winger, a creator who defended, and the Robben-Ribéry pairing remains the benchmark for complementary wide players, one a direct shooter and the other a dribbling provider. His disputed 2013 Ballon d'Or remains the most-cited case for a defender of UEFA's own award.
