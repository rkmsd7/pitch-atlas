## Overview

Sandro Tonali's career has already contained a lifetime of plot. He was the next Andrea Pirlo at Brescia, a Serie A champion with his boyhood club [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) at 22, a £55m signing for [Newcastle United](/clubs/newcastle-united) in 2023 and, two months later, a player banned for ten months for breaching betting rules. He came back to win Newcastle's first domestic trophy in 70 years, became the club's captain-in-waiting, and in July 2026 moved to [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) for a club-record fee of around £100m, persuaded by [Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi), a fellow son of Brescia.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Lodi, Lombardy, Tonali came through Brescia's academy and made his senior debut in Serie B at 17. His long hair, deep-lying role and elegant passing drew the Pirlo comparisons, which he spent years shaking off; the real model was a more combative midfielder. He led Brescia to promotion in 2019, won Serie A's best young player award in 2019-20 despite relegation and made his Italy debut in 2019. Milan, the club he supported, signed him in 2020, initially on loan, and he took a pay cut to make the deal permanent a year later.

## Peak years

Under Stefano Pioli at Milan he grew from a hesitant teenager into the engine of the 2021-22 Scudetto side, scoring the late winner at Lazio that kept the title race alive. A fine 2022-23, including a Champions League semi-final, persuaded Newcastle to pay around £55m in July 2023. He scored on his debut against Aston Villa, then in October 2023 the Italian federation banned him for ten months for betting on football, including on his own teams. He returned in August 2024, and in 2024-25 he was Newcastle's best player, winning the Carabao Cup against Liverpool in March 2025 and reaching the Champions League. The 2025-26 season, with Eddie Howe's side in Europe, confirmed him as one of the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league)'s best midfielders, and Spurs paid a record fee the following summer.

## How he plays

Tonali plays at full speed. He receives from the centre-backs, turns and immediately looks to drive or to switch play with a flat 40-yard diagonal; his passing is purposeful and vertical rather than patient. Without the ball he is a hunter, pressing the opposition's deepest midfielder, winning tackles cleanly in the middle third and covering for full-backs on both sides. He is strong in the air for a midfielder, defends set pieces well and has a good late run into the box, finishing with a hard, low right-footed shot.

What he is not is a creator in the final third. His through balls are rare and his dribbling is functional. He commits fouls when his aggression outruns his judgement, and in deep roles his positional discipline has sometimes been questioned. He is best as a No.8 who can also do the No.6's work.

## Evolution

At Brescia he was a regista in a 4-3-1-2. Pioli used him as one of two in a double pivot, giving him licence to break forward. Howe's 4-3-3 made him a right-sided No.8 next to [Bruno Guimarães](/players/bruno-guimaraes), with Joelinton on the other side, a trio built on running power. De Zerbi's Tottenham builds more patiently, with the ball played out through the goalkeeper and the midfielders dropping into pockets, and Tonali has been asked to be the tempo-setter as well as the destroyer, a role closer to his Milan years.

## Current situation

Tonali's Spurs career started with a bruising reunion: in his first home league game he was involved in both goals in a 2-0 defeat to Newcastle. De Zerbi, who has backed him to become "a leader" of the rebuild, has since built the midfield around him, and Spurs have had better days, including a 2-0 win at Chelsea in October before a 0-0 draw at Leeds on 7 November. He wears No.16. Tottenham's summer spending passed £300m, with [Omar Marmoush](/players/omar-marmoush) and Savinho also arriving. For [Italy](/nations/italy) under Gennaro Gattuso he is the first name in midfield.
