## Overview

Paul Scholes played 718 games for [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) and never once gave an interview he could avoid. He let the ball talk instead. Across 20 years under [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) he won 11 league titles and two Champions Leagues, and the players who marked him, [Xavi](/players/xavi), [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane), [Andrea Pirlo](/players/andrea-pirlo), spoke of him as the best midfielder of his generation. His game was the simplest and hardest thing in football: receive, look, pass, and make the whole pitch smaller for the opponent.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Salford in November 1974 and raised in Langley, Middleton, Scholes was a small, asthmatic boy with a shot that frightened goalkeepers. He joined United's youth system at 14 and became part of the 1992 FA Youth Cup-winning side with [Ryan Giggs](/players/ryan-giggs), [David Beckham](/players/david-beckham), Gary Neville and Nicky Butt, the Class of 92. He made his debut in September 1994 at Port Vale in the League Cup and scored twice.

In 1995, after Ferguson sold Paul Ince, Mark Hughes and Andrei Kanchelskis, Alan Hansen declared "you can't win anything with kids". Scholes, used as a support striker that season, scored 14 goals as United won the Double. He was a forward first, a midfielder later.

## Peak years

[Roy Keane](/players/roy-keane)'s knee injury in 1997-98 moved Scholes permanently into central midfield, and in 1998-99 he was essential to the Treble, scoring in the FA Cup final against Newcastle and the Champions League semi-final second leg at [Juventus](/clubs/juventus). Suspension kept him out of the final in Barcelona, a wound Ferguson acknowledged when he made sure Scholes played in Moscow nine years later.

From 1999 to 2003 Scholes was arguably the complete midfielder: 20 goals in all competitions in 2002-03 from an advanced role behind [Ruud van Nistelrooy](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Scholes+van+Nistelrooy+2003), a title won, and a hat-trick against Newcastle at St James' Park. After an eye problem cost him most of 2005-06, he returned as a deep conductor in the 2006-07 and 2007-08 title sides alongside Michael Carrick, and scored the goal that sent United to the 2008 Champions League final.

## How he played

Scholes took the ball under pressure with his first touch already pointing where he intended to play. His scanning before receiving was constant, so he knew the picture around him and could play first time, with either foot, at any distance. The 60-yard diagonal to a winger was his trademark, but so was the sharp ten-yard pass through a line to a striker's feet.

His striking of a moving ball was exceptional. He hit volleys with a stiff ankle and no backlift, which is why so many of his famous goals look effortless, and from 25 yards he was a genuine threat. As a younger player he was also a brilliant late runner into the box, scoring 14 for England.

The weakness everyone cites is tackling. He mistimed challenges constantly and collected 97 Premier League yellow cards, and he was not fast. He also retired from England in 2004 at 29, partly from being asked to play on the left to accommodate [Steven Gerrard](/players/steven-gerrard) and [Frank Lampard](/players/frank-lampard-player), which cost his country its best passer for a decade.

## Evolution

Ferguson used him four ways. In the mid-1990s he was a second striker. From 1998 to 2004 he was a box-to-box number eight whose goals mattered as much as his passing. In the 2000s he dropped deeper, became the tempo-setter in a 4-3-3 or 4-4-2 diamond, and essentially played as a regista before the term was common in England. He retired in May 2011 after the Champions League final loss to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), joined the coaching staff, then was persuaded back in January 2012 at 37 and helped win the 2012-13 title, Ferguson's last, playing almost exclusively as a deep passer.

## Legacy

Scholes is the player other great players name. Zidane called him the best midfielder of his generation; Xavi said he was the best central midfielder he had seen in 20 years; [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) said he would have fitted the Barcelona team perfectly. His 499 Premier League appearances, 107 goals and 55 assists understate a career built on the pass before the assist. He has since worked as a pundit, a co-owner of Salford City, and briefly as caretaker at Oldham Athletic. For a generation of English coaches searching for a native [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka) model, Scholes was the proof that it could be done with a Salford accent.
