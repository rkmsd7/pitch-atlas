## Overview

Kevin De Bruyne is the standard against which creative midfielders in England are measured. In ten seasons at [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) he won six [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) titles and the 2023 Champions League, finished with more than 100 league assists, and was twice the PFA Players' Player of the Year. When his City contract ended in June 2025 he chose a new challenge rather than a lap of honour, joining [Napoli](/clubs/napoli) as a free agent. His first season in [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a) was wrecked by injury; his second, under a new coach, has started with goals.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Drongen, near Ghent, De Bruyne came through Gent's and then Genk's academies. He was a Belgian champion with Genk at 19 and joined [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in January 2012 for £7m, but the move never worked: a productive loan at Werder Bremen was followed by nine appearances under José Mourinho, who decided he did not need him. Wolfsburg paid €22m in January 2014 and De Bruyne responded with the most creative season the Bundesliga had seen, a record 21 assists in 2014-15 and the German Footballer of the Year award.

## Peak years

City paid a then club-record £55m in August 2015. Under Manuel Pellegrini he was a scorer and dribbler; under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) from 2016 he became the half-space playmaker who defined a decade. The 2017-18 centurions side ran through him; the 2019-20 season brought 20 league assists, equalling the record; the 2022-23 treble was built on his partnership with [Erling Haaland](/players/erling-haaland), whom he fed with crosses and through balls at a rate no striker had enjoyed. A hamstring tear in the 2023 Champions League final and the surgery that followed cost him most of 2023-24, but he still returned to help win a fourth straight title. For [Belgium](/nations/belgium), whom he captained, he reached the 2018 World Cup semi-finals.

## How he plays

De Bruyne's best position is the right half-space, the channel between the opposition's left-back and left centre-back. From there he produces the pass that made him famous: an in-swinging cross or cut-back, hit with pace and shape so that it arrives at the penalty spot before defenders can turn. He hits it with his right foot and, almost as well, with his left, which makes him impossible to show onto a weaker side.

His through balls are played early and to the exact pace of the runner. His shooting from 20 yards, whether struck or side-footed with the outside of the boot, is among the hardest in the game. He is also a fine set-piece taker. He was never a dribbler in the Messi sense but used strength and a long stride to drive past players in transition.

Age and injury have reduced his pressing and his box-to-box range, and he now needs the game to come to him more than it did. He has always been a poor fit for a deep, patient block, which is part of why his relationship with Antonio Conte's Napoli was strained.

## Evolution

He was a winger and second striker at Genk, a box-to-box midfielder at Wolfsburg and a free No.10 under Pellegrini. Guardiola fixed him as one of two attacking No.8s in a 4-3-3, which gave him both the freedom to roam and a structure to roam from. In his last City seasons he was used more sparingly and more centrally. At Napoli in 2025-26 Conte used him as a left-sided midfielder in a 4-3-3 or behind the striker, and De Bruyne made little secret of his frustration. Napoli changed coach in the summer of 2026, and he has been given a freer attacking role.

## Best plays and moments

- 2015, Bundesliga with Wolfsburg: 21 assists, a league record, and the DFB-Pokal.
- 2018, Premier League v Chelsea: a rocket from 25 yards that typified the centurions season ([best goals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Kevin+De+Bruyne+best+goals)).
- 2018, World Cup quarter-final v Brazil: the goal that sent Belgium into the semi-finals.
- 2020, Premier League: 20 assists to equal Thierry Henry's record and win Player of the Season.
- 2021, Champions League semi-final v PSG: the deflected opener in the second leg that sent City to their first final.
- 2022, Premier League v Wolves: four goals in a 5-1 win on the way to the title ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Kevin+De+Bruyne+4+goals+Wolves+2022)).
- 2023, Champions League semi-final v Real Madrid: a 4-0 destruction at the Etihad with De Bruyne pulling the strings ([best assists](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Kevin+De+Bruyne+best+assists)).
- 2025, Etihad farewell: a final home appearance after ten years and 16 trophies.
- 2025, Serie A debut for Napoli: a goal in his first league start ([Napoli highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Kevin+De+Bruyne+Napoli+goals+assists)).

## Current situation

De Bruyne's first Napoli season was limited to 18 Serie A appearances by a serious muscle injury, and he was openly critical of Conte's tactics. Napoli's sporting director Giovanni Manna insisted in July 2026 that he remained part of the project, and the club appointed Massimiliano Allegri for 2026-27. He has started the new season strongly, with three Serie A goals by mid-September, and his contract runs to 2027 with an option for a further year. His international future after Belgium's 2026 World Cup campaign is not confirmed on this page; as of the 2025-26 season he was still Belgium's captain.
