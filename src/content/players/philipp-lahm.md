## Overview

Philipp Lahm is the model for the modern full-back and the reason the [inverted full-back](/tactics/inverted-full-backs) became a mainstream idea. He captained [Germany](/nations/germany) to the 2014 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) and [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) to the 2013 treble, won eight [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) titles, and was named in the team of the tournament at four consecutive Euros and World Cups. At 1.70 m he was rarely beaten in the air or on the ground, and [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) called him "the most intelligent player I have ever coached".

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Munich, Lahm joined Bayern's youth system at 11 from FT Gern, a local club where his father coached. He captained the under-19s and played for the reserves in the Regionalliga, but with Bixente Lizarazu and Willy Sagnol established, he was loaned to [VfB Stuttgart](/clubs/vfb-stuttgart) in 2003. Felix Magath played him at left-back, his wrong side, and he was so good that he was Bundesliga Rookie of the Year and in the Germany squad for Euro 2004 within a year.

A cruciate injury and a broken foot delayed his return to Bayern, but Jürgen Klinsmann picked him to start the 2006 World Cup, and he scored the opening goal of the tournament against Costa Rica, a curling left-footed strike into the top corner, after six minutes.

## Peak years

From 2006 he was Bayern's first-choice full-back, on the left until Louis van Gaal moved him to the right in 2009 to accommodate Diego Contento and make room for [Arjen Robben](/players/arjen-robben) to cut in. He was a Euro 2008 finalist and scored the 90th-minute winner in the semi-final against Turkey.

He became Germany captain in 2010 when Michael Ballack was injured, and kept the armband. Bayern lost the 2010 and 2012 Champions League finals, the second at home against [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea), before Jupp Heynckes's team won the 2013 treble, beating [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) at Wembley. Guardiola arrived that summer and, in a famous decision, moved Lahm into defensive midfield for most of 2013-14, where he was arguably the best player in the Bundesliga.

The 2014 World Cup was his last tournament. Joachim Löw used him at the number 6 in the group stage, then returned him to right-back for the knockout rounds, including the 7-1 against [Brazil](/nations/brazil) and the final against [Argentina](/nations/argentina). He lifted the trophy at the Maracanã and retired from international football a week later, aged 30, with 113 caps.

## How he played

Lahm defended by positioning and anticipation. He stayed on his feet, showed the winger onto his weaker side, and nicked the ball with a quick, low tackle at exactly the moment the attacker's touch was heavy. He was the fastest decision-maker on the pitch, which compensated for average speed; [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) and [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) both found him unusually difficult.

In possession he was two-footed and preferred short, forward passes. He crossed early and low, and from the right he would move inside into midfield, giving Bayern a 2-3-5 shape in attack years before the idea was common in England. His weaknesses were the obvious ones: no height, a modest goal threat (16 goals in 517 Bayern games) and no spectacular dribbling. He did not need them.

## Evolution

Magath and Klinsmann saw him as a left-back. Van Gaal switched him to the right, where he was also captain. Guardiola's Bayern used him in three roles, sometimes in the same game: right-back who inverted, holding midfielder in a 4-1-4-1, and a right-sided centre-back in a back three. Löw's Germany similarly used him as a number 6 before Toni Kroos and Sami Khedira took that job.

He retired in May 2017 after an eighth Bundesliga title, still first choice, and declined a sporting-director role at Bayern. He has since been tournament director for Euro 2024 in Germany and a columnist, and his book "The Subtle Difference" was unusually candid about Klinsmann and Van Gaal.

## Legacy

Lahm is the full-back that coaches point to when explaining that the position is about brain rather than legs. The inverted full-back that Guardiola later used at [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) with João Cancelo and John Stones, and that [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) used with Oleksandr Zinchenko at [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), began with Lahm at Bayern.

He is the most decorated outfield player in Bayern's history in terms of league titles, a World Cup-winning captain, and with [Bastian Schweinsteiger](/players/bastian-schweinsteiger), Manuel Neuer and Thomas Müller the core of the greatest German team of the 21st century. His standing in Germany is such that he was asked to run Euro 2024, and he remains the most-cited example of a player who never had a bad tournament.
