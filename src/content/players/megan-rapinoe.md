## Overview

Megan Rapinoe was the creative force and public conscience of the [United States](/nations/united-states-women) team that won the 2015 and 2019 [World Cups](/tournaments/womens-world-cup). At the 2019 tournament in France she scored six goals, including the opener in the final against the Netherlands, won the Golden Ball and Golden Boot and then the Ballon d'Or, while publicly feuding with the sitting American president. She retired in 2023 with 203 caps, 63 goals and 73 assists, and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2022.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Redding, California, with a twin sister who also played, Rapinoe starred at the University of Portland, overcame two ACL tears in her early twenties and made her senior debut in 2006. The breakthrough came at the 2011 World Cup, when her 122nd-minute cross for [Abby Wambach](/players/abby-wambach)'s header against Brazil produced one of the sport's most famous goals.

## Peak years

At London 2012 she scored directly from a corner against Canada in a 4-3 semi-final and won gold. She played for [Lyon](/clubs/lyon-women) in 2013-14, reaching a Champions League final, before returning to Seattle Reign, where she spent a decade and won two NWSL Shields. The 2015 World Cup win made her a champion; the 2019 campaign made her a global figure. She scored both goals against Spain in the round of 16, both against France in the quarter-final at the Parc des Princes, and the final's opening penalty, each celebrated with the outstretched-arms pose that became the tournament's image. A 2020 Olympic bronze followed, and her last international appearance came in the 2023 World Cup round-of-16 defeat to Sweden, when she missed in the shootout.

## How she plays

Rapinoe was a right-footed left winger whose game revolved around delivery. Her corners and free-kicks were the USA's main [set-piece](/tactics/set-pieces) weapon, and she scored direct from corners more than once. From open play she cut inside to shoot or clip crosses to the far post, and her disguised through balls from the left half-space fed Alex Morgan for years. She was never fast or a classic dribbler; she beat players with the ball's movement rather than her own, and her composure in the biggest games, notably from the penalty spot in 2019, was absolute.

## Evolution

A pure winger in her twenties, she became a wide playmaker in her thirties under Jill Ellis, drifting inside and running the game from the left. By 2023 she was a substitute and leader. Her off-field evolution, from kneeling during the anthem in 2016 to leading the equal-pay case, is as much a part of her story.

## Best plays and moments

- Cross for Wambach's equaliser against Brazil, 2011 World Cup quarter-final.
- Olimpico goal against Canada, London 2012 Olympic semi-final.
- Two goals against France in the 2019 World Cup quarter-final, Paris.
- Opening penalty in the 2019 World Cup final, Lyon, and the Golden Ball.
- Ballon d'Or and The Best awards, 2019, and the Medal of Freedom, 2022.

## Legacy / Current situation

Rapinoe retired in November 2023 after an Achilles injury in the NWSL Championship final and now works in media and business with her wife, basketball legend Sue Bird, who is a part-owner of [Gotham FC](/clubs/gotham-fc). She remains the most influential figure the women's game has produced off the pitch.
