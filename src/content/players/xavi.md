## Overview

Xavi Hernández is the midfielder who defined how the best teams of the 2008-2012 era played. At [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) and with [Spain](/nations/spain) under Luis Aragonés and Vicente del Bosque, he was the fulcrum of [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka): short passes, constant movement, and a monopoly on the ball that turned defending into an exhausting chase. He won the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), two [Euros](/tournaments/euro) and four [Champions Leagues](/leagues/champions-league), finished third in the Ballon d'Or three years running, and made 767 appearances for Barcelona, a record until [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) passed it.

## Early life and breakthrough

Xavi was born in Terrassa, outside Barcelona, the son of a former Sabadell player, and joined La Masia at 11. He grew up in the academy teaching of [Johan Cruyff](/managers/johan-cruyff)'s model: rondos, positional discipline, and the question every coach asked, "Where is the free man?". He made his first-team debut under Louis van Gaal in August 1998 in the Spanish Super Cup, scoring, and played 26 games in his first season as Barcelona won La Liga.

For several years he was a solid but unspectacular player in a declining team. Barcelona finished sixth in 2003, and when [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho) arrived Xavi was sometimes seen as the functional partner. He won a silver medal at the 2000 Olympics, played at the 2002 World Cup and Euro 2004, and became first-choice as the team revived under Frank Rijkaard, winning La Liga in 2005 and 2006 and the Champions League in 2006.

## Peak years

Euro 2008 changed his status. Aragonés built Spain around a midfield of Xavi, [Andrés Iniesta](/players/andres-iniesta), Marcos Senna and Cesc Fàbregas, and Xavi scored in the semi-final against Russia and assisted Fernando Torres in the final against [Germany](/nations/germany). He was named player of the tournament.

Then Guardiola arrived, sold Ronaldinho and Deco, and told Xavi he was the most important player at the club. In 2008-09 Barcelona won the treble, with Xavi assisting Messi's header in the Champions League final against [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in Rome. In 2009-10 he posted 20 La Liga assists. In 2010 he was the engine of Spain's World Cup win, delivering the corner for [Carles Puyol](/players/carles-puyol)'s semi-final header against Germany, and in 2011 he ran the Wembley final against United, a performance Alex Ferguson described as a "carousel" that left his players dizzy. Euro 2012 followed, with Xavi assisting two goals in the 4-0 final against [Italy](/nations/italy).

He left Barcelona in 2015 after a fourth Champions League, in Luis Enrique's treble, and spent four years at Al Sadd in Qatar before retiring in 2019.

## How he played

Xavi's game started before the ball arrived. Studies of his scanning found he looked over his shoulder far more often than any team-mate, up to eight times in the ten seconds before receiving, so by the time the ball reached him he already knew where the free man was. His first touch was taken on the half-turn with the inside of either foot, opening his body to the widest range of options.

He passed overwhelmingly short, but the short passes were purposeful: they moved opponents, created triangles with [Sergio Busquets](/players/sergio-busquets) and Iniesta, and bought time until a line could be broken. When the gap opened, he played through it with a firm, instep-driven pass to Messi or [Samuel Eto'o](/players/samuel-etoo). His signature move was the pause: a drag-back or a hesitation that invited the press, then a pass to the man the presser had just left.

At 1.70 m he was never a physical presence and did not win many aerial duels; he was also not a tackler, relying on positioning and the team's counter-press to recover the ball. His goal return was modest and his long-range shooting rare. Critics in England said he was slow; Barcelona's answer was that nobody got the ball off him anyway.

## Evolution

Van Gaal used him as a holding midfielder, a pivot who recycled possession. Rijkaard moved him into a more advanced role beside Deco. Guardiola's masterstroke was to pair him with Iniesta ahead of Busquets, two creators who interchanged, with Xavi slightly to the right. As Messi moved into the false-nine role in 2009, Xavi became the player who fed him from the right half-space.

In his thirties, under Tito Vilanova, Gerardo Martino and Luis Enrique, his minutes were managed and the team moved towards a faster, more direct style built on Messi, [Luis Suárez](/players/luis-suarez) and [Neymar](/players/neymar). He adapted gracefully as a senior squad player and captain.

## Legacy

Xavi's influence runs through every team that tries to control possession. Guardiola called him the player who best understood the "Barcelona way"; a generation of Spanish and Catalan midfielders, from Thiago to [Pedri](/players/pedri), has been measured against him, and [Rodri](/players/rodri)'s 2026 World Cup Golden Ball was won playing a game Xavi would recognise. The idea that a 1.70 m midfielder with no pace could be the best player in a team of superstars changed how academies scouted.

He returned to Barcelona as head coach in November 2021, won La Liga in 2022-23, and left in the summer of 2024, replaced by Hansi Flick. The Spain side that won the 2026 World Cup still plays with the principles his generation established.
