## Overview

Ronaldo was the complete modern striker before the phrase existed. At 20 he was already the best player in the world, a forward who ran at defences from the halfway line and beat them with speed, step-overs and a finish that never seemed to miss. Two catastrophic knee injuries robbed him of his explosive years at [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), yet he still came back to score eight goals at the 2002 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), including both in the final, and win a second Ballon d'Or. He is one of only a handful of players to score for [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid), Inter and [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Bento Ribeiro, a working-class suburb of Rio de Janeiro, Ronaldo was turned down by Flamengo as a boy because he could not afford the bus fare to training. São Cristóvão took him instead, and from there he moved to Cruzeiro, where he scored 44 goals in 47 games, including five in one match against Bahia. He was taken to the 1994 World Cup as a 17-year-old and did not play, but watched [Brazil](/nations/brazil) win and absorbed everything.

[PSV Eindhoven](/clubs/psv-eindhoven) signed him on Romário's recommendation. He scored 30 goals in his first [Eredivisie](/leagues/eredivisie) season and 54 in 57 games overall, then Barcelona paid a world-record fee in 1996.

## Peak years

His single season at Barcelona under Bobby Robson, with [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) as assistant, produced 47 goals in 49 games, the Cup Winners' Cup and the famous run through the Compostela defence that left Robson standing with his hands on his head. He won FIFA World Player of the Year at 20.

A contract dispute took him to Inter for another world record in 1997. He won the 1998 UEFA Cup, scoring in the final against [Lazio](/clubs/lazio), and the 1997 Ballon d'Or. At the 1998 World Cup he was the tournament's star until the final, when a convulsive fit hours before kick-off left him a shadow as [France](/nations/france) and [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane) won 3-0.

Then came the knees. His right patellar tendon ruptured in November 1999, and in April 2000, six minutes into his comeback against Lazio, it shattered again on live television. He played 17 league games in the next two and a half years.

The 2002 World Cup was his resurrection. With a bizarre haircut and a rebuilt body, he scored in six of seven games, including both goals in the final against [Germany](/nations/germany) past [Oliver Kahn](/players/oliver-kahn), and won the Golden Boot. Real Madrid signed him that summer as the last of the Galácticos. He scored 23 league goals in 2002-03 as Madrid won La Liga, and his hat-trick at Old Trafford in the Champions League quarter-final earned a standing ovation from the [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) crowd.

## How he played

The young Ronaldo was a phenomenon of acceleration. He wanted the ball to feet with space in front of him, and once he was moving, defenders could not stop him legally. His step-overs were performed at full sprint, which is what made them so hard to read, and he used the elastico, the flip-flap, more than any other elite striker. He was strong enough to hold off centre-backs and had the balance to ride tackles.

His finishing was clinical and varied: the toe-poke, the drag past the keeper and roll into the empty net, the low shot across goal. He was not a target man and not a great header, and he did not press. His decision-making was simple and devastating: if there was a gap, he went through it.

After the injuries, his touch and finishing remained but the acceleration was gone. He became a penalty-box striker who relied on anticipation, and his weight fluctuated. Critics said he coasted at Madrid; the goals say otherwise.

## Evolution

At Cruzeiro and PSV he was a pure dribbling goalscorer in a 4-4-2. At Barcelona Robson built the whole team to feed him, often as the lone striker. At Inter he played off Iván Zamorano and later with Christian Vieri, and under Marcello Lippi briefly tried a deeper role that did not suit him.

Post-injury, for Brazil in 2002, Luiz Felipe Scolari paired him with [Rivaldo](/players/rivaldo) and [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho) in a 3-5-2 that asked him only to finish moves, which he did. At Madrid he played a similar role in front of Zidane and [Raúl](/players/raul), and under Fabio Capello in 2006-07 he was pushed out for fitness reasons before a late move to Milan, where he briefly formed a front line with Kaká. He finished at Corinthians, winning the Paulista and the Copa do Brasil, and retired in 2011 because, as he said, his body would no longer obey him.

## Best plays and moments

- 1996, La Liga, Compostela: a 60-metre solo run through the whole defence. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Ronaldo+goal+vs+Compostela+1996)
- 1997, Cup Winners' Cup final, Paris Saint-Germain: the winning penalty for Barcelona in Rotterdam.
- 1997, Copa América final, Bolivia: scored as Brazil won 3-1 in La Paz.
- 1998, UEFA Cup final, Lazio: the trademark dummy and finish to seal a 3-0 win for Inter.
- 1998, World Cup semi-final, Netherlands: opening goal in a 1-1 draw that Brazil won on penalties.
- 2002, World Cup semi-final, Turkey: a toe-poked winner that became a coaching reference for improvised finishing.
- 2002, World Cup final, Germany: two goals, one from Kahn's spill, one a low placed shot. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Ronaldo+2002+World+Cup+final+Germany+goals)
- 2003, Champions League quarter-final, Manchester United: a hat-trick at Old Trafford and a standing ovation. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Ronaldo+hat+trick+Old+Trafford+2003+Manchester+United)
- 2006, World Cup, Ghana: the goal that took him past Gerd Müller as the World Cup's then record scorer with 15.
- 2011, Serie A exhibition and retirement: a farewell for Brazil against Romania at the Pacaembu.

## Legacy

Ronaldo is the striker that a generation of forwards, from [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry) and [Zlatan Ibrahimović](/players/zlatan-ibrahimovic) to [Kylian Mbappé](/players/kylian-mbappe), cite as their idol. The "what if" of his knees is the great counterfactual of modern football, but even the diminished version won a World Cup Golden Boot and a Ballon d'Or. His 15 World Cup goals stood as the record until Miroslav Klose passed him in 2014.

Since retiring he has become an owner, first of Real Valladolid in [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) and then of Cruzeiro, the club where he started. He remains the first name in any discussion of the great Brazilian number 9s, alongside the men who played behind him: Rivaldo, Ronaldinho and [Kaká](/players/kaka).
