## Overview

Manuel Neuer changed what a goalkeeper is. Before him, keepers who left their line were eccentrics; after his 2014 World Cup, where he played as a sweeper 30 metres from goal against Algeria, every elite coach wanted one. He has won 13 [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) titles, two Champions Leagues and the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), captained [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) and [Germany](/nations/germany), and at 40 is still Bayern's number one. He retired from international football after the 2026 World Cup and has said that 2026-27 is very likely his last season as a player.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Gelsenkirchen, Neuer came through Schalke's academy and was their first-choice goalkeeper at 20 in 2006. He was the best young keeper in Europe by 2008, played in Germany's Euro 2008 squad, and in 2010 was first choice as Germany finished third at the World Cup. He captained Schalke to the 2011 DFB-Pokal and a Champions League semi-final, then joined Bayern in a €22m move that Schalke fans still resent and Bayern ultras initially protested.

## Peak years

Neuer's peak, roughly 2012 to 2016, is the goalkeeping peak of the modern era. He won the treble under Jupp Heynckes in 2013 and the World Cup in 2014, where his sweeping against Algeria and his saves in the final against Argentina won him the Golden Glove and third place in the Ballon d'Or, the highest a goalkeeper had finished since Oliver Kahn. Under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) he was effectively an eleventh outfield player.

A series of foot fractures in 2017 and 2018 cost him a year. He returned, won the 2020 treble under Hansi Flick with a performance in the Champions League final against PSG that was decisive, and was named FIFA's best goalkeeper. A skiing accident in December 2022 broke his leg and he missed the rest of that season; he returned in autumn 2023 and has been Bayern's starter since, winning the 2024-25 title and the 2025-26 double under [Vincent Kompany](/managers/vincent-kompany).

## How he plays

Neuer's starting position is the furthest from goal of any keeper in history. He reads the pass over the top before it is played, and his first step is forward. His footwork and distribution are those of a centre-back, and Bayern's high line has for 15 years been possible because of him. In one-on-ones he comes out early and spreads himself; in the air he dominates his box.

Reflex saves have always been underrated in his game because the sweeping is so visible, but they are elite, especially low to his right. The risks are obvious: a keeper 30 metres from goal is one misjudgement from a lob, and in his late 30s his recovery speed has declined, with a few high-profile errors in 2024 and 2025.

## Evolution

Schalke's shot-stopper became Heynckes's sweeper, Guardiola's eleventh outfielder and Flick's pressing keeper. Under Kompany he has sat a few metres deeper as his legs have aged, but the principle is unchanged. For Germany, he played 124 games, retired after Euro 2024, reversed the decision to play the 2026 World Cup and became the World Cup's all-time record goalkeeper by appearances with 21 before the round-of-32 exit to Paraguay.

## Current situation

Neuer signed a one-year extension in May 2026 and is Bayern's starting goalkeeper and captain in 2026-27 under Kompany, with Jonas Urbig as his understudy and likely successor. In July 2026 he said it was "very likely" to be his last season, while insisting it was not a farewell tour. He retired from Germany after the World Cup; [Joshua Kimmich](/players/joshua-kimmich) captains the national team under Klopp.
