## Overview

Hristo Stoichkov was the rage in [Johan Cruyff](/players/johan-cruyff)'s beautiful machine. Signed by [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in 1990 as the aggressive foreign forward the Dream Team lacked, he won four straight [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) titles and the club's first European Cup, formed a lethal partnership with [Romário](/players/romario), and in 1994 led a Bulgarian side that had never won a World Cup match to the semi-finals, beating the holders [Germany](/nations/germany) on the way. He shared the Golden Boot, won the Ballon d'Or and spent the rest of the decade arguing with referees, coaches and journalists on three continents. Nobody mistook him for a diplomat. Nobody doubted him either.

## Early life and breakthrough

Stoichkov was born in Plovdiv in February 1966, played for the local Maritsa and Hebros Harmanli youth sides, and moved to CSKA Sofia, the army club, in 1984. A year later he was banned for life, later cut to a year, for his part in a brawl during the 1985 Bulgarian Cup final against Levski. The suspension became part of the legend. On his return he scored 81 goals in 119 league games, including 38 in 1989-90 to win the European Golden Shoe, shared with Real Madrid's Hugo Sánchez.

He had made his Bulgaria debut in 1987, and the Golden Shoe put him on Cruyff's list. Barcelona paid about £2 million in the summer of 1990, and in his first season he was banned for two months for stamping on referee Urízar Azpitarte's foot during the Supercopa against Real Madrid. Barcelona won the league regardless, their first in six years.

## Peak years

From 1991 to 1994 Stoichkov was a cornerstone of the side that won four consecutive titles and, at Wembley in May 1992, the European Cup against Sampdoria, Ronald Koeman's extra-time free-kick settling it. Stoichkov scored 22 league goals in 1991-92 and in 1993-94, with Romário arriving, Barcelona produced the most devastating attacking football in Europe, hammering [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) 5-0 and reaching a second European Cup final, lost 4-0 to [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) in Athens.

The 1994 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in the United States was his masterpiece. Bulgaria lost 3-0 to Nigeria, then beat Greece, Argentina, Mexico on penalties and, in the quarter-final at Giants Stadium, Germany, Stoichkov's free-kick and Yordan Letchkov's diving header overturning Lothar Matthäus's penalty. He scored a penalty in the semi-final against [Italy](/nations/italy) too, but [Roberto Baggio](/players/roberto-baggio) scored twice. Six goals shared the Golden Boot with Oleg Salenko. The Ballon d'Or in December 1994 made him the first and only Bulgarian winner.

## How he played

Stoichkov was a left-footed forward who started wide and arrived central. Cruyff's 3-4-3 placed him on the left of the front three, where he could isolate a full-back, drive at goal on the diagonal and shoot across the keeper, or cut back for Michael Laudrup and later Romário. He had genuine sprinter speed, and he timed runs behind a defence off Laudrup's and Pep Guardiola's passes better than anyone in Spain.

His left foot was a weapon from dead balls: struck with the instep so that the ball rose, dipped and swerved late, as against Germany and Italy in 1994. He also finished with power rather than placement from open play, and was happy to shoot from 25 yards. He was a committed, often reckless tackler when the mood took him.

His right foot was ornamental and his temperament cost him dozens of matches to suspension. He drifted out of games he found beneath him. In the modern game he would be a left-sided inside forward in a 4-3-3, a more volcanic Ángel Di María, and every manager would want his free-kicks and transitions while worrying about the red cards.

## Evolution

Stoichkov went from a Bulgarian-league goal machine to a wide forward in Cruyff's [total football](/tactics/total-football) descendant, learning to play off a false nine, Laudrup, and then beside a true one, Romário. He was less prolific but more complete at Barcelona. A season at Parma in 1995-96 did not suit him and he returned to Barcelona under Bobby Robson, winning the 1997 Cup Winners' Cup with [Ronaldo Nazário](/players/ronaldo-nazario) as the star. He finished with short spells at [Al-Nassr](/clubs/al-nassr), Kashiwa Reysol, Chicago Fire and D.C. United, retiring at 37, and the 1998 World Cup, with Bulgaria eliminated in the group, was a sad coda. He played across the arrival of the Champions League, the Bosman ruling and the globalisation of the game, and was one of the first Eastern European stars to become a global brand after 1989.

## Legacy

Stoichkov is the greatest Bulgarian footballer and the player who proved Cruyff's Barcelona needed steel as well as silk. Cruyff said later that the Dream Team was built on "Koeman at the back, Laudrup in the middle and Stoichkov's bad temper up front", and his willingness to fight was the quality Barcelona have most often lacked since. His partnership with Romário in 1993-94 is the template for the wide-forward-plus-poacher pairing that [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique) later built with Neymar and Luis Suárez.

Bulgaria's 1994 run remains the country's greatest sporting achievement, and with [Gheorghe Hagi](/players/gheorghe-hagi) he represents the generation of Eastern European stars who emerged as the Iron Curtain fell. He became a controversial coach of Bulgaria and Celta Vigo, and a louder television pundit, always defending Barcelona and Cruyff. He remains a Barcelona ambassador, and the club's left-sided forwards from Rivaldo to Neymar to Lamine Yamal's inverted mirror image all inherit the lane he once ran down.
