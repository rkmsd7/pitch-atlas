## Overview

Lionel Messi has won more Ballons d'Or than anyone (eight), scored more goals for a single club than anyone (672 for [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona)), won the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) in 2022 with the greatest individual tournament in living memory, and then, at 39, captained [Argentina](/nations/argentina) to another World Cup final in July 2026, where Spain won 1-0 after extra time with Enzo Fernández sent off. He plays for [Inter Miami](/clubs/inter-miami) in [MLS](/leagues/mls), where his arrival in 2023 transformed the league's profile. The debate about whether he is the greatest player ever has, for most people who watched the last five years, been settled.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Rosario, Messi was diagnosed with a growth hormone deficiency at 10, and Barcelona agreed to pay for his treatment when they signed him at 13, a contract famously sketched on a napkin. He came through La Masia, debuted in October 2004 at 17 under Frank Rijkaard and scored his first goal, from a Ronaldinho chip, in May 2005. By 2006 he was a Champions League winner; by 2007 he had scored a solo goal against Getafe that replicated Maradona's 1986 goal against England; by 2009, under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) and playing as a false nine, he was the best player in the world and Barcelona had won the treble.

## Peak years

The Guardiola years, 2008 to 2012, produced the best club side many have ever seen and Messi's most extreme numbers: 91 goals in the calendar year 2012, 73 in the 2011-12 season, four consecutive Ballons d'Or. He scored in the 2009 and 2011 Champions League finals against Manchester United. After Guardiola he kept winning: a second treble in 2015 with Neymar and Luis Suárez, a fifth and sixth Ballon d'Or, six European Golden Shoes, and a run of Clásico performances that made him Real Madrid's tormentor. He left Barcelona in tears in August 2021 when the club could not afford to register his new contract.

For Argentina, the story was long failure and then redemption: lost finals in 2014, 2015 and 2016, a brief international retirement, and then the 2021 Copa América, the 2022 World Cup, where he scored seven goals including two in the final against France and won the Golden Ball, and the 2024 Copa América. The 2026 World Cup, with Messi at 39, produced a semi-final win over England and a final defeat to [Spain](/nations/spain).

## How he plays

Messi's dribbling is built on a low centre of gravity, a left foot that touches the ball every stride and an ability to change direction without slowing. Defenders are beaten not by tricks but by the fact that the ball is never far enough away to tackle. His passing, especially the through-ball into the inside-right channel and the chipped ball over a defence, is the best the game has seen, and his free-kicks are a reliable source of goals. His finishing, with a curled left-footed shot into the far corner, has barely declined.

At 39, in Miami, he walks for long stretches, saving his sprints for the moments that matter, and the team is built to give him the ball in the right half-space with runners ahead. He has never been a presser, and he has never been a header of the ball. For Argentina in 2026 he was a deeper playmaker than ever, with [Julián Álvarez](/players/julian-alvarez) and [Lautaro Martínez](/players/lautaro-martinez) running in front of him.

## Evolution

A right winger under Rijkaard, a false nine under Guardiola, a right-sided playmaker under Luis Enrique next to Neymar and Suárez, a free number 10 in his last Barcelona years, a right winger again at [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) alongside Mbappé and Neymar, and at Inter Miami a roaming playmaker behind the striker. For Argentina, Lionel Scaloni built a team of runners around him from 2019 onwards.

## Best plays and moments

- 2005, La Liga, v Albacete: a first goal, chipped in from Ronaldinho's assist.
- 2007, Copa del Rey, v Getafe: the solo goal that replicated Maradona ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Messi+Getafe+2007+solo+goal)).
- 2009, Champions League final, v Manchester United: a header in Rome to complete the treble.
- 2011, Champions League final, v Manchester United: a goal at Wembley.
- 2012: 91 goals in a calendar year, a world record.
- 2017, La Liga, Real Madrid 2-3 Barcelona: a 92nd-minute winner and the shirt held up to the Bernabéu.
- 2021, Copa America final, v Brazil: a first senior international trophy at 34.
- 2022, World Cup final, v France: two goals in a 3-3 draw and the trophy on penalties ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Messi+World+Cup+2022+final+Argentina+France)).
- 2023, Leagues Cup: ten goals in seven games in his first Inter Miami trophy.
- 2026, World Cup final, v Spain: a runner-up at 39, the oldest outfield player to start a final ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Messi+World+Cup+2026+Argentina+highlights)).

## Current situation

Messi is in his fourth season at Inter Miami, alongside Luis Suárez, Rodrigo De Paul and Casemiro, with a contract that runs beyond 2026 and a new stadium opening in Miami. For Argentina, his international future after the 2026 World Cup final is undecided as of November 2026, as is that of coach Scaloni, whose contract expires in December 2026. Whatever he decides, the record is complete.
