## Overview

Bruno Guimarães was the player who made [Newcastle United](/clubs/newcastle-united)'s new era real. Signed from Lyon in January 2022 as the first marquee arrival of the Saudi-backed ownership, he became the club's captain, its heartbeat and its most popular player, winning the Carabao Cup in 2025 to end a 70-year wait for a domestic trophy. In August 2026, at 28 and with Eddie Howe gone, he joined [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) for a reported £75m, a fee Newcastle described as one of the ten highest ever paid for a player of his age. [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) signed him to give the champions more goals from midfield.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Rio de Janeiro, Guimarães was released by Flamengo's youth system and rebuilt his career at Audax before Athletico Paranaense signed him in 2017. He was the midfield engine of the Athletico side that won the 2018 Copa Sudamericana and three state titles, and Lyon paid around €20m in January 2020. He played in the Champions League semi-final against Bayern Munich in his first months in France, won Olympic gold with [Brazil](/nations/brazil) at the delayed Tokyo Games in 2021, and had become one of Ligue 1's best midfielders when Newcastle paid about £40m in January 2022.

## Peak years

He arrived with Newcastle in a relegation fight and left them a Champions League club. In 2022-23 he was the Player of the Year as Howe's side finished fourth; he scored the goals that mattered in that run, and his "Bruno, Bruno" chant became the Gallowgate's anthem. He took the captaincy in 2024 and in March 2025 lifted the Carabao Cup after a 2-1 win over Liverpool at Wembley. The 2025-26 season brought a second Champions League campaign and a second Player of the Year award. Howe left at the end of it, Matthias Jaissle arrived in August, and the club chose to sell Guimarães and [Sandro Tonali](/players/sandro-tonali) in the same window.

## How he plays

Guimarães is a midfielder who refuses to be categorised. He receives from the centre-backs under pressure and escapes with a drag-back or a quick roll, then carries the ball through the middle third. His passing is ambitious: through balls from deep, early crosses from the left half-space, and switches to the right winger. He tackles with real force, wins aerial duels, and his late runs into the box produce goals, usually with a first-time strike or a near-post header.

He plays with visible emotion, which is both his appeal and his flaw. He picks up bookings for frustration, argues with referees, and has at times drifted out of position to chase the ball or the game. Coaches have also questioned whether he can be the deepest midfielder in a side that wants control: his instinct is to go forward.

## Evolution

Athletico used him as a holding midfielder. Rudi Garcia at Lyon played him in a double pivot. Howe first used him as the No.6 in a 4-3-3, then moved him to the left No.8 with Tonali on the right and Joelinton covering, which freed him and raised his goal output. At Arsenal he has been used as a No.8 alongside [Declan Rice](/players/declan-rice) and Martin Zubimendi, with pre-season analysis suggesting Arteta saw him as an alternative to [Martin Ødegaard](/players/martin-odegaard) in a midfield three, offering a goal threat from deep that Arsenal lacked in 2025-26.

## Best plays and moments

- 2018, Copa Sudamericana final v Junior: a first continental title with Athletico Paranaense.
- 2020, Champions League quarter-final v Manchester City: part of the Lyon side that wins 3-1 in Lisbon.
- 2021, Olympic final v Spain, Yokohama: gold for Brazil after extra time.
- 2022, Premier League v Leicester City: two goals, including a stoppage-time winner, in a 2-1 win that lifted Newcastle clear of relegation ([best goals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Bruno+Guimaraes+best+goals)).
- 2023, Premier League v Tottenham: part of the 6-1 win that clinched Champions League qualification.
- 2023, Champions League v PSG: a 4-1 win at St James' Park.
- 2025, Carabao Cup final v Liverpool: lifts Newcastle's first domestic trophy since 1955 ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Bruno+Guimaraes+Carabao+Cup+final+2025+Newcastle)).
- 2026, 8 August: completes a £75m move to Arsenal and takes the No.39 shirt ([Arsenal debut](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Bruno+Guimaraes+Arsenal+debut+2026)).

## Current situation

Guimarães said on arrival that he wanted to "make history" at Arsenal, and he joined a side that opened its title defence with a 2-0 win over Coventry and sat top after nine games in early November, having conceded only five goals. Sporting director Andrea Berta said he would strengthen the centre of the team and give Arteta greater flexibility. He signed a four-year contract with an option for a fifth. For Brazil under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) he was part of the 2026 World Cup squad and remains a regular.
