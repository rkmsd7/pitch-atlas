## Overview

Alisson Becker is the goalkeeper who completed [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool)'s transformation under [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp). Signed from Roma in July 2018 for a then world-record fee of around £65m, he arrived six weeks after Loris Karius's errors in the Champions League final and gave the team a base of calm that it had lacked for a decade. He has since won every major honour available, the 2019 Yashin Trophy and two Golden Gloves, and in 2026-27 remains first choice for [Andoni Iraola](/managers/andoni-iraola) and for [Brazil](/nations/brazil).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Novo Hamburgo in Brazil's south, Alisson followed his older brother Muriel into Internacional's academy and made his senior debut in 2013. By 2014 he was first choice, winning four consecutive Campeonato Gaúcho titles and earning a first Brazil cap in 2015. Roma signed him in 2016 for around €8m; he spent his first season as Wojciech Szczęsny's understudy, then became a starter in 2017-18 and was outstanding in a Champions League run that ended in the semi-finals against Liverpool. That campaign made him the most sought-after goalkeeper in Europe.

## Peak years

Alisson's first Liverpool season, 2018-19, was close to flawless: 21 clean sheets, the Golden Glove, a last-minute save from Arkadiusz Milik against Napoli that kept Liverpool in the Champions League, and the trophy itself in Madrid. That summer he added the Copa América with Brazil, conceding one goal in the tournament, and he was named the world's best goalkeeper by FIFA and France Football. The 2019-20 title followed. His 2021-22 season brought a second Golden Glove, two domestic cups and another Champions League final, and his form through 2023-24, when Liverpool's defence was in flux, was widely regarded as the best in the league. He kept the clean sheets that secured the 2024-25 title under Arne Slot.

## How he plays

Alisson's defining skill is the one-on-one. He does not rush out; he advances a few steps to narrow the angle, stays tall for as long as possible and then makes himself wide at the last moment, so the forward sees a goal that keeps shrinking. His reactions are excellent but his positioning means he rarely needs them, and he is especially good at saving with his feet and legs on low shots.

Behind a high line he sweeps early and decisively, often outside the box, and his judgement about when to come for crosses is sound. His distribution is a weapon: quick, flat throws to the full-backs to beat a press, and long kicks that find [Mohamed Salah](/players/mohamed-salah) or now [Bradley Barcola](/players/bradley-barcola) in the channels. He is comfortable receiving a back-pass under pressure and playing through midfield.

The weaknesses are few. He can occasionally be beaten at his near post from tight angles, he has had a handful of high-profile errors with the ball at his feet, and persistent hamstring problems have cost him stretches of several seasons, which is why Liverpool have invested in capable deputies.

## Evolution

At Internacional he was a shot-stopper in a league where goalkeepers stayed on their line. Roma, under Luciano Spalletti and then Eusebio Di Francesco, taught him to play higher and to use his feet. Klopp's Liverpool asked him to sweep behind one of the highest lines in Europe and to start attacks. Under Slot the team held more possession, so Alisson's short distribution and composure in build-up became more important than his long kicking. Iraola's Liverpool press from the front and defend aggressively, and Alisson is again being asked to cover a lot of grass behind [Virgil van Dijk](/players/virgil-van-dijk).

## Best plays and moments

- 2018, Champions League v Chelsea: a string of saves for Roma in the group stage that put him on every scout's list.
- 2018, Champions League v Napoli: the 93rd-minute block from Milik at Anfield that sends Liverpool through the group ([best saves](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Alisson+Becker+best+saves+Liverpool)).
- 2019, Champions League final v Tottenham: eight saves in the 2-0 win in Madrid, including a late stop from Son Heung-min.
- 2019, Copa América final v Peru: a clean-sheet tournament bar one goal as Brazil win at the Maracanã.
- 2021, Premier League v West Bromwich Albion: the stoppage-time header, the first goal by a Liverpool goalkeeper, which keeps the club in the Champions League places days after his father's death ([header](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Alisson+header+goal+West+Brom+2021)).
- 2022, FA Cup final v Chelsea: the save from Mason Mount's penalty in the shoot-out.
- 2024, Premier League v Manchester City: a series of saves in a 1-1 draw at Anfield that kept Liverpool in the title race.
- 2025, Premier League v Tottenham: a clean sheet in the 5-1 win that seals Liverpool's 20th league title.

## Current situation

Alisson is still Liverpool's No.1 in 2026-27, though at 34 his contract and his fitness are watched closely, and the club has a ready-made successor in Giorgi Mamardashvili. He started the season in goal for Iraola's side, with Liverpool sitting outside the leading group of [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) and Brighton as of early November. For Brazil under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) he remained first choice through the 2026 World Cup, and he has not indicated any intention to retire from international football.
