## Overview

Martin Ødegaard was a prodigy at 15, a footnote at 20 and a champion at 27. The Norwegian who signed for [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) as a teenager spent years on loan before finding a home at [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal), where [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) made him captain in 2022 and built the team's right side around his passing. In 2025-26 he lifted the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) trophy, Arsenal's first in 22 years, and led the side to the Champions League final in Budapest.

## Early life and breakthrough

Ødegaard grew up in Drammen, where his father Hans Erik was a professional. He became the youngest player in Norway's top flight at 15 with Strømsgodset in 2014 and, that August, the youngest to play for [Norway](/nations/norway). A continent-wide scramble followed, and in January 2015 he chose Real Madrid, training with Carlo Ancelotti's first team while playing for Castilla under Zinedine Zidane. He made one La Liga appearance that May.

The hype outpaced his development. Loans to Heerenveen and Vitesse in the Netherlands rebuilt him, and a season at Real Sociedad in 2019-20 was the real breakthrough: he was La Liga's best creative midfielder for long stretches, and Madrid recalled him a year early.

## Peak years

With no clear role under Zidane he joined Arsenal on loan in January 2021, then permanently that August for around £30m. Arteta gave him the captaincy in July 2022, and Arsenal's near-miss title challenge of 2022-23 was built on his game: 15 league goals, a flood of assists for [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka), and a pressing intensity that set the tone for the whole side. Second-place finishes followed in 2024 and 2025, with a shoulder injury in 2024-25 the main disruption.

The 2025-26 season brought the title. Ødegaard's minutes were shared more than before, with [Kai Havertz](/players/kai-havertz) and [Viktor Gyökeres](/players/viktor-gyokeres) in form and [Eberechi Eze](/players/eberechi-eze) competing for the creative slots, but he started the decisive matches and captained Arsenal to the Champions League final, where they lost 4-3 on penalties to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) after a 1-1 draw.

## How he plays

Ødegaard's football is about where he stands before he gets the ball. He drifts into the right half-space, checks his shoulder and receives on the half-turn so that his first touch is already facing goal. From there the choices are quick: a disguised pass to Saka's feet, a reverse ball for the overlapping full-back, or a clipped cross to the far post. His weight of pass is exceptional, and he sees passes most midfielders cannot.

He is one of the hardest-working elite playmakers in the game. Arteta used him as the first line of the press, with his triggers (a slow pass to a centre-back, a touch towards the touchline) setting off the whole team. His shooting is a genuine weapon from the edge of the box, where the curled left-footed finish into the far corner is his signature.

His weaknesses are physical. He is not quick over ten yards, is not a powerful dribbler and can be nudged off the ball by strong central midfielders. When Arsenal lose control of a game he can disappear, and his goal output dropped after 2022-23.

## Evolution

At Strømsgodset and in Spain he was a left-footed wide player who drifted inside. Real Sociedad used him as a classic interior; Arteta initially played him as a number ten in a 4-2-3-1 before settling him as the right-sided number eight in a 4-3-3, which is where he has been most effective. The captaincy added a leadership layer: the raised fists, the on-pitch instruction, the sense that he was the team's conscience as much as its creator.

From 2024 Arsenal became less dependent on him for goals and chances as Saka, Havertz and then Eze and Gyökeres shared the load. His role in 2025-26 was more about controlling tempo and the press than about end product, and he accepted rotation for the first time in his Arsenal career.

## Best plays and moments

- 2014, Eliteserien: becomes the youngest player in Norway's top flight at 15 years and 118 days.
- 2019-20, La Liga v Real Madrid: a standout display for Real Sociedad against his parent club in a 4-3 Copa del Rey win at the Bernabéu.
- 2022, Premier League v Wolves: two goals in a 2-0 win in Ødegaard's early weeks as captain.
- 2023, Premier League v Chelsea: a goal and a game-controlling performance at Stamford Bridge in a 3-1 win.
- 2023, Premier League v Leeds United: a curling strike in a 4-1 win, the archetypal Ødegaard goal ([best goals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Martin+Odegaard+best+goals)).
- 2024, Premier League v Tottenham Hotspur: a goal in a 3-2 away win in the north London derby.
- 2025, Champions League quarter-final v Real Madrid: captains Arsenal to a 5-1 aggregate win over his former club.
- 2026, Premier League: lifts the trophy as Arsenal are crowned champions for the first time since 2004 ([captain of the champions](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Martin+Odegaard+Arsenal+captain+Premier+League+champions+2026)).

## Current situation

The winter of 2025-26 brought speculation. Spanish media reported Real Madrid interest in February 2026 and there were suggestions that Ødegaard, whose role had shrunk when Havertz and Gyökeres were flying, might consider leaving. Arsenal made clear they would not sell, he said publicly that he was happy, and at the start of 2026-27 he indicated a willingness to follow Arteta in committing to a new deal beyond his current contract to 2028, though no extension had been announced by early November. He remains captain of a side top of the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league), still the orchestrator of its right side, and the captain of [Norway](/nations/norway) alongside [Erling Haaland](/players/erling-haaland).
