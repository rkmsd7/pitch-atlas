## Overview

Pau Cubarsí plays the most dangerous position in European football, the last defender in [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick)'s [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) high line, and he does it with the serenity of a 32-year-old. He debuted at 17 in January 2024, started a Champions League quarter-final against PSG three months later, and in summer 2026 was named the Best Young Player of the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) as [Spain](/nations/spain) won their second title.

## Early life and breakthrough

From Estanyol in Girona province, Cubarsí joined La Masia at 11 and captained every age group he played in. He was a defensive midfielder as a boy, which explains the passing. Xavi gave him a first-team debut in the Copa del Rey in January 2024 and, with Barcelona's defence depleted, he started the Champions League tie against Napoli weeks later. By April he was the best player on the pitch in the quarter-final first leg at PSG, at 17.

He was named in Spain's Euro 2024 squad without playing, then went to the Paris Olympics and played the tournament for the under-23s.

## Peak years

Under Flick in 2024-25, Cubarsí and Iñigo Martínez formed the partnership behind the most aggressive offside line in Europe. Barcelona caught opponents offside at a historic rate, won [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga), the Copa del Rey and the Supercopa and reached the Champions League semi-final. He signed a contract to 2029 with a €1bn release clause.

He retained the title in 2025-26 and went to the 2026 World Cup as Spain's first-choice centre-back. In the knockout wins over Austria, [Portugal](/nations/portugal), [France](/nations/france) and [Argentina](/nations/argentina), the clean sheets that won Unai Simón the Golden Glove were built on his anticipation, and FIFA named him the tournament's best young player.

## How he plays

Cubarsí's defining skill is his timing in the step-up. Flick's line requires every defender to move together on a trigger, and Cubarsí is the one who sets the trigger: he reads the passer's body shape and steps a fraction before the ball is played. He defends forward, intercepting rather than tackling, and when he does have to go to ground his recovery speed is better than his build suggests.

With the ball he has one of the best long diagonals in the game, regularly finding [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal) on the far touchline from his own half, and he is calm enough to dribble out of pressure. The weaknesses are aerial: at 1.84 m he can be targeted by tall strikers, and he is not yet a dominant force at defensive set-pieces. Occasional lapses of concentration late in games are the other thing coaches will want to iron out.

## Evolution

Xavi used him as a conventional right-sided centre-back in a four. Flick turned him into the organiser of a high line, often the deepest player when the goalkeeper joins the build-up. For Spain, [Luis de la Fuente](/managers/luis-de-la-fuente) pairs him with a more physical partner and sits the line a little deeper, which has suited him.

## Best plays and moments

- 2024, Champions League, v Napoli: a composed knockout debut at 17.
- 2024, Champions League quarter-final, PSG 2-3 Barcelona: man of the match in Paris.
- 2024, La Liga, Real Madrid 0-4 Barcelona: the high line that caught Madrid offside repeatedly.
- 2025, Copa del Rey final, v Real Madrid: 120 minutes in a 3-2 win.
- 2025, Champions League semi-final, v Inter: two legs of chaos, 7-6 on aggregate.
- 2026, World Cup final, v Argentina: a clean sheet over 120 minutes and the Best Young Player award ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Pau+Cubarsi+World+Cup+2026)).

## Current situation

Cubarsí is a guaranteed starter in Flick's 2026-27 side, now partnered by a rotating cast after Ronald Araújo's departure, and he is increasingly the vocal organiser of the defence despite being 19. For Spain he is a fixture under de la Fuente, who extended his contract after the World Cup. The only serious debate in Barcelona is how long before he is captain.
