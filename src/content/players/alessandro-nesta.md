## Overview

Alessandro Nesta is the defender other defenders watch. Four years running he was named Serie A's Defender of the Year, he captained [Lazio](/clubs/lazio) to the 2000 Scudetto at 23, won two [Champions Leagues](/leagues/champions-league) with [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan) and started every knockout game as [Italy](/nations/italy) won the 2006 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) until a groin injury took him out of the semi-final and final. The grace of his tackling, long legs stretching to take the ball cleanly off a striker in full flight, made him the romantic's centre-back.

## Early life and breakthrough

Nesta was born in Rome and grew up a Lazio fan; he joined the club's academy aged nine and made his debut in 1994. Zdeněk Zeman gave him a regular place in 1995-96 and Sven-Göran Eriksson built the great Lazio side of the late 1990s around his partnership with Siniša Mihajlović. Nesta was captain by 21. Lazio won the Coppa Italia in 1998, the last ever Cup Winners' Cup in 1999 and the Scudetto and Coppa Italia double in 2000, when Nesta was already considered the best centre-back in the world. He had played at Euro 1996 as a 20-year-old reserve, started Euro 2000 and reached the final.

## Peak years

Lazio's financial collapse in 2002 forced the sale to Milan for €30m, a transfer that Lazio fans still mourn. At Milan Nesta joined [Paolo Maldini](/players/paolo-maldini) in the finest centre-back pairing of the decade, in front of Dida and behind [Andrea Pirlo](/players/andrea-pirlo), Gennaro Gattuso and [Clarence Seedorf](/players/clarence-seedorf). He won the Champions League in his first season, the 2003 final against [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) at Old Trafford in which neither defence conceded in 120 minutes, scored his penalty in the shoot-out and added the 2004 Scudetto. The 2005 final in Istanbul, where Milan threw away a 3-0 lead against [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool), was the low point. In 2007 Milan beat Liverpool in Athens, Nesta immaculate.

## How he played

Nesta's tackling was his signature. He would hold off a yard, let the attacker take a heavy touch and then extend a long leg to flick the ball away with the toe or the inside of the foot, usually standing or in a controlled slide that took ball and never man. His disciplinary record is absurd for a defender: he was sent off only a handful of times in two decades. He was fast over ten metres, which meant he could defend high and recover, and his heading was strong in both boxes.

His use of the ball was ahead of its time. He played the first pass into midfield cleanly, carried the ball out when space opened and could switch play. Weaknesses were almost all physical: a cruciate injury in 1998, repeated hamstring and groin problems, a back injury that required surgery, a shoulder dislocation. He missed at least a third of Milan's matches over his decade there. Tactically, if there was a flaw, he could be caught upfield when his partner was slower than him, which was the story of Milan's later years with Thiago Silva's predecessors.

## Evolution

At Lazio under Eriksson he played in a flat back four with licence to step up; at Milan in [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti)'s 4-3-1-2 and later 4-3-2-1 he was the deeper, covering centre-back while Maldini or Kaladze attacked the ball. In his thirties, with the pace reduced by injury, he moved his starting position deeper and leaned on anticipation. He retired from international football in 2006 as a World Cup winner with 78 caps and no goals, and from Milan in 2012 after a final Scudetto in 2011 and a last Champions League tie against [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in which, at 36, he held [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) for most of two legs. Spells at Montreal Impact and Chennaiyin in India followed, then management: Miami FC, Perugia, Frosinone, Reggiana and Monza, where he coached in Serie A in 2024-25.

## Best plays and moments

- 1998, Coppa Italia final v Milan: Lazio's first trophy of the Eriksson era, Nesta captaining at 22.
- 1999, Cup Winners' Cup final v Mallorca: the last edition of the competition, a 2-1 win at Villa Park.
- 2000, Serie A, Lazio v Reggina: the 3-0 final-day win that secured the Scudetto as Juventus lost at Perugia ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Nesta+Lazio+2000+scudetto)).
- 2000, Euro 2000 final v France: part of the Italian wall that was breached only by Wiltord in stoppage time.
- 2003, Champions League final v Juventus: 120 minutes of clean defending and a converted penalty at Old Trafford.
- 2004, Serie A, Milan: a title with the meanest defence in Italy.
- 2006, World Cup group v Ghana and USA: solid starts before the groin injury against the Czech Republic.
- 2007, Champions League final v Liverpool: the 2-1 win in Athens as Milan's defence held firm ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Nesta+AC+Milan+2007+Champions+League)).
- 2011, Serie A, Milan: an 18th Scudetto at 35 under Massimiliano Allegri.
- 2012, Champions League quarter-final v Barcelona: the duel with Messi, including a famous flick to deny him in the Camp Nou ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Nesta+Messi+Milan+Barcelona+2012)).

## Legacy

Nesta is the aesthetic ideal of Italian defending, and he and [Fabio Cannavaro](/players/fabio-cannavaro) represent the last generation of Italian centre-backs to dominate the world. Thiago Silva, Leonardo Bonucci and Milan Škriniar have all cited him. If he had stayed fit he might have challenged Maldini's status as Italy's greatest; as it is, he is in the Milan Hall of Fame, in the UEFA Team of the Year four times and in the FIFA 100. His quiet, almost shy demeanour off the pitch only reinforced the sense of a player who said everything with his timing.
