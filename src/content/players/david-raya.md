## Overview

David Raya was [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta)'s most controversial signing and then one of his most vindicated. Brought in from Brentford in 2023 to replace a fit and popular Aaron Ramsdale, the Spaniard won consecutive Golden Gloves and then kept goal for the [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) side that won the 2025-26 [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league), the club's first title in 22 years. A sweeper-keeper in the modern mould, he is also an understudy to [Unai Simón](/players/unai-simon) for world champions [Spain](/nations/spain).

## Early life and breakthrough

Raya grew up in Pallejà, near Barcelona, and left Cornellà's academy for Blackburn Rovers at 16 in 2012. A loan to Southport in the National League toughened him up, and he became Blackburn's first choice in 2017, helping them win promotion from League One in 2018. [Brentford](/clubs/brentford) signed him in 2019 for around £3m, and under Thomas Frank he was the keeper for the 2021 play-off final win that took the club into the Premier League for the first time.

## Peak years

Two strong Premier League seasons at Brentford, including a knee injury in 2021-22, made him one of the most admired keepers outside the top six. Arsenal took him on loan in August 2023 with an option to buy for £27m, which they exercised in 2024. The decision to drop Ramsdale for him was debated for months; by the end of 2023-24 Raya had 16 clean sheets and the Golden Glove, and he retained the award in 2024-25.

The 2025-26 season was the peak: the league title with the best defensive record in the division, and the Champions League final in Budapest, where Arsenal lost 4-3 on penalties to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain) after a 1-1 draw.

## How he plays

Raya is the last line of a high line. He starts well outside his box, sweeps balls played over Arsenal's defence and is comfortable heading or passing under pressure. In build-up he is a genuine 11th outfield player: he receives back-passes, draws the press and plays through it, and when the short option is blocked he launches long, flat kicks towards [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka) that bypass the whole press. His long kicking is among the most accurate in the league.

As a shot-stopper he is efficient rather than flamboyant, making himself big in one-on-ones and delaying his dive until the striker commits. He is a strong penalty saver, with two in a Champions League shoot-out against Porto in March 2024 and another from the spot at Atalanta later that year. His claiming of crosses has improved since his Brentford days.

At 1.83 m he is small for an elite goalkeeper, and his high starting position can be punished by lobs from distance. He has also had moments of indecision on through balls where sweeping and staying were both options.

## Evolution

At Blackburn he was a conventional line goalkeeper. Frank's Brentford turned him into a distributor, with the long, accurate kick to a target man a core part of their attacking plan, and gave him licence to sweep. Arteta wanted exactly that profile, plus a keeper who could play short under pressure, and Raya's integration into Arsenal's build-up is why the club pursued him despite having a good goalkeeper already. He is now a quieter, more controlled presence than the busier Ramsdale, which suits a team that prefers to defend by keeping the ball.

For Spain he has been a squad regular since 2022, winning the 2023 Nations League and travelling to Euro 2024 and the 2026 World Cup as back-up to Simón.

- 2018, League One: promotion with Blackburn Rovers as a first-choice keeper.
- 2021, Championship play-off final v Swansea City: a clean sheet at Wembley as Brentford reach the top flight.
- 2024, Champions League last 16 v Porto: two shoot-out saves send Arsenal into their first quarter-final in 14 years ([Porto penalties](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=David+Raya+penalty+saves+Porto+Champions+League+2024)).

- 2026, World Cup: part of the Spain squad that wins the tournament in New Jersey.

## Current situation

Raya is Arsenal's undisputed first choice in 2026-27, with the champions top of the table in early November and once again conceding fewer goals than anyone. Behind [William Saliba](/players/william-saliba) and [Gabriel Magalhães](/players/gabriel-magalhaes) he has the most settled defence in the league in front of him. For [Spain](/nations/spain), where Simón's seven clean sheets won the 2026 Golden Glove, he remains the dependable deputy under [Luis de la Fuente](/managers/luis-de-la-fuente).
