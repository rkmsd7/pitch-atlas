## Overview

Sergio Ramos captained [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) to four Champions Leagues in five seasons and won the World Cup and two European Championships with [Spain](/nations/spain), for whom his 180 caps are a national record. He scored 101 goals for Madrid, more than most midfielders, and was sent off a Spanish record 26 times in La Liga. No defender of the era combined quality, longevity and controversy so completely. As of November 2026 he is 40 and without a club, having left Monterrey in December 2025 without announcing retirement.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Camas, outside Seville, in March 1986, Ramos joined [Sevilla](/clubs/sevilla)'s academy at nine and made his first-team debut in February 2004 at 17 under Joaquín Caparrós. He played mostly at right-back and was so quickly obvious that Madrid paid his €27 million release clause in the summer of 2005, the most ever for a Spanish teenager, and gave him the number four shirt once worn by Fernando Hierro. Luis Aragonés gave him his Spain debut in March 2005 at 18, and he started every game at the 2006 World Cup.

## Peak years

Ramos spent his early Madrid years at right-back, winning La Liga in 2007 and 2008 and Euro 2008 with Spain, then moved permanently to centre-back under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) in 2011-12, when Madrid won the title with 100 points. He was central to Spain's World Cup win in 2010 and Euro 2012 alongside [Carles Puyol](/players/carles-puyol) and then Gerard Piqué.

The signature moment came on 24 May 2014 in Lisbon: 92 minutes and 48 seconds into the Champions League final against [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid), he rose to head Luka Modrić's corner into the net, and Madrid won 4-1 in extra time for La Décima. He became captain in 2015 and led the side to three consecutive Champions Leagues from 2016 to 2018 under [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager), scoring in the 2016 final and the shoot-out, and his challenge on [Mohamed Salah](/players/mohamed-salah) in the 2018 final remains one of the most argued incidents in the competition's history.

## How he played

Ramos defended by anticipation. He stepped out of the line to intercept and liked to meet strikers early, before they could turn, which is why he collected so many fouls and so many cards. In the air he was dominant, attacking the ball rather than waiting for it, and he could run with pace in his twenties, which made the recovery tackle a staple.

On the ball he was a confident, direct passer who could switch play and carry the ball forward, a comfortable fit in a possession-based Spain side that played [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka). His goal threat at set pieces was unmatched among defenders: 101 goals for Madrid, most of them headers, plus a run of Panenka penalties that defined his self-belief. His weaknesses were well documented: red cards, late lunges, and a reputation for gamesmanship. He lost a yard of pace in his thirties and compensated with positioning and the occasional professional foul.

## Evolution

He arrived as a marauding right-back under Vanderlei Luxemburgo and Fabio Capello, spent a season as a makeshift centre-back under Manuel Pellegrini, and became a full-time central defender under Mourinho, paired with Pepe. As captain under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) and Zidane he was the emotional centre of a dressing room with [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) in it. He left Madrid in June 2021 after a contract dispute and had an injury-affected two years at [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain), winning two Ligue 1 titles. He went home to Sevilla in 2023-24, then joined Monterrey in Liga MX in 2025, where he scored 15 goals from centre-back including at the Club World Cup, before leaving at the end of the year. He was left out of Spain's squads from 2021 and retired internationally in February 2023.

## Legacy

Ramos is the most decorated Spanish footballer by number of major titles and arguably the most influential captain Madrid have had since Raúl. His blend of defending, goals and menace gave Madrid's European runs their spine, and his set-piece threat changed how teams prepared for them. He is also a case study in the limits of aggression: the cards, the Salah incident and the Madrid exit are all part of the record. He has spoken about ownership and a return to football in a different role, and in 2026 was linked with investment in Sevilla. Whether he plays again or not, his standing as one of the great centre-backs of the 2000s and 2010s, alongside Puyol and [Fabio Cannavaro](/players/fabio-cannavaro), is secure.
