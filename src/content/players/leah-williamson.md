## Overview

Leah Williamson lifted the trophy at Wembley in 2022 and in Basel in 2025 as captain of [England](/nations/england-women), the first Lionesses skipper to win a major tournament and the only one to win two. A one-club player at [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal-women) since the age of nine, she won the 2025 [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) and the 2026 FIFA Women's Champions Cup, signed a new two-year contract in April 2026 and was promoted from OBE to CBE in the 2026 New Year Honours.

## Early life and breakthrough

From Milton Keynes, Williamson joined Arsenal's academy at nine and debuted at 17 in 2014. Her first famous moment came with England Under-19s in 2015, when she had to retake a penalty five days after the original was wrongly disallowed, and scored. She was a midfielder in her early years and moved to centre-back under Joe Montemurro, winning the 2018-19 [WSL](/leagues/wsl) title.

## Peak years

Sarina Wiegman made her England captain in April 2022, ahead of the home Euro, and she led a side that beat Germany in the Wembley final. An ACL tear in April 2023 ruled her out of the World Cup. She returned in 2024, played a central role in Arsenal's 2025 Champions League run, including the semi-final comeback against Lyon and the 1-0 Lisbon final win over Barcelona, and captained England through the comebacks of Euro 2025. Knee surgery in the winter of 2025-26 kept her out until March 2026, when she returned for the World Cup qualifiers against Ukraine and Iceland.

## How she plays

Williamson is the modern ball-playing defender. She builds from the back with vertical passes into midfield, steps into the middle to create overloads, and defends with anticipation and positioning rather than brute force. She is comfortable as the right-sided centre-back in a four or the central defender in a [back three](/tactics/back-three), and Wiegman has occasionally used her in midfield. Her leadership is understated but total. Pace is adequate rather than elite, and she is best protected by a disciplined midfield.

## Evolution

Midfielder to centre-back at 21, a conversion that unlocked her passing from deeper. Captaincy of club and country followed, and after two serious knee injuries she has become a more conservative defender who picks her moments to step out. Under Renée Slegers she is Arsenal's vice-captain behind Kim Little.

## Best plays and moments

## Legacy / Current situation

As of November 2026 Williamson, 29, is Arsenal's and England's captain, fit again and leading the Lionesses into the December play-off against Ukraine for a place at the [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup).
