## Overview

Jack Grealish is a throwback: a winger who wants the ball at his feet, who beats defenders by inviting them in, and who makes a crowd lean forward. He captained [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa), his boyhood club, from the Championship into the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league), became the first £100m British footballer when he joined [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city) in 2021, and won the treble in 2023. The last two seasons have been about rediscovery: after losing his place at City he moved to [Everton](/clubs/everton) on loan in 2025, and in 2026-27 he is back there for a second year under [David Moyes](/managers/david-moyes).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Birmingham and raised in Solihull, Grealish joined Villa at six. He played for the Republic of Ireland at youth level before committing to [England](/nations/england), spent 2013-14 on loan at Notts County in League One, and made his Villa debut in May 2014. The early years were uneven, with Villa relegated in 2016 and Grealish's off-field discipline questioned, but Dean Smith made him captain in 2019 and he was the driving force behind promotion through the play-offs that May. His 2019-20 season, keeping a limited Villa side up almost single-handedly, and his 2020-21 campaign, when he was the most-fouled player in Europe, made him one of the game's most coveted attackers.

## Peak years

City paid £100m in August 2021. [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) turned him into a different player: less of a free-roaming creator, more of a disciplined left winger who held width, kept the ball and set the tempo. The transformation peaked in 2022-23, when he was a starter in the run-in and in the Champions League final as City won the treble. He also started every game for England at [Euro 2020](/tournaments/euro) as a substitute weapon and in the 2022 World Cup. Thereafter his minutes shrank: injuries in 2023-24, a marginal role in 2024-25, and a decision in the summer of 2025 to leave on loan.

## How he plays

Grealish's dribbling is unusual. He does not rely on bursts of pace. He carries the ball on his right foot with his body between the defender and the ball, slows down, waits for the defender to commit and then rolls past or is fouled. Few players in the league are harder to dispossess, and few draw more free-kicks in dangerous areas. His low centre of gravity and strong frame let him ride challenges that would knock other wingers over.

He creates more than he scores: his best passes are the cut-back from the byline and the clipped ball to the far post. He also has a good eye for the reverse pass inside to a runner. His weaknesses are a modest goal return, a tendency to slow attacks when a quick pass is on, and a right foot that defenders can show him onto. He presses adequately rather than ferociously, which was part of the reason his City role faded.

## Evolution

At Villa he was a No.10 and then a left-sided free role in a 4-3-3 where he could roam and shoot. Guardiola restricted him to the left touchline and asked him to secure possession, which cut his goals but gave City control. At Everton under Moyes he has been given the Villa freedom back: a left winger who comes inside to combine, creates for the striker and takes set pieces. The foot injury he suffered in January 2026 ended his first Everton season early, but the club brought him back on deadline day in September 2026.

## Best plays and moments

- 2019, Championship play-off final v Derby County: captain as Villa return to the Premier League.
- 2020, Premier League v Manchester United: the opening goal in a 2-2 draw in a season he dragged Villa to safety.
- 2020, Premier League v Liverpool: two assists and a goal in a 7-2 win over the champions ([Villa years](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Jack+Grealish+Aston+Villa+goals+skills)).

- 2023, Champions League quarter-final v Bayern Munich: a goal in the 3-0 first leg at the Etihad.
- 2023, Champions League final v Inter: starts as City win the treble in Istanbul ([treble season](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Jack+Grealish+Manchester+City+treble+2023)).
- 2024, Premier League v Chelsea: a stoppage-time equaliser in a 1-1 draw.
- 2025, Premier League with Everton: a run of assists early in his loan spell that made him an instant favourite at Everton's new stadium ([Everton highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Jack+Grealish+Everton+goals+assists+2025)).

## Current situation

Grealish is on a second season-long loan at Everton for 2026-27, a deal that runs to the end of his City contract in the summer of 2027, after which he is expected to become a free agent. He wears No.10 for Everton. Everton started the season well and sat fifth after ten games in early November. City's new manager [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca) left the door open in July but the move was completed on deadline day. His England career has stalled under [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel) and he was not part of the squad that reached the 2026 World Cup semi-finals.
