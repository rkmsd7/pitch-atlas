## Overview

Nemanja Vidić arrived at [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in January 2006 for 7 million pounds and left eight years later as a five-time [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) champion, a [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) winner and the club captain. Alongside Rio Ferdinand he formed what many consider the finest centre-back pairing of the Premier League era, and he remains the only defender to win the league's Player of the Season award twice.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Titovo Užice in what was then Yugoslavia, Vidić came through Red Star Belgrade's academy, with a loan to Spartak Subotica before establishing himself. He captained Red Star to the Double in 2004 and was part of the famous "Famous Four" defence of Serbia and Montenegro that conceded a single goal in ten 2006 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) qualifiers.

Spartak Moscow bought him in 2004, and [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) brought him to Manchester in the same January window as Patrice Evra. Both struggled at first; Vidić was overrun on his debut against Blackburn and looked slow to adapt to the speed of the English game.

## Peak years

By the autumn of 2006 he had settled, and United won the title in 2007, 2008 and 2009. With Ferdinand he conceded 22 goals in 2007-08, and the side went a record 14 consecutive clean sheets in 2008-09, during which [Edwin van der Sar](/players/edwin-van-der-sar) went 1,311 minutes unbeaten. Vidić was named Player of the Season in 2009 and again in 2011, by which point he was captain, succeeding [Gary Neville](/players/gary-neville).

The 2008 Champions League final against [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in Moscow, won on penalties, was the peak of the side, and Vidić's header-ready aggression against [Didier Drogba](/players/didier-drogba) through a rain-soaked night was a large part of why Chelsea scored only once.

## How he played

Vidić was a traditional centre-back in the best sense. He wanted contact: he stepped into strikers to win the first ball, headed everything that came into the box and tackled with a timing that belied his reputation as a bruiser. In the air he was dominant at both ends, scoring 21 goals for United, many from [Ryan Giggs](/players/ryan-giggs) corners.

The partnership with Ferdinand worked because of complementary profiles. Vidić engaged; Ferdinand covered. Vidić took the striker; Ferdinand took the space and brought the ball out. In Ferguson's 4-4-2 and later 4-2-3-1 the pair sat relatively deep, protected by [Michael Carrick](/managers/michael-carrick), and Vidić's aggression was always underpinned by cover.

His weaknesses were exposed in specific match-ups. Fernando Torres famously tormented him in 2008 and 2009, exploiting his tendency to step in and a lack of recovery pace, and he was sent off in three consecutive meetings with Liverpool. He was also injury-prone late in his United career, missing most of 2011-12 with a cruciate ligament injury.

## Evolution

At Red Star he was a ball-playing captain who scored freely. At United he narrowed his game to what Ferguson needed: win the duel, win the header, organise. As captain from 2010 he became the voice of the back line, and after the knee injury he returned leaner and more cautious, though never less brave. David Moyes's single season in 2013-14 ended the era, and Vidić left for [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan) on a free. His body had gone; he played 28 games in Italy and retired in January 2016 at 34.

## Best plays and moments

- 2008, Champions League final v Chelsea: a towering, bloodied performance in the Moscow rain ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Manchester+United+2008+Champions+League+Vidic)).
- 2009, Premier League: 14 straight clean sheets and the Player of the Season award.
- 2008, Champions League semi-final v Barcelona: shut-out over two legs against Messi and Eto'o.
- 2011, Premier League v Chelsea: a header in a 2-1 win that decided the title race.
- 2007, Premier League v Liverpool: a goal in a 1-0 win at Anfield.
- 2010, Premier League v Manchester City: a winning header in the derby.
- 2008, Club World Cup final v LDU Quito: a red card, then United won anyway; the symbol of his bravery and risk.
- 2004, Serbian Double: captaining Red Star to league and cup.
- 2006, World Cup qualifying: the Famous Four concede one goal in ten games.
- 2014, final United appearance v Hull City: a farewell at Old Trafford after 300 games.

## Legacy

Vidić is the archetype of the fearless modern centre-back, the one every subsequent United signing has been compared with and found wanting. He has coached briefly in Serbia and worked as a pundit, and United supporters still sing his name. His career is also a reminder of how scouting a league like Russia's paid off for Ferguson: a 7 million pound fee for a decade of defending excellence.
