## Overview

Rasmus Højlund's story is one of pressure and release. [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) paid Atalanta £72m for him in August 2023, when he was 20 and had scored ten goals in a single Serie A season, and the two years that followed were a struggle: 26 goals in 95 games, long droughts, and a club in permanent upheaval. A season-long loan to [Napoli](/clubs/napoli) in 2025-26 gave him Antonio Conte, a settled system and 16 goals in 44 games, and when Napoli qualified for the Champions League the move became permanent, announced on 3 June 2026 for a fee reported at between €44m and €50m.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Copenhagen, Højlund came through FC Copenhagen's academy and made his debut in 2020. With first-team chances limited, he moved to Sturm Graz in Austria in January 2022 and scored 12 goals in half a season, which was enough for Atalanta to pay around €17m that summer. Gian Piero Gasperini used him as a channel-running striker and he scored nine Serie A goals in his first season, including a run of goals in the spring that put him on the radar of every big club in Europe. He had already scored five goals in his first two games for [Denmark](/nations/denmark) in March 2023.

## Peak years

At United, under Erik ten Hag and then Ruben Amorim, he showed flashes: five Champions League goals in his first group stage, a run of goals in early 2024, and a part in the 2024 FA Cup win. But he was isolated in sides that did not create much, his confidence visibly drained, and by the summer of 2025 United were ready to let him go. Napoli, Serie A champions, took him on loan with an obligation tied to Champions League qualification. Conte's 4-3-3 gave him service, [Kevin De Bruyne](/players/kevin-de-bruyne) behind him when fit, and a clear role, and he responded with his best season yet.

## How he plays

Højlund is a runner first. He pulls the centre-backs wide, then sprints diagonally into the channel the moment a midfielder's head comes up, and he finishes early, often first time across the keeper. He is strong on the ball at full speed and difficult to knock off it. Inside the box his movement is sharp, with a good sense of where the cut-back will arrive.

The criticism at United was that he offered too little in the build-up: his first touch with his back to goal was heavy and his link play rushed. That has improved in Italy, but he remains a striker who needs a team playing forward. He is 1.91 m but not a dominant header of the ball, and he has never been a volume shooter, which means his scoring depends on the chances created for him. His work rate in pressing is good.

## Evolution

Gasperini's Atalanta used him as a direct No.9 in a 3-4-1-2 with space to run into. Ten Hag asked him to be a focal point in a possession side, which did not suit him; Amorim's 3-4-2-1 gave him more runners around him but less service. Conte's Napoli played to his strengths and, in 2026-27, Massimiliano Allegri has compared him to [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) and asked him to add link play and a 20-goal season, a relationship that has already produced friction.

- 2023, Serie A v Lecce: a goal in a run of scoring form that made Atalanta's price soar.
- 2023, Euro 2024 qualifying v Finland and Kazakhstan: five goals in his first two Denmark games.
- 2023, Champions League group stage v Galatasaray: scores twice at Old Trafford ([United highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Rasmus+Hojlund+Manchester+United+goals)).

- 2025, Serie A debut for Napoli v Fiorentina: scores on his first appearance in a 3-1 win ([Napoli goals](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Rasmus+Hojlund+Napoli+goals+2025)).
- 2026, Serie A v Monza: the opening goal in a 4-1 win, his first since early September of the new season.

## Current situation

Højlund began 2026-27 as Napoli's first-choice striker under new coach Allegri, who in August said he had "significant margins for growth". The relationship has been bumpy: Allegri called him "pig-headed" after a 1-1 draw with Fiorentina, and Højlund publicly disagreed with his coach's assessment of a 4-1 Champions League defeat at Porto. His goal at Monza in late October was only his second of the season in ten appearances, though Napoli went top of [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a) that weekend. He wears No.19. For Denmark he was the starting striker at the 2026 World Cup.
