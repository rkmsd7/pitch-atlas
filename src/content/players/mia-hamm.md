## Overview

Mia Hamm was women's football's first global icon. She won the first [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) in 1991 at 19, the 1999 edition in front of 90,000 at the Rose Bowl, and Olympic golds in 1996 and 2004, and when she retired in December 2004 her 158 international goals were a record for any player, male or female. Nike named a building after her, Pelé included her in his FIFA 100, and she was the first woman inducted into the World Football Hall of Fame.

## Early life and breakthrough

The daughter of an Air Force pilot, Hamm grew up on bases across America and Italy and was spotted by Anson Dorrance, who gave her a US debut at 15 in 1987, still the youngest in the team's history. At North Carolina she won four NCAA titles and lost one game in four years. She was the youngest player in the 1991 World Cup-winning squad in China.

## Peak years

Through the 1990s Hamm was the face of the [United States](/nations/united-states-women) team's rise: top scorer in the 1995 World Cup bronze campaign, a gold medal in Atlanta 1996 in front of 76,000, and the 1999 World Cup, where she scored in the opener against Denmark and converted in the final shootout against China. She became the world's leading international scorer in May 1999 with her 108th goal. The WUSA, the first professional league, was built around her at Washington Freedom, and she won its 2003 title. FIFA named her World Player of the Year in 2001 and 2002, the first two editions of the award. Her career ended with gold at Athens 2004, a 2-1 extra-time win over Brazil, and a farewell match against Mexico in December.

## How she plays

Hamm was a complete forward. She played wide left or centrally, ran at defenders with the ball and had the acceleration to beat them, and finished with both feet. Her passing was underrated: 144 international assists, the most in USWNT history. She was a lethal free-kick taker and penalty taker. Above all she was competitive, famously uncomfortable with the spotlight but unforgiving of herself and team-mates on the pitch, a trait that the "99ers" generation made the national team's identity.

## Evolution

A pacy winger as a teenager, a central striker in her mid-twenties and a creative forward in her final years under April Heinrichs, when she set up as much as she scored. She played 276 internationals over 17 years.

## Best plays and moments

- World Cup winner at 19, China 1991.
- Olympic gold in Atlanta, 1996, in front of 76,481.
- 108th international goal, a world record, against Brazil, May 1999.
- Goal against Denmark and shootout conversion, 1999 World Cup.
- Olympic gold in Athens, 2004, and a 158-goal farewell.

## Legacy / Current situation

Hamm retired in 2004, married baseball player Nomar Garciaparra, and is a co-owner of MLS club Los Angeles FC and holds a stake in Serie A's Roma. The Mia Hamm Foundation supports bone-marrow transplant research and girls' sport. Every American forward since, from [Abby Wambach](/players/abby-wambach) to [Sophia Wilson](/players/sophia-smith), has been measured against her.
