## Overview

Cristiano Ronaldo has scored more goals than any player in the history of the game, more than 950 in official matches and counting, and he is still adding to the total at 41 in the [Saudi Pro League](/leagues/saudi-pro-league). He won the Ballon d'Or five times, the Champions League five times, Euro 2016 and two Nations Leagues with [Portugal](/nations/portugal), and league titles in England, Spain and Italy. He is the all-time record scorer in international football and in the Champions League. His rivalry with [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) defined 15 years of the sport, and his insistence on continuing to play, at [Al Nassr](/clubs/al-nassr) and for Portugal, has turned his final years into a running argument about longevity, ego and greatness.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Funchal, Madeira, the youngest of four children of a gardener and a cook, Ronaldo joined [Sporting CP](/clubs/sporting-cp) at 12 and debuted at 17. A pre-season friendly against Manchester United in August 2003, in which he tormented John O'Shea, persuaded [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) to sign him for £12m. He was given the number 7, spent three seasons as a stepover-heavy winger who was mocked for diving, and then became the best player in the world: 42 goals in 2007-08, the Premier League and Champions League double and his first Ballon d'Or.

## Peak years

[Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) paid a world-record £80m in 2009. What followed is the most prolific nine-year spell any footballer has produced: 450 goals in 438 games, 311 in La Liga, 105 in the Champions League, four European Cups between 2014 and 2018, four more Ballons d'Or and a transformation from winger to centre-forward. He scored in the 2014 and 2017 finals, a hat-trick against Atlético in the 2017 semi-final and a bicycle kick at Juventus in 2018 that drew a standing ovation from the home crowd.

For Portugal, he captained the side to Euro 2016, limping off injured in the final and coaching from the touchline as Éder scored the winner, and to the inaugural Nations League in 2019. [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) paid €100m in 2018 and he scored 101 goals in three seasons, winning two Serie A titles and a Capocannoniere award. A return to Manchester United in 2021 produced 24 goals and an acrimonious exit; in January 2023 he joined Al Nassr, the move that started the Saudi transfer boom.

## How he plays

The modern Ronaldo is a penalty-box specialist. He has shed the dribbling and the pace of his youth and kept the things that age slowly: his heading, which combines a vertical leap of over 70 cm with timing nobody has matched; his first-time finishing with both feet; and his positioning, which puts him on the end of crosses and cutbacks with a consistency that still looks supernatural. His penalty record is excellent. His free-kick knuckleball, once feared, has become a liability.

The weaknesses are those of a 41-year-old who refuses to adjust his status. He does not press, does not track back and rarely links play; the team must be built to serve him. For Portugal in particular, his insistence on starting became a tactical problem that Roberto Martínez struggled to resolve, and Portugal's quarter-final exit to Spain at the 2026 World Cup, which led to Martínez's replacement by Jorge Jesus, reopened the debate.

## Evolution

Ferguson's winger became Mourinho's inside-left forward at Madrid, Ancelotti's left-sided striker in a 4-3-3 and Zidane's penalty-box poacher. At Juventus and in his second United spell he was a pure number 9. At Al Nassr, under a succession of coaches including Luís Castro, Stefano Pioli and [Jorge Jesus](/managers/jorge-jesus) in 2025-26, he has been the fixed point around which everything else rotates. The club's current coach should be checked against the latest reports.

## Current situation

Ronaldo is in his fourth full season at Al Nassr, where the squad now includes Sadio Mané, Kingsley Coman, João Félix and Iñigo Martínez, and his contract runs to 2027. He has spoken of wanting to reach 1,000 career goals. For Portugal, his future under Jorge Jesus after the 2026 World Cup is, as of November 2026, the most-debated question in Portuguese football and should be checked against current reports.
