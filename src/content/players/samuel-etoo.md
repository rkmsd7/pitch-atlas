## Overview

Samuel Eto'o is the only player to score in [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) finals in consecutive seasons for different clubs, and he did it in two different roles. In 2009 he was [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona)'s centre-forward in [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola)'s first treble; in 2010 he was [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan)'s hard-running wide forward, often effectively a wing-back, in [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s treble. He won three Champions Leagues, four African Footballer of the Year awards, Olympic gold and two [AFCON](/tournaments/afcon) titles. He is the greatest African striker of the modern era.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Douala, Eto'o was spotted by [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) scouts and moved to Spain at 15. Madrid never really gave him a chance, loaning him to Leganés and Espanyol before a loan to Mallorca became permanent in 2000 for 7.2 million euros. He scored 70 goals in four seasons on the island and won the 2003 Copa del Rey, scoring twice in the final.

He had already made his [Cameroon](/nations/cameroon) debut at 15 and played at the 1998 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) at 17. In 2000 he won AFCON and the Olympic gold medal in Sydney, and he retained the AFCON title in 2002.

## Peak years

Barcelona signed him in 2004 for 24 million euros, with Madrid retaining half the fee, which Eto'o never forgave. Under Frank Rijkaard he was the spearhead of a front three with [Ronaldinho](/players/ronaldinho) and Ludovic Giuly, winning [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) in 2005 and 2006 and the Pichichi with 26 goals in 2006. In the 2006 Champions League final he equalised against [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) to set up a 2-1 win.

A serious knee injury cost most of 2006-07 and his relationship with Ronaldinho and Rijkaard deteriorated. Guardiola initially wanted to sell him in 2008 but kept him, and Eto'o scored 36 goals in 2008-09, including the opener in the final against [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united), as Barcelona won the treble alongside the emerging [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi).

Guardiola then swapped him for Zlatan Ibrahimović. At Inter, Mourinho asked him to play on the right of a 4-2-3-1 and track the opposing full-back. He did, with 16 league goals, and in the semi-final second leg at the Camp Nou he played almost as a left-back as ten-man Inter held on. He scored in the Coppa Italia final and was man of the match in the Club World Cup final.

## How he played

Eto'o was built for the counter-attack. He ran onto balls over the top with a sprinter's acceleration, and his finishing from those situations was ruthless, usually low and across the keeper with either foot. He also scored headers and poacher's goals, and in Rijkaard's team he was the one who gave width when Ronaldinho drifted.

His work rate was his second gift. He pressed centre-backs, dropped into midfield to help and, at Inter, covered the full-back for 90 minutes without complaint. He was proud and difficult, with frequent clashes with coaches and officials, but he was also prepared to subordinate his ego to a system in a way few great strikers have been.

Weaknesses: he was not a natural link player or creator, and he lacked the dribbling of Ronaldinho or Messi. His temperament produced flashpoints, including a walk-off in protest at racist abuse at Zaragoza in 2006, an act later recognised as a landmark moment.

## Evolution

At Mallorca he was a pure number nine. At Barcelona he learned to play in a fluid front three. At Inter he became a tactical weapon, a striker who defended. At 30 he took the money at Anzhi Makhachkala, where he was the world's best-paid player, then had a productive season with Mourinho at [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) in 2013-14, scoring a hat-trick against United. Spells at [Everton](/clubs/everton), Sampdoria and in Turkey followed, where he was still scoring freely at 36. He retired in 2019 after a year in Qatar.

## Legacy

Eto'o's 56 goals for Cameroon and four African Footballer of the Year awards make him the benchmark for the continent's forwards. He became president of the Cameroonian Football Federation in 2021, a tenure marked by controversy and sanctions, and remains a powerful figure in African football politics. On the pitch his legacy is the combination that so few strikers manage: elite finishing and elite sacrifice, both at the very highest level.
