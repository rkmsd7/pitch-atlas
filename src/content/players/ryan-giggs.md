## Overview

Ryan Giggs played for [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) from 1990 to 2014, scored in 21 consecutive [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) seasons and won more trophies than any player in British football history. He was the first of the Class of 92 to break through, the last to leave, and the player [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) trusted above all others. The winger of the early years became the midfield playmaker of the later ones, and in both guises he was decisive.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Cardiff and raised in Salford, Giggs played for [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city)'s school of excellence before Ferguson personally visited his home to sign him for United on his 14th birthday. He debuted in March 1991 at 17, scored in the derby in May and became a regular in 1991-92, winning the first of two PFA Young Player of the Year awards.

He was the first teenage superstar of the Premier League era, compared with George Best for his looks, pace and the way he bent full-backs out of shape. Ferguson protected him fiercely from the media.

## Peak years

Giggs's first peak was 1992 to 1999, when he was the left wing of the 4-4-2 that won the inaugural Premier League and the 1999 treble. The FA Cup semi-final replay goal against [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) in April 1999, a 60-yard run through Vieira, Dixon and Keown before a thrash past Seaman, is probably the most replayed goal in the competition's history. In the Champions League final he was one of the few United players to trouble [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich).

A second peak came in his thirties. From 2006 he was used more centrally, and in 2008 he came off the bench to score in the Champions League final shootout against [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea). In 2009, at 35, he won the PFA Players' Player of the Year award, and he was still starting Champions League games in 2011 and 2014.

## How he played

The young Giggs was all balance. He ran at defenders with short steps and a low centre of gravity, shifted the ball with the outside of his left foot and accelerated through the gap. He did not need to beat a man with a trick; he beat him with change of pace. His crossing was fast and flat, suited to the strikers Ferguson favoured, and he scored regularly cutting inside on his left foot.

The older Giggs was a different player. He moved to central midfield, sometimes to the number 10 role, and played with his head up and his feet still. He dictated tempo, found runners early and conserved energy. His yoga routine, famous in the dressing room, was credited with the hamstrings holding up.

Weaknesses were a one-footedness that opponents could sometimes exploit, inconsistent end product in the mid-2000s and a scoring record that was good rather than great for a player of his gifts. He never played at a major tournament, [Wales](/nations/wales) falling short in his era.

## Evolution

Giggs played under one manager for 23 years but in several systems: a 4-4-2 winger in the 1990s, a wide player in the 4-5-1 of the mid-2000s, then a central midfielder, deep playmaker and finally a rotation option under David Moyes, who made him player-coach. He took over as interim manager for the last four games of 2013-14 after Moyes was sacked, and retired that summer with 963 appearances.

## Best plays and moments

- 1999, FA Cup semi-final replay v Arsenal: the slalom run and the shirt-off celebration ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Giggs+goal+Arsenal+1999+FA+Cup)).
- 1999, Champions League final v Bayern Munich: part of the comeback that won the treble.
- 2008, Champions League final v Chelsea: a shootout goal on the night he broke Bobby Charlton's appearance record.
- 1994, Premier League v QPR: a trademark run and finish in the Double season.
- 2007, Premier League v Manchester City: a derby winner.
- 2009, PFA Players' Player of the Year: a lifetime award at 35.
- 2010, Premier League v Chelsea: a goal in the title race.
- 2011, Champions League semi-final v Schalke: an opener in Gelsenkirchen at 37.
- 2014, Premier League v Hull City: his final appearance, as interim manager and substitute.
- 1991, Premier League v Manchester City: his first goal, in a derby, aged 17.

## Legacy

Giggs holds records that may never be broken: 13 league titles, 963 United games, 21 consecutive seasons as a Premier League scorer. He managed Wales from 2018 and took them to Euro 2020 qualification, stepping down in 2022 amid legal proceedings that were later discontinued, and has since coached in League Two. On the pitch he remains the measure of longevity, adaptation and loyalty in English football.
