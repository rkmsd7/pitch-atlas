## Overview

Francesco Totti played for [Roma](/clubs/roma) from his debut at 16 in March 1993 to his farewell at 40 in May 2017, 786 games and 307 goals, every one of them in the same shirt. He won one Scudetto, in 2001, two Coppa Italias and the 2006 World Cup with [Italy](/nations/italy), and he could have won far more had he taken the offers from [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) and [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan). He stayed, and in staying became something larger than his trophies: the embodiment of a city.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Rome in September 1976 and raised in the Porta Metronia district, Totti was a Roma supporter who joined the club's youth system at 13, with his mother reportedly turning down an approach from Milan. Vujadin Boškov gave him his Serie A debut on 28 March 1993 at Brescia. Carlo Mazzone made him a regular and then a creative focal point, and in 1998, at 22, Zdeněk Zeman handed him the captaincy. He scored 12 goals in Zeman's thrilling 4-3-3 in 1998-99 and was called up to Italy the same year.

## Peak years

Under Fabio Capello, Roma won the 2001 Scudetto with Totti scoring 13 goals as the number ten behind Gabriel Batistuta and Vincenzo Montella. That summer Real Madrid made a serious attempt to sign him and he refused. He finished fifth in the 2001 Ballon d'Or and was named Italian Footballer of the Year for the first of five times.

Luciano Spalletti's arrival in 2005 produced his most prolific years. Deployed as a false nine in a 4-6-0 that was decades ahead of its time, he scored 26 league goals in 2006-07 to win the European Golden Shoe, having returned from a broken leg to play in the 2006 World Cup, where his injury-time penalty beat Australia in the last 16. He retired from international football at 30 with the trophy, aged by the physical cost of coming back for it.

## How he played

Totti received the ball with his back to the defender and used his thighs and arms to hold position before turning, then looked for the pass before the shot. His vision was the whole pitch: he played 40-yard passes with the outside of his right foot, sent through balls that split lines on the diagonal, and could chip a goalkeeper from 30 yards, the cucchiaio, with no backlift.

He was a fine finisher with either foot and a dependable penalty taker, and he used his body to protect the ball in a way that compensated for average pace. As a false nine he dropped deep to draw centre-backs out and then slipped wingers like Mancini and Rodrigo Taddei into the vacated space.

Weaknesses: he was never quick and he could be short-tempered, spitting at Christian Poulsen at Euro 2004 and earning a three-match ban. He was criticised for failing to transfer his club form to Italy consistently, partly because he was asked to play deeper or wider for the national team.

## Evolution

He began as a left winger and second striker under Mazzone, became a number ten under Zeman and Capello, then a false nine under Spalletti, which extended his career by removing the need to beat players. A cruciate ligament injury in 2008 and the arrival of younger strikers changed him into a deep-lying playmaker in his mid-thirties under Rudi Garcia, where his passing still ran the game. Under Spalletti's second spell he was reduced to a substitute, a painful public dispute, but he still scored decisive late goals, including a brace against Torino in 2016 at 39. His last game, against Genoa on 28 May 2017, saw the Olimpico weep.

## Legacy

Totti is Serie A's second-highest scorer of all time with 250 goals and holds the Roma records for appearances and goals by distance. He was arguably the first modern false nine, a concept later associated with [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), and his cucchiaio is still imitated. After a short unhappy spell as a Roma director he left the club in 2019 and has since worked as an agent and ambassador; a return to Roma under new ownership has been discussed many times. In Rome he is simply Il Capitano, and the number ten shirt has not been worn since he took it off.
