## Overview

Phil Foden is the boy from Stockport who grew up in [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city)'s academy, was told by [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) at 17 that he would not be sold at any price, and went on to win six [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) titles before his 25th birthday. His 2023-24 season, 19 league goals and the Player of the Season award, was the high point so far. Small, left-footed and sharp between the lines, he is the kind of player English football has rarely produced, and the club's hope is that he can anchor the post-Guardiola rebuild under [Enzo Maresca](/managers/enzo-maresca).

## Early life and breakthrough

Foden joined City's academy at four and was identified early as the best technician of his age group. He won the Golden Ball at the 2017 Under-17 World Cup, scoring twice in the final against Spain, and made his first-team debut that November in the Champions League against Feyenoord. Guardiola rationed his minutes carefully through 2018 and 2019, but by 2019-20 he was starting big games, and in 2020-21 he became a regular as City won the league and reached the Champions League final, picking up the PFA Young Player award.

## Peak years

From 2021 onwards Foden was a fixture on the left or right of Guardiola's front three. He played a full part in the 2022-23 treble, scoring in the Champions League quarter-final against Bayern, and then had his outstanding season in 2023-24 when he moved more centrally after [Kevin De Bruyne](/players/kevin-de-bruyne)'s injury: 19 league goals, a hat-trick against Aston Villa and a brace at Tottenham in the run-in, and the Player of the Season award as City won a fourth consecutive title.

The two seasons that followed were harder. He struggled for form and fitness in 2024-25 as City's whole machine misfired, and in 2025-26 he was in and out as the club finished behind [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal). He still went to the 2026 World Cup with [England](/nations/england), who reached the semi-finals and beat France 6-4 in the third-place match.

## How he plays

Foden receives on the half-turn in the pockets between an opponent's midfield and defence and his first touch takes him away from pressure before the defender has reacted. He is at his best moving infield from the left onto his stronger foot, or from the right where he can combine with the full-back and arrive in the box. His shooting from the edge of the area is a genuine weapon: low, placed, and usually across the goalkeeper.

He plays short combinations, one-twos and third-man runs rather than long passes, and his close control in congested areas is exceptional. He presses willingly and works back. His vision is good rather than extraordinary, which is why he is more productive as a scorer than as a creator, and physically he can be bullied by big full-backs. Consistency has been the criticism: his best season was so good that the following two inevitably suffered by comparison.

## Evolution

Guardiola first used him as a No.8 in the academy mould, then as a wide player in a 4-3-3 where he could cut inside. His 2023-24 peak came as a free-roaming inside-left or No.10, feeding off [Erling Haaland](/players/erling-haaland)'s runs and finishing moves rather than starting them. Under Maresca in 2026-27 City build through a box midfield, which tends to use attacking midfielders as inside forwards in the half-spaces, a role suited to his game. With England, [Gareth Southgate](/managers/gareth-southgate) and then [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel) have tried him on both wings and centrally, where he has sometimes been in [Jude Bellingham](/players/jude-bellingham)'s shadow.

## Current situation

The summer of 2026 changed everything around Foden: Guardiola left after ten years, Maresca arrived from [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) and City spent close to £458m, including £125m on [Enzo Fernández](/players/enzo-fernandez). Foden is one of the few senior survivors of the treble side and one of the club's biggest commercial faces, and after nine games City were second in the table. His place is not guaranteed in the way it was in 2024, but Maresca's possession-heavy system gives a player of his technique every chance. He wears 47, the number he has kept since his debut.
