## Overview

Morgan Rogers is the most expensive British footballer in history. [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) paid [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa) £117m for him in the summer of 2026, a figure that would have seemed absurd three years earlier, when he was a Middlesbrough player bought from Manchester City's reserves for around £1m. What happened in between was one of the sharpest rises the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) has seen: two seasons as the driving force of [Unai Emery](/managers/unai-emery)'s Villa, an England debut, a World Cup, and a reputation as the most physically dominant attacking midfielder in the division.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Halesowen in the West Midlands, Rogers came through West Bromwich Albion's academy before Manchester City paid a reported £4m for him in 2019, aged 16. He won the FA Youth Cup with City in 2020 but never made a senior appearance, and loans at Lincoln City, where he reached a League One play-off final, Bournemouth and Blackpool were uneven. Middlesbrough signed him in the summer of 2023, and under Michael Carrick he had the half-season that changed everything, including a goal against Chelsea in the League Cup semi-final. Villa paid an initial £8m in February 2024.

## Peak years

Emery used him first as a left winger and then as a No.10, and he became Villa's most important attacker: goals in the Champions League against Bayern Munich and Celtic in 2024-25, the most goal involvements of any Villa player across his two full seasons, and a maiden England call-up in 2024. He was named Villa's player of the season in 2024-25, started regularly for [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel)'s England, and played at the 2026 World Cup, where England reached the semi-finals.

Chelsea moved early in the 2026 window. The fee, £117m, broke the British record Liverpool had set for [Alexander Isak](/players/alexander-isak) a year earlier, and Rogers scored in his final pre-season game against Real Sociedad and then on his Premier League debut at Fulham.

## How he plays

Rogers is 1.88 m and runs with the ball like a man half that size. He receives on the left or in the left half-space, turns his marker with a heavy first touch that becomes a carry, and uses his frame to hold defenders off. His signature action is the drive from the left touchline towards the penalty area, followed by a square pass or cut-back, and he finishes well with both feet and his head. He presses from the front with real intent and wins the ball back high.

His weaknesses are about refinement. He can over-carry into traffic, his final pass is sometimes hit a beat late, and his output in front of goal has been good rather than prolific. His passing range in deeper areas is limited, so he is a transitional attacker rather than a controlling playmaker. At 24 he is still improving in every one of those areas.

## Evolution

At City's academy he was a left winger. Carrick at Middlesbrough played him as a free attacking midfielder; Emery used him on the left of a 4-2-3-1 and then as the No.10 behind [Ollie Watkins](/players/ollie-watkins), with licence to drift left and overload with the full-back. [Xabi Alonso](/managers/xabi-alonso) has paired him with [Cole Palmer](/players/cole-palmer) in a front line that interchanges constantly, with Rogers often starting on the left and arriving in the box while Palmer drops to create, and João Pedro ahead of them.

## Current situation

Rogers rated the start of his Chelsea career "10 out of 10" and by late September had three or four goals and four assists across competitions, depending on the source. He wears No.17. Chelsea sat fourth after nine games in early November, and Alonso has spoken of Rogers and Palmer "enjoying themselves" together, though Palmer's October suspension put more creative responsibility on Rogers. For England he is now part of Tuchel's core, competing with [Eberechi Eze](/players/eberechi-eze) and Palmer for the attacking midfield roles.
