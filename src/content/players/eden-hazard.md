## Overview

Eden Hazard spent seven seasons at [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) and was the most entertaining player in the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) for most of them: two league titles, two Europa Leagues, an FA Cup, 110 goals, 92 assists and a PFA Player of the Year award in 2015. He captained [Belgium](/nations/belgium) to third place at the 2018 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), winning the Silver Ball, then moved to [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) for €100m plus add-ons in 2019 and played only 76 times in four injury-wrecked seasons before retiring at 32 in October 2023.

## Early life and breakthrough

Hazard was born in La Louvière to two footballing parents, played for Tubize as a boy and moved to Lille's academy at 14. He debuted at 16 in November 2007, became Ligue 1's Young Player of the Year in 2009 and 2010 and then its Player of the Year in 2011 and 2012, the first non-French winner. In 2010-11 he led Lille to the league and cup double under Rudi Garcia with 20 goals and assists. He also became the youngest Belgian international for decades in November 2008.

## Peak years

Chelsea signed him in June 2012 for around £32m, and his first season ended with the Europa League under Rafael Benítez. Under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) he became a title winner in 2014-15, scoring 14 league goals and providing nine assists, named PFA Player of the Year and FWA Footballer of the Year. A poor 2015-16 ended with Mourinho's dismissal, but under Antonio Conte's 3-4-3 Hazard was reborn: 16 goals in the 2016-17 title season including a 60-yard solo goal against [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal). In 2017-18 he scored the FA Cup final penalty against [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united), and in 2018-19 under Maurizio Sarri he produced 16 goals and 15 assists, scored two in the Europa League final against Arsenal in Baku, and left for Madrid as a hero.

The 2018 World Cup was his international peak. He scored three, created more chances than anyone in the tournament and was outstanding in the 2-1 quarter-final win over [Brazil](/nations/brazil), one of the great individual displays of the decade.

## How he played

Hazard was the best close-control dribbler of his era in England, with 90-plus completed take-ons in several seasons. His technique was unusual: he kept the ball directly under his body, used both feet to shift it, and accepted contact, riding tackles with a balance that made him almost impossible to foul cleanly. He slowed defenders down by moving slowly himself, then burst for two strides. He was not an explosive sprinter over distance.

He was a creator first and a scorer second, happiest cutting inside from the left and picking the pass to an overlapping [Marcos Alonso](/players/marcos-alonso) or a run from [Diego Costa](/players/diego-costa). His finishing improved over time; he scored 16 league goals twice and converted penalties with a stuttering run-up that frustrated goalkeepers. Weaknesses: his defensive effort was minimal and both Mourinho and Conte publicly questioned his work-rate; he was inconsistent across seasons, with 2015-16 a notorious dip; and his fitness and weight management became an open issue in Madrid, where he arrived out of shape and suffered a fractured ankle that required two surgeries.

## Evolution

At Lille he played as a number 10 and left winger in a 4-3-3; Mourinho pinned him to the left and asked him to run at full-backs; Conte freed him from defending by playing wing-backs behind him; Sarri used him as a false nine for part of 2018-19, where he was surprisingly effective. At Madrid under [Zinedine Zidane](/managers/zinedine-zidane-manager) and [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) he never regained his burst and was used sparingly as a left winger and attacking midfielder; he nonetheless collected a La Liga and [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) medal in 2022. He retired from Belgium duty after the 2022 World Cup and from football in October 2023.

## Best plays and moments

- 2011, Ligue 1, Lille: the double-winning season, 20 goal involvements at 20.
- 2013, Europa League final v Benfica: Chelsea's 2-1 win in Amsterdam in his first season.
- 2015, Premier League, Chelsea 1-0 Manchester United: the goal that effectively sealed the title.
- 2015, PFA and FWA Player of the Year: the first Chelsea winner since Frank Lampard.
- 2017, Premier League, Chelsea 3-1 Arsenal: the 60-yard solo run past five defenders ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Eden+Hazard+solo+goal+Arsenal+2017)).
- 2017, FA Cup semi-final v Tottenham: a late goal in a 4-2 win at Wembley.
- 2018, FA Cup final v Manchester United: the winning penalty, won by his own run.
- 2018, World Cup quarter-final, Brazil 1-2 Belgium: ten completed dribbles, man of the match in Kazan ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Eden+Hazard+Belgium+Brazil+2018+World+Cup)).
- 2019, Premier League, Chelsea 2-0 West Ham: a solo goal from his own half that was widely voted goal of the season.
- 2019, Europa League final, Chelsea 4-1 Arsenal: two goals and an assist in his final Chelsea match ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Eden+Hazard+Europa+League+final+2019+Arsenal)).

## Legacy

Hazard is remembered in England as a joy, in Belgium as the captain of the best team the country has had, and in Madrid as one of the most expensive disappointments in football history. The three are the same player. He never won the Ballon d'Or or the Champions League as a protagonist, which keeps him out of the top tier of his generation, but Chelsea supporters would place him alongside [Didier Drogba](/players/didier-drogba), [Frank Lampard](/players/frank-lampard-player) and [John Terry](/players/john-terry) in the club's modern pantheon, and the 2018 World Cup performance against Brazil is a reference for how a winger can dominate a knockout game. His candour after retiring about his dislike of training and his love of life has only reinforced the affection.
