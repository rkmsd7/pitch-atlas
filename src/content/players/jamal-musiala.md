## Overview

Jamal Musiala is the player [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) built their decade around, a number 10 whose dribbling in tight spaces has drawn comparisons with Messi since he was a teenager. He won five [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) titles before 23 and scored the goal that decided the 2023 championship with two minutes to play. His last 18 months have been defined by the body: a fractured fibula and dislocated ankle at the 2025 Club World Cup, a long rehabilitation, and then, in pre-season 2026, two collapses attributed to a neurological problem that delayed the start of his 2026-27 campaign.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Stuttgart to a Nigerian-British father and a German mother, Musiala moved to England at seven and came through Southampton's and then Chelsea's academies. He played for England's youth teams and was considered one of Chelsea's brightest prospects when Bayern persuaded him to return to Germany in 2019, at 16. He debuted in June 2020 under Hansi Flick, scored on his first start that September, at 17 the club's youngest ever scorer, and chose Germany over England in early 2021.

By 2021-22 he was a regular, and in 2022-23, under Julian Nagelsmann and then Thomas Tuchel, he was the Bundesliga's Player of the Season, scoring 12 and creating 10, including the title-winner against Cologne on the final day as Dortmund stumbled.

## Peak years

Euro 2024 on home soil was his international breakthrough: three goals, joint top scorer, as [Germany](/nations/germany) reached the quarter-final. In 2024-25 under [Vincent Kompany](/managers/vincent-kompany) he was Bayern's most creative player until a hamstring injury in spring; Bayern won the title. He signed a contract to 2030 that February. Then, in July 2025, Gianluigi Donnarumma's challenge in the Club World Cup quarter-final against PSG fractured his fibula and dislocated his ankle.

He returned in early 2026 as Bayern won the 2025-26 double, with [Harry Kane](/players/harry-kane) scoring 73, and went to the 2026 World Cup, where Germany were eliminated in the round of 32 by Paraguay on penalties, a result that cost Julian Nagelsmann his job and brought [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) to the national team.

## How he plays

Musiala's genius is in the hips. He receives the ball with his body between it and the defender, drops a shoulder and is through a gap that did not seem to exist. His close control at speed is the best in Germany, and because he is tall and long-legged he can also hold off challenges. He is comfortable on either foot, finishes well in the box, and his through-balls after a dribble are as dangerous as his shots.

The weaknesses are physical and tactical. He is not a strong presser, his defensive positioning is loose, and his durability has been a growing concern: hamstring, ankle and now a health issue that Bayern have handled with care. He can also hold the ball too long when a simpler pass is on.

## Evolution

Flick used him as a wide midfielder and 8. Nagelsmann made him a 10 in a 4-2-3-1. Tuchel kept that role; Kompany has used him as the left-sided of two 10s and as the central one, with [Michael Olise](/players/michael-olise) on the other side. For Germany, Nagelsmann played him as an inside-left; Klopp's plans are still forming in late 2026.

## Current situation

Musiala has been eased back into Kompany's side in 2026-27, with a substitute appearance on matchday two and his first goal and assist in the weeks that followed; Bayern have been cautious with his minutes. His contract runs to 2030. For Germany, Klopp has made him central to the post-World Cup rebuild. His health status should be checked against current reports.
