## Overview

Bastian Schweinsteiger was the heartbeat of [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) and [Germany](/nations/germany) for a decade. He arrived as a bleach-haired winger from Bavaria, became a deep-lying midfielder under Louis van Gaal, and captained Germany to the 2014 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), where his bloodied, battered performance in the final against [Argentina](/nations/argentina) is one of the defining images of the tournament. He won eight [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) titles, the 2013 treble and 121 caps, then had a brief and unhappy spell at [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) before ending in MLS.

## Early life and breakthrough

Schweinsteiger grew up in Kolbermoor, south-east of Munich, a talented skier who chose football and joined Bayern's academy at 14. He made his first-team debut under Ottmar Hitzfeld in November 2002 in the Champions League against Lens, aged 18, and in 2004-05 broke through properly as a wide midfielder, alongside his friend Lukas Podolski in the national team. The two were the cheeky faces of the 2006 home World Cup, where Schweinsteiger scored twice in the third-place play-off against [Portugal](/nations/portugal).

## Peak years

For several seasons he was a good but inconsistent winger, criticised in the German press for lifestyle and for drifting out of games. The turning point was Van Gaal's arrival in 2009. He moved Schweinsteiger into central midfield next to Mark van Bommel, and almost immediately he was one of the best in Europe in the position. Bayern reached the 2010 Champions League final, losing to [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan), and he was outstanding at the 2010 World Cup, where Germany beat [England](/nations/england) 4-1 and Argentina 4-0 with him as the deep controller.

The 2012 Champions League final at the Allianz Arena was heartbreak: he hit the post with the decisive penalty in the shoot-out against [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea). A year later he was in tears of a different kind, winning the treble under Jupp Heynckes with victory over [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) at Wembley, and was named German Footballer of the Year. [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) kept him central in the next two titles.

In Brazil in 2014, Joachim Löw's Germany beat the hosts 7-1 and then Argentina 1-0 in the final, where Schweinsteiger was fouled repeatedly, cut under the eye by Sergio Agüero's elbow, and kept playing. He became Germany captain after [Philipp Lahm](/players/philipp-lahm) retired and led the team to the Euro 2016 semi-finals before retiring from internationals.

## How he played

The mature Schweinsteiger was a controlling midfielder with the mentality of a box-to-box player. He received from the centre-backs, usually with a touch that set up a long diagonal to [Arjen Robben](/players/arjen-robben) or [Franck Ribéry](/players/franck-ribery) on the opposite flank, and he could play the ball through midfield lines with either foot. When Bayern were established in the final third he arrived late at the edge of the box for a long shot or a header from a corner, which accounts for his 68 Bayern goals.

Defensively he was aggressive, a strong tackler who read the second ball well and covered for Lahm's forward runs. He was not quick and, in his twenties, could be caught on the ball by fast pressers; Guardiola's criticism that he sometimes held the ball too long was fair. Injuries to knees and ankles after 2013 stripped away his mobility, and by Manchester United in 2015 he was a slow, elegant passer who struggled with the Premier League's tempo.

## Evolution

Hitzfeld and Magath used him as a right or left winger. Van Gaal moved him to the number 6 role in a 4-2-3-1, where Lahm's inversion and Schweinsteiger's passing made Bayern's build-up. Heynckes paired him with Javi Martínez as a complete double pivot in the treble season; Guardiola rotated him with Lahm, Xabi Alonso and Thiago as his body gave out.

Van Gaal brought him to Manchester United in 2015. He made 35 appearances, won the FA Cup, and was then frozen out by Mourinho, who later apologised. He joined Chicago Fire in 2017, played as a sweeper and midfielder, was a three-time MLS All-Star and retired in October 2019.

## Legacy

In Germany he is simply "Basti", a national figure whose career tracked the country's rebuild from the embarrassment of Euro 2004 to the 2014 World Cup. His transformation from winger to midfielder is a standard example of how a player's best position is not always his first, and his 500 Bayern appearances put him among the club's all-time icons with [Oliver Kahn](/players/oliver-kahn), Lahm and Thomas Müller.

He has worked as a pundit for German television since retiring and is married to the tennis player Ana Ivanović. The bloodied image from the Maracanã in 2014 remains the picture most Germans would choose to summarise him.
