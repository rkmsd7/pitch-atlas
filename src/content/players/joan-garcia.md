## Overview

Joan García did something few players have done and survived it: he moved directly from Espanyol to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona). Signed in June 2025 for his €25m release clause, he arrived as the best shot-stopper in [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) and was immediately asked to do the hardest goalkeeping job in Europe, sweeping behind [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick)'s high line. A league title in his first season and a place in [Spain](/nations/spain)'s World Cup-winning squad followed.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Sallent, in the Catalan interior, García joined Espanyol's academy at 14 after spells at Gimnàstic de Manresa. He came through the B team, debuted in the first team in 2021 and became undisputed number one during Espanyol's promotion season of 2023-24, including the play-off final. In 2024-25, in a struggling side, he produced one of the great individual goalkeeping seasons in La Liga, making more saves than any other keeper and keeping Espanyol up almost single-handed.

He had already won Olympic gold with Spain in Paris in 2024, starting every game and saving in the 5-3 final win over France.

## Peak years

Barcelona triggered his release clause in June 2025 and he replaced Marc-André ter Stegen, who then left the club. His first season produced a second straight league title for the club and a string of saves that justified the fee, notably in the Champions League group stage. In 2026-27, he is the established number one, and his distribution has improved to the point that Flick starts build-up through him in most games.

He went to the 2026 World Cup as understudy to Unai Simón, the tournament's Golden Glove winner, and did not play, but he is widely considered the long-term successor.

## How he plays

García's starting position is what defines him. Flick's Barcelona defend 30 to 40 metres from goal, and García frequently stands on the edge of his penalty area or beyond, ready to run out and clear balls over the top. His reading of through-balls is excellent, and he is quick enough over ten metres to win most races.

As a shot-stopper he is reflexive and reactive, with good low saves and a strong record in one-on-ones, where he stays big and delays. With the ball he is dependable rather than spectacular: short passes to the centre-backs, a reliable medium-range ball into the full-backs. His long distribution and his command of crosses remain the areas critics point at, and his first Barcelona season had one or two errors from being caught too far off his line.

## Evolution

At Espanyol he was a traditional line goalkeeper asked to make save after save in a deep block. At Barcelona his role inverted entirely: fewer shots, far more interventions outside the box, and far more involvement in possession. The transition took a few months, but by spring 2026 he looked native to the system.

## Current situation

García is Flick's first-choice goalkeeper in 2026-27, with a contract to 2031, and his sweeping behind [Pau Cubarsí](/players/pau-cubarsi) is the foundation of Barcelona's defensive structure. For Spain, [Luis de la Fuente](/managers/luis-de-la-fuente) has him as the clear number two behind Simón, with the expectation that the roles will reverse before Euro 2028.
