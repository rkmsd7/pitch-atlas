## Overview

Marquinhos is [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain)'s most-capped player, the captain who lifted the club's first Champions League in Munich in May 2025 and its second in Budapest a year later. He arrived from Roma at 19 in 2013 as Thiago Silva's understudy, became his partner, then his successor, and has been the one constant through Ibrahimović, Neymar, Messi and Mbappé. For [Brazil](/nations/brazil), he has more than 90 caps, Olympic gold and a Copa América.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in São Paulo, Marquinhos came through at Corinthians and played seven games in the 2012 Copa Libertadores-winning season before Roma signed him at 18. One excellent season in Serie A under Zdeněk Zeman and Aurelio Andreazzoli was enough for PSG to pay €31.4m in July 2013, a record for a defender under 20.

## Peak years

He spent his early PSG years at right-back and as a reserve centre-back before becoming a starter under Unai Emery in 2017-18 and the leader of the defence under Thomas Tuchel. Tuchel played him as a defensive midfielder for much of 2019-20, the season PSG reached their first Champions League final, where Marquinhos scored in the semi-final against Leipzig and lost 1-0 to Bayern. He became captain in 2020.

The 2024-25 season under [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique) was his crowning: a near-faultless campaign in a very high line, the domestic double, and the 5-0 Champions League final win over Inter. In 2025-26 PSG won the league and retained the European Cup.

## How he plays

Marquinhos is a front-foot defender. He steps in to intercept rather than drop to block, which is why he suits a high line, and his reading of the game is among the best in Europe. He is quick enough to recover when beaten and, despite being 1.83 m, strong in the air through timing. In possession he is a reliable, progressive passer who breaks lines with the first pass.

His weaknesses have been occasional lapses in the biggest games (the 2020 final, the 2023 collapse against Bayern) and a tendency to be physically dominated by the largest strikers. He has also been the public face of PSG's failures as captain, which has made him a target of criticism that his performances rarely justified.

## Evolution

Right-back and reserve centre-back under Blanc, starting centre-back under Emery, defensive midfielder under Tuchel, then back to centre-back and captain under Pochettino, Galtier and Luis Enrique, who paired him first with Lucas Hernández and then with Willian Pacho in the highest line in Europe. For Brazil, he has been a starter under Tite, Dorival and [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti).

Marquinhos's leadership has evolved with the club. In the Neymar and Mbappé years he was a diplomat, managing egos; in Luis Enrique's younger, more collective squad he is the senior voice on the pitch and the man who organises the line. His communication with Pacho, Hakimi and Nuno Mendes is constant, and PSG's offside trap, one of the most aggressive in Europe, depends on his timing. He has also become a reliable scorer from corners again, usually from Vitinha's deliveries, and he remains the club's penalty-taker of last resort. The record books will show him as PSG's most-capped player for many years to come.

## Best plays and moments

- 2012, Copa Libertadores: a winner's medal at 18 with Corinthians.
- 2016, Olympic final, v Germany: gold in the Maracanã.
- 2019, Copa America final, v Peru: a continental title.
- 2020, Champions League semi-final, v RB Leipzig: a goal on the way to the final.
- 2025, Champions League final, PSG 5-0 Inter: lifting the club's first European Cup as captain ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Marquinhos+captain+Champions+League+final+2025)).
- 2026, Champions League final, v Arsenal: a second European Cup in Budapest.
- 2026, Ligue 1: a tenth league title.

## Current situation

Marquinhos is PSG's captain in 2026-27 and still a first-choice centre-back under Luis Enrique at 32, with a contract to 2028. He remains in the Brazil squad under Ancelotti after the 2026 World Cup.
