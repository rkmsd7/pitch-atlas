## Overview

Aitana Bonmatí is the best footballer in the world by the only measure that counts for three years running: the Ballon d'Or Féminin in 2023, 2024 and 2025, the first time anyone has won the women's award three times in a row. She is the heartbeat of [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona-women) and [Spain](/nations/spain-women), a midfielder who presses, dribbles, passes and scores, and whose 2023 [World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) Golden Ball confirmed her as the successor to [Alexia Putellas](/players/alexia-putellas) at the centre of the Spanish game.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Vilanova i la Geltrú in January 1998 to two Catalan-language teachers, who successfully campaigned to give her her mother's surname first, Bonmatí grew up playing with boys and joined La Masia at 14. She made her first-team debut in 2016 under Xavi Llorens, broke through as a regular in 2018-19 with 12 league goals, and started the 2019 [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) final defeat to Lyon. By 2021 she was indispensable: she was named player of the match in the 4-0 final win over Chelsea in Gothenburg that gave Barça their first European title.

## Peak years

Bonmatí's rise coincided with Putellas's long ACL absence from 2022, and she took the team over. In 2022-23 she won the UEFA Player of the Year award and scored in a Champions League final comeback against Wolfsburg; in the 2023 World Cup she scored three goals, assisted two and won the Golden Ball as Spain beat England 1-0 in Sydney. The first Ballon d'Or followed, then a second in 2024 after another Champions League and a Nations League, and a third in 2025 after a Euro final in which she scored the extra-time winner against Germany in the semi-final.

A broken leg suffered in training during the Nations League finals in November 2025 cost her most of the 2025-26 winter; she returned in spring, played in the 4-0 Champions League final win over Lyon in Oslo in May 2026 and scored for Spain against Iceland in June. In September 2026 she spoke publicly about the growing financial gap between [Liga F](/leagues/liga-f) and England.

## How she plays

Bonmatí is the archetype of the La Masia interior. She plays as the right-sided number eight in Barcelona's 4-3-3, receiving between the lines with her body open, and her first touch is almost always a forward one. Her signature move is the drive: a low, quick carry through two opponents followed by a slide pass or a shot across the keeper. She arrives in the box like a striker, often unmarked because the ball has gone wide first, and scores eight to twelve league goals a season from midfield. Out of possession she is the trigger of Barça's counter-press. Her weaknesses are few: aerial duels, inevitably, and a tendency to be carded for the tackles that come with her intensity.

## Evolution

She started as a winger in the youth teams and was converted to midfield at La Masia. Under Lluís Cortés she was a rotation interior; under Jonatan Giráldez she became the first name on the team sheet and added goals; under Pere Romeu she has become the senior leader, especially since Putellas's departure in summer 2026. Her game has moved from pure positional discipline to something more assertive, taking the ball on and shooting from range when the structure allows.

## Best plays and moments

- Player of the match, 2021 Champions League final, Barcelona 4-0 Chelsea, Gothenburg.
- Goal and assist in the 2023 Champions League final comeback from 2-0 down against Wolfsburg, Eindhoven.
- Golden Ball, 2023 World Cup: goals against Zambia, Switzerland and a relentless final against England.
- Extra-time winner against Germany in the Euro 2025 semi-final in Zurich, a near-post finish from a tight angle.
- Comeback from a broken leg to play in the 2026 Champions League final against Lyon.

## Legacy / Current situation

Still only 28, Bonmatí is Barcelona's leader and the face of a Spain side ranked first in the world. As of November 2026 she is fit, captaining the club in Putellas's absence and the obvious favourite to carry Spain to the [2027 World Cup](/tournaments/womens-world-cup) in Brazil, where a second world title would settle any argument about her place in the sport's history.
