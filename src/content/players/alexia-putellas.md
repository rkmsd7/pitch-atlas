## Overview

Alexia Putellas is the player who made Barcelona Femení a global brand and the first woman to win back-to-back Ballons d'Or, in 2021 and 2022. A third, on 26 October 2026, came at 32 after a season in which she captained [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona-women) to a clean sweep, was named Champions League Player of the Season and then, stunningly, left the club after 14 years to join London City Lionesses in the [WSL](/leagues/wsl). She is [Spain](/nations/spain-women)'s most-capped player with 150 appearances and a World Cup winner in 2023.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Mollet del Vallès near Barcelona, Putellas joined Barça's youth setup as a child, was released, and went to Espanyol, where she made her senior debut at 16 and won a Copa de la Reina. After a season at Levante she returned to Barcelona in 2012 and never left: a title in her first season and a steady rise to captain by 2020, as the club professionalised around her.

## Peak years

The 2020-21 season was the summit of the first act. Putellas scored a penalty in the 4-0 [Champions League](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) final win over Chelsea, won a domestic treble and took the 2021 Ballon d'Or, UEFA Player of the Year and The Best. She was even better in 2021-22, with 34 goals in all competitions, before an ACL tear on the eve of Euro 2022 cost her a year. She returned to win the 2023 World Cup from the bench, then recovered her level to win two more Champions Leagues in 2024 and 2026. In 2025-26 she played her 500th game for Barcelona in April, scored twice in the semi-final against Bayern and captained the 4-0 Oslo final win over Lyon.

On 26 May 2026 she announced she was leaving when her contract ended. On 8 July she signed a three-year deal with London City Lionesses, the ambitious WSL club owned by Michele Kang, was named captain in September and scored her first English goal in a 2-0 win over Brighton on 26 September.

## How she plays

Putellas is a left-footed playmaker who operates in the left half-space, either as an interior in a 4-3-3 or behind the striker. Her passing is her defining gift: switches of play, disguised through balls and first-time lay-offs that keep Barcelona's rhythm. The goals come from late runs into the box, from distance, and from a trademark left-footed half-volley. She is a superb free-kick taker and penalty taker, and her leadership, especially since Spain's 2022 player revolt, has been as influential as her football. Her limitations are pace, never her strength and reduced after the ACL injury, and the defensive running her older legs no longer provide.

## Evolution

She began as a left winger, was moved into midfield by Xavi Llorens around 2015, and became a complete interior under Lluís Cortés. After the ACL injury she played deeper and more as a conductor, relying on timing rather than acceleration. At London City she plays as a number 10 in a more direct team, with the freedom to roam and shoot.

## Best plays and moments

- Penalty and all-round performance, 2021 Champions League final, Barcelona 4-0 Chelsea.
- 34-goal 2021-22 season, including a hat-trick against Real Madrid.
- Two goals in the 2026 Champions League semi-final, Barcelona 4-2 Bayern.
- Captaining Barcelona to a 4-0 win over Lyon in the 2026 Oslo final.
- Back-to-back Ballons d'Or 2021 and 2022, then a third in 2026 at the London Palladium.

## Legacy / Current situation

Putellas is the defining Spanish footballer of her generation and the player who proved a women's club could fill Camp Nou. As of November 2026 she is captaining London City Lionesses, still a regular for Spain and aiming at a second World Cup in Brazil in 2027 at the age of 33.
