## Overview

Vincent Kompany was the first great player of [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city)'s modern era and the man who captained the first four of their [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) titles. He arrived in August 2008, days before the Abu Dhabi takeover, for 6 million pounds, and left in 2019 after 360 games, eleven trophies and two of the most important goals in the club's history. He was elegant, intelligent and injury-prone, and he is now a manager at the very top of the game.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Uccle, Brussels, to a Congolese father and Belgian mother, Kompany came through the [Anderlecht](/clubs/anderlecht) academy and debuted at 17 in 2003. He won two Belgian titles and was named the league's best player in 2004 and 2005, then moved to Hamburg in 2006. Injury and a difficult relationship with the club limited him; he played as a defensive midfielder for much of his time there.

Mark Hughes signed him for City in 2008 as a midfielder. Within months he had moved back to centre-back, where his composure and reading of the game immediately stood out in a squad in transition.

## Peak years

Under [Roberto Mancini](/managers/roberto-mancini) Kompany became captain in 2011 and the symbol of a club finding its feet. In April 2012 his header against [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) won the derby 1-0 and put City top on goal difference; weeks later Sergio Agüero's 93:20 goal sealed the title. Kompany was named Premier League Player of the Season.

He won a second title in 2014 under Manuel Pellegrini, then two more under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola), including the 100-point season of 2017-18. In May 2019, with City needing to beat Leicester to stay ahead of Liverpool, he struck a 25-yard shot into the top corner in the 70th minute. Teammates had been shouting at him not to shoot. It was his last Premier League goal, and City won the title the following weekend.

For [Belgium](/nations/belgium) he captained the golden generation of [Eden Hazard](/players/eden-hazard) and Kevin De Bruyne to third place at the 2018 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup).

## How he played

Kompany combined the physical gifts of a classic centre-back with the technique of a midfielder, which is what he had been. He was dominant in the air, strong in the tackle and quick enough to recover. On the ball he was comfortable under pressure, able to carry into midfield or hit an accurate long diagonal, and he was an early example of the kind of defender Guardiola's [positional play](/tactics/positional-play) demands.

His leadership was intellectual as well as vocal. He spoke several languages, studied business at Manchester Business School while playing, and organised the back line with a clarity that managers relied on. He was a serious set-piece threat and scored several derby goals.

The weakness was availability. From 2015 onwards he suffered a long series of calf and muscle injuries, missing large parts of four seasons. When fit he was still elite, which made the absences more frustrating. He also carried a slight tendency to overcommit, stepping out and leaving space, which the best teams exploited on the counter.

## Evolution

Kompany began as a midfielder and became a centre-back, then adapted from Mancini's pragmatic 4-4-2 and [back-three](/tactics/back-three) experiments to Pellegrini's open attacking play and finally Guardiola's high line. Under Guardiola he played fewer games but set the tone when available, and in his last two seasons he was the elder statesman of a squad full of younger stars. He left in 2019 to become player-manager of Anderlecht, retiring from playing in 2020.

## Best plays and moments

- 2019, Premier League v Leicester City: the 25-yard winner that kept City ahead in the title race ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Kompany+goal+Leicester+2019)).
- 2012, Premier League v Manchester United: the derby-winning header that put City top.
- 2012, Premier League v QPR: captaining the side on the day of Agüero's title winner.
- 2014, Premier League v West Ham: a goal on the day City won the title.
- 2018, Premier League v Manchester United: a header in a 3-2 defeat, then captaining the 100-point season.
- 2011, FA Cup final v Stoke City: City's first trophy in 35 years.
- 2018, World Cup quarter-final v Brazil: his return from injury anchored a 2-1 win.
- 2018, League Cup final v Arsenal: a goal in a 3-0 win.
- 2019, Premier League: the domestic treble of league, FA Cup and League Cup.
- 2016, Champions League semi-final v Real Madrid: City's first semi, despite the defeat.

## Legacy

Kompany is on the short list of players who changed what a club was. Before him City were a punchline; after him they were champions. He has carried that stature into management: promotion with Burnley in 2023, then [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), where he won the [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) in 2025 and again in 2025-26. His coaching career is covered on [his manager page](/managers/vincent-kompany). A statue of him stands outside the Etihad, next to those of Agüero and David Silva.
