## Overview

John Terry captained [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) for 13 years and played 717 games for the club, a record for an outfield player. He was the backbone of every title-winning side from [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s first in 2005 to Antonio Conte's in 2017, and the defensive heart of the squad that finally won the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) in 2012. He was also, for his sins and his style, among the most divisive footballers of his era: suspended for the 2012 final, stripped twice of the [England](/nations/england) captaincy, and revered at Stamford Bridge regardless.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Barking, Terry joined Chelsea's academy at 14 after being courted by West Ham and [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united). He debuted in 1998 under Gianluca Vialli, spent a short loan at Nottingham Forest in 2000 and became a regular under Claudio Ranieri in 2001. By 2003 he was partnered with William Gallas and Marcel Desailly and had earned his first England cap.

Mourinho arrived in 2004 and made him captain. The 2004-05 season brought the league title, 15 goals conceded, and the PFA Players' Player of the Year award, the first defender to win it since Paul McGrath.

## Peak years

Between 2004 and 2010 Terry was the best centre-back in England and among the best in Europe. He and [Ricardo Carvalho](/players/ricardo-carvalho) formed an almost impassable pairing in front of [Petr Čech](/players/petr-cech), with [Claude Makélélé](/players/claude-makelele) screening ahead. Chelsea won back-to-back titles, and Terry was named UEFA's defender of the year three times.

The 2008 final in Moscow brought his lowest moment: slipping as he took the penalty that would have won the Champions League against Manchester United. Four years later he was suspended for the final against [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich) after a red card in the semi against [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), but he had played a central role in the run and collected the trophy in full kit, an image that both amused and annoyed the football world.

## How he played

Terry's defining quality was anticipation. He stepped in front of forwards to intercept rather than waiting to tackle, and he was prepared to throw his body into any block, including the famous one that saw him kicked unconscious by Abou Diaby in the 2007 League Cup final. He was superb in the air in both boxes, scoring 67 goals for Chelsea, most of them from set pieces.

He was a better footballer than his reputation suggests: comfortable with both feet, able to switch play with a long diagonal, and a calm organiser who moved his line with hand signals and a constant stream of instruction. Under Conte he played the left of a [back three](/tactics/back-three) at 36 and looked natural.

His weakness was pace, especially recovery pace when turned, and it became a serious issue after 2010 when quick strikers started to run in behind him. He compensated by dropping deeper and relying on his reading of the game. His discipline and temperament also drew criticism; he was sent off twice in Champions League ties.

## Evolution

Under Ranieri he was a raw, aggressive stopper. Mourinho taught him to lead a [low block](/tactics/low-block) and made him the on-pitch manager. Under Carlo Ancelotti he was part of a side that scored 103 goals and won the Double in 2010. Age forced him to play further off the ball under Roberto Di Matteo and Mourinho again in 2015, when he started every league game of a title season at 34. Conte reduced him to a squad player in 2016-17, and he left after a guard-of-honour farewell in May 2017, spending a final season at [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa) in the Championship before retiring in 2018.

## Best plays and moments

- 2005, Premier League v Manchester United: Terry's leadership in the win that all but sealed the first title ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Chelsea+2004-05+title+John+Terry)).
- 2007, League Cup final v Arsenal: the block that knocked him out, then returning to the ground to collect the trophy.
- 2012, Champions League semi-final v Barcelona: the red card, and watching the second half from the dressing room as ten men held on.
- 2008, Champions League final v Manchester United: the slip in the shootout, the defining heartbreak.
- 2009, FA Cup final v Everton: the captaincy of a 2-1 win in a Double-chasing spring.
- 2010, Premier League v Manchester United: a header in a 2-1 win at Old Trafford that swung the title race.
- 2011, England v Montenegro: his last international goal.
- 2015, Premier League v Everton: a stoppage-time equaliser in a 3-3 draw at 34.
- 2017, Premier League v Sunderland: substituted in the 26th minute of his last game, to match his shirt number.
- 2012, Europa League final v Benfica: a European trophy lifted at full strength in Amsterdam.

## Legacy

Terry's tally of five league titles, five FA Cups, three League Cups and both European trophies makes him the most decorated captain in Chelsea's history. He was a totem for a certain kind of English defending, brave and vocal, that managers still ask for in pressing teams. He later coached under Dean Smith at Aston Villa and has since worked in academy football and as a pundit. The debates about his character have not faded; neither has the respect for his defending.
