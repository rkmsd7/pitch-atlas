## Overview

Didier Drogba did not play top-flight football until he was 24, and did not win a league title until 27. By the time he left [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) for the first time in 2012, after scoring the equalising header and the winning penalty in the Champions League final, he had become the defining striker of the club's modern history and one of the great big-game players anywhere. He scored in nine finals for Chelsea and won all nine.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Abidjan in March 1978, Drogba was sent to France at five to live with his uncle, the professional footballer Michel Goba, and spent his childhood shuttling between the two countries. He played amateur football in the Paris suburbs, studied accountancy, and did not turn professional with Le Mans until he was 21. Injuries limited him in Ligue 2 and he moved to Guingamp in 2002, where 17 Ligue 1 goals in his first full top-flight season made France take notice.

[Marseille](/clubs/marseille) signed him in 2003 and he scored 32 goals in a single season, including 11 in Europe as they reached the UEFA Cup final. He was voted Ligue 1 Player of the Year, and [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho), newly appointed at Chelsea, made him his first striker signing for £24 million in July 2004.

## Peak years

Drogba's first two seasons under Mourinho brought back-to-back titles, including the winning goals in the 2005 League Cup final against [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool), but his finishing was erratic and English crowds accused him of diving. The transformation came in 2006-07: 33 goals in all competitions, the Premier League Golden Boot with 20, and both winners in the League Cup final and FA Cup final, the first at the new Wembley.

Under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) in 2009-10 he scored 29 league goals for a second Golden Boot as Chelsea won the Double. His final game of his first spell, in Munich on 19 May 2012, was the perfect ending: an 88th-minute near-post header from a corner to equalise against [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), and the decisive penalty in the shoot-out after [Petr Čech](/players/petr-cech) had saved from Ivica Olić and Bastian Schweinsteiger had hit the post.

## How he played

Drogba's hold-up play was the foundation. He would come short, take the ball on his chest or instep with a centre-back on his back, protect it with his frame and lay it off to [Frank Lampard](/players/frank-lampard-player) arriving from deep. His first touch under contact was better than his reputation suggested, and he had enough pace to run in behind once the centre-back had been dragged out.

In the air he was dominant from open play and set pieces, attacking the near post with a run that was almost impossible to track. He struck the ball with enormous power, especially from 25 yards on the run, and became a dangerous free-kick taker with a knuckling technique. His mentality was his signature: he grew with the occasion, and his record in finals is unmatched for a Premier League striker.

Weaknesses: inconsistency in ordinary league games, a tendency to go to ground that irritated opponents and officials, and emotional flashpoints, most famously the "it's a disgrace" outburst at the referee after the 2009 Champions League semi-final against [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona).

## Evolution

Mourinho used him as a lone striker in a 4-3-3 whose job was to fix two centre-backs and bring wide players and Lampard into the game. Under Ancelotti he partnered Nicolas Anelka in a diamond and his output peaked as a penalty-box finisher. He became more of a leader than a runner in his thirties, a presence used for occasions rather than every week, which is why Roberto Di Matteo's cup-run team of 2012 suited him. He returned to Chelsea for 2014-15 under Mourinho as a squad elder, winning a fourth title, and finished in MLS and the USL. For [Ivory Coast](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Didier+Drogba+Ivory+Coast+goals) he was captain and record scorer with 65 goals, and his 2005 plea for peace after World Cup qualification is part of the country's modern history.

## Legacy

Drogba is Chelsea's fourth-highest scorer and, by consensus of the supporters who voted him their greatest ever player in 2012, the most important. He was the prototype for a generation of powerful African strikers in Europe, alongside [Samuel Eto'o](/players/samuel-etoo), and won two African Footballer of the Year awards. Off the pitch his foundation builds clinics and schools in Ivory Coast, and in 2022 he ran for the presidency of the Ivorian federation. He remains a UEFA and Premier League ambassador and a familiar presence at Champions League draws, where his name still provokes a shudder in Munich.
