## Overview

Ollie Watkins is the striker who took the long road. He was playing non-league football on loan at Weston-super-Mare at 19, scored 49 goals for [Brentford](/clubs/brentford) in the Championship, and then became the centre-forward around whom [Unai Emery](/managers/unai-emery) built [Aston Villa](/clubs/aston-villa)'s return to the Champions League. His stoppage-time winner against the Netherlands in the [Euro 2024](/tournaments/euro) semi-final is one of [England](/nations/england)'s great tournament moments. After six seasons, 100 goals and a difficult summer of 2026, he joined [Al-Hilal](/clubs/al-hilal) in the [Saudi Pro League](/leagues/saudi-pro-league) on 30 August 2026 for around £50m.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Torquay, Watkins came through Exeter City's academy, spent a season on loan in the National League South and scored 13 league goals for Exeter in 2016-17 as a wide forward, winning the League Two apprentice award. Brentford paid £1.8m in 2017 and used him on the wing for two seasons before Thomas Frank moved him to centre-forward in 2019-20, when he scored 26 Championship goals, won the Golden Boot and reached the play-off final. Villa paid a club-record £28m in September 2020.

## Peak years

He scored a hat-trick against champions Liverpool in a 7-2 win within weeks of arriving, and was Villa's leading scorer every season thereafter. Emery's arrival in November 2022 transformed him: 15 league goals in the second half of that season, 19 goals and 13 assists in 2023-24 as Villa finished fourth, and a run to the Champions League quarter-finals in 2024-25, where he scored in the second leg against PSG. He passed 100 Villa goals in 2025-26, the club's best return since the 1980s. For England he scored the 90th-minute winner against the Netherlands in Dortmund in July 2024 and was part of the 2026 World Cup squad that finished third.

## How he plays

Watkins's game is movement. He plays on the shoulder of the last defender and runs the channels constantly, which stretches the pitch for the midfielders behind him and tires centre-backs. His best finishes are first-time efforts at the near post from low crosses, and the sweeping run onto a through ball followed by a swift strike across the keeper, as in Dortmund. He finishes with both feet and is a willing header of the ball.

He presses hard and is a team striker rather than a selfish one, which is why his assist numbers have been high. His link play with his back to goal is adequate rather than refined, his conversion can fluctuate wildly from month to month, and he has rarely been a dribbler. Against a deep block that denies him runs, he can be quiet.

## Evolution

He was a winger at Exeter and Brentford before Frank made him a striker. Dean Smith used him as a lone centre-forward in a 4-2-3-1 behind which [Jack Grealish](/players/jack-grealish) created; Emery's Villa, with its high line and quick combinations, turned him into a volume scorer who also created for [Morgan Rogers](/players/morgan-rogers) and the wingers. At Al-Hilal, in a squad that also signed Gabriel Martinelli and Crysencio Summerville, he leads the line in a possession-heavy side built for the Asian Champions League.

## Current situation

The summer of 2026 was messy. Fenerbahçe agreed personal terms with Watkins in July but could not meet Villa's valuation, and he made himself unavailable for Villa's opening Premier League game, for which Emery said he had apologised. Al-Hilal completed a three-year deal on 30 August, and Villa replaced him with Nicolas Jackson for £65m. He scored off the bench on his debut, added two goals in an Asian Champions League win over Shabab Al-Ahli and scored in a 3-2 win over Al-Ittihad in October that took Al-Hilal top of the table. His shirt number is listed as 11 by stats sites but not confirmed by the club on this page. His England career under [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel) is likely to be affected by the move.
