## Overview

Wesley Sneijder packed an enormous amount of influence into a small frame. At 1.70 m he was rarely the most athletic player on the pitch, but for one extraordinary year in 2010 he was arguably the best. He was the brain of [Inter Milan](/clubs/inter-milan)'s treble under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho), then the creative engine of a [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) side that reached the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) final. He finished fourth in that year's Ballon d'Or, a result many still regard as a robbery.

He played 134 times for his country, a national record, and won league titles in the Netherlands, Spain, Italy and Turkey.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Utrecht in 1984, Sneijder joined the [Ajax](/clubs/ajax) academy at seven and came through alongside [Rafael van der Vaart](/players/rafael-van-der-vaart) and John Heitinga. Ronald Koeman gave him his debut in February 2003 and he scored in it. By 2004 he was a title winner and a regular for the national team, having debuted under Dick Advocaat at 18.

His Ajax years established a reputation for a thunderous right foot and a tendency to play with his head up. He scored 57 goals in 180 games, a remarkable return for a midfielder, and was named Dutch Footballer of the Year in 2007.

## Peak years

[Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) paid 27 million euros for him in 2007 and he was excellent in his first season, scoring nine as Bernd Schuster's side won [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga). The arrival of [Kaká](/players/kaka) and [Cristiano Ronaldo](/players/cristiano-ronaldo) in 2009 pushed him out, and he joined Inter for a bargain 15 million euros days before the window closed.

What followed was the season of his life. Mourinho built a 4-2-3-1 around him, with [Samuel Eto'o](/players/samuel-etoo) and Goran Pandev working the flanks and Diego Milito as the target. Sneijder tied it all together. Inter won [Serie A](/leagues/serie-a), the Coppa Italia and the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league), beating [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in a semi-final that became a template for [counter-attacking](/tactics/counter-attacking) football against [tiki-taka](/tactics/tiki-taka).

That summer he scored five goals at the World Cup in South Africa, including the winner against [Brazil](/nations/brazil) in the quarter-final, as the Dutch lost the final to [Spain](/nations/spain) in extra time.

## How he played

Sneijder was a classic trequartista who nonetheless thrived in a Mourinho team built on discipline. He received between the lines, usually on the half-turn, so that one touch set him facing goal. His decision-making was fast and simple: the through ball if it was on, the switch to a winger if not, the shot if the defence sat off. He was genuinely two-footed, which made him hard to show onto a weaker side.

His free kicks were struck with a dipping, knuckling technique, and from 25 yards he was a constant threat. He was also an underrated worker; in the Inter system he dropped onto the opposing holding midfielder without the ball.

Weaknesses were physical. He lacked a dribble to beat a man in a tight space, and over a long season his body often broke down, which is why his peak was so short. He was also temperamental, and quick to fall out with coaches who moved him from his preferred central slot.

## Evolution

At Ajax he was a goalscoring midfielder in a 4-3-3. At Madrid he was asked to play wider and deeper, which never suited him. Mourinho's gift was to put him exactly where his qualities were maximised. When Rafael Benítez and then Gian Piero Gasperini followed, Inter declined and Sneijder's form went with them. A move to [Galatasaray](/clubs/galatasaray) in 2013 revived him; he scored the goal that won the 2015 title on the final day and became a cult figure in Istanbul. Brief spells at Nice and in Qatar closed his career in 2019.

## Best plays and moments

- 2010, Champions League semi-final v Barcelona: the assist for Milito and a masterclass in game management over two legs ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Sneijder+Inter+Barcelona+2010)).
- 2010, Champions League final v [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich): the pass that released Milito for the opener in Madrid.
- 2010, World Cup quarter-final v Brazil: two goals, the second a header that sent the Dutch through.
- 2010, World Cup semi-final v Uruguay: a deflected strike that put the Netherlands 2-1 up.
- 2010, Serie A v Roma: the free kick in a title-deciding spell.
- 2008, Euro 2008 v Italy: a volley in a 3-0 group win that announced the Dutch as contenders.
- 2008, Euro 2008 v France: a curling finish in a 4-1 win.
- 2015, Super Lig v Beşiktaş: the last-minute derby winner that sealed Galatasaray's title.
- 2007, La Liga v Atlético Madrid: a brace in his first Madrid derby.
- 2004, Eredivisie: 2003-04 title with Ajax at 19.

## Legacy

Sneijder is the great "what if" of modern Dutch football: the 2010 Ballon d'Or he did not win, the longer peak he might have had with a different body. But the achievements are concrete. He is one of very few players to be the central creative force of a treble-winning side and a World Cup finalist in the same year, and he did it as a traditional number 10 in an era that was supposedly killing the role. He retired in 2019 and has since worked as a pundit in the Netherlands.
