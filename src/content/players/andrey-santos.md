## Overview

Andrey Santos is the midfielder [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) bought at 18, developed at arm's length for three years and then sold to [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) for £48m without ever really using. Two outstanding loan seasons at Strasbourg, Chelsea's sister club in France, turned him from a prospect into a complete box-to-box player, and United, rebuilding their midfield under [Michael Carrick](/managers/michael-carrick), made him one of their first signings of the summer of 2026. He is a [Brazil](/nations/brazil) international and was captain of the side that won the 2023 South American Under-20 Championship.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Rio de Janeiro, Santos came through Vasco da Gama's academy and was promoted to the first team in 2022 at 17. He scored eight league goals from midfield as Vasco won promotion from Série B, numbers that no teenager in Brazil's second tier had produced, and Chelsea paid around £11m in January 2023. Work-permit problems meant he went straight back to Vasco on loan, and a few weeks later he captained Brazil to the Under-20 continental title, finishing as the tournament's top scorer with six goals from midfield.

## Peak years

Chelsea's squad was too crowded for him. A half-season loan at Nottingham Forest in early 2024 produced two appearances; the move to Strasbourg that summer produced a career. Under Liam Rosenior he was the best midfielder in Ligue 1 outside Paris in 2024-25, scoring ten goals, and Strasbourg qualified for Europe. He returned to Chelsea for the 2025 Club World Cup, was part of the squad that won it, made his senior Brazil debut under [Carlo Ancelotti](/managers/carlo-ancelotti) and then went back to Strasbourg for the first half of 2025-26 before Chelsea recalled him. United agreed a £48m deal in the early summer of 2026 and he made his debut in a pre-season friendly against Wrexham in Helsinki in July.

## How he plays

Santos is a connector. He receives from the centre-backs, takes the ball on the half-turn and either carries it ten yards or plays a short pass and moves again; his best football is in tight combinations around the halfway line, where his balance and strength let him ride challenges. Without the ball he reads interceptions well and tackles with good timing, though he is not a ferocious ball-winner in the [Moisés Caicedo](/players/moises-caicedo) mould.

His distinctive quality is his goal threat. He times late runs from deep, finishes well with his right foot and is a strong header of the ball from set pieces, which is why his scoring numbers at Vasco and Strasbourg were so high. His passing range is the main area for growth: he plays safely rather than incisively over distance, and in a struggling side he can look hesitant about whether to sit or to go.

## Evolution

He started as an attacking No.8 at Vasco. Strasbourg's Rosenior used him as the left-sided No.8 in a 4-3-3 and later as a No.6 in a double pivot, where his pressing and ball retention stood out. Carrick signed him to play the No.6 role in a 4-2-3-1, and in pre-season he looked undroppable there; in the league, where United's start was poor, he has been rotated with [Kobbie Mainoo](/players/kobbie-mainoo) and Youri Tielemans, with his two starts in the autumn coming in defeats to Hull City and Brighton.

## Current situation

Santos's first autumn at Old Trafford has been complicated by the team around him. United's worst start in 40 years, six points from six games and a League Cup exit to Brighton, put Carrick under pressure and led to [Bruno Fernandes](/players/bruno-fernandes) being dropped and then restored as captain in late October. Santos had five league appearances and around 260 minutes by the end of October, last featuring in a 3-0 home defeat to Bournemouth on 25 October. He wears No.17. For Brazil he is part of Ancelotti's wider squad after the 2026 World Cup.
