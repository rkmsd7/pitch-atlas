## Overview

Alexandra Popp is the most important German player since [Birgit Prinz](/players/birgit-prinz). Captain of [Germany](/nations/germany-women) from 2019 until her international retirement in October 2024, she won Olympic gold in Rio, scored in every game of Euro 2022 until injury ruled her out of the final, and spent 14 years at [Wolfsburg](/clubs/wolfsburg-women), winning two [Champions Leagues](/tournaments/womens-champions-league) and seven Bundesligas. In March 2026 she agreed a three-year deal to join Borussia Dortmund's ambitious women's project from 2026-27, where she is captain.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Witten in the Ruhr, Popp trained as a zookeeper while coming through Recklinghausen and Duisburg, where she won the 2009 UEFA Women's Cup at 18. She was the star of Germany's 2010 Under-20 World Cup win on home soil, scoring in every match and taking both the Golden Ball and Golden Shoe.

## Peak years

Wolfsburg signed her in 2012 and she scored in the 2013 Champions League final win over Lyon in London, then won the 2014 final too. Knee injuries interrupted her twenties, but she was a starting striker at Rio 2016, when a 2-1 final win over Sweden brought gold. Euro 2022 was her defining tournament: six goals, including two in the semi-final against France, before a warm-up injury kept her out of the Wembley final against England. She captained Germany to bronze in Paris 2024 and retired from international football with 145 caps and 67 goals. Her final Wolfsburg season in 2025-26 ended with a second-place finish in the [Frauen-Bundesliga](/leagues/frauen-bundesliga); Dortmund, who have invested heavily in their women's team, made her their marquee signing.

## How she plays

Popp is a target striker in the German tradition: strong, aerially dominant, and ruthless from close range. She holds the ball under pressure, brings midfielders into play and attacks crosses at the near post. Her leadership is her second gift; she has captained every team she has played for since her mid-twenties. Under Martina Voss-Tecklenburg she was even deployed as a defensive midfielder, where her reading of the game and tackling were good enough. She is not quick and never was; she wins through positioning and will.

## Evolution

A box-to-box forward at Duisburg, a Champions League-winning striker at Wolfsburg, a midfielder for Germany in 2019, and a striker-captain again from 2022. At Dortmund she is the veteran around whom a young squad is being built.

## Best plays and moments

- Golden Ball and Golden Shoe at the 2010 Under-20 World Cup.
- Goal in the 2013 Champions League final, Wolfsburg 1-0 Lyon.
- Olympic gold, Rio 2016, as a starting striker.
- Two goals against France in the Euro 2022 semi-final, Milton Keynes.
- Captaining Germany to bronze at Paris 2024 and a farewell Wolfsburg season in 2025-26.
