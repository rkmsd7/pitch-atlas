## Overview

Gavi is the fire in a [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) midfield that is otherwise all silk. He debuted at 17, became [Spain](/nations/spain)'s youngest ever international a month later, won the Golden Boy and the Kopa Trophy at 18 and, after an ACL injury that cost him most of 2024, came back to win [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) twice and the [2026 World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Los Palacios y Villafranca near Seville, Gavi was in Real Betis's academy before Barcelona took him to La Masia at 11. He never played for Barcelona B in a regular capacity; Ronald Koeman promoted him straight from the under-19s in August 2021 and started him against Getafe at 17 years and 24 days. Luis Enrique called him into the Spain squad in October 2021 and he started the Nations League semi-final against Italy, the youngest Spanish international ever at 17 years and 62 days.

His 2021-22 season, 47 games for Barcelona, made him a regular at a club in crisis, and his energy in the 2022 World Cup, where he scored against Costa Rica, confirmed him as a player for the biggest stage.

## Peak years

Under Xavi, Gavi was the midfielder who gave Barcelona bite. The 2022-23 title was built on the league's best defence, and Gavi's pressing in front of it was central. He scored in the 2023 Supercopa final against Real Madrid and in the 2023 Nations League final. Then, in November 2023, he tore his ACL playing for Spain against Georgia and missed a year, including Euro 2024.

He returned in late 2024 to a Barcelona reshaped by [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick), where he had to fight for minutes with [Pedri](/players/pedri), Frenkie de Jong and Dani Olmo. He won the fight through the second half of 2024-25, won a second title in 2025-26 and went to the 2026 World Cup as a squad-first option who grew into Spain's knockout-stage line-up.

## How he plays

Gavi's defining trait is intensity. He covers more ground than any other Barcelona midfielder, presses from the front with the strikers and is unusually good at winning the ball back within seconds of losing it. Technically he is better than his reputation: he receives under pressure cleanly, plays quick first-time passes and has an eye for a late run into the box.

The weaknesses are self-inflicted. He picks up too many cards, sometimes loses his head in duels and, in possession, occasionally rushes passes where Pedri would wait. He also carries the memory of a serious knee injury, and his explosiveness is not quite what it was at 18.

## Evolution

Koeman and Xavi used him as a left-sided interior, almost a winger-midfielder, who would drift out to combine with the full-back. Flick has made him a more orthodox 8, often as the more advanced of the pair in a 4-2-3-1, trusted to press the opposition's pivot. For Spain, [Luis de la Fuente](/managers/luis-de-la-fuente) has used him as an impact option and a starter in games where energy matters more than control.

## Best plays and moments

- 2021, La Liga, v Getafe: debut at 17, four days after his first training session with the seniors.
- 2021, Nations League semi-final, v Italy: Spain's youngest ever international, in a 2-1 win in Milan.
- 2022, World Cup, v Costa Rica: a volley to become Spain's youngest World Cup scorer.
- 2023, Supercopa final, v Real Madrid: a goal and two assists in a 3-1 win.
- 2023, Nations League final, v Croatia: started and won on penalties.
- 2024, La Liga: return from a year out, to a standing ovation at Montjuïc.
- 2026, World Cup: part of Spain's second world title ([highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Gavi+Spain+World+Cup+2026)).

## Current situation

Gavi is a regular in Flick's rotation in 2026-27, often starting alongside Pedri when Barcelona need energy against pressing sides, and his contract runs to 2030. He has also become a leader in the dressing room behind captain [Raphinha](/players/raphinha). For Spain, under a re-signed de la Fuente, he is part of the young core expected to carry the world champions towards Euro 2028.
