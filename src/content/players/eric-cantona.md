## Overview

Eric Cantona played 185 games for [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) in four and a half seasons and the club won the league in four of them. Before he arrived, in November 1992, United had not been champions for 26 years; by the time he retired, abruptly, in May 1997 at the age of 30, they had won four Premier Leagues, two doubles, and had Ryan Giggs, Paul Scholes, David Beckham and Gary Neville in the side, each of whom cited him as the teacher. He had been a problem at every French club he played for, had been banned in France for throwing a ball at a referee and calling each member of a disciplinary panel an idiot, and had been sentenced to community service in England for the kung-fu kick at Selhurst Park. [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson) said he was the catalyst for everything that followed.

## Early life and breakthrough

Cantona was born in Marseille in May 1966 to a family of Sardinian and Spanish immigrants. He joined Auxerre at 15 under Guy Roux, the great developer of French talent, and made his debut in 1983. Roux indulged him, but the fights were already happening: a punch to a teammate's face, a suspension for a tackle on Nantes' Michel Der Zakarian. In 1987 he was a France international; in 1988 his hometown club [Marseille](/clubs/marseille) paid a French record for him.

He was there three seasons and spent most of them on loan, after kicking the ball into the crowd and tearing off his shirt in a friendly. At Montpellier he won the Coupe de France and threw his boots at a teammate. In 1991, after the ball-throwing incident at Nîmes and the "idiot" hearing, he announced his retirement. Michel Platini, then France coach, persuaded him to try England.

## Peak years

Howard Wilkinson took him to [Leeds United](/clubs/leeds-united) in February 1992, where he played a cameo role in the title win that spring. In November 1992 Wilkinson, tired of him, sold him to Ferguson for £1.2 million, the best piece of business United have ever done.

United, third in the table and struggling to score, won the first Premier League by ten points. In 1993-94 they won the double, with Cantona the PFA Player of the Year. Then January 1995: sent off at Crystal Palace, he launched himself feet first at a supporter abusing him from the touchline and was banned for eight months. He came back in October 1995, scored in his first match against [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool), and in the spring of 1996 scored winner after winner in 1-0 victories as United overturned Newcastle's 12-point lead. He scored the goal that won the FA Cup final and a second double. Captain in 1996-97, he won a fourth title and then, after an uninspired Champions League semi-final exit to Dortmund, walked away.

## How he played

Cantona was not a striker in the English sense. He played behind Mark Hughes or Andy Cole as the link between midfield and attack, a position English football barely recognised in 1992. He received with his back to goal, took the ball with a touch that killed it instantly, and turned slowly, almost theatrically, because his control was so complete that nobody dared commit. From there he had every option: a through-ball with the outside of the foot, a one-two, a shot from the edge of the box, or the chip, his signature, lifted over goalkeepers who had assumed he would shoot.

His leadership was a matter of standards. He stayed behind after training to practise, which the young United players copied. His weaknesses were a lack of pace, limited defensive work and a temper that, as Selhurst Park showed, could remove him from a title race.

In today's game he would be a number 10 or a false nine, a Dimitar Berbatov with a nastier streak or a less mobile Harry Kane, and the question of whether a pressing coach would accommodate his lack of running is a real one. Ferguson's answer was to build the team around him.

## Evolution

In France Cantona was a conventional, if unconventionally tempered, centre forward. Wilkinson and then Ferguson moved him deeper, and the move suited both his technique and his temperament: he was involved in everything and the responsibility settled him. After the ban he returned a more disciplined player, less inclined to react, more inclined to decide matches with single moments, and the 1995-96 run of winners is his best football. By 1997 he had lost some sharpness. He was the first great foreign import of the Premier League era, and his success opened the door to Dennis Bergkamp, Gianfranco Zola and the rest.

## Legacy

Cantona's legacy at [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) is the dynasty itself. He was the bridge between the Ferguson who nearly lost his job in 1990 and the treble-winning side of 1999, and the Class of '92 learned professionalism from watching a Frenchman practise volleys after everyone else had gone home. In the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league), he was the first foreign player to be the best player in the competition, and the model for the imports who came after. Tactically, he brought the number 10 and the deep-lying forward to a league that played 4-4-2 with two target men, a role later occupied at United by Teddy Sheringham, Dimitar Berbatov and Wayne Rooney. His relationship with [France](/nations/france) was never resolved: he was dropped by Aimé Jacquet in 1995 during his ban and watched his country win the 1998 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) without him. He became an actor and an occasional provocateur, and when asked what his greatest moment was, he said it was the kick at Selhurst Park. The collar stayed up.
