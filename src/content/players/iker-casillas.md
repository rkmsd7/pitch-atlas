## Overview

Iker Casillas was the goalkeeper and captain of the greatest [Spain](/nations/spain) team in history, which won [Euro](/tournaments/euro) 2008, the 2010 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) and Euro 2012, and the youngest goalkeeper to win the [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league) when he did so with [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) at 18 in 2000. He won three European Cups, five [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) titles and 167 caps, and was voted the world's best goalkeeper five years running. Spanish fans called him San Iker, Saint Iker, for the saves that seemed to arrive exactly when needed.

## Early life and breakthrough

Casillas was born in Móstoles, a working-class city south of Madrid, and joined Real Madrid's academy at nine. He was on the bench for a Champions League game at 16 and made his first-team debut in September 1999 under John Toshack, at 18, after Bodo Illgner was injured. Within eight months he had started the 2000 Champions League final against [Valencia](/clubs/valencia), winning 3-0.

His youth was not smooth. In 2001-02 Vicente del Bosque dropped him for César Sánchez after a run of errors, and he began the 2002 final against [Bayer Leverkusen](/clubs/bayer-leverkusen) on the bench. César was injured in the 68th minute, Casillas came on and made three extraordinary late saves to protect [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane)'s volley. He never lost his place again.

## Peak years

Through the Galáctico years and the leaner period that followed, Casillas was the one constant. He won La Liga in 2003, 2007 and 2008 and was consistently among Europe's top goalkeepers, with a one-on-one save rate no contemporary matched. He became Madrid captain in 2010 after [Raúl](/players/raul) left.

For Spain he was first choice from Euro 2004, and under Luis Aragonés and then Del Bosque he captained the team through three straight tournament wins. His save from Yakubu in the Euro 2008 quarter-final shoot-out against Italy ended a 44-year quarter-final curse; his saves from Robben and the tournament's Golden Glove defined 2010; and Spain conceded one goal in the whole of Euro 2012, where he kept clean sheets in the semi-final and final.

Under [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho) he won La Liga in 2012 with a record 100 points and saved two penalties in the Champions League semi-final shoot-out against [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), a game Madrid still lost. His relationship with Mourinho broke down over leaks and the Clásico wars, and he was dropped in December 2012 for Antonio Adán, then Diego López. He never fully regained his status, though Carlo Ancelotti picked him in cups, and he won the 2014 Champions League, La Décima, against [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) in Lisbon.

## How he played

Casillas was short for a goalkeeper at 1.85 m, and he played in a way that turned that into a strength: his set position was low and his reaction time over the first metre was extraordinary. He was at his best in one-on-ones, staying big and upright until the last moment and then spreading his legs or stabbing a boot at the ball. The 2010 final save from Robben, with his right foot, was a pure expression of this.

He was quick off his line for through-balls but not a sweeper in the modern sense. Crosses were his weakness; he was sometimes beaten in the air by big strikers, and his punching could be inconsistent. His distribution was ordinary, with throws better than his kicking, and the pass-out-from-the-back era arrived after his peak.

What he had was composure. Team-mates said he never looked rushed, and his shoot-out record, with saves in the 2008 and 2012 Euros and the 2012 Bayern semi-final, reflected a calm that did not transfer to his off-pitch life, where the Mourinho episode and his departure from Madrid in 2015 were bitter.

## Evolution

Under Toshack and Del Bosque he was a pure reaction keeper in a 4-4-2 or diamond. Under Mourinho's deeper, counter-attacking Madrid he was asked to face more shots and command a lower defensive line, which suited him. Ancelotti's higher line in 2013-14 asked him to sweep more, and he was at times exposed.

At [Porto](/clubs/porto) from 2015 he reinvented himself as a veteran leader, winning two [Primeira Liga](/leagues/primeira-liga) titles and setting the record for Champions League appearances by a player at the time, passing 180 in 2019. A heart attack in training in May 2019 ended his playing career; he announced his retirement in August 2020.

## Best plays and moments

- 2000, Champions League final, Valencia: a clean sheet at 18 in Paris in a 3-0 win.
- 2002, Champions League final, Bayer Leverkusen: three late saves off the bench, including a sprawling stop from Dimitar Berbatov. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Casillas+2002+Champions+League+final+Leverkusen+saves)
- 2008, Euro quarter-final, Italy: two shoot-out saves that broke Spain's quarter-final hoodoo.
- 2008, Euro final, Germany: a clean sheet and the captaincy of Spain's first title in 44 years.
- 2010, World Cup final, Netherlands: the outstretched foot that denied Robben in the 62nd minute. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Casillas+save+Robben+2010+World+Cup+final)
- 2011, Copa del Rey final, Barcelona: a crucial stop from Pedro before Cristiano Ronaldo's extra-time winner.
- 2012, Champions League semi-final, Bayern Munich: saved from Toni Kroos and Philipp Lahm in the shoot-out. [Watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Casillas+Bayern+2012+penalty+shootout)
- 2012, Euro final, Italy: Spain's third straight tournament win, with just one goal conceded all summer.
- 2014, Champions League final, Atlético Madrid: La Décima in Lisbon, despite an error for Atlético's goal.
- 2018, Champions League, Porto vs Lokomotiv Moscow: his 177th appearance in the competition, then a record.

## Legacy

Casillas is Real Madrid's record appearance maker until [Karim Benzema](/players/karim-benzema) and others closed in, and he, [Xavi](/players/xavi) and [Andrés Iniesta](/players/andres-iniesta) form the spine of the most successful national team of the modern era. His reconciliation with Madrid came slowly after 2015; he was welcomed back as a club ambassador in 2019 and returned to the Bernabéu to lift the trophy for the 2024 Champions League celebrations as a figurehead.

He is a reference for goalkeepers of modest height, and his one-on-one technique is still taught. With [Gianluigi Buffon](/players/gianluigi-buffon) and [Petr Čech](/players/petr-cech) he represents the last great generation of reflex goalkeepers before the sweeper-keeper took over, and the 2010 final save from Robben remains the single most replayed moment in Spanish football.
