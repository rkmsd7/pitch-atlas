## Overview

Robert Lewandowski spent 16 years as the standard against which European centre-forwards were measured. He scored 344 goals for [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), broke Gerd Müller's single-season Bundesliga record, was twice named FIFA's best player and then, at 34, moved to [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) and won three [La Liga](/leagues/la-liga) titles in four seasons. In June 2026, after Barcelona confirmed he would leave at the end of his contract, he signed for Chicago Fire in [MLS](/leagues/mls).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Warsaw to a judo champion father and a volleyball-playing mother, Lewandowski was released by Legia Warsaw's reserves as a teenager, a decision that still rankles in Poland. He rebuilt his career in the third tier with Znicz Pruszków, won the Ekstraklasa with Lech Poznań and moved to [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) in 2010 for around €4.5m. Under [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp) he was first a number 10, then the spearhead of two Bundesliga titles, and in April 2013 he scored all four goals in a 4-1 Champions League semi-final win over Real Madrid.

## Peak years

He joined Bayern on a free transfer in 2014 and became the most reliable scorer in world football. Five goals in nine minutes as a substitute against Wolfsburg in 2015 remains one of the sport's great statistical freaks. He won the Bundesliga every season he was in Munich, the treble in 2020 under Hansi Flick (15 Champions League goals, FIFA Best), and in 2020-21 scored 41 league goals to beat Müller's 49-year-old record. Many felt he should have won the Ballon d'Or in 2020, the year it was cancelled.

Barcelona paid around €45m in 2022. He was Pichichi in his first season as Barcelona won the league, scored 42 in 2024-25 as they won a domestic treble under [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick) again, and added a third title in 2025-26 before leaving as the club's 14th all-time scorer.

## How he plays

Lewandowski's movement in the box is the textbook: a check away from the near-post defender, then a sharp move across him as the cross is struck. He finishes with either foot, with his head, from any angle, and his first touch under pressure is good enough that half-chances become clear ones. He is also a better link player than his goal totals suggest, holding the ball up for wingers and laying off first time.

The weakness, especially after 35, is mobility. He does not press hard, does not stretch defences in behind, and in Flick's high-pressing system he was sometimes a passenger out of possession. His penalty record, while good, has had notable failures in big matches.

## Evolution

Klopp used him as a second striker before making him the focal point of a gegenpressing 4-2-3-1. At Bayern under Guardiola he learned to play as the reference point of a possession team; under Flick in 2020 he became the hardest-working forward in a pressing side. At Barcelona he was a pure finisher, the man who turned [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal) and [Raphinha](/players/raphinha)'s creation into goals. In Chicago, under Gregg Berhalter, he is the veteran reference point of a rebuilding franchise.

## Current situation

Lewandowski joined Chicago Fire on a deal running through the 2027-28 MLS season, announced on 29 June 2026 and formalised with his first training session on 14 July. The club's head coach and director of football Gregg Berhalter has made him the centrepiece of an ambitious rebuild. For [Poland](/nations/poland), he remains the all-time record scorer; his future with the national team beyond the 2026 World Cup cycle is, as of November 2026, undecided.
