## Overview

Birgit Prinz was the dominant forward of the 2000s and the player who turned [Germany](/nations/germany-women) from European champions into world champions. She won two [World Cups](/tournaments/womens-world-cup), five Euros and three Olympic bronzes, was FIFA World Player of the Year three times running from 2003 to 2005, and retired in 2011 with 128 goals in 214 internationals, a German record, and 14 World Cup goals, second only to Marta.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Frankfurt, Prinz debuted for FSV Frankfurt at 16 and for Germany at 16 in 1994, scoring on her debut against Canada. She was a European champion at 17 in 1995 and at the 1995 World Cup reached the final, lost to Norway, as a teenager.

## Peak years

Prinz moved to 1. FFC Frankfurt in 1998 and, apart from one season in the American WUSA with Carolina Courage in 2002, stayed until retirement, winning nine Bundesliga titles and three UEFA Women's Cups. The 2003 World Cup in the United States was her peak: seven goals, the Golden Ball and Golden Boot, and a 2-1 extra-time win over Sweden in the final. She was so dominant that Serie A club Perugia offered her a contract to play in the men's league, which she declined. Germany retained the title in 2007 in China without conceding a goal, Prinz scoring in the 2-0 final against [Brazil](/nations/brazil-women) and lifting the trophy as captain. She also won Euros in 1997, 2001, 2005 and 2009. Her international career ended after the 2011 home World Cup, when Germany were knocked out by Japan in the quarter-finals.

## How she plays

Prinz was built like a modern athlete at a time when few women's teams trained professionally. She was quick over any distance, physically dominant in duels and clinical with both feet; her heading was a weapon and her work rate in pressing was years ahead of its time. She could play as the sole striker or alongside a partner, and she carried the ball from deep when needed. Critics of her era noted that she lacked the flair of Marta or Mia Hamm's dribbling; her answer was goals.

## Evolution

A raw teenage forward in 1995, a complete striker by 2003, and a captain and leader by 2007. In her final years she was used less as a runner and more as a target, but she remained Germany's record scorer and the standard for every German forward since, including [Alexandra Popp](/players/alexandra-popp).

## Best plays and moments

- Debut goal for Germany at 16, 1994.
- Seven goals, Golden Ball and Golden Boot at the 2003 World Cup.
- Goal in the 2007 World Cup final, Germany 2-0 Brazil, Shanghai.
- Three consecutive FIFA World Player of the Year awards, 2003 to 2005.
- Three UEFA Women's Cups with Frankfurt, the last in 2008.

## Legacy / Current situation

Prinz retired in 2011, trained as a psychologist and worked with the German federation and Hoffenheim's men's team. She remains Germany's all-time leading scorer and the benchmark against which the [Frauen-Bundesliga](/leagues/frauen-bundesliga)'s strikers are still measured.
