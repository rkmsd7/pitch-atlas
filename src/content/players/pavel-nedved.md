## Overview

Pavel Nedvěd arrived at [Juventus](/clubs/juventus) in 2001 as the replacement for [Zinedine Zidane](/players/zinedine-zidane), a task that seemed impossible, and two years later won the Ballon d'Or. He was the engine of two Scudetto-winning sides, the heart of [Lazio](/clubs/lazio)'s 2000 champions, and the captain of a Czech Republic team that reached the Euro 1996 final and the Euro 2004 semi-finals. He is the only Czech to win the Ballon d'Or since Josef Masopust in 1962.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Cheb in western Bohemia in August 1972, Nedvěd came through at Dukla Prague and moved to Sparta Prague in 1992, winning three league titles. His breakthrough was Euro 96 in England, where the Czech Republic beat Italy and [Portugal](/nations/portugal) and reached the final against [Germany](/nations/germany), losing to Oliver Bierhoff's golden goal. Nedvěd, 23, scored against Italy and ran games from midfield. Lazio signed him within weeks.

## Peak years

Under Sven-Göran Eriksson, Nedvěd was a left-sided midfielder in a side packed with stars, and in 1999 he scored the winning goal in the last-ever Cup Winners' Cup final against Mallorca. In 2000 Lazio won the Scudetto and Coppa Italia double, with Nedvěd a relentless presence beside Juan Sebastián Verón.

Juventus paid around €41 million for him in July 2001 when Zidane left for [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid). Under Marcello Lippi he was moved around but found his home on the left of a midfield diamond or as a trequartista, winning Serie A in 2002 and 2003. In 2002-03 he was the best player in Europe: 11 goals, decisive strikes against [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) in the quarter-final and Real Madrid in the semi-final, and then a yellow card in that semi-final second leg that suspended him from the final against [AC Milan](/clubs/ac-milan), which Juventus lost on penalties. He won the Ballon d'Or that December ahead of [Thierry Henry](/players/thierry-henry) and Paolo Maldini.

## How he played

Nedvěd was a wide player who refused to stay wide. From the left he cut inside onto his right foot to shoot, or went outside on his left to cross, and he was so close to genuinely two-footed that defenders could not show him one way. His striking technique on volleys and half-volleys was exceptional, and he scored many goals from 25 yards.

His stamina was the engine of everything. He covered the left flank from box to box, pressed, tackled hard and often, and arrived late in the area. He was an aggressive, almost reckless competitor, which brought cards and the 2003 suspension that still hurts him. He was not a natural creator in the Zidane sense; he was a destroyer-and-driver whose end product came from running and shooting rather than slow vision.

Weaknesses: discipline, a limited passing range in tight areas compared with the great number tens, and a reluctance to rest that cost him in big moments.

## Evolution

At Sparta and early Lazio he was a left midfielder in a flat four. Eriksson made him a wide forward in a 4-4-2 and then a free midfielder in a 4-3-1-2 diamond. Lippi used him on the left of the diamond behind [Alessandro Del Piero](/players/alessandro-del-piero) and David Trezeguet, where his inside runs created the overload that won two titles. Under Fabio Capello in 2005-06 he was a wide midfielder in a 4-4-2 with [Gianluigi Buffon](/players/gianluigi-buffon) and [Fabio Cannavaro](/players/fabio-cannavaro) behind him. After Calciopoli he stayed through Serie B at 34, like Del Piero and Buffon, then played two more Serie A seasons under Claudio Ranieri before retiring in 2009 at 36. He retired from the Czech national team after Euro 2004 but returned for the 2006 World Cup.

## Legacy

Nedvěd's Ballon d'Or is one of the least argued over of the 2000s: he was the best player in Europe in 2002-03 by the simplest measures of goals, running and influence. He is second only to Masopust among Czech footballers and the last of the great two-footed wide midfielders of the pre-inverted-winger era. After retirement he became a Juventus director and then vice-chairman, serving from 2015 until the entire board resigned in November 2022 during the club's accounting investigation. He has since been involved with the Czech national federation. The nickname Furia Ceca, the Czech Fury, describes him exactly.
