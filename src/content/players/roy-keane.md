## Overview

Roy Keane was the captain of [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united)'s most successful period, a midfielder whose refusal to accept anything less than victory became the defining culture of [Alex Ferguson](/managers/alex-ferguson)'s dynasty. He won seven [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) titles and the 1999 [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league), though suspension kept him out of the final after the greatest individual performance of his career in the semi. He left Ireland's 2002 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) squad before a ball was kicked, and he left United after a television interview that criticised his teammates. Nothing about him was quiet.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Cork, Keane was turned down by several English clubs as a teenager for being too small. He played for Cobh Ramblers in the League of Ireland until Nottingham Forest scout Noel McCabe spotted him in 1990. Brian Clough signed him for 47,000 pounds and threw him into the first team at 19. He reached the 1991 FA Cup final and scored in the 1992 League Cup final, and when Forest were relegated in 1993, Ferguson beat Blackburn to his signature for a British record 3.75 million pounds.

## Peak years

Keane won the Double in his first season at United and became captain in 1997 when Eric Cantona retired. A cruciate injury that autumn cost him the rest of the campaign, but he returned in 1998-99 to drive the treble. In the semi-final second leg at [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), with United 2-0 down, he scored with a header and then, after a booking that ruled him out of the final, played with a fury and control that Ferguson called the most emphatic display of selflessness he had seen.

He was PFA and FWA Player of the Year in 2000 and the key figure in the three successive titles from 1999 to 2001. He captained United to further titles in 2003, when he was outstanding in the run-in.

## How he played

Keane was the complete midfielder of his era, less a specialist than the sum of everything a central midfielder is asked to do. He won the ball through anticipation and a tackle that was hard and usually legal. He took the ball under pressure, kept it with two touches and moved it to Scholes, Beckham or Giggs, always turning his body to keep options open. He pressed the opposing playmaker personally, and he scored enough, 51 for United, to matter in big games.

His great gift was intensity: the capacity to run at the same speed in the 90th minute as in the first, and to demand the same of everyone else. He would berate teammates for passing backwards or for losing concentration in a game already won.

Weaknesses were discipline and injuries. He was sent off 13 times at United, including a premeditated tackle on Alf-Inge Haaland in 2001 that he later admitted in his autobiography. His hip and knee injuries after 2002 reduced his range, and his temper eventually made the dressing room unmanageable.

## Evolution

Under Clough he was an attacking midfielder who scored. Under Ferguson he became a box-to-box engine in a 4-4-2 and then, after the 1997 injury and a 2002 hip operation, a deeper, more positional player who read the game rather than covered it. The feud with Mick McCarthy at Saipan in 2002 ended his international career for two years. In 2005 an MUTV interview attacking younger teammates was pulled, and Ferguson released him. He finished with a short spell at [Celtic](/clubs/celtic), winning the Scottish title before retiring in 2006.

- 2000, Premier League: a title and both Player of the Year awards.

- 1994, Premier League v Chelsea: the FA Cup final run in his first Double.

- 2003, Premier League v Arsenal: the 2-2 at Highbury in the title turnaround.
- 1992, League Cup final v Manchester United: a Forest goal against his future club.
- 1999, Intercontinental Cup v Palmeiras: a goal in Tokyo to complete the year.
- 2006, Scottish Cup v Clyde: his Celtic debut, a defeat he later called the worst of his career.

## Legacy

Keane managed Sunderland to promotion in 2007 and later Ipswich, and was Martin O'Neill's assistant with Ireland from 2013 to 2018. He is now a television pundit whose bluntness has made him as famous to a new generation as his tackling was to the last. For United he remains the embodiment of Ferguson's standards, and in the argument about the greatest Premier League midfielder, his name is always on the shortlist with [Patrick Vieira](/players/patrick-vieira), [Steven Gerrard](/players/steven-gerrard) and [Paul Scholes](/players/paul-scholes).
