## Overview

Arjen Robben played a decade for [Bayern Munich](/clubs/bayern-munich), won eight Bundesliga titles and the 2013 [Champions League](/leagues/champions-league), in which he scored the 89th-minute winner against [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund) at Wembley. Together with [Franck Ribéry](/players/franck-ribery) on the opposite wing he formed "Robbery", the most feared wing pairing in Europe for the best part of ten years. For the [Netherlands](/nations/netherlands) he reached the 2010 [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup) final and was the standout player of the 2014 tournament, winning the Bronze Ball as the Dutch finished third.

## Early life and breakthrough

Robben was born in Bedum in the far north of the Netherlands and came through at Groningen, debuting at 16 in December 2000. [PSV](/clubs/psv-eindhoven) signed him in 2002 and he won the Eredivisie in his first season under Guus Hiddink; he was named the league's Talent of the Year. [Manchester United](/clubs/manchester-united) wanted him but [Chelsea](/clubs/chelsea) paid £12m in 2004, and after a broken foot delayed his debut he was electrifying in [José Mourinho](/managers/jose-mourinho)'s first title season, scoring seven and setting the tone for a 4-3-3 that won the Premier League with a record 95 points. He won a second title in 2006.

## Peak years

Two years at [Real Madrid](/clubs/real-madrid) produced a La Liga title in 2008 but a difficult relationship with the club; he was sold to Bayern for €25m in August 2009 and scored on his debut against Wolfsburg. The 2009-10 season was the breakout: 23 goals, a Bundesliga and DFB-Pokal double, German Footballer of the Year, a volley against Manchester United that sent Bayern to the Champions League semi-final, and a run to the final, lost to Mourinho's [Inter](/clubs/inter-milan). In 2012 he missed a penalty in extra time of the Champions League final in Bayern's own stadium against Chelsea; in 2013 he redeemed himself with the goal against Dortmund, dribbling past Roman Weidenfeller after Ribéry's backheel. Under [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) from 2013 to 2016 he was still Bayern's most dangerous player, scoring 21 in 2014-15. His final Bayern season, 2018-19, ended with a goal in his last game.

At the 2010 World Cup he played the final against [Spain](/nations/spain) despite a hamstring injury and had the chance to win it, denied by [Iker Casillas](/players/iker-casillas)'s outstretched foot. In 2014 he scored twice in the 5-1 demolition of Spain, running from his own half past [Sergio Ramos](/players/sergio-ramos) for the second, and won the penalty that beat [Mexico](/nations/mexico).

## How he played

Robben's move is the most famous signature in the modern game: a drop of the shoulder to the right, a drag with the outside of the left foot and then a curling shot into the far corner from the edge of the box. The defender knew; the full-back was told; it rarely mattered. The reasons were his acceleration, measured as among the fastest in the Bundesliga into his thirties, the quality of his first touch at speed and the consistency of his strike, which hit the same spot at the same pace every time.

He was a willing, intelligent presser and a dangerous late arriver at the far post for the diagonal from Ribéry. Weaknesses: his right foot was weak, and when forced wide he was far less dangerous; he was constantly injured, with muscle problems ruining the 2010-11 and parts of nearly every season; and he was accused of diving, notably against Mexico in 2014, which he admitted in one case. He could be selfish in the final third, shooting when a pass was better.

## Evolution

At PSV and Chelsea he was a touchline winger on the left or right; at Madrid and Bayern he was almost exclusively an inverted right winger, and Louis van Gaal built Bayern's 4-2-3-1 around him and Ribéry. Guardiola occasionally used him as a striker or in a front two with Robert Lewandowski and drilled his movement in the half-spaces. He played deeper and less often in his final years under Carlo Ancelotti and Niko Kovač, accepting a rotation role, and retired in 2019 before a brief, injury-blighted comeback at Groningen in 2020-21. He retired from international football in October 2017 after the Netherlands failed to qualify for the 2018 World Cup, scoring twice in his final match against Sweden.

## Best plays and moments

- 2005, Premier League, Chelsea 1-0 Everton: a solo run and finish in Mourinho's first title season.
- 2010, Champions League quarter-final, Bayern 2-3 Manchester United (agg. 4-4): the volley from Ribéry's corner that sent Bayern through on away goals ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Arjen+Robben+volley+Manchester+United+2010)).
- 2010, Champions League semi-final v Lyon: a 30-yard goal in the first leg.
- 2010, World Cup final v Spain: the one-on-one with Casillas, saved by a toe.
- 2012, Champions League final v Chelsea: the extra-time penalty saved by Petr Čech.
- 2013, Champions League final, Bayern 2-1 Borussia Dortmund: the 89th-minute winner ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Arjen+Robben+winner+Champions+League+final+2013+Dortmund)).
- 2013, DFB-Pokal final v Stuttgart: a goal to complete the treble.
- 2014, World Cup, Spain 1-5 Netherlands: two goals, the second a 70-metre run past Ramos ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Arjen+Robben+Netherlands+Spain+2014+World+Cup)).
- 2015, Bundesliga, Bayern 2-0 Hannover: a typical cut-inside goal in a 21-goal season.
- 2019, Bundesliga, Bayern 5-1 Eintracht Frankfurt: a goal in his final Bayern game to secure an eighth title.

## Legacy

Robben is the proof that predictability is not a flaw if the execution is good enough. Inverted wingers who cut onto their stronger foot, from [Mohamed Salah](/players/mohamed-salah) to [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal), are working in a template he and [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) perfected at the same time. At Bayern he is in the Hall of Fame and the Robben-Ribéry decade is the most successful in the club's history; with [Philipp Lahm](/players/philipp-lahm), [Bastian Schweinsteiger](/players/bastian-schweinsteiger), [Thomas Müller](/players/thomas-muller) and [Manuel Neuer](/players/manuel-neuer) he defined the 2013 treble side. For the Netherlands, the 2010 final chance is the great what-if; he never quite forgave himself.
