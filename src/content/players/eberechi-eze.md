## Overview

Eberechi Eze is the player who gave [Crystal Palace](/clubs/crystal-palace) their first major trophy and then gave [Arsenal](/clubs/arsenal) a different kind of creativity. His goal won the 2025 FA Cup final against [Manchester City](/clubs/manchester-city); three months later he joined Arsenal, the club that had released him as a boy, and won the [Premier League](/leagues/premier-league) in his first season. He is a [England](/nations/england) international who went to Euro 2024 and the 2026 World Cup.

## Early life and breakthrough

Eze was born in Greenwich to Nigerian parents and was released by Arsenal at 13, then by Fulham, Reading and Millwall. Queens Park Rangers took him in 2016, and after a Football League loan at Wycombe Wanderers he became QPR's standout player in the Championship, scoring 14 goals in 2019-20. Palace paid around £17m in August 2020.

His first Palace season was promising; his second was wrecked by an Achilles tendon rupture in May 2021 that kept him out for seven months. The player who returned under Patrick Vieira and then Oliver Glasner was better: more direct, more decisive and fitter.

## Peak years

Under Glasner in 2024-25 Eze was the creative heart of a Palace side playing the best football in the club's history, finishing with 14 goals in all competitions and the winner at Wembley against City in May 2025. He had already established himself with England, making the Euro 2024 squad and starting matches under [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel).

Arsenal triggered his release clause in August 2025 for around £60m. In 2025-26 he shared creative duties with [Martin Ødegaard](/players/martin-odegaard) and gave [Mikel Arteta](/managers/mikel-arteta) a dribbler from the left and the middle that the team had lacked. The season ended with the title, Arsenal's first in 22 years, and a Champions League final in Budapest lost 4-3 on penalties to [Paris Saint-Germain](/clubs/paris-saint-germain).

## How he plays

Eze's movement looks lazy and is anything but. He drifts into pockets of space between the opposition's midfield and defence, receives with his back half-turned and then does the thing that makes him special: a drop of the shoulder, a slow step, a sudden burst that takes him clear of two defenders. He is genuinely two-footed, which makes him hard to show one way, and he shoots from distance with real venom, including free-kicks from 25 yards.

His decision-making has matured from the QPR days when he would beat three men and lose the ball to the fourth. He now picks the moment to release [Bukayo Saka](/players/bukayo-saka) or the full-back, and his through balls from the left half-space are a growing feature. He is an average presser by Arsenal's standards and can disappear in games where the opposition sits deep and denies him space to run into, which is why Arteta often uses him as an impact option.

## Evolution

At QPR he was a number ten and a roaming left-sided player with licence to do what he liked. Palace under Roy Hodgson played him as an attacking midfielder in a 4-4-2; Vieira and then Glasner used him as a number ten in a 3-4-2-1, pairing him with Ismaïla Sarr and Jean-Philippe Mateta, where his dribbling in transition was devastating. At Arsenal he has mostly played off the left of a 4-3-3 or as a left-sided number eight, roles that ask him to combine more and carry less, and he has adapted without losing his edge.

## Current situation

Eze remains an Arsenal player in 2026-27, where he competes with Ødegaard and [Kai Havertz](/players/kai-havertz) for the creative roles behind the striker and with [Gabriel Martinelli](/players/gabriel-martinelli) on the left. The champions started the season with a 2-0 win over Coventry City and sat top of the table in early November. For [England](/nations/england) under Tuchel he is a squad regular whose ability to change a game from the bench makes him a tournament asset.
