## Overview

Luis Suárez is the most prolific [Uruguay](/nations/uruguay) forward ever, with 69 international goals, and one of the few strikers to win the European Golden Shoe twice. He scored 111 goals for [Ajax](/clubs/ajax), 82 for [Liverpool](/clubs/liverpool) and 198 for [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona), where he formed the MSN trident with [Lionel Messi](/players/lionel-messi) and Neymar and won the 2015 treble. He has also been banned for biting three opponents and for racially abusing Patrice Evra, which is why his name is spoken differently in different cities.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Salto in January 1987 and raised in Montevideo, Suárez came through at Nacional, where he was nearly released as a teenager for lack of discipline. He scored 12 goals in his first season and was signed by Groningen in the Netherlands at 19. A year later Ajax paid €7.5 million, and in 2009-10, as captain, he scored 49 goals in all competitions, a figure that won him Dutch Footballer of the Year. The bite on PSV's Otman Bakkal in November 2010 brought a seven-match ban, and in January 2011 Liverpool paid £22.8 million, the deal overshadowed on the day by Andy Carroll's arrival.

## Peak years

At Liverpool under Kenny Dalglish and then Brendan Rodgers he became the Premier League's most dangerous forward. The 2011 Evra incident cost him an eight-match ban and much of his reputation in England; the football that followed was extraordinary. In 2013-14, after a ten-game ban for biting Branislav Ivanović, he scored 31 league goals in 33 games, won the Golden Boot and Footballer of the Year, and drove Liverpool to within two points of the title alongside [Steven Gerrard](/players/steven-gerrard) and Daniel Sturridge.

Barcelona paid £65 million in July 2014 while he served a four-month ban for biting Giorgio Chiellini at the World Cup. Under [Luis Enrique](/managers/luis-enrique) he adjusted his game to Messi, scored 25 goals in his first season including the decisive goal in the Champions League final against [Juventus](/clubs/juventus), and then 59 in all competitions in 2015-16, when he won the Pichichi and a second Golden Shoe. MSN scored 364 goals in three seasons.

## How he played

Suárez's defining quality was persistence. He pressed centre-backs and goalkeepers alone, won the ball high, and chased lost causes until defenders made errors. His movement was unpredictable, dropping into the left channel to receive, then spinning in behind. He dribbled with a scurrying, off-balance style that made him hard to tackle cleanly.

His finishing was varied: chips, near-post drives, first-time volleys, headers despite modest height, and a special ability from tight angles. He was a clever passer in the final third, with 16 league assists in 2015-16. His weaknesses were temperament and theatrical reactions to contact. Pace was average and he relied on anticipation rather than acceleration, which is why he aged well in the box.

## Evolution

At Ajax he was a wide forward or second striker with a huge goal return. Rodgers used him as the spearhead of a 4-3-3 and then alongside Sturridge in a 3-5-2 diamond that was the most exciting attack in England. At Barcelona he became a pure number nine who worked the right channel to give Messi the central space, a sacrifice that underpinned MSN. Knee surgery in January 2020 and a worn body saw Barcelona release him at 33, after which he won La Liga with [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid) in 2021, scoring the title-clinching goal. He went home to Nacional, had a prolific year at Grêmio in the [Brasileirão](/leagues/brasileirao), and reunited with Messi at [Inter Miami](/clubs/inter-miami) in 2024, winning the Supporters' Shield, the 2025 Leagues Cup and the 2025 MLS Cup. He re-signed for one final season in 2026, which the club confirmed as his last. He retired from Uruguay in September 2024.

## Legacy

Suárez is a divisive giant. On the pitch he ranks among the elite finishers of the 21st century: 500-plus career goals, two Golden Shoes, and a treble as a central striker in an attack with the best player who ever lived. His combination of pressing, link play and ruthlessness influenced a generation of South American forwards. The bans and the Evra case remain part of his story, and he has acknowledged them with varying degrees of contrition. With Uruguay he won the 2011 Copa América and reached a World Cup semi-final, and he leaves the international game as its record scorer. As of November 2026 he is closing out his career at Inter Miami alongside Messi, the partnership that defined his best years.
