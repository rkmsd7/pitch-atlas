## Overview

Karim Adeyemi is pure speed with a growing football brain. He was the fastest player in the [Bundesliga](/leagues/bundesliga) for four seasons at [Borussia Dortmund](/clubs/borussia-dortmund), scored one of the Champions League goals of 2023 against Chelsea and a hat-trick against Celtic in 2024, and reached the 2024 final at Wembley. In July 2026, after years of speculation, [Barcelona](/clubs/barcelona) signed him for around €22m on a contract to 2031, as a counter-attacking option behind [Raphinha](/players/raphinha) and [Lamine Yamal](/players/lamine-yamal).

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Munich to a Nigerian father and a Romanian mother, Adeyemi was released by Bayern's academy at 10, came through at Unterhaching and was signed by Red Bull Salzburg at 16 in 2018. He broke through in 2020-21 and in 2021-22 scored 23 goals in all competitions, winning three Austrian doubles and making his Germany debut in September 2021, scoring with his first touch against Armenia. Dortmund paid around €30m in summer 2022.

## Peak years

His Dortmund years were a cycle of injury and brilliance. The 2022-23 season brought a stunning solo goal against Chelsea in the Champions League and the agonising final-day title collapse against Mainz. In 2023-24 he was part of the run to the Champions League final, and in 2024-25 he scored a hat-trick against Celtic in a 7-1 win and was Dortmund's most dangerous attacker under [Niko Kovač](/managers/niko-kovac). He was a squad player for Germany at Euro 2024 and missed the 2026 World Cup squad. Barcelona, which had admired him since the 2022 win in Dortmund, moved in summer 2026.

## How he plays

Adeyemi's acceleration over the first ten metres is the best in Germany. He plays off the left or as a second striker and his game is running in behind, receiving the through-ball and finishing early with his right foot across the keeper. He is aggressive in the press and tracks back willingly. His dribbling is straight-line rather than tricky.

The weaknesses are decision-making and consistency. He has periods where he makes the wrong choice at the end of every run, his finishing has fluctuated, and his crossing is average. Hamstring injuries have interrupted every Dortmund season. At Barcelona, in a possession system, he will need to learn to play with less space.

## Evolution

A striker at Salzburg in a 4-4-2 counter-pressing system, a left winger under Edin Terzić at Dortmund, a wide forward under Kovač, and now, under [Hansi Flick](/managers/hansi-flick), a rotation option across the front three who gives Barcelona a direct outlet when Raphinha or Yamal rest. For [Germany](/nations/germany), he is on the fringes of [Jürgen Klopp](/managers/jurgen-klopp)'s squad.

The Barcelona move is a test of whether Adeyemi can become a possession-team footballer. Flick's side keep the ball for long spells and attack a set defence far more often than Dortmund did, which means fewer of the open-field sprints that defined his best Bundesliga moments. His early appearances suggest a willingness to adapt: more touches inside the box, more runs off the shoulder of the last defender timed to Pedri's passes, and a pressing intensity that fits Flick's demands. Whether the decision-making catches up with the athleticism will determine whether he becomes a starter or a specialist substitute, but at 24 and with a contract to 2031 Barcelona have bought time as much as a player.
