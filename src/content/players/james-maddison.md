## Overview

James Maddison is the kind of playmaker English football keeps saying it does not produce. From Coventry's academy via Norwich and Leicester, he reached [Tottenham Hotspur](/clubs/tottenham-hotspur) in 2023 as the Premier League's most inventive No.10, and his first autumn in north London, with Ange Postecoglou's side top of the table, was the most exciting football of his career. He won the Europa League with Spurs in 2025, then ruptured his anterior cruciate ligament in pre-season that August and missed almost all of 2025-26. His return in April 2026 coincided with [Roberto De Zerbi](/managers/roberto-de-zerbi)'s arrival, and in 2026-27 he is working his way back to the centre of the team.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Coventry, Maddison came through Coventry City's academy and made his debut at 17 in League One. Norwich City signed him in 2016, loaned him to Aberdeen, where he scored a stoppage-time free-kick against Rangers, and then built their Championship side around him in 2017-18, when he scored 14 goals and was named in the division's team of the year. Leicester City paid around £22m in June 2018.

## Peak years

At Leicester under Claude Puel and Brendan Rodgers he was the creative hub of a side that finished fifth twice and won the FA Cup in 2021, when he played in the final against Chelsea. His best season was 2021-22 with 18 goals in all competitions; his best half-season was the autumn of 2022, which earned him a place in England's World Cup squad. Leicester were relegated in 2023 and Spurs paid £40m.

He was Spurs' player of the season by Christmas 2023, with Postecoglou's team top of the league in November and Maddison pulling every string, before an ankle injury derailed both. He was in and out in 2024-25, a season that ended with the Europa League win over Manchester United in Bilbao, though he missed the final through injury. The ACL rupture came in a pre-season friendly against Newcastle in Seoul in August 2025.

## How he plays

Maddison lives between the lines. He finds the pocket behind the opposition midfield, takes the ball on the half-turn and either threads a through ball to a runner or plays a disguised reverse pass to the overlapping full-back. His set pieces are among the best in the league: in-swinging corners, free-kicks curled over the wall with his right foot, and the occasional direct strike from 30 yards. He shoots well from the edge of the box and scores a steady eight to ten a season when fit.

He is not a runner or a presser, and his defensive contribution has been an issue for every manager he has played under. He is slight and not quick. When a team builds through him he can decide a match; when it bypasses him he can disappear, which is why his international career has been sporadic despite his talent.

## Evolution

Norwich and Leicester used him as a No.10 in a 4-2-3-1 and, under Rodgers, as a left-sided No.8 in a 4-3-3 with freedom to drift. Postecoglou used him as the left No.8 in a 4-3-3 with [Son Heung-min](/players/son-heung-min) inside him, which produced his best Spurs form. De Zerbi's possession system, with its emphasis on provoking the press and then playing through it, suits a receiver of his type, and he has been used as the No.10 in a 4-2-3-1 ahead of [Sandro Tonali](/players/sandro-tonali).

## Best plays and moments

- 2016, Scottish Premiership v Rangers: a stoppage-time free-kick winner for Aberdeen at Pittodrie.
- 2018, Championship with Norwich: 14 goals and a place in the team of the year.
- 2019, Premier League v Southampton: part of Leicester's 9-0 win, the joint-biggest in Premier League history.
- 2021, FA Cup final v Chelsea: a first major trophy at Wembley.
- 2022, Premier League v Brentford: a goal and an assist in a season of 18 goals.
- 2023, Premier League v Manchester United: a goal on his home Spurs debut in a 2-0 win ([Tottenham highlights](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=James+Maddison+Tottenham+goals+assists)).
- 2023, Premier League v Burnley: two assists and a goal as Spurs go top in September.
- 2025, Europa League: a part in the run that brought Spurs their first trophy since 2008.
- 2026, Premier League v Leeds, April: returns from a nine-month ACL absence as a late substitute ([return](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=James+Maddison+return+injury+2026+Tottenham)).

## Current situation

Maddison returned to the bench against Brighton on 18 April 2026, De Zerbi's first match in charge, and came on against Leeds to end nine months out. He spoke of "dark days" in his rehabilitation. In October 2026 he made his first start in 17 months in a 2-0 win at Chelsea, but limped off after 51 minutes; De Zerbi confirmed him available again for the Crystal Palace game that followed and said "I hope he will be crucial for us". Spurs, who spent more than £300m in the summer on Tonali, [Omar Marmoush](/players/omar-marmoush) and others, drew 0-0 at Leeds on 7 November. He wears No.10. His England career under [Thomas Tuchel](/managers/thomas-tuchel) is on hold.
