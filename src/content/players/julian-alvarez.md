## Overview

Julián Álvarez has won almost everything available to a footballer before 27: the [World Cup](/tournaments/world-cup), two [Copa Américas](/tournaments/copa-america), the Champions League, the Premier League, the Copa Libertadores. He chose to leave a treble-winning Manchester City in 2024 to be the main man at [Atlético Madrid](/clubs/atletico-madrid), and under Diego Simeone he has become exactly that. In 2026 he was Argentina's starting striker in the World Cup final against [Spain](/nations/spain), lost 1-0 after extra time.

## Early life and breakthrough

Born in Calchín, a small town in Córdoba province, Álvarez trialled at Real Madrid at 11 and was turned down for being too young to sign. [River Plate](/clubs/river-plate) took him, and under Marcelo Gallardo he debuted at 18 in 2018, coming off the bench in the Copa Libertadores final against Boca Juniors in Madrid that December. In 2021 he scored 18 league goals in a title-winning season and was named South American Footballer of the Year.

Manchester City paid around £14m in January 2022, leaving him at River until the summer. He arrived at the Etihad as Erling Haaland's understudy and ended his first season with a Champions League, a Premier League and an FA Cup, having also won the World Cup in between, scoring four goals in Qatar including two in the semi-final against Croatia.

## Peak years

His second City season, 2023-24, brought 19 goals from a hybrid second-striker role and another league title, but [Pep Guardiola](/managers/pep-guardiola) could not give him the central role he wanted. Atlético paid around €75m in August 2024. His first season produced 29 goals in all competitions, a fourth-place finish and a Champions League last-16 exit to Real Madrid on penalties that turned on his disallowed double-touch spot-kick. In 2025-26 he was again Atlético's top scorer as the club finished in the Champions League places. At the 2026 World Cup he started every knockout game for Lionel Scaloni's Argentina, who beat England in the semi-final before losing the final.

## How he plays

Álvarez is the model of the modern pressing striker. He hunts centre-backs, closes passing lanes and recovers the ball high up the pitch more often than almost any other forward in Europe. In possession he drops into the space between the lines, links with one or two touches and then attacks the box again. His finishing is varied: first-time strikes, chips, headers despite his height, and a reliable penalty.

He is not a target man and does not win much in the air, and in a team that plays long balls to him he can be marginalised. His individual dribbling is good rather than exceptional. His most productive football has come with a creative partner, Griezmann at Atlético or Messi for Argentina, to play off.

## Evolution

Gallardo used him as a winger and striker at River. Guardiola used him as a false 9, a 10 and a right winger, rarely as the main striker. Simeone built his 2024-25 attack around Álvarez and [Antoine Griezmann](/players/antoine-griezmann) as a front two; since Griezmann's departure in summer 2026 he is the undisputed reference point, often partnered by [Álex Baena](/players/alex-baena) behind him. For Argentina, Scaloni uses him as the striker in front of Messi.

## Best plays and moments

- 2018, Copa Libertadores final, v Boca Juniors: a winner's medal at 18 in Madrid.
- 2021, Argentine Primera: 18 goals and the South American player of the year award.
- 2022, World Cup semi-final, v Croatia: two goals in a 3-0 win ([watch](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Julian+Alvarez+goals+Croatia+World+Cup+2022)).
- 2022, World Cup final, v France: started and won on penalties.
- 2023, Champions League final, v Inter: a treble winner in Istanbul.
- 2024, Copa America final, v Colombia: a second continental title.
- 2025, La Liga, v Real Madrid: a goal in the Madrid derby.
- 2026, World Cup final, v Spain: a runner-up medal in New Jersey.

## Current situation

Álvarez is Atlético's captain-in-waiting and undisputed first-choice striker in 2026-27 under Simeone, with Griezmann gone and [Álex Baena](/players/alex-baena) supplying him. His contract runs to 2030. For [Argentina](/nations/argentina), he is the starting striker in a team whose coach, Scaloni, has a contract to December 2026 and an unresolved future.
